Standard RSA, compared on the reference-game literature

Sixty-six textbook rational-speech-act theories, crossed on recursion depth, where a salience prior enters, what makes an object salient, how literal word meanings are, and whether words cost. Each is fitted with grouped cross-validation; every number here is out-of-fold log predictive density, in nats, against plain RSA (depth 1, nothing else).

One cell per theory. Blue cells predict people's choices better than plain RSA, orange worse; the number is the difference in nats. Click a cell to look at that theory's displays below.

How to read it

Fits: grouped 5-fold cross-validation (whole experimental conditions held out together), NUTS with 4 chains. Uncertainty: in the grid's tooltips, the difference from plain RSA over its stimulus-clustered standard error. A display's predictions are exact out-of-fold probabilities, recovered from the per-trial scores; where two or more kinds of object were never chosen, they share the remaining probability equally (marked "est.").

Theories marked † did not converge (R-hat or divergences); read their numbers with care. Built from scripts/rsa/open_compute_checks/grid_viz.py; method and findings in docs/auto_open/RSA_GRID.md.
