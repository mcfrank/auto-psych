Refinement of `mixed_strong_weak_sampling_concepts`: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over rules (family-organised prior, exception rules as their own kind) and magnitude ranges judged as a typical share of the 1–100 line, with occasional stray examples, and they are unsure whether the examples were drawn at random from the concept (strong sampling, the size principle) or merely picked as numbers it contains (weak sampling). The one change is that they hold this belief about the example set as a whole — the program generated all its examples one way or the other — instead of deciding it separately for each example. So the weak-sampling possibility does not fade with every added example: after three or four examples that fit both a narrow and a broad concept (multiples of 9 vs multiples of 3, a tight range vs a wide one) people still keep the broad concept in play and say "yes" to its other members more than the per-example mixture predicts, while after a single example they generalise almost as pure strong sampling does.
