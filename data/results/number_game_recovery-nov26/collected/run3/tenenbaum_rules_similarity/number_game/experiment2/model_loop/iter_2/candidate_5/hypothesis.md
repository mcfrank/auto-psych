Refinement of `beta_share_parity_range_concepts`: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over rules (family-organised prior, exception rules as their own kind), magnitude ranges and odd/even-within-a-range concepts judged as a typical share of the 1–100 line, with the size principle. The one change is that, within the "multiples of m" kind, the rules are no longer equally plausible a priori: people's prior belief in a multiples rule grows (or shrinks) as a power of its base m, with that exponent inferred, so a rule picking out a more specific, larger-base set (multiples of 9, of 12) is favoured over the coarser rule nested in it (multiples of 3, of 4). As a result, after examples such as 36, 45, 54, 81 people say "yes" to other multiples of 9 much more readily than to multiples of 3 that are not multiples of 9 — a stronger preference for the most specific multiplicative rule than the size principle alone gives.
