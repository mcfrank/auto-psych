People do the same Bayesian concept learning as the best model (rules with a family-organised prior and arbitrary-exception rules as their own kind, magnitude ranges with a beta prior over the share of the 1–100 line they cover, and the size principle), but their space of rules also contains near-misses of the sparse multiplicative rules: "one more than" or "one less than" a power or a square (e.g. 3, 5, 9, 17, 33, 65 or 1, 3, 7, 15, 31, 63, or 2, 5, 10, 17, 26, 37), forming their own kind of rule with its own inferred, presumably low, plausibility. So after examples such as 31, 3, 1, 15 or 33, 17, 5, 9 people say "yes" to the next shifted power (63, 65) and "no" to other odd numbers or nearby numbers, rather than spreading "yes" over all odd numbers or a range.
