People do not infer one concept that explains all the examples together; they store each example as a separate exemplar and judge the target by its average similarity to the stored exemplars, where the similarity of two numbers is how readily a familiar number concept (a rule such as "multiples of 9", or a magnitude range of typical width) suggested by the exemplar would also contain the target, with more specific shared concepts counting for more. So a target strongly similar to just one example (e.g. a number close to only one of several widely spread examples, or an odd number after 3, 9, 27, 81) still gets a fair share of "yes" answers, and adding consistent examples narrows generalisation much less than a learner who requires one concept to cover them all.
