Refinement of `beta_share_width_exception_concepts`: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over rules (family-organised prior, exception rules as their own kind) and magnitude ranges judged as a typical share of the 1–100 line, with the size principle. The one change is how prior belief is shared among the rules of one kind: not equally, but in proportion to a power of how many numbers each rule contains, with the exponent inferred — so if specific rules ("multiples of 9", "powers of 4") are a priori more natural to people than broad ones of the same kind ("multiples of 3", "powers of 2"), then after 36, 45, 54, 81 they say "yes" to other multiples of 9 and "no" to multiples of 3 that are not multiples of 9 more firmly than the size principle alone predicts (or, with the opposite sign, generalise to the broader rule more).
