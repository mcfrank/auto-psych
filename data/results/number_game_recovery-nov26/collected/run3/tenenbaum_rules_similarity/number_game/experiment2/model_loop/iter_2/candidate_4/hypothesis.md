Refinement of `beta_share_width_exception_concepts`: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over rules (family-organised prior, exception rules as their own kind) and magnitude ranges judged as a typical share of the 1–100 line, with the size principle. The one change is how prior belief is shared among the rules of one kind: instead of treating every rule of a kind as equally likely a priori, people find the more specific rules of a kind (those picking out fewer numbers, e.g. "multiples of 9" over "multiples of 3", "powers of 4" over "powers of 2") more (or, if the inferred tilt is negative, less) plausible, with the strength of this preference inferred. So after 36, 45, 54, 81 people say "yes" to other multiples of 9 and "no" to multiples of 3 that are not multiples of 9 more sharply than the size principle alone predicts.
