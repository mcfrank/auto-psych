Refinement of `beta_share_width_exception_concepts`: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over rules (family-organised prior, exception rules as their own kind) and magnitude ranges judged as a typical share of the 1–100 line, with the size principle. The one change is which rules they seriously entertain given the examples: when every example also fits a strictly narrower rule (all multiples of 9 fit "multiples of 3"; 4, 16, 64 fit both "powers of 4" and "powers of 2"), they settle on the narrower rule and discount the broader, dominated one by an inferred factor beyond what the size principle already does (arbitrary-exception rules never count as the narrower rule). So after 36, 45, 54, 81 people say "yes" to other multiples of 9 and "no" to multiples of 3 that are not multiples of 9 more sharply than the incumbent predicts — unlike a prior favouring specific rules, the discount applies only when a narrower rule actually fits the examples.
