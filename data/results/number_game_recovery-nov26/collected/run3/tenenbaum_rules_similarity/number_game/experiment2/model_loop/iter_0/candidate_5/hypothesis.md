Refinement of `doubling_chain_concepts`: people do Bayesian concept learning over arithmetic rules (organised by kind, with exception rules as their own kind), magnitude ranges and odd/even-within-a-range concepts, using the size principle, and they also entertain multiplicative chains — each number double (or triple) the one before, starting from a number other than 1 (e.g. 5, 10, 20, 40, 80) — as a kind of rule with its own plausibility. The one change, taken from the best model `gamma_width_exception_family_concepts`, is their expectation about how wide a range is: a gamma prior whose sharpness is inferred along with its typical width, in place of the fixed, loose Erlang form. With ranges judged as the best model judges them, chains are put to a fair test: after a chain-like set such as 5, 10, 20 this model still predicts "yes" to 40 and 80 and "no" to other multiples of 5 such as 15, 25 and 30, where the best model spreads "yes" over all multiples of 5.
