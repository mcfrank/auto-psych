People infer the hidden concept as a Bayesian concept learner does (rules organised by kind with exception rules as their own kind, magnitude ranges judged as a typical share of the 1–100 line, the size principle), but they also summarise the examples by a prototype, their average magnitude, and their yes/no answer is pulled by the target's numerical similarity to that prototype. That similarity falls off exponentially with the target's distance on the number line from the examples' mean, with an inferred rate and strength. So targets near the centre of the examples get more "yes" than the inferred concept warrants (or fewer, if the strength is negative), even when no example lies near them (e.g. 50 after 13, 91, or 33 after 2, 64), while targets equally close to a single example but far from the centre get no such pull.
