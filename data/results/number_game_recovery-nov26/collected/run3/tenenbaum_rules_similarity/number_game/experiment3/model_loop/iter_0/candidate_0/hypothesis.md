People infer how likely the target is to belong to the hidden concept as the simplest Bayesian concept learner does (rules with a family-organised prior and exception rules as their own kind, magnitude ranges judged as a typical share of the 1–100 line, the size principle), but each person brings a stable bias toward answering "yes" or "no" that holds across their whole session: their decision criterion is shifted, by a person-specific amount drawn from a population distribution, so the log-odds of saying "yes" is the inferred membership log-odds plus that person's own bias. So, for the same examples and target, a yes-prone person accepts more targets of every kind (including clear non-members), and a no-prone person rejects more (including shown examples), rather than everyone answering with the same probability.
