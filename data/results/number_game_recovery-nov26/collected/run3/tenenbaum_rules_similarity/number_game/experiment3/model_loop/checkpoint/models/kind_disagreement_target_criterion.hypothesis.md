Refinement of `kind_uncertainty_shifted_criterion`: people infer the hidden concept exactly as its Bayesian concept learner does and shift their yes/no criterion when their doubt comes from not knowing what kind of program they face. The one change is that this doubt is judged for the target in question rather than for the example set as a whole: the criterion moves toward "no" (or "yes") in proportion to how much of their uncertainty about this particular target stems from the open kinds disagreeing about it (e.g. a range would include it but the favoured rule would not), as opposed to fuzziness within one kind (where a range's edge lies, which multiples rule) — so on the same set, a target that the remaining kinds agree on is answered by the membership probability alone, while one they disagree on is shifted.
