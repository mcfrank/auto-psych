Refinement of `kind_uncertainty_shifted_criterion`: people infer the hidden concept exactly as its Bayesian concept learner does and shift their yes/no criterion with how unsure they are about the kind of program, but the one change is how coarse that uncertainty is. What moves their criterion is only whether the program follows an arithmetic rule at all or picks out a stretch of the number line — not which kind of rule (parity, primes, powers, multiples, digits) it is. So examples that leave rule-versus-range open (e.g. 12, 16, 20) shift answers toward "no" (or "yes"), while examples that clearly call for some rule but leave several kinds of rule open (e.g. 4, 16, 64: powers, squares, even numbers) do not.
