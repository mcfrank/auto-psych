People read a number not only by its magnitude and arithmetic properties but also by the sum of its digits, and they hold "numbers whose digits add up to the same total" (e.g. 19, 28, 37, 46, 55, 64, 73, 82, 91) as a further kind of rule with its own prior plausibility, alongside the parity, prime, power, multiples and digit rules and the magnitude ranges of the best model, judged with the size principle and with the same criterion shift from doubt about which kind of rule it is. So after examples sharing a digit sum (e.g. 19, 73 or 92, 56) people say "yes" to other numbers with that digit sum more than to equally near numbers with a different digit sum.
