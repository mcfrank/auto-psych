Refinement of `uncertainty_shifted_criterion`: people infer the hidden concept exactly as its Bayesian concept learner does, and their uncertainty about which concept the program uses (how spread their belief is over rules and range widths) again shapes the yes/no answer — but the one change is that uncertainty does not shift the criterion toward "no" or "yes"; it changes how decisively they act on the inferred membership probability. When the examples leave many concepts open, people answer closer to a coin flip in both directions (saying "no" more to likely members and "yes" more to unlikely ones), and when the examples point clearly to one concept they follow it sharply, to an inferred degree.
