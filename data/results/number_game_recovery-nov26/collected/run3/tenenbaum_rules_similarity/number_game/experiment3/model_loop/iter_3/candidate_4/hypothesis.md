Refinement of `rule_family_doubt_criterion` with one component taken from `specificity_biased_rule_prior`. People infer the hidden concept as the incumbent's Bayesian learner does and shift their yes/no criterion with how unsure they are which kind of arithmetic rule the program follows (weighted by how much they believe it is a rule at all). The one change is that, within each kind of rule, they find specific rules a priori more plausible than broad ones: a rule's prior grows (or shrinks) with how few numbers it contains, to an inferred degree. So after examples that fit a sparse rule nested in a broader rule of the same kind (81, 9; 8, 32), people confine "yes" to the sparse rule more firmly than the size principle alone would, while the family-doubt criterion shift stays as in the incumbent.
