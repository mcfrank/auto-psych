People do not infer one concept that explains all the examples; they store each example as a separate exemplar and judge the target by its similarity to each stored exemplar, where two numbers are similar to the extent that the familiar number concepts (arithmetic rules and stretches of the number line of a typical width) one of them brings to mind would also contain the other. The comparisons combine multiplicatively, as in an exemplar model's conjunctive similarity rule: the target is accepted to the degree that its geometric-mean similarity to all the exemplars clears a criterion, so a target must resemble every example — being very dissimilar to just one example is enough to reject it.
