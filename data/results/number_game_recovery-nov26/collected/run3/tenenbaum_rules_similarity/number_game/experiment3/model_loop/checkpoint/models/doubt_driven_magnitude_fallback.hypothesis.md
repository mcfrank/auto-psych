Refinement of the incumbent `rule_family_doubt_criterion`: people infer the hidden concept as its Bayesian concept learner does, and their doubt about which kind of arithmetic rule the program follows (parity, primes, powers, multiples, final digit, ...), weighted by how much they believe it is a rule at all, shapes their answer. The one change is what that doubt does: rather than shifting their yes/no criterion toward "no" or "yes", it makes them fall back on the number line, so the more unsure they are which kind of rule it is, the more (to an inferred degree) they judge the target by how a stretch of the number line around the examples would generalise. Under rule-kind doubt, numbers near the examples therefore gain "yes" answers and members of only one candidate rule lose them, while sets that clearly point to one kind of rule are judged by the full posterior.
