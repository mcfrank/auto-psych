People do not infer a hidden concept; they compare the target with each stored example using a shared-feature (additive-clustering) similarity: two numbers are similar to the extent that they share familiar number properties (parity, multiples, powers, squares, primes, final digit, digits contained), each property weighted by how salient its kind is and how distinctive it is (properties held by few numbers count more), plus their closeness in magnitude. The judgment is conjunctive: the target is accepted to the extent that it is similar to every example (the geometric mean of its normalised similarities to the examples), so a target must resemble all the examples, not just one, to be called "yes".
