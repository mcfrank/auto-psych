When people are asked about a number, that number itself brings to mind the arithmetic properties it has (being even, a multiple of 7, a square, ending in 5), so while they judge it, the rules that contain the target get extra consideration beyond what their prior and the examples warrant, to an inferred degree (or less consideration, if the degree is negative). Magnitude ranges are not primed this way, because a bare number does not suggest a stretch of the line. Otherwise they infer the hidden concept as a Bayesian concept learner does, so the priming raises "yes" most for targets that share a still-plausible rule with the examples, and leaves targets reached only by a range unchanged.
