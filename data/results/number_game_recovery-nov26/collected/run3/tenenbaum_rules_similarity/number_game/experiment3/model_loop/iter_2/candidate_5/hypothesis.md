Refinement of `uncertainty_shifted_criterion`: people infer the hidden concept exactly as its Bayesian concept learner does (family-organised rules with exception rules as their own kind, ranges judged as a typical share of the 1–100 line, the size principle) and shift their yes/no criterion with uncertainty, but the one change is what that uncertainty is about: not how spread their belief is over concepts for the example set as a whole, but how much the plausible kinds of program disagree about this particular target. When one live kind of explanation (say a multiples rule) says the target belongs and another live kind (say a range) says it does not, people move their answer away from the averaged membership probability toward "no" (conflict aversion) or, if the inferred shift is negative, toward "yes"; targets on which all plausible kinds agree are answered at their membership probability, even for sets that leave the kind of program open.
