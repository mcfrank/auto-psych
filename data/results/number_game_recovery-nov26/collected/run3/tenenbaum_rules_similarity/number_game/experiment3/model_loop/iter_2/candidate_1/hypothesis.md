People do Bayesian concept learning over arithmetic rules (organised by kind, with exception rules as their own kind) and magnitude ranges judged as a typical share of the 1–100 line, using the size principle, but they misjudge how many numbers an arithmetic rule picks out: a rule's felt size grows only as an inferred power of its true number of members (e.g. "even numbers" does not feel fifty times bigger than "powers of 2"), while the size of a stretch of the number line is judged correctly. Because the distortion acts on the evidence each example gives, it grows with the number of examples: with a compressed sense of rule size, examples that fit both a sparse and a broad rule (2, 8, 16; 9, 27, 81) narrow generalisation onto the sparse rule less than a normative learner would, and broad rules compete with ranges more strongly than their true size warrants.
