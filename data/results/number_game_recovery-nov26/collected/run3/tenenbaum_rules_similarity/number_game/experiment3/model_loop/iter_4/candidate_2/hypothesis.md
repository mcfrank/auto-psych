People assume the examples they are shown are representative of the hidden program's output: a fair sample spreads out over the concept rather than bunching in one corner of it. So, beyond the size principle, they discount a candidate concept (rule or range) to an inferred degree when most of its members lie outside the stretch the examples cover (e.g. "all even numbers" after 2, 4, 6, 8) and favour concepts the examples span well (e.g. powers of 2 after 2, 8, 16, 64), and they say "yes" to the target by how strongly the concepts they then believe contain it.
