People infer the hidden concept as a Bayesian concept learner does (rules organised by kind with exception rules as their own kind, magnitude ranges judged as a typical share of the 1–100 line, the size principle), but their yes/no answer is also pulled by raw numerical similarity: the closer the target lies on the number line to the nearest example, the more readily they say "yes", with this pull falling off exponentially with distance at an inferred rate and having an inferred strength. So even after a clear rule (e.g. 16, 8, 64), numbers right next to an example (15, 17, 63) get more "yes" than the inferred concept warrants, and rule members far from every example get fewer.
