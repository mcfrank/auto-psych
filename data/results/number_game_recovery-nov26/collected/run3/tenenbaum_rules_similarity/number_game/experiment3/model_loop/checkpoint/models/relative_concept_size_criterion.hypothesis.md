People infer the hidden concept as the simplest Bayesian concept learner does, but they set their yes/no criterion relative to how many numbers they believe the program produces (an adaptation-level, or "yes-rationing", criterion): when the examples suggest a broad concept that contains much of the 1–100 line, they demand more before saying "yes" to any one target, and when the examples suggest a sparse concept they accept a member more readily — or the reverse, if the inferred dependence is positive. So two targets with the same membership probability are answered differently depending on how large the inferred concept is expected to be.
