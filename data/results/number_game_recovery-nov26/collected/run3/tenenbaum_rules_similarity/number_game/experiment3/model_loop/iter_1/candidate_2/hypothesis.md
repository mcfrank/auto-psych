People infer the hidden concept as the best model's Bayesian concept learner does (and shift their criterion with how unsure they are what kind of program they face), but the example sets of successive trials interfere in memory: on an inferred share of judgments, the concept suggested by the previous trial's examples intrudes and the target is judged by whether that previous concept would contain it rather than the current one (proactive interference). So the same (set, target) pair gets more "yes" when the target also fits the set seen on the trial just before, and more "no" when it does not, which a learner that treats every trial in isolation cannot produce.
