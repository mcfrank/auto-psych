Refinement of `individual_rule_vs_range_tendency`: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over rules (family-organised prior, exception rules as their own kind) and magnitude ranges judged as a typical share of the 1–100 line, with the size principle, and they differ stably across their session — but the one change is in *what* they differ on: not how readily they think of a rule rather than a range, but how wide they expect a range to be (each person has their own expected share of the line, drawn from a population distribution whose mean and spread are inferred; the sharpness of that expectation is shared). So for the same examples a "broad" person says "yes" to numbers well outside the examples' span while a "narrow" person confines "yes" to the immediate neighbourhood, and this difference shows up only for targets reached by magnitude, not for rule members. (The model also counts participants correctly; the earlier model silently fell back to the population average when there were more than 12.)
