People do not average their predictions over every number concept consistent with the examples; they commit to (approximately) the single most plausible concept and generalize only from it. Concretely, given the same rules and magnitude ranges, family-organised rule prior and size-principle likelihood as the current best model, the concept a person generalizes from is chosen with probability proportional to its posterior raised to an inferred decisiveness exponent (above 1 = near-MAP hypothesis selection, so generalization collapses onto the smallest well-fitting concept more sharply as examples accumulate), and "yes" means the chosen concept contains the target.
