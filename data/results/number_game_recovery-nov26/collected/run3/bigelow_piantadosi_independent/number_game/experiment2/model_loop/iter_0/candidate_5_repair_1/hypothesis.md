Refinement of the pruned `final_digit_multiplicative_concepts`: people still build number concepts from mental primitives (a fitted cost per extra primitive, a fitted extra cost on powers, squares and cubes, a fitted prior discount on salient "ends in d" rules, Erlang-weighted ranges, the size principle and near-all-or-none commitment). The one change, taken from `forward_counting_range_sampling`, is how people think a range-type program produces its numbers: they picture it counting upward from where the range starts, so the examples are read as early, low members of the range, with the strength of this forward-counting assumption fitted. So when a set is explained by a range, people say yes more to numbers a little above the examples than to numbers equally far below them, while their rule-based answers (parity, multiples, final digits) are unchanged.
