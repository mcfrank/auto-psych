Counting by fives is a drilled skill and membership in "multiples of 5" can be read straight off the written numeral (it ends in 0 or 5), so this one times table is far more available as a candidate rule than the other multiples rules; everything else in how people infer the rule follows the current best model (primitive-built rules with simplicity costs, final-digit discount, power and square costs, graded times tables, "prime" preferred over "odd", hundred-chart ranges, the size principle and near-all-or-none commitment). So after sets whose examples are all multiples of 5 (e.g. 90, 100, 5 or 15, 35, 60) people commit to "multiples of 5" and say yes to other numbers ending in 0 or 5 and no to their numerical neighbours much more sharply than the best model predicts, while sets fitting other times tables are unaffected.
