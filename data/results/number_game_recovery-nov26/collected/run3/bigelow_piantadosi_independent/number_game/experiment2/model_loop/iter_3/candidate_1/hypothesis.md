People treat the boundary numbers 1 and 100 differently from other examples: when one of them is shown, they suspect it marks the edge of the number line the program works on rather than a sample of its rule, so they are more ready to set it aside as a stray when inferring the concept. Everything else in how they infer the rule is as in the best model (primitive-built rules with simplicity costs, final-digit discount, costs on powers and squares, times-table-graded multiples, hundred-chart row ranges, "prime" preferred over "odd", the size principle and near-all-or-none commitment). So in sets such as 31, 19, 49, 100 or 20, 8, 84, 100 or 31, 3, 1, people generalise from the remaining examples (to nearby numbers or their shared rule) instead of stretching a range or rule to cover 1 or 100.
