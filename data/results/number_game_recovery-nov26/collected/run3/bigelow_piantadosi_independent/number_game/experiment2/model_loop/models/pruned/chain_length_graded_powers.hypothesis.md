Refinement of `factor_graded_multiples_concepts`. Everything in it is kept: concepts built from mental primitives with a fitted cost per extra primitive, the "ends in d" discount, extra costs for powers/cubes and for squares, factor-graded multiples costs, Erlang-weighted ranges, the size principle and near-all-or-none commitment. The one change is that a "powers of b" rule is held in mind as a chain of repeated multiplications, so it is harder to conceive the longer the chain it spans within 1-100. Its prior cost grows with the logarithm of the chain's length at a fitted rate. Short chains made of familiar times-table facts (1, 8, 64; 1, 9, 81; 1, 10, 100) are therefore readily entertained, while long chains such as powers of 2 stay costly. After 8, 64, people commit to "powers of 8" and say no to the other multiples of 8 (24, 40, 56) more than the incumbent predicts.
