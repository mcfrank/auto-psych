Refinement of `prime_over_odd_reading`. Everything is kept: primitive-built rules with a per-primitive cost, the final-digit discount, the costs on powers/cubes and squares, times-table-graded multiples, Erlang ranges with a bonus for whole hundred-chart rows, the single preference moving plausibility from "odd numbers" to "prime numbers", the size principle and near-all-or-none commitment. The one change is that "multiples of 3" is hard to notice as a rule and carries its own fitted extra prior cost. Multiples of 3 have no visible signature in the written numeral: they cycle through every final digit and alternate between odd and even, so membership has to be checked by a digit-sum test. As a result, people rarely read odd multiples of 3 (69, 9, 39, 21; 63, 27, 99, 33) or mixed sets (39, 60, 12, 45) as "multiples of 3". They extend such sets by parity, final digit or range instead, and say no to other multiples of 3 (45, 57, 3, 24) more often than the incumbent predicts.
