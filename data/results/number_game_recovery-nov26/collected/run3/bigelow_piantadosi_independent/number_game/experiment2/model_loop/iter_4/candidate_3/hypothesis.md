Refinement of the incumbent `prime_over_odd_reading`. Everything in it is kept: primitive-built rules with a per-primitive cost, the final-digit discount, costs on powers/cubes and squares, times-table-graded multiples, the single preference that moves plausibility from "odd" to "prime", Erlang ranges with a bonus for whole hundred-chart rows, the size principle and near-all-or-none commitment. The one change is that "multiples of 5" is a privileged times table. Counting by fives is a drilled skill, and membership can be seen in the numeral (it ends in 0 or 5), so this rule gets its own fitted prior bonus. So after sets of multiples of 5 that are not all multiples of 10 (90, 100, 5; 35, 15, 85), people commit to "multiples of 5": they say yes to 25, 45, 65, 95 and no to non-multiples, more sharply than the incumbent predicts, while sets that fit other times tables are unaffected.
