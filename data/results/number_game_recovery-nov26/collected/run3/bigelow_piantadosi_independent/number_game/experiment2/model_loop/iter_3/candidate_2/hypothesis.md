People infer the hidden rule as the current best concept learner does: primitive-built rules with simplicity costs, hundred-chart ranges, and a preference for "prime" over "odd". The one distortion is how they pool evidence across examples. They do not treat each example as an independent draw that adds a fixed amount of evidence. Instead, examples that agree with each other are more convincing together than one at a time, so the weight each example carries grows with how many examples are shown, at a fitted rate. As a result, people commit to a tight rule or range only weakly from a single example and generalise broadly from it, but commit more sharply than an ideal learner once three or four examples agree.
