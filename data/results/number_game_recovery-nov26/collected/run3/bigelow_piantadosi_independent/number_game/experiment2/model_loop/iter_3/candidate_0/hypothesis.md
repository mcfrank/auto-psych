People's "multiples" concepts are not confined to the school times tables (3 to 12): they can skip-count by any step size that lands on at least two numbers in 1-100 (counting by 14s, 25s or 33s), and such large-step rules are about as easy to entertain as other two-part rules, at one fitted extra cost. Because these rules are small, the size principle makes them win when the examples fit them. So after 3, 33, 99 people read "counting by 33s", say yes to 66 and no to 23. After 14, 98, 91 they read "counting by 14s" with one stray example, and say no to 7 and 77.
