People infer the hidden concept as the simple concept-search learner does, but they turn that belief into an answer by a two-route decision rule: when their belief makes the target clearly in or clearly out they answer from it, and the closer the belief is to a toss-up, the more likely they are to give up deliberating and simply guess. So answers to targets the concept clearly decides are near-deterministic (rare stray answers even to the examples themselves), while targets the learner is torn about are answered close to a coin flip rather than in proportion to the belief.
