Refinement of `prime_salient_concepts`: people build number concepts from mental primitives (a fitted cost per extra primitive, a final-digit discount, extra costs for powers/cubes and for squares, times-table-graded multiples costs, Erlang-weighted ranges, the size principle, near-all-or-none commitment), and "prime numbers" is a familiar school-math category with a fitted prior bonus, so after all-prime sets such as 2, 47 or 67, 31, 17, 61 people say yes to other primes and no to odd composites. The one change, taken from `hundred_chart_row_ranges`, is how people picture numerical ranges: they lay 1-100 out as a hundred chart of ten rows, so ranges made of whole rows (1-50, 21-40, 81-90) get a fitted prior bonus, and when no rule (primes included) explains the examples they fall back on a broad round block of the chart rather than a tight range hugging the examples.
