Refinement of `prime_salient_hundred_chart` (everything kept: primitive-built rules with a per-primitive cost, final-digit discount, costs on powers/cubes and squares, times-table-graded multiples, Erlang ranges with a bonus for whole hundred-chart rows, a fitted prior bonus for the familiar category "prime numbers", the size principle and near-all-or-none commitment). The one change, taken from `marked_odd_parity_concepts`, is that "odd" is a marked, negatively defined category ("not even"), so the rule "odd numbers" carries a fitted extra prior cost that "even numbers" does not. As a result, after all-prime sets of odd primes (53, 47, 41; 67, 31, 17, 61) "odd" competes less with "primes", and people say yes to other primes but no to odd composites (9, 21, 63, 81) more sharply than prime salience alone predicts, while after all-even sets they still extend "even" readily.
