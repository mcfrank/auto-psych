Refinement of the incumbent `prime_over_odd_reading`, with everything in it kept: primitive-built rules with a per-primitive cost, the final-digit discount, costs on powers/cubes and squares, times-table-graded multiples, the single preference moving plausibility from "odd" to "prime", Erlang ranges with a bonus for whole hundred-chart rows, the size principle and near-all-or-none commitment. The one change is that "multiples of 5" is unlike the other times tables: membership can be read straight off the numeral (it ends in 0 or 5), and counting by fives is a drilled skill, so the rule gets its own fitted prior bonus. So after sets such as 90, 100, 5, people commit to "multiples of 5" and say yes to 15, 25, 35 ... 95 almost always and no to 16, 92 or 93, more sharply than the incumbent predicts, while sets that fit other times tables are unaffected.
