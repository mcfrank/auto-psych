People judge a target by its similarity to the examples, and they have a fixed budget of attention that they split across ways the examples could be alike. The budget goes to each property all the examples share (parity, primality, squares, cubes, powers of 2, multiples of 3-12, final digit, hundred-chart row, repeated digits, a digit contained), more to rarer properties and more the more examples share them. The remainder goes to position on the number line, more when the examples cluster tightly. A target's psychological distance is the attention-weighted share of that budget it violates: lacking a shared property, or lying outside the examples' span, scaled by how far outside. The probability of saying yes falls off with this distance. Because attention is normalised, a set with a striking shared property (such as all multiples of 5, or all repdigits) makes magnitude nearly irrelevant, while a set sharing nothing rare is judged almost purely by nearness on the number line.
