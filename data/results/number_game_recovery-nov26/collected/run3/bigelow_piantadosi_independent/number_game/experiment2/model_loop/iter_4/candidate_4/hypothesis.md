Refinement of the incumbent `prime_over_odd_reading`, with everything in it kept: primitive-built rules with a per-primitive cost, the final-digit discount, costs on powers/cubes and squares, times-table-graded multiples, the single preference that moves plausibility from "odd" to "prime", Erlang ranges with a bonus for whole hundred-chart rows, the size principle and near-all-or-none commitment. The one change is that "multiples of 5" is special among the times tables, because membership can be read straight off the numeral (it ends in 0 or 5) and counting by fives is a drilled skill. So that rule gets its own fitted prior bonus. After sets of multiples of 5 that are not all multiples of 10 (e.g. 90, 100, 5 or 15, 35, 60), people commit to "multiples of 5": they say yes to other multiples of 5 and no to neighbouring non-multiples much more sharply than the incumbent predicts, which closes the critique's under-generalisation of fives. Sets that fit other times tables are unaffected.
