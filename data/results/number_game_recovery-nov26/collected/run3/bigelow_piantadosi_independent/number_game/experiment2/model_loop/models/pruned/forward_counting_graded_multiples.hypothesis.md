Refinement of `forward_counting_final_digit_concepts`: people build number concepts from mental primitives (a cost per extra primitive, an extra cost on powers/squares/cubes, a discount on "ends in d" rules, Erlang-weighted ranges, the size principle, near-all-or-none commitment) and picture a range-type program as counting upward from its start, so they generalise a little more above the examples than below. The one change, taken from `factor_graded_multiples_concepts`, is that "multiples of m" rules are not equally available for every m: divisibility is checked by recalling a times table, and larger tables are less fluent, so a multiples rule's prior cost grows with the logarithm of its factor at a fitted rate. People therefore readily read sets as multiples of 3, 4 or 5 but seldom commit to multiples of 7, 8, 11 or 12, and when they do not, they fall back on an upward-tilted range around the examples.
