Refinement of `factor_graded_multiples_concepts` that changes how likely one concept is a priori. "Prime numbers" is a familiar school-math category. People keep it ready to hand, so it carries a fitted prior bonus over other compound rules (multiples, powers, final digits, digits contained). Everything else is kept: primitive costs, the final-digit discount, the costs on powers and squares, factor-graded multiples, Erlang ranges, the size principle and near-all-or-none commitment. So after sets such as 2, 47 or 53, 2 or 67, 31, 17, 61, people commit to "primes": they say yes to other primes (5, 13, 73, 89) and no to non-prime numbers inside the examples' range, more often than the current best model predicts.
