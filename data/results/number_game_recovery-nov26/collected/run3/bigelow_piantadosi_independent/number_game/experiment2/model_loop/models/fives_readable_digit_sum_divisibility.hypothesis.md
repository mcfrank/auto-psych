Refinement of `hidden_divisibility_by_three`. Everything in it is kept: primitive-built rules with a per-primitive cost, the final-digit discount, costs on powers/cubes and squares, times-table-graded multiples, Erlang ranges with a bonus for whole hundred-chart rows, the prime bonus, the marked-odd cost, the shared extra cost on the digit-sum rules (multiples of 3, 6, 9, 12), the size principle and near-all-or-none commitment. The one change, taken from `fives_readable_multiples`, is that "multiples of 5" is the opposite case to the digit-sum rules. Its membership can be read straight off the numeral (it ends in 0 or 5), and counting by fives is a drilled skill, so the rule gets its own fitted prior bonus. So after sets such as 90, 100, 5 or 15, 35, 60, people commit to "multiples of 5": they say yes to 25, 45, 85 and no to non-multiples such as 16 or 92 much more sharply than the best model predicts, while still seldom committing to "multiples of 3".
