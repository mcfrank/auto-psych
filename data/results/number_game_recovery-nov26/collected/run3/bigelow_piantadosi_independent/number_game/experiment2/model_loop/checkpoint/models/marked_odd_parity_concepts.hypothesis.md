"Odd" is a marked, negatively defined category: people think of an odd number as one that is "not even" rather than by a positive property, so "odd numbers" is harder to entertain as the program's rule than "even numbers", even though both are equally simple and equally large. As a result, after all-odd examples (e.g. 19, 73 or 7, 31) people often reject other odd numbers (63, 81, 87, 21) and fall back on a range or a narrower rule. After all-even examples they readily extend "even". Everything else in how they infer the rule is unchanged: primitive-built rules, the size principle, hundred-chart ranges and near-all-or-none commitment.
