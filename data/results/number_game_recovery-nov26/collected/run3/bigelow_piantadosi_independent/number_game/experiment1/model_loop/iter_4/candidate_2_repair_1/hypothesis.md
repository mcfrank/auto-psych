People assume the hidden program produces a numerical range by counting upward from its starting point, so the examples they see are taken to be early, low members of the range rather than evenly spread across it; a range that starts near the examples and extends above them therefore explains the examples better than one extending below them, with the strength (and direction) of this counting-forward assumption fitted from data. Everything else follows the best model (primitive-built rules with a per-primitive cost and an extra cost on powers/squares/cubes, the size principle, Erlang-weighted ranges, near-all-or-none commitment), so people generalise from a set to numbers just above the examples more than to numbers just below them.
