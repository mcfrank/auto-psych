People think of a range-type program as counting upward from where its range starts, so they expect the examples they have seen to be bunched toward the bottom of the range, with the rest of the range lying above them. A range that starts just below the examples and runs well above them therefore explains the examples better than one centred on them, and people generalise more to numbers a little above the examples than to numbers equally far below. Rule-type concepts (parity, multiples, final digits, with powers, squares and cubes costing extra) are judged as before.
