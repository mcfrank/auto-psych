Refinement of `multiplicative_not_exponential_concepts`: people still build number concepts from mental primitives. They pay a fitted cost per extra primitive and a fitted extra cost for concepts built on repeated multiplication (powers, squares, cubes), they weigh examples by the size principle, and they commit nearly all-or-none to the concept that best explains the examples. The one change, taken from `final_digit_salient_concept_search`, is that a written number's final digit is perceptually privileged: "ends in d" rules get a fitted prior discount over other compound rules, including "contains the digit d". So when every example contains some digit, people read the set as "ends in d" where they can, and say no to targets that only contain that digit somewhere else. This addresses the critique that people use "contains digit d" less than the incumbent predicts.
