People read a written numeral by its places (a tens digit and a ones digit), so a concept that requires scanning for a digit regardless of its position — "contains a 2", "has an 8 in it" — is harder to conceive than other rules of the same length and pays a fitted extra prior cost, on top of the per-primitive simplicity cost, the extra cost of power/square/cube concepts, the size principle and near-all-or-none commitment. So when every example happens to contain some digit d, people extend that "contains d" rule to new numbers (e.g. 27 after 92, 82, 42) less than a learner who finds digit search as natural as any other rule, falling back on "ends in d", ranges or arithmetic rules instead.
