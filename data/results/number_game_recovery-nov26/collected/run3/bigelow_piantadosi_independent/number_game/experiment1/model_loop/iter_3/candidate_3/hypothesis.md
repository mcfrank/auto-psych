Refinement of `erlang_interval_simplicity_search`: people still build concepts from mental primitives (a fitted cost per extra primitive, the size principle, an Erlang size prior on intervals, near-all-or-none commitment to the best-explaining concept). The one change is that divisibility — "multiples of m" — is a privileged, highly accessible kind of arithmetic rule whose prior is shifted by its own fitted salience bonus, rather than costing exactly what every other two-primitive rule (powers, final digits, containing a digit, squares) costs. If divisibility is salient, a set whose examples all share a factor (e.g. 18, 45, 72 or 39, 60, 12, 45) is read as "multiples of m" more often than as a power, a final digit or a range, so people say yes to other multiples of m more than the incumbent predicts, as the critique reports.
