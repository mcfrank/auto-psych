Refinement of `final_digit_salient_concept_search`: people still build concepts from mental primitives (a fitted cost per extra primitive, Erlang-weighted intervals, the size principle, near-all-or-none commitment) and treat the final digit as a privileged feature, so "ends in d" rules get a fitted prior discount. The one change, grafted from `multiplicative_not_exponential_concepts`, is that rules built on repeated multiplication (squares, cubes, powers and their exception variants) pay a fitted extra prior cost. The claim is that people find final-digit rules easy and exponent rules hard. After 32, 4, 16 they extend "multiples of 4" to 12 and 72. After 62, 32, 22 they commit to "ends in 2" and do not fall back on "contains a 2", which is the critique's over-predicted contains-digit generalisation.
