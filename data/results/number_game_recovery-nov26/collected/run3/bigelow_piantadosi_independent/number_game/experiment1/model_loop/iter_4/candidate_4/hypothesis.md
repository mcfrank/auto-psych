Refinement of `multiplicative_not_exponential_concepts`: people still build number concepts from mental primitives on a multiplicative, not exponential, basis (a fitted cost per extra primitive, a fitted extra cost on powers, squares and cubes, Erlang-weighted intervals, the size principle and near-all-or-none commitment to the best-explaining concept). The one change, taken from `final_digit_salient_concept_search`, is that a number's final digit is a privileged, perceptually salient feature: "ends in d" rules get a fitted prior discount relative to other compound rules, including "contains the digit d". So when the examples share a digit, people read it as a shared ending (allowing a stray) rather than "contains d", and they say no to targets that contain that digit somewhere other than the final position, fixing the incumbent's over-prediction of yes for contained-digit targets with other endings.
