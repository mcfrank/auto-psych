Refinement of `simplicity_cost_concept_search`: people still build concepts from simple mental primitives, paying a fitted cost per extra primitive, weighing examples by the size principle and committing nearly all-or-none to the best-explaining concept. The one change is that rules built on repeated multiplication — squares, cubes, powers of a number and their exception variants — are harder to conceive than other rules of the same length and pay a fitted extra prior cost, so when examples such as 32, 4, 16 fit both "powers of 2" and "multiples of 4", people favour the multiplicative rule and extend it to new multiples (12, 72) far more than the size principle predicts — addressing the critique that people extend shared-divisor rules more than the best model does.
