People's number concepts are built from multiplication (parity, multiples of m, final digits, ranges, with powers/squares/cubes costing extra), but a digit buried anywhere inside a numeral is hard to notice as the basis of a rule: concepts of the form "contains the digit d" carry a fitted extra prior cost over other rules of the same length. So when all examples happen to contain a 2 (e.g. 92, 82, 27, 42), people read them as "ends in 2" with a stray, or as a range, rather than "contains a 2", and say no to targets such as 23 or 25 that share a contained digit but not a final digit.
