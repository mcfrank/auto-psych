I refined the incumbent `round_landmark_veto` and kept all of it: people infer the program with the set-size-graded privileged-fives concept learner, and when no example is a round number they veto round targets (10, 20, ..., 100) on a fitted share of trials. The one change is taken from `example_spacing_sparsity_bias`: people also read how far apart the examples are as a clue to how sparse the program's output is, so the wider the average gap between neighbouring examples, the less ready they are to say yes to any target, beyond what the inferred concept implies. So after widely spaced sets such as 3, 48, 99 or 8, 64, people say yes less often than the incumbent predicts even to targets that fit the inferred rule or range, and after tightly clustered sets they say yes more often.
