Refinement of the incumbent `repdigit_pattern_chart_concepts`, with everything kept: primitive-built rules with a per-primitive cost, the final-digit discount, costs on powers/cubes and squares, times-table-graded multiples, the repeated-digit rule with its own bonus, Erlang ranges with a bonus for whole hundred-chart rows, the size principle and near-all-or-none commitment. The one change is about "prime" versus "odd". The incumbent treats them as one trade-off: whatever plausibility "prime numbers" gains, "odd numbers" loses. Here they are two separate facts about people, taken from `prime_salient_marked_odd`. "Prime" is a familiar category with its own fitted bonus, and "odd" is a marked "not even" category with its own fitted cost of a different size. So after all-prime sets people still commit to "primes", but after odd sets with no primes in them (63, 27, 99, 33) they extend "odd" to odd non-examples (23, 31, 37, 43) more than the incumbent's coupled trade-off allows.
