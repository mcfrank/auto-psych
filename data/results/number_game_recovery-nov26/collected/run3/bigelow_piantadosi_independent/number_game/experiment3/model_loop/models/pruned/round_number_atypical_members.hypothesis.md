I refined the incumbent `set_size_evidence_privileged_fives` and kept everything in it: primitive-built rules with a per-primitive cost, the final-digit discount, power and square costs, graded times tables, the prime bonus and odd cost, repdigits, privileged multiples of 5, hundred-chart ranges, the size principle, near-all-or-none commitment and evidence that grows stronger with set size. The one change is about round numbers. Multiples of 10 (10, 20, ..., 100) are landmarks and reference points, not typical outputs of a pattern, so people are less ready to accept a round target as a member of the concept they inferred, by a fitted factor. This holds for "even", "multiples of 4" or a range around the examples, but not for a concept that is explicitly about numbers ending in 0. So after 8, 64 or 12, 84, 8, people say yes to even targets such as 24 or 56 but less often to 20, 40 or 70, which the incumbent treats as equally good members.
