Refinement of `prime_salient_marked_odd`, with everything in it kept: primitive-built rules with a per-primitive cost, the final-digit discount, costs on powers/cubes and squares, times-table-graded multiples, a fitted prior bonus for the familiar category "prime numbers", a separate fitted extra cost for the marked category "odd", Erlang ranges with a bonus for whole hundred-chart rows, the size principle and near-all-or-none commitment. The one change, taken from `repdigit_pattern_chart_concepts`, adds a concept that model lacks: people notice when a numeral's two digits are the same (11, 22, ..., 99), so "repeated-digit numbers" is a candidate rule, priced like other two-primitive rules plus its own fitted prior bonus. Unlike the best model, which moves plausibility from "odd" to "prime" in one shared step, here primes gain more than odd loses. So people extend "odd" readily after all-odd sets that are not all prime (63, 27, 99, 33; 31, 3, 1, 15; 1, 8, 27), yet still pick out primes after all-prime sets and other repdigits after repdigit sets.
