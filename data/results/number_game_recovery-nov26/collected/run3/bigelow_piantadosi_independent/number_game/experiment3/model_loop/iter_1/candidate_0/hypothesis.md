People do not weigh every conceivable rule and range when they infer the program; they first call to mind a limited number of candidate concepts from memory, each concept's chance of coming to mind set by how accessible it is (simple, familiar rules readily, odd ranges rarely), and then choose among only the concepts they retrieved by how well each explains the examples. Because a highly accessible concept is retrieved almost surely however many retrievals are made, retrieval flattens the advantage of the most familiar rules over moderately familiar ones, so a narrower but less familiar rule that fits the examples better (e.g. "ends in 3", "multiples of 7") wins against a broad familiar one more often than accessibility-weighted Bayesian inference predicts.
