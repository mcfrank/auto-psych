I refined the incumbent `forward_continuation_response_bias` and kept all of it: people infer the program with the set-size-graded privileged-fives concept learner, veto round targets that no round example supports, and at the answer stage lean toward yes above the largest example and toward no below the smallest. The one change is taken from `example_spacing_sparsity_bias`: people also read the average gap between neighbouring examples as a clue to how sparse the program's output is, so the wider the examples are spread, the less ready they are to say yes to any target, on top of the upward lean. So after widely spaced sets such as 8, 64 or 14, 98, 91 people say yes less often than the incumbent predicts, even above the examples, and after tightly clustered sets they say yes more often.
