I refined `round_number_atypical_members` and kept all of it. People infer the program with the privileged-fives concept learner, whose evidence per example grows with set size, and they accept a round target (10, 20, ..., 100) as a member of the concept they inferred only by a fitted typicality factor, unless the concept is explicitly about a final or contained 0. The one change is taken from `example_spacing_sparsity_bias`: people read wide gaps between neighbouring examples as a sign that the program produces only a few scattered numbers, so the wider the examples' mean gap, the less ready they are to say yes to any target. So after widely spaced sets such as 8, 64 or 14, 98, 91, people say yes less often across the board, and less still to round targets that the inferred concept contains.
