I refined the incumbent `round_landmark_veto` and kept all of it: concept inference by the set-size-evidence privileged-fives learner, plus the check that vetoes a round target (10, 20, ..., 100) on a fitted share of trials when no example is round. The one change is taken from `example_spacing_sparsity_bias`. People read how widely spaced the examples are as a clue to how sparse the program's output is, so the wider the average gap between neighbouring examples, the less ready they are to say yes to any target beyond what the inferred concept implies. So after widely spaced sets such as 8, 64 or 14, 98, 91, people say no even to targets that fit "even" or "multiples of 7" (16, 24, 35, 63), and round targets after them are vetoed on top of that. Closely spaced sets get relatively more yeses.
