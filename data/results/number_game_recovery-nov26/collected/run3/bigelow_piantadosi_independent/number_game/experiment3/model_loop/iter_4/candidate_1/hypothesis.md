People do not infer a concept: they store the examples as exemplars and judge a target by how similar it is to them, where similarity between two numbers falls off with their distance in a space of magnitude and familiar number properties (parity, primality, squares, cubes, powers of 2, times tables, final digit, tens digit). They say yes when the target is more similar to the stored examples than it is to numbers in general, so the decision rests on the target's familiarity relative to its baseline similarity to all of 1-100. A target that shares a rare property with the examples (e.g. 27 after 1, 8, 64) therefore stands out more than one that shares only a common property, without any explicit rule or size principle.
