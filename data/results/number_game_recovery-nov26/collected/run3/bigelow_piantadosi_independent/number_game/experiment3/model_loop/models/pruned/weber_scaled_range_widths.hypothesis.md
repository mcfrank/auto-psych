People represent numbers on a compressed mental number line, so the same numerical gap feels smaller among large numbers than among small ones (Weber's law). When they consider that the program produces a stretch of the number line, the range width they find natural is therefore proportional to the magnitude of the examples: after examples in the 70s-90s they readily extend to numbers ten or twenty away, while after examples below 20 they expect a tight range and reject numbers only a few steps away. How they infer rules (parity, primes, times tables, final digits, repdigits, powers) is unchanged from the current best model.
