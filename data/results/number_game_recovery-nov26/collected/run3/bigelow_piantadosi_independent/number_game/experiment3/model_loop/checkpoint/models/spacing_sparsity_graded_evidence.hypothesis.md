I refined `example_spacing_sparsity_bias` and kept all of it. People read wide gaps between neighbouring examples as a sign that the program produces only a few scattered numbers, so they say yes less often. Underneath that sits the privileged-fives concept learner: primitive-built rules with per-primitive costs, the final-digit discount, power and square costs, graded times tables, a separate prime bonus and odd cost, repdigits, multiples of 5, hundred-chart ranges, the size principle and near-all-or-none commitment. The one change is taken from `set_size_graded_evidence_weight` and concerns how people pool evidence: the weight each agreeing example carries grows with the set's size at a fitted rate. So after a lone example people commit weakly and generalise broadly. After three or four widely spaced examples they commit sharply to the shared rule but still hold back from saying yes, because the gaps suggest a sparse program.
