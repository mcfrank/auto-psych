Refinement of the incumbent `set_size_evidence_privileged_fives`, with all of its concept inference kept: primitive-built rules with per-primitive costs, the final-digit discount, power and square costs, graded times tables, a prime bonus and odd cost, repdigits, privileged multiples of 5, hundred-chart ranges, the size principle, set-size-graded evidence and near-all-or-none commitment. The one change is that round numbers (10, 20, ..., 100) are seen as a distinct kind of number, so when none of the examples is round, people often reject a round target because it does not look like the examples, even if it fits the rule they inferred. So after sets such as 8, 64 or 32, 4, 16 people say yes to round evens (40, 80) less often than to other evens that fit the rule equally well (24, 48, 88), and less often than the incumbent predicts, which it cannot do because it treats every member of a rule alike.
