I refined `set_size_graded_evidence_weight` and kept everything in it: primitive-built rules with a per-primitive cost, the final-digit discount, costs on powers and squares, graded times tables, prime over odd, the repdigit rule, hundred-chart ranges, the size principle, near-all-or-none commitment, and evidence from agreeing examples that grows stronger with the set's size. The one change is taken from `fives_privileged_decoupled_prime_odd`. "Multiples of 5" is a privileged times table, because counting by fives is drilled and membership can be read off the numeral (it ends in 0 or 5), so that rule gets its own fitted prior bonus. So after sets such as 35, 15, 85 people commit to "multiples of 5" and say yes to other numbers ending in 0 or 5. That commitment is weak after a single example such as 35 and sharp after three or four.
