Refinement of `variable_rule_range_expectation`: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over equally plausible mathematical rules, rules held within a magnitude range, and magnitude ranges of 1-100 (size principle, uncertain belief about how often an example is a stray, strays sometimes familiar numbers), and their expectation that the program follows a rule rather than produces a magnitude range still varies from one judgement to the next. The one change, taken from `cluster_cued_range_salience`, is that the average of that expectation depends on how the examples look: the more tightly the examples cluster on the number line, the more people expect a "numbers around here" program instead of a rule. So when examples that share a rule sit close together (31, 3, 1, 15 or 37, 57), people fill in the rule's non-members inside the examples' span more often than for the same rule spread widely across 1-100.
