Refinement of `variable_rule_range_expectation`: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over equally plausible mathematical rules, rules held within a magnitude range, and magnitude ranges of 1-100, with the size principle, an uncertain belief about how often an example is a stray, strays that are sometimes familiar numbers, and an expectation of a rule rather than a range that varies from one judgement to the next. The one change, taken from `thurstonian_decision_noise`, is how that belief becomes an answer: people do not read out their belief exactly. Their felt confidence that the target belongs fluctuates from trial to trial around the belief, by a stated typical amount (about half a unit of log-odds), and they say yes when it favours membership. So near-certain judgements, such as "no" to a number outside every plausible concept or "yes" to a member of a decisively winning rule, are pulled slightly towards even odds, in proportion to how extreme they are, while judgements where rule and range compete stay as variable as before.
