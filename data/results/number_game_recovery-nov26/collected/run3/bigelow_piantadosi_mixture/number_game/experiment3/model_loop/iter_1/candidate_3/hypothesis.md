Refinement of `range_limited_rule_familiar_strays`: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over equally plausible mathematical rules, magnitude ranges of 1-100 and rules held within a range, with the size principle, an uncertain belief about how often an example is a stray, and strays that are sometimes familiar numbers. The one change is the prior over magnitude ranges: besides an arbitrary stretch of the number line, people readily think of one-sided thresholds ("numbers below 32", "numbers above 60"), which get an inferred extra share of the prior on ranges. So when examples sit together at one end of the number line (31, 3, 1, 15), people fill in the non-members of the fitting rule (2, 4, 16) more readily than when the same rule's examples span an arbitrary middle stretch.
