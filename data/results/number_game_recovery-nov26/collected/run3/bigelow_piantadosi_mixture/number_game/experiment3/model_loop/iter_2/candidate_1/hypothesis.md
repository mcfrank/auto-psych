Refinement of `variable_rule_range_expectation`, adding one representativeness bias to its Bayesian concept learning. That learning is unchanged: equally plausible rules, rules held within a range, and magnitude ranges; the size principle; uncertain reliability with familiar strays; and a rule-versus-range expectation that varies from judgement to judgement. The bias is this: people expect a sample from a rule that runs across the whole number line (such as "odd numbers" or "multiples of 4") to look like that rule, spread over its members. So examples that fit such a rule but sit unusually close together (31, 3, 1, 15 or 37, 57) count against it, to an inferred degree, beyond what the size principle implies. In those narrow clustered sets people then lean towards a magnitude range and fill in the rule's non-members inside the examples' span, while widely spread examples keep the rule.
