Refinement of `range_limited_rule_familiar_strays` (the same Bayesian concept learning over equally plausible rules, range-limited rules and magnitude ranges, with the size principle, uncertain reliability and familiar strays): people expect a magnitude-range program to cover a typical width of numbers, so ranges a few dozen numbers wide are a priori plausible while very wide ranges are not (an Erlang prior on range size, typical width inferred), instead of every range being equally plausible. The disagreement with the best model lives in sets whose examples share a rule: when they span a narrow stretch, people believe a range and fill it with the rule's non-members (e.g. 7, 13, 15 after 4, 16, 12), but when the examples span more than about 40 numbers the range explanation collapses and non-members inside the span are rejected more firmly than the best model predicts.
