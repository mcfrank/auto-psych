Refinement of `variable_rule_range_expectation`. People do the same Bayesian concept learning over equally plausible mathematical rules, rules held within a magnitude range, and magnitude ranges of 1-100, with the size principle and an uncertain belief about how many examples come from the main concept, and their expectation that the program follows a rule rather than produces a magnitude range still varies from one judgement to the next. The one change, taken from `two_source_range_limited_rules`, is what people think the off-pattern examples are: either glitches (sometimes familiar numbers) that license no generalisation, or the output of a second familiar rule the program also runs, whose other members they then also endorse. So an off-pattern example that fits a small rule (e.g. 64 among 13, 23, 43) makes people say yes to that rule's other members, while the main pattern's commitment to rule or range stays as variable as before.
