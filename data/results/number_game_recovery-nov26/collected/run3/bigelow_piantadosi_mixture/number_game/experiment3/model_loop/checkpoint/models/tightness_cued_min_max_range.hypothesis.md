Refinement of `variable_min_max_bounded_range`: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over equally plausible mathematical rules, rules held within a magnitude range, and magnitude ranges of 1-100 (size principle, uncertain stray rate, strays sometimes familiar numbers), the range from the smallest to the largest example comes to mind far more readily than any other range, and their expectation of a rule rather than a range varies from one judgement to the next. The one change, taken from `cluster_cued_range_salience`, is that the average of that expectation depends on how the examples look: the more tightly the examples cluster on the number line, the more people expect a "numbers from the lowest to the highest example" program instead of a rule. So clustered examples that share a rule (37, 57 or 4, 16, 12) lead people to fill in the rule's non-members between the examples (but not beyond them) more often than the same rule's examples spread widely across 1-100.
