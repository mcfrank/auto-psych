Refinement of `two_source_range_limited_rules`. People think the hidden program may run two sources at once: a main concept (a rule over 1-100, a magnitude range, or a rule held within a range), learned with the size principle and an uncertain belief about how many examples come from it, and a secondary source behind the off-pattern examples, which is either a second familiar rule they generalise from or glitches (sometimes familiar numbers) that license no generalisation. The one change, taken from `cluster_cued_range_salience`, is that the main program's being a magnitude range comes to mind more readily the more tightly the examples cluster on the number line. So when examples that share a broad rule sit close together (31, 3, 1, 15 or 37, 57), people fill in the rule's non-members inside the examples' span, while for widely spread sets they keep to the rule, and an off-pattern example that fits a small rule still leads them to accept that rule's other members.
