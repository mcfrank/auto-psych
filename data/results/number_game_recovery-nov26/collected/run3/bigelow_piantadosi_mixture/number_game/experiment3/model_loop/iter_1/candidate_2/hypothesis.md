Which kind of explanation comes to mind depends on how the examples look. People infer the hidden program by ordinary Bayesian concept learning over mathematical rules, rules held within a magnitude range, and magnitude ranges (the size principle, an uncertain belief about how often an example is a stray, strays sometimes familiar numbers). But the more tightly the examples cluster on the number line, the more readily the idea "it produces numbers around here" comes to mind, so magnitude ranges gain prior weight in proportion to the cluster's tightness. So when examples that share a rule sit close together (e.g. 31, 3, 1, 15 or 63, 23, 43), people accept the rule's non-members inside the examples' span (even numbers between them) more than the size principle predicts, while for widely spread examples they keep to the rule.
