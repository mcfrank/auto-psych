Refinement of `variable_rule_range_expectation`. People do the same Bayesian concept learning over equally plausible mathematical rules, rules held within a magnitude range, and magnitude ranges of 1-100, with the size principle, an uncertain belief about how often an example is a stray, strays that are sometimes familiar numbers, and an expectation of a rule rather than a range that varies from one judgement to the next. The one change, taken from `one_sided_threshold_ranges`, is which magnitude ranges come to mind: besides an arbitrary stretch of the number line, people readily think of one-sided thresholds ("numbers below 32", "numbers above 60"), which get an inferred extra share of the prior on ranges. So when the examples sit together at one end of the number line (31, 3, 1, 15), people fill in the numbers between them and the nearer end of 1-100, including the fitting rule's non-members (2, 4, 16), more readily than when the same rule's examples span an arbitrary middle stretch.
