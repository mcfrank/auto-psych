Besides exact mathematical rules, people entertain approximate versions of sparse rules: "numbers at or just next to a power of 2 / square / multiple of 10", so a set of near-misses of a small rule (1, 3, 15, 31 are each one off a power of 2) is explained by the approximate rule, and people then endorse the rule's exact members and its other near-misses (2, 4, 16, 17). These approximate rules hold a small stated share of the belief in rules (about 3%), split equally among them; otherwise learning is the incumbent's Bayesian concept learning over equally plausible rules, rules held within a range, and magnitude ranges with a salient min-to-max range, the size principle, uncertain reliability with familiar strays, and a rule-versus-range expectation that varies across judgements.
