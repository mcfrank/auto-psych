People hold familiar number concepts (equally plausible mathematical rules and magnitude ranges of 1-100) as stored lists of member numbers. They judge how alike two numbers are by their ratio rather than their difference, as on a logarithmic mental number line: 8 and 10 are as similar as 80 and 100, so similarity falls off steeply among small numbers and gently among large ones, at an inferred rate. A concept is retrieved to the extent that each example resembles its members on this ratio scale (allowing an uncertain rate of stray examples, sometimes familiar numbers), and a target is endorsed by its ratio-similarity to the nearest member of the retrieved concepts. So near-misses next to large examples (e.g. 62 or 66 after 64) are partly endorsed, but near-misses next to small ones (3 or 5 after 4) are not.
