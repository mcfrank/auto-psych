Refinement of `variable_min_max_bounded_range`. People do the same Bayesian concept learning over equally plausible mathematical rules, rules held within a magnitude range, and magnitude ranges of 1-100 (size principle, uncertain stray rate, strays sometimes familiar numbers), with a rule-versus-range expectation that varies from one judgement to the next, and the range from the smallest to the largest example comes to mind more readily than any other range. The one change is how readily it comes to mind: each example after the first that falls between those limits adds another bit of evidence that "numbers from the lowest to the highest example" is the program, so the extra salience of that range grows in proportion to the number of examples minus one, with no new parameter. With two examples people therefore fill in the span between them less often than the incumbent predicts, and with four clustered examples they fill it in more often.
