People learn the hidden concept by the simplest Bayesian concept learning (equally plausible mathematical rules and magnitude ranges, the size principle, an uncertain belief about how often an example is a stray, strays sometimes familiar numbers), but they do not read out their belief exactly when answering: on every judgement their felt confidence that the target belongs fluctuates randomly around that belief, and they say "yes" when the fluctuating confidence favours membership. This decision noise pulls near-certain judgements slightly towards even odds in proportion to how extreme they are (unlike a lapse's constant floor), while leaving uncertain judgements almost unchanged.
