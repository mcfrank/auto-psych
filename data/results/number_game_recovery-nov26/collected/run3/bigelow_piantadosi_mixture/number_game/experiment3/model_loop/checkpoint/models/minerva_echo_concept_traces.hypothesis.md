People do not infer which concept generated the examples; they hold familiar number concepts (mathematical rules and magnitude ranges of 1-100) as stored memory traces and use the examples together as a retrieval probe, as in an echo-based exemplar memory. Each stored concept is activated by a steep, inferred power of its similarity to the probe (how many examples it contains relative to its own size and the number of examples), and a target is endorsed in proportion to the share of the total activation coming from stored concepts that contain it. Because activation grows only by a fixed power of overlap, small concepts gain from more examples far less than under Bayesian inference, and a concept missing one example is merely weakened, not excluded.
