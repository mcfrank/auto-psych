People infer the hidden concept by ordinary Bayesian concept learning (equally plausible mathematical rules and magnitude ranges of 1-100, the size principle, an uncertain belief about how often an example is a stray, strays sometimes being familiar numbers), but they answer through a consensus check rather than by reporting their belief: they bring a few candidate concepts to mind, sampled from what they believe, and say "yes" only if every one of them contains the target. How many concepts must agree is inferred. A demanding consensus turns uncertain targets into "no" while barely affecting near-certain ones, a lenient one ("any concept that fits will do") does the reverse, and a single sample is plain probability matching.
