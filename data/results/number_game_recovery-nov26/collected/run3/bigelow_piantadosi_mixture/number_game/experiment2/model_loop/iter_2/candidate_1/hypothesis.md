People do Bayesian concept learning over mathematical rules and magnitude ranges of 1-100 (size principle, a rule prior set by kind of rule, their own trust in the first example and an uncertain belief about how often the later examples are strays), but their idea of what a stray looks like follows subjective randomness (representativeness): a glitch or random draw should look random, so they expect strays to be unremarkable numbers (58, 87, 94) and not familiar, patterned ones (1, 2, 16, 64, 100), with how strong this expectation is inferred. So an unremarkable off-pattern example is easily written off and the majority rule survives, while a familiar off-pattern example is hard to dismiss as noise and counts as genuine evidence against tight rules — the opposite of the familiar-stray account.
