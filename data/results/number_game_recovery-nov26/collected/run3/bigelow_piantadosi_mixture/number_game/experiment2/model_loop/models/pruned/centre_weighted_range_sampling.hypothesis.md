People do Bayesian concept learning over familiar mathematical rules (plausibility set by kind of rule) and magnitude ranges of 1-100 (size principle, an uncertain belief about how often an example is a stray, strays sometimes familiar numbers), but they believe a program that produces numbers from a magnitude range produces numbers near the middle of its range more often than numbers near its edges. So examples bunched together are taken as the centre of a wider range rather than as filling a narrow one, examples sitting at the extremes of a range count as weak evidence for it, and generalisation from clustered examples extends further beyond them than if people assumed every number in the range were equally likely.
