Refinement of `familiar_strays_family_level_prior`: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over mathematical rules (plausibility set only by kind of rule) and magnitude ranges of 1-100, with the size principle and an uncertain belief about how often an example is a stray. The one change is how they judge which numbers are likely strays. The incumbent mixes random numbers with members of familiar rules, so every number has a fixed floor chance of being a stray. Here the chance is a graded function of how familiar the number is (how readily small, well-known rules produce it), sharpened or flattened to an inferred degree that could even reverse it so that strays are expected to look random. So the ease of writing off an off-pattern example changes smoothly along the whole familiarity scale (64 > 36 > 50 > 58), not in two tiers.
