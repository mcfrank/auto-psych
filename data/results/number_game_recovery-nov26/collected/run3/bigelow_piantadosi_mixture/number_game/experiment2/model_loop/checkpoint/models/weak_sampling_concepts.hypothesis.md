People treat the examples only as numbers the program is allowed to produce, not as random samples drawn from its concept (weak sampling): a candidate rule or magnitude range gains plausibility merely by containing the examples (and loses it for each example it misses, which they may write off as a stray), but not for being small, so a concept's plausibility rests on its prior — one plausibility per kind of rule (parity, primes, squares and cubes, powers, multiples, final digit, contained digit) shared by the rules of that kind, and a preference for narrower ranges — and does not tighten as more consistent examples arrive. This predicts that adding examples consistent with both a broad and a tight concept (e.g. 16, 8, 64 under "even" and "powers of 2") leaves generalisation about as broad as after the first example, unlike the size principle.
