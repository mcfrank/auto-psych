Refinement of `graded_exemplar_concept_retrieval`: people hold familiar number concepts (rules by kind, and magnitude ranges of 1-100) as stored member lists, retrieve a concept to the extent that each example resembles its members on the number line, allow for an uncertain rate of unrelated stray examples, and endorse a target by its closeness to the nearest stored member of the retrieved concepts. The one change, taken from `familiar_strays_family_level_prior`, is what a stray looks like: a stray is sometimes a "familiar" number (a member of some well-known rule) rather than always a random number from 1-100, with how often inferred, so examples that many small rules produce (1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 64, 100) are easily written off as strays and constrain retrieval less, while unremarkable examples (58, 87, 94) must be resembled by the retrieved concept.
