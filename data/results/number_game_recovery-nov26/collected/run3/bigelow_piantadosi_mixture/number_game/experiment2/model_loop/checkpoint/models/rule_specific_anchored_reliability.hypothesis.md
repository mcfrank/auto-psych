Refinement of `first_example_anchored_reliability`. People do the same Bayesian concept learning over mathematical rules and magnitude ranges of 1-100: the size principle, a prior over rules organised by kind of rule, the first example trusted with its own reliability, and the later examples pooled into an uncertain belief about how often the program produces a stray. The one change is that each rule also has its own plausibility. It is drawn close to the shared level of its family (a small fixed spread, taken from the partially pooled prior of `family_pooled_uncertain_reliability`), so particular rules such as "powers of 5" or "ends in 5" can be adopted somewhat more or less readily than other rules of their kind, instead of all rules in a family being exactly equally plausible.
