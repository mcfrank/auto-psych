People do the same Bayesian concept learning as `first_example_anchored_reliability`: a family-organised prior over mathematical rules, magnitude ranges of 1-100, the size principle, their own trust in the first example, and uncertain reliability for the later ones. On top of that, they also consider *range-restricted rules*, meaning a rule's members inside a magnitude range ("multiples of 4 between 52 and 88", "even numbers below 30"). A priori these restricted rules take a small, stated share of belief (about 5%). The one change is this larger concept space. It predicts that when examples fit a rule and also cluster in magnitude (e.g. 92, 56), people say "yes" to rule members near the examples but not to members far from them, and say "no" to non-members inside the range.
