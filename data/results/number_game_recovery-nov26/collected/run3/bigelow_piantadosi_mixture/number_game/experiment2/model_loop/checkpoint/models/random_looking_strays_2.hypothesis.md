People do Bayesian concept learning over mathematical rules and magnitude ranges of 1-100 (size principle, a prior over rules set by kind of rule, an uncertain belief about how often an example is a stray), but their idea of what a stray looks like follows their intuition about randomness: a stray is a "random-looking", unremarkable number (58, 87, 94), not a special one, so examples that many familiar rules produce (1, 4, 8, 16, 64, 100) are hard to write off as strays and must be covered by the concept, while unremarkable outliers are easily excused. This disagrees most sharply with the best model (which treats familiar numbers as the easiest strays) on sets where all but one example fit a tight rule: when the lone outlier is a familiar number, people should abandon the majority rule for a concept that covers it and endorse the majority rule's other members less than when the outlier is unremarkable.
