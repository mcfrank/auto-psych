Refinement of `first_example_anchored_reliability`: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over mathematical rules and magnitude ranges of 1-100 (rule plausibility set by kind of rule, the first example trusted at its own inferred reliability, the later examples pooled into an uncertain belief about how often the program produces a stray). The one change is the sampling assumption behind the size principle: people know the program never repeats a number within a set, so they treat the genuine examples as drawn from the concept without replacement, and each genuine example after the first must be one of the concept's remaining members. This adds no free parameter. It makes sets that nearly exhaust a tiny concept (cubes, powers of 3, 5 or 10, a range only a few numbers wide) much stronger evidence for that concept, while leaving large concepts unchanged, so people commit more sharply to tiny concepts and generalise less beyond them.
