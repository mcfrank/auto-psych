Refinement of `family_pooled_uncertain_reliability`: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over the 45 rules (with the family-structured, partially pooled rule prior) and all intervals of 1-100, with the size principle and an uncertain belief about how reliable the program is on each set. The one change, taken from `rule_generated_stray_concepts`, is what people think a stray example is: some strays are random numbers from 1-100, but a share of them (how large is inferred) are numbers that some *other* familiar rule produced, picked in proportion to how plausible people find each rule. So examples that many small rules produce (1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 64, 81, 100) are easily written off as strays and constrain the concept less, while unremarkable examples that few rules produce (e.g. 58, 87, 94) are hard to dismiss and the concept has to cover them.
