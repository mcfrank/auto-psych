People hold familiar number concepts (parity, primes, squares and cubes, powers, multiples, final digit, contained digit, and magnitude ranges of 1-100) as stored lists of member numbers, but they compare numbers to those members by their written form, not by their place on the number line: two numbers are similar to the extent that they have the same digit in the same position (tens, ones), with similarity falling off by a constant inferred factor for each digit position that differs. A concept is retrieved to the extent that each example resembles its members in this digit-wise way (allowing for an uncertain rate of unrelated stray examples, rules plausible only by kind), and a target is endorsed by its digit-wise similarity to the nearest member of the retrieved concepts, so a non-member that differs from a member by one digit (84 or 65 under "powers of 2" via 64) is partly endorsed while one that is numerically close but differs in both digits (59 next to 60) is not.
