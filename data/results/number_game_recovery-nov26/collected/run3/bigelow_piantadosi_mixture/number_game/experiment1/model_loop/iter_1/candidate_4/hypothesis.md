Refinement of `bigelow_piantadosi_independent`: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over its 45 rules (each with its own inferred prior weight) and all intervals of 1-100, but they weigh the examples' evidence with an inferred strength rather than as an ideal sampler — the likelihood of the examples under each concept (the size principle) is raised to a free exponent, so values below 1 mean people generalise more broadly than the examples warrant (weaker sampling assumptions) and values above 1 mean they commit to the smallest consistent concept faster. The single change is this evidence-weighting exponent, which governs how sharply additional examples narrow generalisation, e.g. from "even numbers" to "powers of 2" after 16, 8, 64.
