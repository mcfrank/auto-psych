People do not reason about which concept is literally consistent with the examples; they hold number concepts in memory as stored sets of member numbers (the familiar rules like even, squares, powers of 2, multiples, final and contained digits, and magnitude ranges), and judge by exemplar similarity on the number line. Each stored concept is retrieved in proportion to how similar the examples are, on average, to its stored members (a concept with few members that lie near every example is retrieved most strongly, so an example close to but not in a concept still partly supports it), and a target is endorsed by its similarity to the nearest stored member of the retrieved concepts, so endorsement falls off gradedly for targets just outside a concept rather than dropping to zero.
