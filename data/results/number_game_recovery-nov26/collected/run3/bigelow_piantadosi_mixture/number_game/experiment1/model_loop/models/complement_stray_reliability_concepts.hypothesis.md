Refinement of `uncertain_reliability_noise_concepts`: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over the 45 rules (each with its own inferred prior weight) and all intervals of 1-100 with the size principle, and they are just as unsure how reliable the program is on each set — but they think of a stray example as a mistake, a number that does *not* belong to the concept, rather than a draw that could land anywhere in 1-100. The one change is this stray distribution, drawn from the concept's complement: an example that a broad concept (e.g. "even", "contains a 3") misses is better explained as a stray than under a uniform stray generator, whereas a tight concept gains nothing. So people keep broad rules that miss one example more readily, and examples that a concept already contains are always taken as genuine evidence for it.
