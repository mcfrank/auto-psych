Refinement of the size-principle Bayesian concept learner (45 rules with their own prior weights plus all magnitude intervals of 1-100, with an uncertain belief about how often an example is a stray draw): people know the program never repeats a number within a set, so they reason as if the genuine examples were drawn from the concept without replacement, not independently. This makes each additional example much stronger evidence for small concepts that the examples nearly exhaust (cubes, high powers, very narrow ranges) while leaving large concepts unchanged, so the disagreement with the best model lies in sets that sit inside a tiny concept: there people should commit to that concept and reject neighbouring or merely broader-rule targets more sharply than independent sampling predicts.
