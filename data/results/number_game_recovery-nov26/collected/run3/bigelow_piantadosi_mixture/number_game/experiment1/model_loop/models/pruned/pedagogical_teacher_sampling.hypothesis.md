People reason about the examples as a communicative act: they assume the program's examples were picked by a helpful teacher who chooses numbers that point to the intended concept and away from other plausible concepts, rather than drawn at random from it. So an example that belongs to few other plausible rules or ranges (e.g. 64, which says "powers of 2") counts as strong evidence for the concepts containing it, while an example that belongs to many (e.g. 16 or 50) counts as weak evidence; the strength of this pedagogical assumption is inferred, and with none people reduce to ordinary size-principle Bayesian concept learning over the 45 rules and all intervals of 1-100.
