Refinement of `equal_prior_concepts_with_guess_lapse`: people do Bayesian concept learning over the same 45 arithmetic and digit rules and every interval of 1-100 (size principle, 10% of examples random, occasional guessing at their own yes rate), but they bring one general Occam-style specificity bias to every candidate concept, rules and numeric ranges alike: before seeing any examples, a concept's prior plausibility falls (or rises) as a power of how many numbers it contains. The one change is replacing the flat priors within the rules and within the intervals with this single shared size-graded prior, which predicts not only weaker generalization to broad rules (e.g. all odd numbers after 85, 19, 91) but also tighter, more local "yes" ranges around clustered examples than an equal-prior learner, with both effects set by the same one parameter.
