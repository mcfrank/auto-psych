People treat the examples as chosen on purpose by a helpful teacher rather than drawn at random from the hidden concept: they assume the program picks, from among the concept's members, numbers that would best lead a learner to that concept (numbers that few other plausible concepts contain), and they infer the concept by reasoning about why these particular examples were chosen. Over the same arithmetic rules, digit concepts and numeric ranges (with occasional guessing), this pedagogical reading predicts that an example typical of many concepts (e.g. a small even number) says less about the concept than a distinctive one, so generalization follows the concept the examples single out more than the size principle alone implies, with the strength of this teacher assumption inferred from the answers.
