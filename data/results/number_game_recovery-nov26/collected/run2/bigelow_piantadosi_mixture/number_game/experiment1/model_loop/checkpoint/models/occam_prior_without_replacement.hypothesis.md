Refinement of `occam_specificity_prior_all_concepts`: people do Bayesian concept learning over the same 45 arithmetic and digit rules and every interval of 1-100, with one shared Occam-style specificity bias that makes every concept's prior (rules and ranges alike) fall or rise as a power of its size, 10% of examples random and occasional guessing at their own yes rate. The one change, taken from `without_replacement_size_principle`, is that people take the program's examples to be distinct, so each further example is a draw from the concept's members not yet shown (strong sampling without replacement). Together with the shared specificity prior, this predicts that examples that nearly use up a small rule or a short range (e.g. 16, 8, 64 for powers of 2, or 60, 62, 64 for a tight range) make that concept even more credible, so people say "yes" less to members of broad rules and wide ranges than either change alone predicts.
