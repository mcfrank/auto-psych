Refinement of `concept_learning_with_guess_lapse`: people do Bayesian concept learning over the same 45 rules (including the digit concepts) and every interval of 1-100. They apply the size principle, treat 10% of examples as random, and on a fixed fraction of trials ignore the concept and guess "yes" at their own baseline rate. The one change is that their prior over rules is neither 45 free weights nor flat. It follows a specificity bias: a rule's prior plausibility falls (or rises) as a power of how many numbers it contains, so narrow rules like "powers of 3" are favoured over broad ones like "odd numbers" before any example is seen. This predicts weaker generalization to broad rules (e.g. all odd numbers after 85, 19, 91) than an equal-prior learner, with the strength of the bias set by a single parameter.
