People do not infer a single concept that covers all the examples; they store each example and judge the target by its summed (averaged) similarity to those stored examples, as in an exemplar model. The similarity of the target to one example is how likely the two are to share a number concept — an arithmetic or digit rule (e.g. "powers of 2", "contains a 3") or a numeric range — judged from that example alone, so sharing a narrow property counts for more than sharing a broad one, and the more summed similarity, the more likely a "yes". Because similarity is added across examples rather than requiring one concept to fit them all, a target resembling only one of the examples (e.g. 17 after 16, 8, 64) still draws a substantial share of "yes" answers.
