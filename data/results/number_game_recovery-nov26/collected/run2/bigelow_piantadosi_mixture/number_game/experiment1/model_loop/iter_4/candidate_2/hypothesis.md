People do Bayesian concept learning over arithmetic rules, digit concepts and numeric ranges. They assume the program shows distinct numbers, sampled from the concept, and they sometimes guess. Considering a rule costs mental effort that grows with how long the rule takes to state, so wordier rules are less often entertained. Single-word rules like "odd", "squares" or "primes" are cheapest. Rules with a parameter ("multiples of 7", "ends in 3", "contains a 5") cost more. Rules with an exception ("powers of 2 except 32") cost the most. This description-length cost replaces any preference for broad or narrow rules. It predicts fewer "yes" answers for exception rules and parameterised families than their size alone implies, while simple rules keep their pull.
