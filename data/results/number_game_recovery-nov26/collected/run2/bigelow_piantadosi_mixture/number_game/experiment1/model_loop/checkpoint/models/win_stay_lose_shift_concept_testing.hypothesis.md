People do not hold a full posterior over concepts; they read the examples one at a time while holding a single candidate concept (from the same arithmetic and digit rules, with a breadth-graded rule prior, and numeric ranges), keeping it as long as each new example is one of its members ("win-stay") and, only when an example falls outside it, discarding it and drawing a fresh concept in proportion to how well concepts explain all the examples so far ("lose-shift"). Because a concept that survives is never re-weighed, later examples that fit a broad concept do not sharpen belief toward a narrower one, so this sequential hypothesis-testing process predicts more "yes" answers to members of broad rules and wide ranges after several consistent examples (e.g. 16, 8, 64 or 60, 62, 64) than full Bayesian updating, and it makes the answer depend on which example came first.
