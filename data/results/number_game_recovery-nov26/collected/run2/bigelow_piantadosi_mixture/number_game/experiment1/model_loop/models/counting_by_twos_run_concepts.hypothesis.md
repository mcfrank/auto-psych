Refinement of `rule_breadth_prior_concepts`: people do Bayesian concept learning over the same arithmetic and digit rules (with their size-graded rule prior) and every interval of 1-100, with the size principle, 10% of examples random and occasional guessing. They also entertain one more kind of concept, "counting by twos" runs: a bounded stretch of every other number, such as 60, 62, 64, 66 or 31, 33, 35, i.e. the even or the odd numbers within a limited range. All such runs are equally likely a priori, and the family as a whole takes an inferred share of prior belief relative to the rules. This predicts that examples spaced by two (e.g. 60, 62, 64) draw "yes" to nearby same-parity numbers but not to same-parity numbers far away, a pattern that neither "even numbers" nor a numeric range gives.
