People do Bayesian concept learning over the same arithmetic and digit rules (with a size-graded rule prior) and every interval of 1-100, with occasional guessing, treating the examples as distinct draws from the hidden concept (strong sampling without replacement, with some stray random examples). The one new claim is about how they believe the program picks numbers: not uniformly, but with round numbers (multiples of 5 and 10) more or less likely to be produced than other numbers, with the strength and direction of this round-number bias inferred from their answers. Because a concept's members are then weighted by how readily the program would emit them, this predicts that concepts made mostly of round numbers (e.g. "multiples of 10", "ends in 0" or "ends in 5") gain or lose credibility relative to other rules and ranges. It also predicts that a round example says less about the concept than a non-round one does, or more.
