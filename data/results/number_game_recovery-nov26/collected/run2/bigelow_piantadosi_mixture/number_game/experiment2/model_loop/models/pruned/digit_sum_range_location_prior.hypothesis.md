Refinement of `digit_sum_concepts` (the incumbent): people do Bayesian concept learning over the same arithmetic and digit rules, the 18 "digits add up to s" concepts and every numeric range of 1-100, reading the examples as distinct draws with some strays, sometimes guessing, and favouring narrow ranges. The one change, the component taken from `range_location_gradient_prior`, is that the range prior also depends on where a range sits on the number line: before seeing examples, people find a stretch that starts higher up (e.g. "70 to 80") more (or less) plausible than an equally wide one that starts low (e.g. "5 to 15"), with prior plausibility rising or falling steadily with the range's lower bound (strength and direction inferred). This predicts that "yes" answers spread further on one side of a cluster of examples than on the other, while digit-sum and rule generalization are kept as in the incumbent.
