Refinement of `digit_sum_concepts` (the incumbent): people do Bayesian concept learning over the same arithmetic and digit rules, the 18 "digits add up to s" concepts and every numeric range of 1-100, reading the examples as distinct draws with some strays, sometimes guessing, and with the same width-graded range prior. The one change, the component taken from `open_ended_range_discount`, is that a range running to an end of the number line ("anything up to 30", "anything from 70 on") is a threshold rule, not a bounded stretch, and people hold these open-ended ranges at a different prior weight from bounded ranges of the same width. That weight is inferred from their answers, and the data suggest it is lower. This predicts fewer "yes" answers to numbers far below small examples, or far above large ones, all the way down to 1 or up to 100, while digit-sum and rule generalization and "yes" to nearby numbers on both sides of the examples are kept.
