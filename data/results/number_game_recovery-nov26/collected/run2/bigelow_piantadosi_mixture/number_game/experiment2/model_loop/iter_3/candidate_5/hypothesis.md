Refinement of `heading_direction_range_extrapolation`: people do Bayesian concept learning over the same arithmetic, digit and digit-sum rules and every numeric range of 1-100. They read the examples as distinct draws with some strays, sometimes guess, favour narrow ranges, and let the direction of the last step (up or down) stretch the ranges they consider further in the direction the sequence is heading. The one change, the component taken from `open_ended_range_discount`, is that a range running all the way to an end of the number line ("anything up to 30", "anything from 70 on") counts as a threshold rule rather than a bounded stretch, and gets its own prior weight relative to bounded ranges (inferred; the data suggest lower). The prediction: the heading still pushes "yes" answers further on the side the sequence was moving toward, but that spread stops short of 1 or 100 instead of running to the end of the line, so far-out numbers get fewer "yes" answers on both sides than the heading model gives.
