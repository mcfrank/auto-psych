Refinement of `occam_prior_without_replacement`: people do Bayesian concept learning over the same 45 arithmetic and digit rules and every interval of 1-100, reading each example as a distinct draw from the concept's members not yet shown (sampling without replacement), with 10% stray examples and occasional guessing. The one change is where the Occam bias applies. It works only on numeric ranges: before seeing examples, a range's plausibility falls (or rises) as a power of its width, and all rules keep an equal prior. People's preference for narrow concepts is then a magnitude habit about short stretches of the number line, not a preference among arithmetic rules. This predicts that after a single example (e.g. 10 or 49), "yes" falls off faster with distance beyond the immediate neighbours than an equal-prior learner predicts, while rule-based generalization (e.g. powers of 2 after 16, 8, 64) is unchanged.
