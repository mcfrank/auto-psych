People are unsure how the program's examples were chosen: they partly believe the examples were sampled from the hidden concept (so a small concept that fits them all is a suspicious coincidence, the size principle) and partly believe the examples are just arbitrary numbers that happen to carry the label (so every concept that contains them all stays equally credible however broad it is), with the strength of that belief inferred. Otherwise they learn as the best model does (the same rules, digit-sum concepts and narrow-favouring, open-ended-discounted, margin-credited ranges, with strays and guessing). This predicts that broad rules and wide ranges consistent with the examples keep a floor of "yes" that does not shrink as more examples are added, e.g. more "yes" to far members of a wide consistent range or to other even numbers after 16, 8, 64 than the pure size principle gives.
