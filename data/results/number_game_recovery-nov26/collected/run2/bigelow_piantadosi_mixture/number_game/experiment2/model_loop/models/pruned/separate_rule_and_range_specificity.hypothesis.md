Refinement of `narrow_range_specificity_bias`: people do Bayesian concept learning over arithmetic and digit rules and every numeric range, reading the examples as distinct draws from the concept (with some strays) and sometimes guessing, and they already judge a narrow range a likelier program than a wide one, as a power of its width. The one change, the component taken from `rule_breadth_prior_concepts`, is that the rules no longer share an equal prior: a second, separate breadth bias makes a rule's prior rise or fall as a power of how many numbers it contains. The claim is that people bring two distinct specificity habits, one about stretches of the number line and one about arithmetic rules, each with its own strength and direction (the data suggest a narrow-range preference alongside a slight preference for broad rules). This predicts that broad rules such as "odd numbers" (after 85, 19, 91) or "even numbers" win a different share of "yes" than the range-only bias allows, while the tight "yes" gradients around clustered examples (e.g. 60, 62, 64) are kept.
