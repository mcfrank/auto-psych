Refinement of `without_replacement_size_principle` (the incumbent): people do Bayesian concept learning over the same 45 arithmetic and digit rules (equal rule prior) and every interval of 1-100, reading each further example as a draw from the concept's members not yet shown (sampling without replacement), with 10% of examples random and occasional guessing at their own yes rate. The one change is that, before seeing any examples, they do not find all numeric ranges equally plausible: a range's prior plausibility falls (or rises) as a power of its width, with the strength of this preference for narrow ranges inferred from their answers, while the rules keep their flat prior. This predicts that when the examples point to a range, people generalize more tightly around the examples (fewer "yes" answers to numbers far beyond them) than an equal-prior learner does, and that a range explanation loses a little ground to the rules on scattered sets.
