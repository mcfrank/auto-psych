Refinement of `without_replacement_size_principle`: people do Bayesian concept learning over the same 45 arithmetic and digit rules and every interval of 1-100. As in that model, they read the examples as distinct draws (sampling without replacement), with 10% random examples. The one change is that, before seeing any examples, they find narrow numeric ranges more plausible than wide ones: each range's prior weight falls as a power of its width, with the strength of this preference inferred from the answers. This predicts tighter "yes" regions around clustered examples, and fewer "yes" answers to distant numbers that only a wide range would cover. Their occasional off-concept answers are a bare "yes", which is what the incumbent's guessing rate converges to on this data, so the model has no more free parameters than the incumbent.
