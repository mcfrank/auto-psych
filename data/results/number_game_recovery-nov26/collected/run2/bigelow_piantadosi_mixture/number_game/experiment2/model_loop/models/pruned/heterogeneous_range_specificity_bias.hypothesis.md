Refinement of `narrow_range_specificity_bias` (the incumbent): people do Bayesian concept learning over the same 45 arithmetic and digit rules (equal prior) and every numeric range of 1-100, reading the examples as distinct draws with some strays and sometimes guessing, and each person's prior over ranges rises or falls as a power of the range's width. The one change is that the strength of this specificity bias differs from person to person: the population holds a spread of width biases, with some people strongly preferring narrow ranges and others preferring wide ones, and the spread is inferred from the answers, where the incumbent assumes a single shared bias. This predicts the extra disagreement between respondents on items of moderate confidence, such as targets just beyond a cluster like 60, 62, 64, where narrow-range people say "no" and wide-range people say "yes". It also predicts a population "yes" gradient that is sharp near the examples but has longer tails than any single shared bias gives, while generalization by rules is left unchanged.
