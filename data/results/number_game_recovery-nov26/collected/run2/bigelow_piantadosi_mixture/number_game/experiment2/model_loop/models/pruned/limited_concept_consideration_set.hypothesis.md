People do not weigh every conceivable concept: they bring to mind a limited sample of candidate concepts (arithmetic and digit rules, digit-sum concepts, numeric ranges), each coming to mind in proportion to how plausible it seems before any examples, and then judge the target only by those candidates that came to mind, weighted by how well they explain the examples, as in the best model. Because a simple, a priori plausible rule is almost always among the candidates however plausible it is, the advantage of plausible concepts saturates, so relative to full Bayesian weighing this predicts somewhat more "yes" by low-prior concepts such as a particular narrow range or a digit-sum family and less by broad rules, with the size of the consideration set inferred from the answers.
