People do Bayesian concept learning over the same arithmetic and digit rules, digit-sum concepts and narrow-favouring numeric ranges as the best model, but they read the order of the examples as a direction of travel (rising if the last example is above the first, falling if below) and take it as evidence that the program's range runs on in that direction, crediting ranges by how far they extend ahead of the sequence rather than behind it (with an inferred strength). The disagreement with the best model lives just outside the examples' span: this predicts more "yes" to numbers beyond the last-shown end of a set than beyond the first-shown end (e.g. 70 after 52, 60, 66 but less so after 66, 60, 52), whereas the best model ignores order for ranges and generalizes symmetrically.
