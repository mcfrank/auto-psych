People do not infer one concept for the whole set; they store each example and compare the target to every stored example in turn, judging each pairwise resemblance as how likely the two numbers are to share an arithmetic or digit rule (e.g. "powers of 2", "contains a 3") or a numeric range, so sharing a narrow property counts for more than sharing a broad one. These resemblances combine multiplicatively, as a geometric mean over the examples (a conjunctive exemplar rule: the target must resemble every example, and one poor match drags the whole judgment down), and the greater this overall resemblance, the more likely a "yes". A target that matches only some of the examples (e.g. 17 after 16, 8, 64) is therefore strongly rejected, unlike in an additive exemplar model.
