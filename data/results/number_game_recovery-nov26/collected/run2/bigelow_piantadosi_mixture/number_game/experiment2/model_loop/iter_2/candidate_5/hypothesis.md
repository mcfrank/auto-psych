Refinement of `open_ended_range_discount`: people do Bayesian concept learning over the same 45 arithmetic and digit rules and every numeric range of 1-100. They read the examples as distinct draws with 10% strays and sometimes guess. They keep the Weber-like prior that favours ranges narrow relative to their magnitude, and they hold open-ended threshold ranges ("up to 30", "from 70 on") at a lower prior than bounded ones. The one change, the component taken from `digit_sum_concepts`, is that they also read the numbers as written numerals and entertain "numbers whose digits add up to s" as candidate concepts, all 18 equally likely, with the family taking an inferred share of the prior given to rules. This predicts that examples sharing a digit sum (e.g. 36, 45, 54, 81) draw "yes" to other numbers with that digit sum (63, 72, 27), together with fewer "yes" answers far out toward 1 or 100 beyond small or large examples than a bounded-range-only learner gives; the digit-sum concepts and the open-ended discount act on separate parts of the hypothesis space.
