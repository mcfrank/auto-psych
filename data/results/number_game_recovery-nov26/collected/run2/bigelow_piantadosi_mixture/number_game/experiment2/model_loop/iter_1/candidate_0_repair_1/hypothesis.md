People judge the target by its feature-contrast similarity to the example set as a whole: they notice the properties every example shares (parity, final digit, a contained digit, being a square, power or multiple of something), and the target counts as similar to the extent it keeps those shared properties and dissimilar to the extent it lacks them, with rarer, more distinctive shared properties (e.g. "power of 2") weighing far more than common ones (e.g. "even"); on magnitude, similarity falls off steadily with how far the target lies outside the stretch the examples span. The more similar on this metric, the more likely a "yes" — there is no weighing of alternative hypotheses, only matching against the set's common features.
