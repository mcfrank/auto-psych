Refinement of `narrow_range_specificity_bias` (the incumbent): people do Bayesian concept learning over the same 45 arithmetic and digit rules (equal prior) and every numeric range of 1-100. As in the incumbent, they read the examples as distinct draws from the concept (sampling without replacement), allow 10% stray examples, sometimes guess, and bias the range prior by width. The one change is that the range prior also depends on where the range sits on the number line. Before seeing examples, people find a stretch that starts higher up (e.g. "70 to 80") more (or less) plausible than an equally wide one that starts low (e.g. "5 to 15"), and a range's prior plausibility rises or falls steadily with its lower bound. This predicts that generalization from a cluster of examples is not symmetric about it: "yes" answers spread further on one side of the examples than on the other (upward or downward, set by the bias), which a width-only prior cannot produce.
