Refinement of `heading_direction_range_extrapolation`: people do Bayesian concept learning over the same arithmetic, digit and digit-sum rules and every numeric range of 1-100. They read the examples as distinct draws with some strays, sometimes guess, weigh ranges by their width, and let the direction of the last step (up or down from the example before) stretch the ranges they consider further in the direction the sequence is heading. The one change, the component taken from `range_location_gradient_prior`, is that before seeing any examples people also find a range more (or less) plausible according to where it starts on the number line, with prior plausibility rising or falling steadily with its lower bound (inferred; the data suggest ranges higher up are favoured). This predicts that, on top of the push toward where the sequence is heading, "yes" spreads further above a cluster of examples than below it even for a single example or a set whose last step points down, an order-independent asymmetry that the heading model cannot produce.
