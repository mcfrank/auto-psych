Refinement of the incumbent `margin_beyond_examples_range_prior`: people do Bayesian concept learning over the same arithmetic and digit rules, the 18 digit-sum concepts and every numeric range of 1-100. They read the examples as distinct draws with some strays, sometimes guess, favour narrow ranges, discount open-ended ranges that run to 1 or 100, and credit a range by how far it reaches past the examples. The one change is a centring expectation: people expect the examples they were shown to sit in the middle of the program's range rather than off to one side of it, so a candidate range that leaves much more room on one side of the examples than on the other is held less plausible, in proportion to how lopsided that room is (the strength is inferred; the data suggest a penalty for lopsided ranges). This predicts that "yes" spreads evenly around a cluster. Numbers just beyond one end get "yes" mainly through ranges that also reach past the other end, so the far reaches on one side only (e.g. 75 after 60, 62, 64) get fewer "yes" than the incumbent gives, while numbers a little beyond either end keep their credit. The effect does not depend on the order the examples were shown in, and rule and digit-sum generalization are unchanged.
