Refinement of `without_replacement_size_principle` (the incumbent): people do Bayesian concept learning over the same 45 arithmetic and digit rules and every numeric range of 1-100, reading the examples as distinct draws (sampling without replacement), with 10% stray examples and occasional guessing at their own yes rate. The one change is a Weber-like sense of how narrow a range is: before seeing examples, people judge a range more (or less) plausible as a power of its width *relative to its magnitude* (width divided by the range's midpoint), so "60 to 64" counts as a tight, specific range while "2 to 6", equally wide in absolute terms, counts as a broad one. This predicts sharper, more local "yes" gradients around clustered large examples (e.g. 60, 62, 64 or 83, 77) than around clustered small ones (e.g. 3, 7 or 5, 9), a magnitude dependence that neither the flat range prior nor an absolute-width prior produces.
