Refinement of `without_replacement_size_principle` (the incumbent): people do Bayesian concept learning over the same 45 arithmetic and digit rules and every interval of 1-100, reading each further example as a draw from the concept's members not yet shown (sampling without replacement), with 10% of examples random and occasional guessing at their own yes rate. The one change is that the final-digit rules ("ends in 3", "ends in 7", ...) are not as plausible a program as the other rules: before seeing examples, people hold the "ends in d" rules at a different prior weight (inferred from their answers; lower if a shared last digit reads as a coincidence of notation rather than a rule), while all other rules keep an equal prior. This predicts fewer (or more) "yes" answers to numbers that share only the examples' final digit (e.g. 53 after 13, 73) than the incumbent gives, while range-based and arithmetic generalization are left as they are.
