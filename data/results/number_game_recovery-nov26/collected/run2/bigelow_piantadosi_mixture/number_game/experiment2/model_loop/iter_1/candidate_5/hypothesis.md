Refinement of `relative_width_range_prior`: people do Bayesian concept learning over the same 45 arithmetic and digit rules and every numeric range of 1-100, reading the examples as distinct draws (sampling without replacement) with 10% strays, occasional guessing, and the same Weber-like prior that favours ranges narrow relative to their magnitude. The one change is that a range running to an end of the number line ("anything up to 30", "anything from 70 on") is a threshold rule rather than a bounded stretch, and people hold these open-ended ranges at a different prior weight from bounded ones (inferred from their answers; the data suggest lower). This predicts fewer "yes" answers to numbers far below small examples, or far above large ones, all the way to 1 or 100, while generalization to nearby numbers on both sides of the examples' span is kept.
