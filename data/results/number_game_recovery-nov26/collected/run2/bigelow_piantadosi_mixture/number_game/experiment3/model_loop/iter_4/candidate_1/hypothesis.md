People do not infer one concept for the whole set; they store each shown example and judge the target by its best match to any single stored example, answering "yes" as readily as the target resembles its closest exemplar. Resemblance between two numbers is how likely they are to fall under a common number concept (an arithmetic, digit or digit-sum rule, or a stretch of the number line) learned from that one example alone, so sharing a narrow concept counts for more than sharing a broad one. Because the other examples neither add to nor dilute the closest match, each added example opens its own neighbourhood of "yes" (e.g. a fourth example 15 after 31, 3, 1 broadens generalization rather than narrowing it), and a target close to just one example is accepted as readily as one fitting them all.
