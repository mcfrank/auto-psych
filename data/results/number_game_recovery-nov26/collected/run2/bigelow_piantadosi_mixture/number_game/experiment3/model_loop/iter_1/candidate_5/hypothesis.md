Refinement of `upward_room_range_prior`: people do Bayesian concept learning over the same arithmetic, digit and digit-sum rules and every numeric range of 1-100, reading the examples as distinct draws with some strays, sometimes guessing, discounting open-ended ranges, and expecting the program's numbers to carry on upward past the largest example (crediting a range by how far it reaches above the examples, whatever the order shown). The one change, the component taken from the incumbent `heading_extrapolation_weber_width`, is that people judge a range's breadth Weber-like, by its width relative to the magnitude of the numbers it covers rather than its absolute width, so "60 to 70" counts as a more specific range than "2 to 12". This predicts an order-free upward spread of "yes" above clusters whose falloff is tighter around large examples than around small ones, while rule and digit-sum generalization are unchanged.
