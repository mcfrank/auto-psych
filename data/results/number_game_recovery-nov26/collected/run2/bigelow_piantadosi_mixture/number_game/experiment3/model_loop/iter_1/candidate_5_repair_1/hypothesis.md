Refinement of `heading_weber_with_upward_counting`: people do Bayesian concept learning over the same arithmetic, digit and digit-sum rules and every numeric range of 1-100. They read the examples as distinct draws with some strays, sometimes guess, judge a range's breadth Weber-like (relative to its magnitude), and expect the program's numbers to carry on past the largest example. The one change drops the order-dependent heading: whatever order the examples came in, people also expect room below the smallest example, and they credit how far a range reaches below at its own strength, separate from how far it reaches above. This predicts "yes" spreading beyond both ends of a cluster by unequal amounts (e.g. 57 and 67 after 60, 62, 64), the same for every presentation order, while rule and digit-sum generalization are unchanged.
