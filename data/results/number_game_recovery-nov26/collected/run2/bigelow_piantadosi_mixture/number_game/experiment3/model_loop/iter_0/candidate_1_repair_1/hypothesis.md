People do Bayesian concept learning over the same arithmetic, digit and digit-sum rules and numeric ranges as the best model (distinct examples with some strays, occasional guessing, ranges stretched toward where the sequence is heading), but they also hold the ten decades ("the single-digit numbers", "the twenties", ..., "the nineties") as named concepts, all equally likely, with a stated prior: naming a decade adds about one-sixth to the prior plausibility of that stretch of the number line, beyond what an unnamed range of the same width gets. This predicts that examples inside one decade (e.g. 83, 87 or 24, 28) draw somewhat more "yes" to the rest of that decade and a sharper drop just past its round-number boundaries (e.g. fewer "yes" to 91 after 83, 87) than a learner who weighs ranges by width alone.
