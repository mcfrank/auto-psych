People only start looking for an arithmetic or digit rule when there is a pattern across several examples to notice: with a single example they think mainly in terms of a stretch of the number line around it, and each further example shown makes a rule (e.g. "powers of 2", "ends in 3", digits adding to 9) come to mind more readily relative to a numeric range, with the strength of this shift inferred. Otherwise they do Bayesian concept learning as the best model does (distinct examples with some strays, occasional guessing, Weber-like range breadth, ranges stretched toward where the sequence is heading). This predicts weaker rule-based generalization after one or two examples (e.g. fewer "yes" to 32 after 16, or to 64 after 16, 8) and stronger after three or four than a learner whose rule-versus-range prior ignores how many examples were shown.
