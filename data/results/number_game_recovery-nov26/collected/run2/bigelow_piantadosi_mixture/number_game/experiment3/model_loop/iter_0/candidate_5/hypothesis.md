Refinement of `margin_beyond_examples_range_prior`: people do Bayesian concept learning over the same arithmetic and digit rules, the 18 digit-sum concepts and every numeric range of 1-100. They read the examples as distinct draws with some strays, sometimes guess, favour narrow ranges and discount open-ended ranges. The one change is the direction of the margin expectation. People expect the program's numbers to carry on upward past the largest example they were shown, as counting does, but expect no extra room below the smallest one, so they credit a candidate range only by how far it reaches above the examples (strength inferred). Unlike the heading model, this does not depend on the order the examples were shown in. It predicts more "yes" to numbers a little above a cluster than below it (e.g. 67 versus 57 after 60, 62, 64, and the same after 64, 62, 60), while rule and digit-sum generalization are unchanged.
