Refinement of `outermost_examples_range_evidence`, the pruned model that let only the smallest and largest example bear on how narrow a stretch of the number line is. People do Bayesian concept learning over arithmetic, digit and digit-sum rules and every numeric range. They read examples as independent draws with some strays, sometimes guess, judge a range's breadth Weber-like, stretch ranges toward the heading and expect the numbers to carry on upward. The outermost examples mark where a range must reach and count in full. The one change is that the examples lying in between are not ignored: they count as evidence about a range's width at an inferred partial (or extra) weight, while rule and digit-sum evidence still uses every example in full. This predicts that adding an in-between example (e.g. 15 to 31, 3, 1, or 62 to 60, 64) narrows range generalization by less than a full-weight learner expects if the weight is below one, or by more if it is above one, and leaves sets of one or two examples unchanged.
