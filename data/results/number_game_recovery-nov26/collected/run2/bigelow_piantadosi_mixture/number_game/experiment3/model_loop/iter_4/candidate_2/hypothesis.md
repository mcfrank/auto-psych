People infer the program by the same Bayesian concept learning as the best model, but they do not answer "yes" by how sure they are that the program makes the target; they answer by representativeness, judging how much the examples have raised the target's plausibility above how common it is as a program output in general, so a target that many programs produce anyway (even numbers, numbers in the middle of the line) needs more belief to earn a "yes" than an a priori unusual one (the strength of this base-rate discount is inferred). This predicts fewer "yes" answers to commonplace targets and more to unusual targets (large primes, numbers near 1 or 100) than their posterior probability alone implies, for the same examples.
