# Existing hypotheses

Each model below is ONE cognitive hypothesis, with how it stands on the current data by ELPD-LOO (best first). `elpd_diff ± dse` is a model's deficit against the best and the standard error of that difference (with the responses to each stimulus pair counted together, as they are correlated): within about 2·dse the two are statistically tied on this data; beyond it the model has lost. "PSIS-LOO unreliable" means the estimate itself is untrustworthy (too many high-Pareto-k trials), not that the model is bad. Propose a hypothesis that is genuinely different from these, or a refinement of a single one of them — never a combination of several.

## heading_direction_range_extrapolation  — rank 0, the best model on this data, ELPD-LOO -3199.7

People read the examples as a sequence and take the direction of the last step (whether the final example went up or down from the one before it) as the direction the program is heading, so the numeric ranges that come to mind as candidate concepts extend further past the examples in that direction and less in the other; otherwise they do Bayesian concept learning over the same arithmetic, digit and digit-sum rules and ranges as the best model. This predicts more "yes" answers to numbers far beyond the examples on the side the sequence was last moving toward (e.g. above 60 after 40, 20, 60) and fewer on the side it moved away from, while rule-based generalization and sets of a single example are unchanged.

## probe_with_margin_range_prior  — rank 1, 0.7 ± 2.2 nats behind the best (0.3× dse: statistically tied with the best), ELPD-LOO -3200.4

Refinement of `probe_as_weak_example`: people do Bayesian concept learning over the same arithmetic and digit rules, digit-sum concepts and numeric ranges (distinct examples with some strays, narrow-favouring and open-ended-discounted ranges, occasional guessing), and they read the number they are asked about as a further, partially trusted example of the program, with an inferred strength. The one change, the component taken from `margin_beyond_examples_range_prior`, is that people also expect the shown examples not to sit right at the edges of the program's range, so they credit a candidate range by how far it reaches past the examples on either side. This predicts that the probe's pull toward small concepts that contain it is joined by more "yes" to numbers a little beyond either end of a cluster (e.g. 57 or 67 after 60, 62, 64), both effects together, while rule and digit-sum generalization remain as in the probe model.

## digit_sum_open_ended_range_discount  — rank 2, 0.8 ± 1.2 nats behind the best (0.6× dse: statistically tied with the best), ELPD-LOO -3200.5

Refinement of `digit_sum_concepts` (the incumbent): people do Bayesian concept learning over the same arithmetic and digit rules, the 18 "digits add up to s" concepts and every numeric range of 1-100, reading the examples as distinct draws with some strays, sometimes guessing, and favouring narrow ranges. The one change, the component taken from `open_ended_range_discount`, is that a range running to an end of the number line ("anything up to 30", "anything from 70 on") is a threshold rule rather than a bounded stretch, and people hold such open-ended ranges at their own prior weight relative to bounded ones (inferred; the data suggest lower). This predicts fewer "yes" answers to numbers far below small examples or far above large ones, all the way to 1 or 100, while digit-sum and rule generalization and "yes" to numbers just beyond the examples' span are kept.

## probe_as_weak_example  — rank 3, 1.0 ± 1.5 nats behind the best (0.7× dse: statistically tied with the best), ELPD-LOO -3200.7

People do Bayesian concept learning over the same arithmetic and digit rules, digit-sum concepts and numeric ranges as the best model (distinct examples with some strays, narrow-favouring and open-ended-discounted ranges, occasional guessing), but with one mis-weighting of evidence: they treat the question itself as a hint, reading the number they are asked about as if it were a further, partially trusted example the program produced (with an inferred strength, which could also be negative if probes are read as tricks). Because a probe counts most for small concepts that contain it, this predicts extra "yes" to asked-about numbers that fit a narrow rule or a short range alongside the examples (e.g. 32 after 16, 8, 64), and the reverse if people discount probes.

## first_example_marks_range_edge  — rank 4, 1.0 ± 1.3 nats behind the best (0.8× dse: statistically tied with the best), ELPD-LOO -3200.7

People read the examples in order and take the first number they are shown as marking an edge of the program's range whenever it is the smallest or the largest of the set: they expect the program's range to reach beyond the other examples (as in the best model, crediting ranges by how far they extend past the examples) but not past that first-shown end. This predicts that "yes" stops at the first example's side of a cluster but spreads beyond its other side (e.g. 66 is accepted after 60, 62, 64 but 58 is not, and the reverse after 64, 62, 60), while single examples, rules and digit-sum generalization are unchanged.

## open_ended_ranges_heading_direction  — rank 5, 1.2 ± 1.1 nats behind the best (1.0× dse: statistically tied with the best), ELPD-LOO -3200.9

Refinement of the incumbent `digit_sum_open_ended_range_discount`: people do Bayesian concept learning over the same arithmetic and digit rules, the 18 "digits add up to s" concepts and every numeric range of 1-100. They read the examples as distinct draws with some strays, sometimes guess, favour narrow ranges, and hold open-ended threshold ranges ("up to 30", "from 70 on") at their own, lower prior weight. The one change, the component taken from `heading_direction_range_extrapolation`, is that they also read the examples as a sequence and take the direction of the last step (up or down from the example before) as where the program is heading, so the ranges they entertain reach further past the examples on that side and less on the other. This predicts more "yes" to numbers beyond the examples in the last-step direction (e.g. above 60 after 40, 20, 60) and fewer on the opposite side, on top of the incumbent's overall discount of ranges running to 1 or 100; single-example sets and rule generalization are unchanged.

## margin_beyond_examples_range_prior  — rank 6, 1.2 ± 2.0 nats behind the best (0.6× dse: statistically tied with the best), ELPD-LOO -3200.9

Refinement of `digit_sum_open_ended_range_discount` (the incumbent): people do Bayesian concept learning over the same arithmetic and digit rules, the 18 digit-sum concepts and every numeric range of 1-100, reading the examples as distinct draws with some strays, sometimes guessing, favouring narrow ranges and discounting open-ended ranges that run to 1 or 100. The one change is a margin expectation: people do not expect the few numbers they were shown to sit right at the edges of the program's range, so they credit a candidate range by how far it reaches past the examples on either side (strength inferred; the data suggest more credit for ranges with room beyond the examples). This predicts more "yes" to numbers a little beyond either end of a cluster (e.g. 57 or 67 after 60, 62, 64) than the incumbent gives, symmetrically on both sides and regardless of the order the examples were shown in, while rule and digit-sum generalization are unchanged.

## margin_and_heading_range_prior  — rank 7, 1.7 ± 1.9 nats behind the best (0.9× dse: statistically tied with the best), ELPD-LOO -3201.4

Refinement of the incumbent `margin_beyond_examples_range_prior`: people do Bayesian concept learning over the same arithmetic and digit rules, the 18 digit-sum concepts and every numeric range of 1-100. They read the examples as distinct draws with some strays, sometimes guess, favour narrow ranges, discount open-ended ranges that run to 1 or 100, and credit a range by how far it reaches past the examples on both sides. The one change, the component taken from `open_ended_ranges_heading_direction`, is that people also read the examples as a sequence and take the direction of the last step (up or down from the example before) as where the program is heading, so the room they expect beyond the examples is shifted toward that side and away from the other (strength inferred). This predicts the incumbent's extra "yes" just beyond both ends of a cluster, but tilted: more beyond the end the sequence last moved toward (e.g. above 64 after 60, 62, 64) than beyond the end it moved away from (e.g. below 60), and the reverse for the same numbers shown in falling order. Single-example sets and rule and digit-sum generalization are unchanged.

## drifting_yes_criterion  — no comparison row

People infer the hidden concept exactly as the simplest current model does (Bayesian concept learning over arithmetic, digit and digit-sum rules and numeric ranges, with distinct examples, some strays, narrow-favouring and open-ended-discounted ranges, and occasional guessing), but their decision criterion for saying "yes" is not fixed over the session: as the session goes on, they need to be steadily more (or less) sure that the target belongs before they answer "yes", which shifts their odds of a "yes" by a constant step per trial (strength and direction inferred). This predicts that the same set-and-target pair gets fewer "yes" answers late in the session than early on, with the largest drop for targets the concept makes moderately likely, while the ordering of targets by plausibility is unchanged.
