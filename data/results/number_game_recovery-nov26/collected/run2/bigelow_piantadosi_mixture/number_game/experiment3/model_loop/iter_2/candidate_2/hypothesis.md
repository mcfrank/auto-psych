People do not picture "stretches" of numbers on a continuous number line; they read each number as a two-digit numeral in a tens-by-ones place-value grid, and the magnitude-like concepts they entertain are blocks of that grid (a band of tens digits combined with a band of ones digits, e.g. "fifties and sixties ending in 0-4", or "the sixties"), weighed by the size principle alongside the usual arithmetic, digit and digit-sum rules. This predicts that generalization from clustered examples spreads readily within their decade and to the same ones-digit positions of neighbouring decades, but drops sharply across a decade boundary (e.g. fewer "yes" to 58 or 57 after 60, 62, 64, but "yes" to 70-74 nearly as often as to 66-69), unlike a number-line range learner.
