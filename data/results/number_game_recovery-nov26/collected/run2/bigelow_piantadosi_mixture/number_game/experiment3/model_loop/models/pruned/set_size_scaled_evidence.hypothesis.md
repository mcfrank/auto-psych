Refinement of the incumbent `heading_weber_upward_with_replacement`. People do Bayesian concept learning as before: arithmetic, digit and digit-sum rules and every numeric range, examples read as independent draws with some strays, occasional guessing, Weber-like range breadth, ranges stretched toward the heading and upward past the largest example. The one change is that the weight people give each example depends on how many examples they were shown. Each example's evidence is scaled by a power of the set size (strength and direction inferred), so a lone example counts as a weaker hint than one that comes alongside others that agree with it, or as a stronger one. This predicts that "yes" to members of broad rules and wide ranges falls more (or less) steeply from one example to four than an ideal learner who counts every example equally gives, while the order of targets within a set is unchanged.
