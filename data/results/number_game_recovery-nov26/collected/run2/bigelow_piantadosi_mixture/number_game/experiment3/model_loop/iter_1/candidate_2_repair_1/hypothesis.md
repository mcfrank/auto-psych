People learn the program as the best model does (Bayesian concept learning over arithmetic, digit and digit-sum rules and Weber-weighted, heading-stretched numeric ranges, with distinct examples and some strays), but their occasional lapses are not random guesses: on a lapse they ignore the examples and answer by how plausible the target is as an output of the program in general, that is, by the prior probability over the same concepts that the program makes that number. This predicts that lapse "yes" answers go mostly to numbers that many concepts contain (even numbers, numbers in the middle of the line) rather than to odd outliers such as large primes, with no free guess rate.
