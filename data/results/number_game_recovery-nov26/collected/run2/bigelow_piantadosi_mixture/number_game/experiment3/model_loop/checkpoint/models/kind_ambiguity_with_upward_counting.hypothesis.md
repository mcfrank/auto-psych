Refinement of `kind_ambiguity_aversion`. People infer the program by Bayesian concept learning over arithmetic, digit and digit-sum rules and Weber-weighted numeric ranges that stretch toward where the sequence is heading. When the examples leave them torn between a symbolic rule and a stretch of the number line, they withhold "yes" in proportion to how evenly split that belief is. The one change, the component taken from `heading_weber_with_upward_counting`, is an order-independent counting habit: people expect the program's numbers to carry on upward past the largest example, so they credit a range by how far it reaches above the examples. Because this changes how much belief ranges win against rules, it also shifts which sets people find ambiguous, and so where they hold back "yes". It predicts more "yes" just above a cluster than below it, and fewer "yes" overall for sets that fit a rule and an upward-reaching range about equally well (e.g. 70, 10, 88, 40).
