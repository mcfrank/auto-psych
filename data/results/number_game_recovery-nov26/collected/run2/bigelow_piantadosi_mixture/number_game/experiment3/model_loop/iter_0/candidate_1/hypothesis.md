People do Bayesian concept learning over the same arithmetic, digit and digit-sum rules and every numeric range of 1-100 as the best model (distinct examples with some strays, occasional guessing, ranges stretched toward where the sequence is heading). They also hold one more kind of named concept: the decades, "numbers in the twenties", "in the eighties", and so on, plus "the single-digit numbers". All ten decades are equally likely a priori, and the family as a whole takes an inferred share of the prior belief given to rules. Because a decade is a named stretch of the number line, this predicts that examples falling inside one decade (e.g. 83, 87 or 24, 28) draw more "yes" to the rest of that decade and a sharper drop just past its round-number boundaries than a learner who weighs all ranges by width alone.
