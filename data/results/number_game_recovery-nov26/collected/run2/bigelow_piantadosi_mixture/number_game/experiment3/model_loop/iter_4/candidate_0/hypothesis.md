Alongside the arithmetic, digit and digit-sum rules and the numeric ranges of the best model, people entertain arithmetic progressions that run with an offset through the whole number line, "numbers that are r more than a multiple of m" (e.g. 2, 7, 12, 17, ... or 1, 4, 7, 10, ...) for steps of 3 to 12. All of these progressions are equally likely a priori, and the family as a whole takes an inferred share of the prior belief given to rules; otherwise learning is as in the best model (size principle with some strays, position-decaying example evidence, occasional guessing). This predicts that examples sharing a remainder (e.g. 4, 16, 28 or 17, 32, 47) draw "yes" to other numbers with that remainder spread across the whole line (40, 52, 88 or 2, 62, 92), beyond what "even numbers", "ends in d" or a range gives.
