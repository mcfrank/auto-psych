Refinement of `heading_weber_with_upward_counting`. People do Bayesian concept learning over the same arithmetic, digit and digit-sum rules and every numeric range of 1-100. They read the examples as distinct draws with some strays, sometimes guess, judge a range's breadth relative to its magnitude, and expect the program's numbers to carry on upward past the largest example. The one change is what tells them which way the sequence is going: they read the overall trend from the first example shown to the last, not the direction of the final step, and that trend tilts how far they expect the program's range to reach beyond the examples on each side. Its strength and sign are inferred. The data suggest a negative sign: people take the run they were shown to have started partway into the program's range, so ranges reach back past where the sequence began.

This predicts that sets whose final step reverses the overall trend are judged by the trend (e.g. after 40, 70, 60, more "yes" just below 40, relative to just above 70, than a last-step reader gives), the opposite of `first_example_marks_range_edge`, while single examples and rule and digit-sum generalization are unchanged.
