A refinement of noisy_examples_erlang_intervals: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over the 45 independently weighted rules and all intervals of 1-100, with the size principle, an inferred rate of random examples and an Erlang prior favouring a typical range width, but they do not find every range equally natural: ranges whose ends fall on decade boundaries (starting at 1, 11, 21, ... or ending at 10, 20, ..., 100, such as "the 30s" or "1 to 20") are a priori more plausible than equally wide ranges with arbitrary ends. The one change is this decade-alignment boost to the interval prior (its strength inferred, zero recovering the incumbent), which predicts that "yes" around examples that fit no rule stops sharply at decade boundaries, e.g. examples 32 and 37 generalise to 30-39 more than to 28 or 41.
