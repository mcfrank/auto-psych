People build number concepts compositionally: besides the 45 simple rules and the ranges of 1-100 (learned by Bayesian inference with the size principle, an inferred rate of random examples and a typical-width prior on ranges), they also entertain conjunctions of two simple rules, such as "odd multiples of 3" or "even numbers containing the digit 4", each equally likely a priori with the conjunctions as a group taking an inferred share of the rule prior. Because a conjunction is much smaller than either of its parts, the size principle makes it dominate when all examples share two properties. The sharpest disagreement with the current best model is on such sets (e.g. 69, 9, 39, 21 or 63, 27, 99, 33), where this model predicts "no" to targets that have only one of the two shared properties (even multiples of 3) and the best model predicts substantial "yes".
