A refinement of noisy_examples_erlang_intervals: people do the same Bayesian concept learning (size principle, inferred share of random examples, Erlang prior on interval width), but their space of rules also includes arithmetic progressions that do not start at zero — every number that leaves a given remainder when divided by 3 to 12 (e.g. 2, 5, 8, 11, ... or 5, 11, 17, 23, ...), each such "step" rule equally likely a priori and the group as a whole given a prior share inferred from the data. The one change is this added family of offset arithmetic-progression concepts, which predicts that examples sharing a step-and-offset pattern (e.g. 23, 35, 83, 95, all 5 more than a multiple of 6) are generalised to that narrow progression rather than to a broad rule such as odd numbers.
