People do the same Bayesian concept learning as noisy_examples_erlang_intervals (45 independently weighted rules, intervals of 1-100 with an Erlang prior on width, size principle, inferred rate of random examples), but their working memory for the examples is unreliable: each example shown is, independently and with an inferred probability, simply not retained when they come to judge the target, and they generalise from whichever examples they still hold. Because a forgotten example removes its evidence regardless of how well it fit, this predicts broader, less rule-tight generalisation from larger sets than full-memory inference (e.g. some "yes" to even non-powers after 16, 8, 64, 2), and a target that matches only some of the examples still gets partial "yes".
