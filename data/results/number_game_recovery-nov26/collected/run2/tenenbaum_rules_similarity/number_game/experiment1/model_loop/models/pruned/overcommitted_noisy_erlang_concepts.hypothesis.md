I chose posterior_confidence_miscalibration and refined it. People turn their posterior belief that the target belongs to the concept into a yes/no answer by scaling its log-odds with a single confidence factor. The factor is inferred; above one means they commit more firmly than their belief warrants. The one change is the concept learner underneath: the old one assumed a fixed 10% of random examples and treated all intervals as equally likely. It is replaced by one that infers how often examples are random and expects ranges of a typical width (Erlang). This claims that people's beliefs about the concept are broad, because they discount examples as possibly random, but that they answer more decisively than those broad beliefs imply, giving firmer yes and no answers than calibrated Bayesian inference would.
