A refinement of noisy_examples_erlang_intervals: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over the 45 independently weighted rules and all intervals of 1-100 (size principle, an inferred share of random examples, a prior favouring a typical range width), but they think of ranges in round, decade-aligned terms ("20 to 40", "the 60s", "1 to 10"), so a range whose endpoints fall on decade boundaries is a priori more plausible than an equally wide range with arbitrary endpoints such as 23-47. The one change is this preference for decade-aligned range endpoints, with its strength (and sign) inferred from the data, which predicts that "yes" answers to examples that fit no rule extend up to, and stop at, decade lines.
