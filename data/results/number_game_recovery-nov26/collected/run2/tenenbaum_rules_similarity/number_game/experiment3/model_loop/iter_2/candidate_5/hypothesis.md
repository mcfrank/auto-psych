I chose above_max_stricter_criterion and refined it. People do the same Bayesian concept learning as the best model (arithmetic and final-digit rules plus multiples of 13-50, rule runs confined to a stretch of the number line, ranges of a typical width, an inferred share of random examples, examples drawn without replacement, no "contains digit d" concepts), and when they turn belief into a yes/no answer the edges of the examples are directional: just past the largest example they demand more certainty to say "yes", and just past the smallest example they demand less. The one change is that the criterion shift is not a fixed step for every target above the largest example but a single signed edge bias, mirrored at both ends and fading over about eight numbers beyond the edge. So at equal belief, people say "yes" more to a target a few numbers below the smallest example than to one a few numbers above the largest, and targets far outside the examples are judged on belief alone.
