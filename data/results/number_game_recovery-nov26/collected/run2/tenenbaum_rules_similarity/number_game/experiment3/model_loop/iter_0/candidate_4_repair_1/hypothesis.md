A refinement of no_digit_containment_span_concepts: people do the same Bayesian concept learning (arithmetic and final-digit rules plus multiples of 13-50, rule runs confined to a stretch of the number line, ranges of a typical width, an inferred share of random examples, examples drawn without replacement, no "contains digit d" concepts), but when they answer they are swayed by how familiar the target number is. Because small numbers are met far more often in everyday life, the one change is a decision-stage bias toward "yes" that grows as the target's (logarithmic) magnitude shrinks, its strength and sign inferred (zero recovers the incumbent), which predicts more "yes" to small targets just below the examples than to large targets just above them, whatever concept the examples suggest.
