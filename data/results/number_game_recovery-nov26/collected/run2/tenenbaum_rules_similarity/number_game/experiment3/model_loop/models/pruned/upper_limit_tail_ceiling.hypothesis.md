I refined the incumbent graded_ratio_ceiling_criterion. People do the same Bayesian concept learning (arithmetic and final-digit rules plus multiples of 13-50, rule runs confined to a stretch of the number line, ranges of a typical width, an inferred share of random examples, examples drawn without replacement, no "contains digit d" concepts) and treat the largest example as a soft ceiling graded by the ratio of the target to it. The one change is what the ceiling does: rather than raising the certainty they demand (a log-odds criterion shift that barely touches confident beliefs), people believe the program has an unknown upper output limit somewhere at or above the largest example, with larger limits proportionally less likely (a power-law tail whose steepness is inferred), and they say "yes" only if the target fits the concept and also lies under that limit. So the chance of "yes" is their belief multiplied by the chance the limit reaches the target, which cuts even near-certain rule members above the largest example (e.g. 64 after 2, 4, 8, 16) by the same proportion as uncertain ones.
