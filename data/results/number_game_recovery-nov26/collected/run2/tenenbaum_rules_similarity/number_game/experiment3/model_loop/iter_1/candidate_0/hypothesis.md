People do not weigh competing concepts against each other; they check each familiar regularity (an arithmetic or final-digit rule, or a stretch of the number line) on its own and treat it as a real constraint of the program when the examples sharing it would be too much of a coincidence by chance — a graded significance judgement driven by how rare the regularity is among 1-100 and how many examples show it, with at most one example allowed to break it. They then say "yes" only to a target that satisfies every regularity they have accepted (a conjunction of independently detected constraints), so a target that violates any one strongly suspicious regularity is rejected even if it fits another, broader one the examples share.
