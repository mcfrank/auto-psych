A refinement of no_digit_containment_span_concepts: people do the same Bayesian concept learning (arithmetic and final-digit rules plus multiples of 13-50, rule runs confined to a stretch of the number line, ranges of a typical width, an inferred share of random examples, examples drawn without replacement, no "contains digit d" concepts), but they do not find all ranges of a given width equally plausible wherever they sit on the number line. The one change is that their prior over ranges shifts smoothly with where the range lies, favouring ranges among smaller (or larger) numbers, with the strength and sign of this bias inferred and zero recovering the incumbent. This predicts that, for examples that fit no rule, "yes" extends further below the smallest example than above the largest — the downward-over-upward extrapolation asymmetry the incumbent under-predicts.
