I chose sample_count_scaled_ceiling and refined it. People do the same Bayesian concept learning (arithmetic and final-digit rules plus multiples of 13-50, rule runs confined to a stretch of the number line, ranges of a typical width, an inferred share of random examples, examples drawn without replacement, no "contains digit d" concepts) with the largest example as a soft ceiling whose penalty grows with the overshoot ratio and the number of examples shown. The one change, grafted from the pruned new_set_cautious_erlang_concepts but recast as a decision criterion: on the first question after a new set of examples appears, people demand extra certainty before saying "yes" (a log-odds shift of their belief, its size and sign inferred, zero recovering the parent), relaxing to their plain criterion once they have been asked about that set before — so uncertain targets get fewer "yes" answers on a first query than on repeat queries, while near-certain members and non-members barely change.
