People do the same Bayesian concept learning as graded_ratio_ceiling_criterion (arithmetic and final-digit rules plus multiples of 13-50, rule runs confined to a stretch of the number line, ranges of a typical width, an inferred share of random examples, examples drawn without replacement, the largest example as a ratio-graded soft ceiling), but each judgment is not made in isolation: the belief people formed on the previous question in the session lingers and is partly carried into the current answer (sequential assimilation), with the carried-over share inferred. This predicts that, at equal belief about the current target, people say "yes" more often right after a question they believed was a "yes" and less often right after one they believed was a "no", even when the example set has changed.
