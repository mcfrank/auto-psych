People do the same Bayesian concept learning as the best model (arithmetic and final-digit rules plus multiples of 13-50, rule runs confined to a stretch of the number line, ranges of a typical width, an inferred share of random examples, examples drawn without replacement, the largest example as a graded soft ceiling), but their working memory for the examples is unreliable: by the time they judge the target, each example has independently been lost with some inferred probability (at least one is always kept), and they generalise only from the examples they still hold, including taking the largest example they remember as their ceiling. Because a forgotten example removes its evidence entirely, this predicts broader, less rule-tight generalisation from larger sets than full-memory inference and partial "yes" to targets that fit only some of the examples.
