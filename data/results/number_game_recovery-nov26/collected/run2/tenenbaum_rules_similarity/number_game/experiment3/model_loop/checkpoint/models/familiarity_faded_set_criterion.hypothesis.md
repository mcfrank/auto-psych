I chose graded_ratio_ceiling_criterion and refined it. People do the same Bayesian concept learning (arithmetic and final-digit rules plus multiples of 13-50, rule runs confined to a stretch of the number line, ranges of a typical width, an inferred share of random examples, examples drawn without replacement, no "contains digit d" concepts) and treat the largest example as a ratio-graded soft ceiling; the one change is that their yes/no criterion depends on how familiar the example set is within the session: the first time they ever meet a set their criterion is shifted by an inferred log-odds amount (size and sign inferred, zero recovering the parent), and that shift fades in proportion to how often they have already been asked about the set (half on the second encounter, a third on the third, ...), regardless of whether other sets came in between. Unlike a shift on every switch back to a set, this predicts that a set people return to after seeing others is judged with its familiarity, so answers to an often-seen set drift steadily toward the plain criterion over the session.
