People do not infer a rule; they judge the target by its summed similarity to the stored examples (an exemplar model in the Generalized Context Model tradition), but where they direct their attention is set by the examples themselves: number properties (odd/even, prime, square, cube, powers, multiples of 3-12, final digit) that the examples share and that are rare among 1-100 capture attention, while the rest are largely ignored, alongside a ratio-scale sense of magnitude. A target is then similar to an example to the extent it matches it on the attended properties and is close to it in proportion, and people say "yes" in proportion to that summed similarity relative to a fixed criterion, so a target sharing the examples' distinctive common property is accepted even when far away, and with no shared property acceptance falls off smoothly with proportional distance from the nearest examples.
