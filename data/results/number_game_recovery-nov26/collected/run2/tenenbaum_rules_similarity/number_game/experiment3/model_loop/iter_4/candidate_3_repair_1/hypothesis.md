I refined the incumbent new_set_criterion_ratio_ceiling. People do the same Bayesian concept learning (arithmetic and final-digit rules plus multiples of 13-50, rule runs confined to a stretch of the number line, ranges of a typical width, an inferred share of random examples, examples drawn without replacement, no "contains digit d" concepts), with a ratio-graded soft ceiling and a criterion shift on the first question about a new set; the one change is that the ceiling is extrapolated rather than pinned at the largest example: people expect the program could continue past its largest example by an inferred number of the examples' typical proportional steps (how many times larger each sorted example is than the one before), and only targets beyond that extrapolated point make them demand extra certainty (zero steps recovers the incumbent). So widely spread sets (2, 8, 32) license "yes" well above their largest example without penalty, while tightly clustered sets (40, 44, 47) keep their ceiling almost at the largest example.
