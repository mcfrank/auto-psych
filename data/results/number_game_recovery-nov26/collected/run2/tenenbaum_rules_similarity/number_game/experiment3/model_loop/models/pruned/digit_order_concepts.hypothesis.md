People do the same Bayesian concept learning as graded_ratio_ceiling_criterion (arithmetic and final-digit rules plus multiples of 13-50, rule runs confined to a stretch of the number line, ranges of a typical width, an inferred share of random examples, examples drawn without replacement, no "contains digit d" concepts, the largest example as a ratio-graded soft ceiling), but they also notice how the two written digits of a number relate. So they entertain two digit-order concepts: "two-digit numbers whose digits rise" (12, 37, 89, ... — 36 numbers) and "two-digit numbers whose digits fall" (10, 53, 97, ... — 45 numbers). The two are equally likely a priori, and together they take an inferred share of the rule prior. This predicts that a set such as 93, 43, 83, 53 (all with falling digits) generalises somewhat to other falling-digit numbers such as 81 or 20, and not to rising-digit numbers such as 19 or single-digit numbers, beyond what its magnitude and arithmetic rules imply.
