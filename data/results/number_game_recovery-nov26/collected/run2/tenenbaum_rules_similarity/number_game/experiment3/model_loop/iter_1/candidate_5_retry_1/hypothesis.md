A refinement of above_max_stricter_criterion: people do the same Bayesian concept learning as the best model (arithmetic and final-digit rules plus multiples of 13-50, span-restricted rule runs, ranges of a typical width, an inferred share of random examples, examples drawn without replacement, no "contains digit d" concepts) and treat the largest example as a soft ceiling at the decision stage, but the extra certainty they demand is not a fixed step: it grows with how far, in proportion to the largest example, the target lies beyond it (a ratio, Weber-like judgement of "how much bigger"). The one change is this graded criterion, so a target just above the largest example is barely penalised while one twice as large needs much stronger evidence, and the same absolute overshoot matters more above small examples than above large ones.
