People do the same Bayesian concept learning as graded_ratio_ceiling_criterion (arithmetic and final-digit rules plus multiples of 13-50, rule runs confined to a stretch of the number line, ranges of a typical width, an inferred share of random examples, examples drawn without replacement, the largest example as a ratio-graded soft ceiling), but how firmly they act on that belief depends on how much evidence they were shown: a metacognitive sense of evidence sufficiency scales the log-odds of their belief by a commitment factor that grows (or shrinks) as a power of the number of examples, its exponent inferred and zero recovering the parent. With a positive exponent, after one or two examples people hedge toward 50/50 — saying "yes" to some targets they believe are out and "no" to some they believe are in — and only give crisp yes/no answers once three or four examples are shown.
