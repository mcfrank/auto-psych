A refinement of no_digit_containment_span_concepts: people do the same Bayesian concept learning (arithmetic and final-digit rules plus multiples of 13-50, rule runs confined to a stretch of the number line, ranges of a typical width, an inferred share of random examples, examples drawn without replacement), but they find a truncated rule most natural when it is counted from the rule's beginning up to some point ("powers of 2 up to 64", "even numbers up to 40"), so such start-anchored runs get an inferred a priori boost over runs that begin mid-rule. The one change is this start-anchoring of rule runs (zero boost recovers the incumbent), which predicts that people extend "yes" downward to rule members below the smallest example more readily than upward to members above the largest.
