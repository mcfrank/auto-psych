People do the same Bayesian concept learning as the current best model (arithmetic and final-digit rules plus multiples of 13-50, rule runs confined to a stretch of the number line, an inferred share of random examples, examples drawn without replacement, a preferred typical concept size), but they do not picture "ranges" as stretches of the number line: they picture each number by its two written digits, laid out on a decades-by-final-digits grid, and their magnitude concepts are rectangular blocks of that grid — a run of decades combined with a run of final digits (e.g. "the 40s to 60s ending in 3 to 5", or a whole decade such as "the 30s"). The sharpest disagreement with the best model lives on examples that fit no rule but share a final-digit band across decades: 44, 64 should generalise to 54 (and 34 or 74) far more than to 45 or 47, which sit closer on the number line, and a stretch such as 38-42 that crosses a decade line is a priori an unnatural concept.
