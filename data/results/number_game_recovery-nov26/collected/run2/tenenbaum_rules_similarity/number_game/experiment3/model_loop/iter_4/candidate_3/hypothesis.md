People do the same Bayesian concept learning (arithmetic and final-digit rules plus multiples of 13-50, rule runs confined to a stretch of the number line, ranges of a typical width, an inferred share of random examples, examples drawn without replacement, no "contains digit d" concepts) with the largest example as a soft, ratio-graded ceiling, and on the first question about a new set of examples their yes/no criterion is shifted. The one change is that this new-set caution does not vanish all at once on the second question but wears off gradually: each further question about the same set keeps only a constant inferred fraction of the previous shift (keeping none recovers the incumbent's all-or-none switch), so "yes" to equally believed targets keeps rising over the second, third and fourth questions about a set.
