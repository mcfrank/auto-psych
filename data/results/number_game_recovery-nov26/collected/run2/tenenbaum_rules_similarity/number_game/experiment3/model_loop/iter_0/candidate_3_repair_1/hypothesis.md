A refinement of no_digit_containment_span_concepts: people do the same Bayesian concept learning (arithmetic and final-digit rules plus multiples of 13-50, rule runs confined to a stretch of the number line, ranges of a typical width, an inferred share of random examples, examples drawn without replacement), but a stretch of a rule inherits the plausibility of the rule it is cut from — each familiar rule's stretches share that rule's own prior weight — instead of every stretch of every rule being equally likely. The one change is this rule-inherited prior on rule stretches (no new parameter), which predicts that stretches of small, salient rules such as "powers of 2 up to 16" or "squares from 25 to 81" are far more readily entertained than any single stretch of a huge rule like "even numbers from 42 to 92", so clustered examples of a big rule generalise to that rule's members near them less sharply than the incumbent predicts.
