People do the same Bayesian concept learning as the current best models (arithmetic and final-digit rules plus multiples of 13-50, rule runs confined to a stretch of the number line, ranges of a typical width, an inferred share of random examples, examples drawn without replacement, no "contains digit d" concepts), but their answer is also pulled by magnitude similarity: the further the target lies from its nearest example on a ratio (Weber-like, logarithmic) scale, the more certainty they demand before saying "yes", with the demand growing in proportion to that log-ratio distance. Unlike a ceiling at the largest example, this similarity gradient is symmetric and applies everywhere — above the largest example, below the smallest, and in wide gaps between examples — so at equal belief a target close in proportion to some example (e.g. 9 after 8) gets more "yes" than one in a large proportional gap (e.g. 30 after 8 and 64).
