I refined the incumbent graded_ratio_ceiling_criterion. People do the same Bayesian concept learning (arithmetic and final-digit rules plus multiples of 13-50, rule runs confined to a stretch of the number line, ranges of a typical width, an inferred share of random examples, examples drawn without replacement, no "contains digit d" concepts), and when they turn belief into a yes/no answer they judge how far outside the examples' span the target lies as a ratio, both above the largest example and below the smallest. The one change is that the smallest example also acts as a soft, ratio-graded bound, with its own inferred strength and sign (zero recovers the incumbent): the criterion shifts in proportion to how many times smaller the target is than the smallest example, separately from the shift above the largest, so "yes" below the examples can fall off more slowly (or faster) than above them.
