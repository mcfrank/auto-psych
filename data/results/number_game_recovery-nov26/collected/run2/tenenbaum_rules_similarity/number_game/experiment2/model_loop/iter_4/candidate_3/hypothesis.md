A refinement of span_restricted_rule_concepts: people do the same Bayesian concept learning (45 independently weighted rules plus multiples of 13-50, ranges of 1-100 with a preferred typical width, an inferred share of random examples, examples drawn without replacement, and rules confined to a stretch of the number line), but they do not find every stretch of a rule equally natural. A rule capped from above, starting from the rule's first member ("odd numbers up to 40", "multiples of 3 below 70"), is a priori more plausible than a stretch cut off at both ends. The one change is an inferred extra prior share for these capped-from-above runs (zero recovers the incumbent). It predicts that people extend "yes" to rule members below the examples much more readily than to members above the largest example.
