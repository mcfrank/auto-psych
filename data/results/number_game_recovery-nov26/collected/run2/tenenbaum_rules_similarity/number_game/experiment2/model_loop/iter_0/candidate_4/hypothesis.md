A refinement of without_replacement_example_sampling: people do the same Bayesian concept learning (45 independently weighted rules, all ranges of 1-100 with a preferred typical width, an inferred share of random examples, and examples taken as drawn from the concept without replacement), but they do not find every range equally natural: a range whose ends fall on decade boundaries (starting at 1, 11, 21, ... or ending at 10, 20, ..., 100, such as "the 30s" or "1 to 50") is a priori more plausible than an equally wide range with arbitrary ends. The one change is this decade-alignment boost to the range prior, grafted from decade_aligned_range_concepts, with its strength inferred; it predicts that "yes" around examples that fit no rule stops at decade lines, and because without-replacement sampling sharpens the evidence for tight ranges, a set nearly filling one decade is generalised to exactly that decade.
