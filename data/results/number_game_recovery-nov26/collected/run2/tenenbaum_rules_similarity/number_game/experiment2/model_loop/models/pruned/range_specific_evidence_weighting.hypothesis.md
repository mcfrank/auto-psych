A refinement of without_replacement_example_sampling: people do the same Bayesian concept learning (45 independently weighted rules, ranges of 1-100 with a preferred typical width, an inferred share of random examples, examples taken without replacement), but they weigh the examples' evidence differently for the two kinds of concept — the evidence for an arithmetic or digit rule is used at full strength, while the evidence for a range of magnitudes is weighted by its own inferred power (below one: range generalisation is a looser, similarity-like judgement that tightens slowly as examples accumulate; above one: ranges are pinned down faster). The one change is this range-specific evidence weight, which predicts that for examples fitting no rule "yes" spreads around the examples more (or less) than calibrated inference implies, while generalisation within rule-like sets is unchanged.
