I chose log_number_line_interval_prior (pruned in experiment 1, 6.5 nats behind) and refined it. People picture ranges of numbers on a compressed mental number line, so before seeing any examples they find a range among small numbers more plausible than an equally wide range among large numbers. As a result, "yes" around examples that fit no rule reaches further below the smallest example than above the largest. The one change is the concept learner underneath. The old learner treated every range width as equally likely and drew examples with replacement. It is replaced by the current best learner (extended_multiples_concepts: 45 independently weighted rules plus a multiples-of-13-to-50 family, an inferred share of random examples, examples drawn without replacement, and a preferred typical range width). The magnitude preference, with its strength and sign inferred, now acts on top of that width prior, aimed at the downward-versus-upward extension gap the critique reports.
