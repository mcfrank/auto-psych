I chose the incumbent's learner, extended_multiples_concepts, and changed one thing: how people believe the program picks its examples from its concept. They do not assume every member is equally likely to be shown. They assume a bias by magnitude that favours the larger members (or the smaller ones, if the inferred bias is negative), with each example drawn without replacement in proportion to its weight. If people think the program favours large members, then the absence of small examples is unsurprising, so the size principle barely penalises concepts that reach well below the smallest example but fully penalises those that reach above the largest one; this predicts that "yes" extends further below the examples than above them, the asymmetry the critique reports.
