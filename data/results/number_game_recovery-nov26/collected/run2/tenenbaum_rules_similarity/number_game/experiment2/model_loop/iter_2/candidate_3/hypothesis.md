A refinement of extended_multiples_concepts: people do the same Bayesian concept learning (45 independently weighted rules plus multiples of 13-50, ranges with a preferred typical width, an inferred share of random examples, examples drawn without replacement), but they find "numbers up to N" ranges — ranges that start at 1 — a priori more (or less) natural than other ranges of the same width, because a program capped from above is a simple idea. The one change is this inferred prior boost for ranges anchored at 1, which predicts that people extend "yes" downward from the smallest example toward 1 more readily than upward beyond the largest example, the asymmetry the incumbent's symmetric ranges miss.
