People infer which concept produced the examples by the same Bayesian concept learning as the current best models for arithmetic and digit rules, but they do not represent a "range of magnitudes" as a sharp-edged interval: they represent it as a fuzzy magnitude prototype — a central value with a spread — and judge a target by its similarity to that prototype, which falls off smoothly (Gaussian) with numerical distance on the linear number line, the spread being inferred from how scattered the examples are. This predicts that, for examples fitting no rule, "yes" is strongest near the middle of the examples and fades gradually beyond them, with no sharp cut-off at the outermost examples and no special treatment of numbers merely inside the examples' span.
