People do not store a range of magnitudes as a sharp-edged interval; they store it as its member exemplars and judge a target outside the range by its similarity to the range's nearest stored member, which falls off smoothly with distance on the number line — and falls off more slowly below the range (toward smaller numbers) than above it. Arithmetic and digit rules are still matched by whether the target has the property, and which rule or range people retrieve is the same Bayesian concept learning as the current best model, so the one claim is this exemplar-similarity comparison at the edges of magnitude concepts, predicting graded "yes" just outside the examples' span that extends further downward than upward.
