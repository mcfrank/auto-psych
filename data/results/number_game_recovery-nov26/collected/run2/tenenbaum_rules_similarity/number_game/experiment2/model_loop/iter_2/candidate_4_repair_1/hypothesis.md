A refinement of extended_multiples_concepts: people do the same Bayesian concept learning (45 independently weighted rules plus the multiples-of-13-to-50 family, ranges of 1-100 with a preferred typical width, an inferred share of random examples, examples drawn from the concept without replacement), but they do not believe the program picks every member of its concept equally often — they believe it favours larger (or, if the inferred bias runs the other way, smaller) numbers, each member's chance of being shown growing as a power of its magnitude. The one change is this magnitude-biased sampling of examples: because small members of a concept are then rarely shown and so cost little in the size principle, a range or rule that stretches down toward small numbers is barely penalised while one that stretches upward is, predicting that people extend "yes" below the smallest example more readily than above the largest.
