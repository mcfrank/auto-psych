A refinement of without_replacement_example_sampling: people do the same Bayesian concept learning (45 independently weighted rules, ranges of 1-100 with a preferred typical width, examples taken from the concept without replacement), but they do not think each example has its own independent chance of being random. They think a set holds at most one stray example: with some inferred probability one of the examples, any of them equally, is a random number, and otherwise every example came from the concept. The one change is this at-most-one-outlier view of noise, which predicts that people forgive a single example that breaks an otherwise clean pattern even in a four-example set, but completely reject any concept that leaves out two or more of the examples, where the incumbent still gives such concepts some weight.
