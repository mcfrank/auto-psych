I chose large_number_biased_sampling (Bayesian concept learning over arithmetic and final-digit rules plus multiples of 13-50, ranges of a typical width, an inferred share of random examples, examples drawn without replacement with larger members output more often) and made one change: people do not entertain "contains the digit d" concepts at all. They notice a shared final digit, but not a digit that merely appears somewhere in the written numbers, so a shared tens digit (as in 32 and 37, or 8, 80, 48) does not make other numbers with that digit more acceptable. This predicts no extra "yes" for near targets that share an example's decade and no generalisation from sets like 8, 80, 48 to other numbers containing an 8, where the current models predict both.
