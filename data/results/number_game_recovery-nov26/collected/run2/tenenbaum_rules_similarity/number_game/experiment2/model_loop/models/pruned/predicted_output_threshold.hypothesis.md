People do the same Bayesian concept learning as the current best model (45 independently weighted rules, ranges of 1-100 with a preferred typical width, an inferred share of random examples, examples taken as drawn without replacement), but their decision rule is not "is the target in the concept?": they ask how probable it is that the program would output this very number, and answer "yes" when that predicted output probability clears an internal threshold (with noise in the comparison). Because a member of a small concept is a far more probable output than a member of a big one, this predicts that, at the same belief that the target belongs to the concept, targets supported by narrow concepts (powers, cubes, tight ranges) get more "yes" than targets supported only by broad ones (even numbers, wide ranges).
