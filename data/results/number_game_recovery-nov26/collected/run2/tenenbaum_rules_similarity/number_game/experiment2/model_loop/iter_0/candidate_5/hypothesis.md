I chose decade_aligned_range_concepts and refined it. People do the same Bayesian concept learning over the 45 independently weighted rules and all ranges of 1-100, with an inferred share of random examples, a prior favouring a typical range width, and a preference for ranges whose ends fall on decade boundaries ("the 30s", "1 to 20"), but they assume the program never produces the same number twice, so each further example that fits a concept is taken as one of its members not yet shown (sampling without replacement, grafted from without_replacement_example_sampling). The one change is this without-replacement size principle, which makes several examples that fall inside a small decade-aligned range (e.g. 32, 35, 37 inside the 30s) much stronger evidence for exactly that range, so "yes" stops more sharply at the decade boundary than under either parent model.
