People do the same Bayesian concept learning as the current best model (45 independently weighted rules plus multiples of 13-50, ranges of 1-100 with a preferred typical width, an inferred share of random examples, examples drawn without replacement), but they also entertain counting concepts of the form "the numbers from 1 up to N" — the program counts up from 1 and stops somewhere — each stopping point equally likely a priori, the family taking an inferred share of the prior. Because such a concept always reaches down to 1 but stops at the largest example, it predicts that people extend "yes" below the smallest example far more readily than above the largest one; the sharpest disagreement with the best model's symmetric ranges lives on sets of small or clustered numbers with no shared rule (e.g. 12, 17, 20), where it predicts many "yes" answers to targets between 1 and the smallest example and few just above the largest.
