I chose large_number_biased_sampling and refined it. People do the same Bayesian concept learning (45 independently weighted rules plus multiples of 13-50, ranges of 1-100 with a preferred typical width, an inferred share of random examples, examples drawn without replacement), and they believe that when the program draws from a stretch of magnitudes it favours the larger numbers in that stretch, but that when it follows an arithmetic or digit rule it picks every member of the rule equally often. The one change is that this magnitude bias in sampling (its strength and sign inferred) applies only to range concepts, so a range reaching down below the smallest example is cheap under the size principle while generalisation within rules (powers of 2, squares, multiples) is not distorted, predicting "yes" to targets just below examples that fit no rule far more than to targets just above them.
