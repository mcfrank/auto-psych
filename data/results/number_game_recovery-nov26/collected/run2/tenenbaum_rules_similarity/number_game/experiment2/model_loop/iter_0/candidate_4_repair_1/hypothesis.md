A refinement of without_replacement_example_sampling: people do the same Bayesian concept learning (45 independently weighted rules, ranges of 1-100 with a preferred typical width, an inferred share of random examples, examples taken from the concept without replacement), but they are unsure how the program chose its examples — whether it picked them from the concept itself (strong sampling, which makes small concepts much more likely) or picked numbers at random and showed the ones that happened to fit (weak sampling, where a concept's size does not matter). The one change is this inferred mixture of strong and weak sampling, which weakens the size principle — so people keep generalising to broader concepts such as "even numbers" after several examples of a narrow one — without making them any more willing to keep a concept that excludes one of the examples.
