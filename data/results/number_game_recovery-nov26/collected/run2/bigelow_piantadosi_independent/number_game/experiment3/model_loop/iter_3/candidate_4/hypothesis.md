Refinement of `effortful_computed_rule_search`: people only sometimes make the deliberate effort to consider computed arithmetic rules (primes, squares and cubes, powers, multiples other than 5), and otherwise weigh only the at-a-glance concepts (ranges, parity, digit patterns, multiples of 5) by the same Bayesian logic. The single change is that how often a person makes this effort is a stable trait of that person rather than a population constant: each participant has their own search propensity, spread around the population by a fixed amount and inferred from their answers. So the same participants consistently extend computed rules to distant members (and reject in-span non-members) across every set they see, while others consistently fall back on ranges and digit patterns, and this split shows only for sets that a computed rule explains.
