People learn the hidden concept by the same Bayesian weighing of arithmetic rules (kind plausibilities, sparse familiarities within kinds) and stretches of the number line as the best model, with a looser reading of the examples as evidence for a tight range, but they are limited in how much of the examples' evidence they can hold and integrate at once, and this working-memory capacity is a stable trait that differs between people. A participant with a tighter capacity limit draws less extra evidence from each additional example, so for that person larger sets are generalized more broadly (broad rules and wide ranges keep more belief), while people barely differ on single examples, and each person's breadth holds consistently across every set they see.
