Refinement of `individual_range_sampling_looseness`: people do the same capacity-limited Bayesian learning over arithmetic rules (kind plausibilities, sparse familiarities within kinds) and numerical ranges. They read the examples more loosely for ranges than for rules, and how loosely they read them as range evidence is a stable trait of each participant. The single change, a component taken from `effortful_computed_rule_search`: considering rules that have to be computed (primes, squares and cubes, powers, multiples other than 5) takes deliberate effort, so people search for them only on a fixed share of judgments. On the rest they weigh only the explanations visible at a glance (stretches of the number line, odd or even, final or contained digits, multiples of 5). So even sets that fit a computed rule well (e.g. 16, 8, 64) get a share of range-based answers, and on those answers each person's own range looseness decides how far beyond the examples' span their yeses reach.
