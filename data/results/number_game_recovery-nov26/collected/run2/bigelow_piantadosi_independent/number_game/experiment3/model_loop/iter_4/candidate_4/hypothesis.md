Refinement of `effortful_computed_rule_search`: people consider effortful rules only on a fixed share of their judgments and otherwise generalize from the explanations available at once, weighing whichever concepts they considered by the same Bayesian logic. The single change is what makes a rule effortful: not being arithmetic as such, but having to be checked number by number. Famous sequences (primes, squares and cubes, powers) are memorized lists that are recognized at once, like odd or even, final digits and multiples of 5, whereas testing divisibility by 3, 4, 6, 7, 8, 9, 11 or 12, and scanning every digit for a shared "contains the digit d", need deliberate work. This predicts that sets fitting a sequence (e.g. 16, 8, 64 or 9, 49, 81) are extended to its distant members on every judgment, while sets fitting a non-decimal multiple or a shared inner digit (e.g. 42, 21, 84 or 31, 13, 35) get a share of range-based answers: fewer yeses to distant members and more to in-span non-members than the incumbent predicts.
