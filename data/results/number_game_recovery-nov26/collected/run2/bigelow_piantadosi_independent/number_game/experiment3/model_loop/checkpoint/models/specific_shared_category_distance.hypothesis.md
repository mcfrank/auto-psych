People judge a target by its similarity to the examples on a category-distance metric: they find the most specific familiar number category that holds all the examples (an arithmetic rule or the stretch of the number line they span) and the most specific one that still holds the examples together with the target, and the distance is how much larger the second category must be than the first, with each kind of category (parity, primes, powers, multiples, digit patterns) costing its own amount to call to mind. The willingness to say yes falls off smoothly with this distance, more steeply the more examples there are, so targets that fit the examples' tightest shared category are accepted however far away they lie, and targets that force a much broader category are rejected even when numerically close.
