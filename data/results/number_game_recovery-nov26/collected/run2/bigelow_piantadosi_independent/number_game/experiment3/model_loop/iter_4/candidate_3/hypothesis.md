Refinement of `effortful_computed_rule_search`: people consider effortful rules only on some of their judgments, and otherwise generalize from what can be seen at a glance, weighing whatever concepts they considered by the same Bayesian logic. The single change is what counts as seen at a glance: only a number's magnitude (stretches of the number line) and what its final digit shows (odd or even, ending in a given digit, multiples of 5). "Contains the digit d" rules require inspecting the whole number, tens place included, so like primes, squares and cubes, powers and other multiples they are considered only when people make the effortful search. This predicts that sets sharing a digit somewhere other than the final place (e.g. 31, 13, 35) are extended to other numbers containing that digit less often than the incumbent predicts, and more often by range, while sets sharing a final digit keep their digit-based generalization on every judgment.
