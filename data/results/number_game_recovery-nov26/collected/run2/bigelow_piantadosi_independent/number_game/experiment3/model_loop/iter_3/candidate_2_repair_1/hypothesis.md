People infer the hidden concept by the same capacity-limited Bayesian learning over arithmetic rules (kind plausibilities, a few exceptionally familiar or unfamiliar rules) and numerical ranges as the simplest current model, but they differ stably in how decisively they turn that belief into a yes/no answer: some participants answer more categorically than their belief (near-unanimous yes to well-supported targets and no to poorly supported ones), others more hesitantly, closer to a coin flip. Unlike a yes-bias, this personal decisiveness leaves a 50/50 belief untouched and pushes answers away from or toward chance symmetrically, so a participant's distinctiveness shows up on confidently judged targets (examples, far non-members) and points the same way across every set they see.
