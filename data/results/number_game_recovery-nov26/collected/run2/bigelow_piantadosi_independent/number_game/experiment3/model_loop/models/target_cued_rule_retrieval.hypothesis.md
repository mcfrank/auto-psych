The number being asked about acts as a retrieval cue: even on judgments where people do not search through the arithmetic rules that have to be computed (primes, squares and cubes, powers, multiples other than 5), the target brings to mind the computed rules it itself belongs to (asked about 32, "a power of 2" comes to mind), and people weigh those cued rules against the explanations visible at a glance (ranges, odd or even, digits, multiples of 5) by the same Bayesian logic. Because only rules the target belongs to are cued, this predicts an asymmetry the search account cannot produce: distant members of a computed rule that fits the examples (e.g. 32 or 2 after 16, 8, 64) are endorsed almost as if the rule had been searched for, while in-span non-members (e.g. 40) cue no such rule and keep their range-based yeses.
