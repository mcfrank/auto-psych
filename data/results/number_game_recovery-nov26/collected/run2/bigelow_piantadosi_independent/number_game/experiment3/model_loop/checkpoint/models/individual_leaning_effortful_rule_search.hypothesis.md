Refinement of `effortful_computed_rule_search`: people search for arithmetic rules that have to be computed (primes, squares and cubes, powers, multiples other than 5) only on a fixed share of judgments, and otherwise weigh only the explanations visible at a glance (ranges, odd or even, digits, multiples of 5) by the same Bayesian logic. The single change, a component taken from `loose_range_individual_rule_leaning`, is that participants also differ stably in how readily they reach for any rule rather than a stretch of the number line: each person's leaning shifts the prior odds of every rule (computed or glance) against the ranges, with a fixed spread around the population. This predicts individual differences that appear even for sets no computed rule fits (e.g. all-even or shared-final-digit sets), where range-leaning people still give yeses to in-span non-members and rule-leaning people to distant same-parity or same-digit targets, consistently across every set each person sees.
