People do not infer the concept behind the examples; they hold familiar number categories in memory (odd, even, primes, squares and cubes, powers, multiples, final or contained digit, multiples of 5, and stretches of the number line) and the example set retrieves them as a memory probe does, each category coming to mind in proportion to how large a share of the examples it contains, with small, specific categories retrieved more readily than broad ones. The target is then judged "yes" to the extent that the retrieved categories contain it. Because retrieval depends on the share of matching examples rather than on how improbable the examples would be under each category, adding further examples that fit a category does not sharpen generalization onto it, and a category that misses one example is still retrieved fairly often.
