Refinement of `effortful_computed_rule_search`: people search for arithmetic rules that have to be computed (primes, squares and cubes, powers, multiples other than 5) only on some judgments, and otherwise generalize from the explanations visible at a glance (ranges, odd or even, digits, multiples of 5), weighing whichever concepts they considered by the same Bayesian logic. The single change is that how often a person invests this effort is a stable personal trait rather than a population constant (each participant's propensity to search differs from the population's by a fixed spread, inferred from their answers). This predicts that some participants consistently extend computed rules to distant members (e.g. 16, 8, 64 → 32, 2) across every rule-fitting set they see, while others consistently answer such sets by range and digit pattern, with no individual differences on sets that no computed rule fits.
