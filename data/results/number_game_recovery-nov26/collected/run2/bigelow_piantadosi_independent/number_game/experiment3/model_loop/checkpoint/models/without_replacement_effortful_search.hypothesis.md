Refinement of `without_replacement_example_sampling`: people understand that the examples are distinct numbers the program produced one after another without repeats, so each example is judged against the members of a concept not yet shown, which makes a small concept that the examples nearly use up (the cubes after 1, 8, 27; a range barely wider than the examples) an especially striking explanation. The single change, a component taken from `effortful_computed_rule_search`, is that rules that have to be computed (primes, squares and cubes, powers, multiples other than 5) are only searched for on a fixed share of judgments; on the rest people weigh, by the same without-replacement logic, only the explanations visible at a glance (stretches of the number line, odd or even, final or contained digits, multiples of 5). This predicts that nearly exhausted ranges and glance rules are generalized narrowly to their few remaining members even when a computed rule fits, while nearly exhausted computed rules (e.g. 1, 8, 27) win out only on the judgments where people search for them.
