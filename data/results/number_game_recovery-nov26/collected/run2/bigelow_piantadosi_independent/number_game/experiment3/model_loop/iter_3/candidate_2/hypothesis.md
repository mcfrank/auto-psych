People infer the hidden concept by the same Bayesian weighing of arithmetic rules (kind plausibilities, a few exceptionally familiar or unfamiliar rules) and numerical ranges as the simplest current model, and normally answer by probability matching to their belief, but on some trials their attention lapses and they answer with a coin-flip guess instead of consulting the belief at all. How often a person lapses is a stable trait that differs between participants. This predicts that lapse-prone participants give "no" to some of the examples themselves and "yes" to some numbers no concept supports, consistently across every set they see, while uncertain targets are barely affected.
