People infer the hidden concept by ordinary Bayesian learning over mathematical rules (with separate prior plausibilities for even, odd and the other kinds of rule) and numerical ranges with a tempered size principle, but they do not answer by probability matching: they say yes only when their belief that the target is in the concept clears an internal criterion of how sure they must be, applied with some trial-to-trial noise. This predicts that targets with intermediate support — common after two examples, when several rules and ranges are still in play — are rejected more often than their belief implies, so moderately uncertain sets get fewer yeses than probability matching allows, while clearly supported and clearly unsupported targets are answered nearly unanimously.
