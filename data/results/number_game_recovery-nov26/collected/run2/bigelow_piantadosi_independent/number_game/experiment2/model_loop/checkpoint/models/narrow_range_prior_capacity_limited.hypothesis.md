Refinement of `capacity_limited_evidence_integration`: people do the same capacity-limited Bayesian learning over rules (separate prior plausibilities for even, odd and the other kinds of rule) and numerical ranges, with each added example contributing less evidence, but before seeing any example they expect the program's numerical range to be narrow — a range's prior plausibility falls as a fitted power of its width, instead of all ranges being equally plausible. The single change is this narrow-range prior expectation, which, unlike the evidence from the examples, is not diluted by the capacity limit as sets grow, so for sets that no rule explains (e.g. mixing odd and even numbers) people keep their yeses inside the examples' span more sharply than the incumbent predicts, as the mixed-parity range-gap critique shows.
