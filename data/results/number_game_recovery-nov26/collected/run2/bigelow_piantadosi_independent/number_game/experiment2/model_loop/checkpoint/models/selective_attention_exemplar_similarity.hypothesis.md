People do not infer a hidden concept; they judge a target by its average similarity to the stored examples (an exemplar account), where similarity is computed over the number's magnitude and its membership in familiar number categories (even, odd, primes, squares, powers, multiples, final digit, contained digit), and attention is allocated selectively by the examples themselves: the categories that all examples share draw attention (with a fitted dependence on how broad each category is), and whatever attention they draw is taken away from magnitude. So when the examples share a category, targets breaking it are judged dissimilar even if numerically close, but when the examples share nothing (e.g. mixed parity) attention falls entirely on magnitude and yeses follow numerical proximity to the examples.
