People do the same capacity-limited Bayesian learning over mathematical rules and numerical ranges as the best model (with "multiples of 5" as its own, readily entertained kind of rule), but they read the smallest and the largest example as marking where the program's range stops: a range whose lower edge sits exactly at the smallest example, or whose upper edge sits exactly at the largest example, is entertained more readily before any evidence is weighed (a fitted boost per anchored edge). This predicts that, when ranges carry the judgment, yeses fall off right at the extreme examples — fewer yeses to targets just beyond them, more to targets inside their span — rather than tapering gradually past them as the size principle alone implies.
