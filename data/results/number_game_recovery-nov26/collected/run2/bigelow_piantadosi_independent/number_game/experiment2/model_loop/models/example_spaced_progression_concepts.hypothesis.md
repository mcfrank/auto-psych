People do the same capacity-limited Bayesian learning over mathematical rules (separate prior plausibilities for even, odd and the other kinds of rule) and numerical ranges as the best model, but their space of concepts also contains evenly spaced sequences of any step read off the examples themselves — "start at an example and keep adding the gap between examples" (every number leaving the same remainder when divided by a step from 3 to 50, e.g. 23, 57, 91) — a new kind of rule with its own prior plausibility. Because any two examples always lie on such a sparse sequence, while three or four examples rarely do, two-example sets are often read as a narrow progression and generalized conservatively (few yeses beyond the progression's continuation), producing a dip in yeses on two-example sets relative to larger ones.
