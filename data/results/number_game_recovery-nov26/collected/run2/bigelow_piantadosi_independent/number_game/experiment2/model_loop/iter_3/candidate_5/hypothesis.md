Refinement of `multiplication_fluency_rule_prior`: people do capacity-limited Bayesian learning over rules and numerical ranges, and a "multiples of m" rule comes to mind less readily the larger m is, because checking divisibility by a larger number is less fluent mental arithmetic. The single change is grafting the base-ten account of multiples of 5 (from `base_ten_multiples_capacity_limited`) onto it: multiples of 5 are not a point on that fluency gradient but their own, separately plausible kind of rule because they fit our decimal notation, so the gradient describes only the non-decimal bases (3, 4, 6, 7, 8, 9, 11, 12). This predicts that sets such as 90, 100, 5 are generalized to every multiple of 5, while sets fitting multiples of 7, 9, 11 or 12 (e.g. 42, 35, 21, 84) are generalized less to the rule's distant members and more to non-member numbers within the examples' span than the best model predicts.
