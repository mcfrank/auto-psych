People infer the hidden concept by Bayesian learning over mathematical rules (with separate prior plausibilities for even, odd and the other kinds of rule) and numerical ranges, with capacity-limited integration of the examples, but they mis-weight the two kinds of evidence: the "suspicious coincidence" that the examples all fall in a narrow stretch of the number line is applied more strongly than the coincidence that they all obey an arithmetic rule, because magnitude is perceived effortlessly while rule membership must be computed. This predicts that when no rule fits well (for example sets mixing odd and even numbers) people confine their yeses to the span of the examples more sharply than a learner who applies one sampling strength to every concept.
