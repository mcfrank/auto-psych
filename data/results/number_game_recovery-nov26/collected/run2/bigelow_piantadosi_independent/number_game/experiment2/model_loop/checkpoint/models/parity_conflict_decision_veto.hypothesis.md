People infer the hidden concept by the same capacity-limited Bayesian learning over rules (with multiples of 5 as its own, base-ten-salient kind of rule) and numerical ranges as the best model, but a number's parity is registered automatically and effortlessly, so at the moment of judging a target whose parity matches none of the examples, that conflict intrudes on the decision and, on a fixed fraction of such trials, overrides the inferred concept and produces a "no". This decision-stage parity interference predicts that in all-odd or all-even sets, opposite-parity targets that the inferred concept covers (for example odd numbers inside the span of 20, 32, 92) get fewer yeses than the concept alone implies, while same-parity targets are unaffected.
