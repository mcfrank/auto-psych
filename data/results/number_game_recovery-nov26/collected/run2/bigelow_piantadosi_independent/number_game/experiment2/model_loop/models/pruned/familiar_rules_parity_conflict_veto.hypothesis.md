Refinement of `rule_familiarity_within_kinds`: people do the same capacity-limited Bayesian learning over rules and numerical ranges, with each kind of rule having its own prior plausibility and each rule its own familiarity within that kind. On top of this, a number's parity is registered automatically, so when the target's parity matches none of the examples, that conflict intrudes at the moment of answering and, on a fixed fraction of those trials, overrides the inferred concept and produces a "no". The single change is grafting this decision-stage parity veto (from `parity_conflict_decision_veto`) onto the familiarity model. It predicts that in all-odd or all-even sets, opposite-parity targets that the inferred concept covers (for example odd numbers inside the span of 20, 32, 92) get fewer yeses than the concept implies, while same-parity targets are unaffected.
