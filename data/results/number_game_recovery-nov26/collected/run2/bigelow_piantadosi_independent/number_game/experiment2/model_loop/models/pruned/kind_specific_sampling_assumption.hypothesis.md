People infer the hidden concept by Bayesian learning over mathematical rules (with separate prior plausibilities for even, odd and the other kinds of rule) and numerical ranges, but they make different sampling assumptions for the two kinds of concept: they treat the examples as deliberately drawn from a magnitude range (a strong size principle, so the tightest range covering the examples wins sharply), while they treat a mathematical rule as merely compatible with the examples, discounting how suspiciously the examples fit a small rule (a separately fitted, weaker size principle). This predicts sharp yes/no edges at the examples' smallest and largest values when no rule explains them (e.g. mixed-parity sets), while broad rules such as parity still survive when the examples do fit them.
