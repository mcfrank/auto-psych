People infer the hidden concept by capacity-limited Bayesian learning over mathematical rules and numerical ranges, but they check the target against that concept through an approximate, logarithmically compressed sense of its magnitude rather than its exact identity: the target is confused with numbers of similar size, more so for larger numbers (Weber's law), and the answer is the concept's average endorsement over those confusable numbers. This predicts that targets just outside a rule but numerically close to members — especially large targets — get more yeses than an exact-identity learner allows (the rule-violating near-target leak), while small targets are judged nearly exactly.
