# Existing hypotheses

Each model below is ONE cognitive hypothesis, with how it stands on the current data by ELPD-LOO (best first). `elpd_diff ± dse` is a model's deficit against the best and the standard error of that difference (with the responses to each stimulus pair counted together, as they are correlated): within about 2·dse the two are statistically tied on this data; beyond it the model has lost. "PSIS-LOO unreliable" means the estimate itself is untrustworthy (too many high-Pareto-k trials), not that the model is bad. Propose a hypothesis that is genuinely different from these, or a refinement of a single one of them — never a combination of several.

## base_ten_multiples_capacity_limited  — rank 0, the best model on this data, ELPD-LOO -2083.6

Refinement of `capacity_limited_evidence_integration`: people do the same capacity-limited Bayesian learning over rules and numerical ranges, with each added example contributing less evidence, but "multiples of 5" is not just one more multiples rule sharing a single plausibility with multiples of 3, 4, 7, 11 and the rest — because it is aligned with our base-ten number notation, it is its own, far more readily entertained kind of rule with a separate prior plausibility inferred from the data. The single change is splitting multiples of 5 out of the multiples family (as even and odd are already split out of parity), predicting that sets such as 90, 100, 5 are generalized to every multiple of 5 across the number line, while sets fitting a less familiar multiple (e.g. 42, 35, 21, 84 as multiples of 7) are generalized by that rule less than the incumbent predicts.

## narrow_range_base_ten_multiples  — rank 1, 0.4 ± 2.0 nats behind the best (0.2× dse: statistically tied with the best), ELPD-LOO -2084.0

Refinement of `base_ten_multiples_capacity_limited`: people do the same capacity-limited Bayesian learning over rules and numerical ranges, with "multiples of 5" being its own readily entertained kind of rule because it fits base-ten notation, and with each added example contributing less evidence. But before seeing any example they also expect the program's numerical range to be narrow: a range's prior plausibility falls as a fitted power of its width, instead of every range being equally plausible. The single change is grafting this narrow-range expectation (from `narrow_range_prior_capacity_limited`) onto the base-ten model. Because the expectation comes before the evidence, the capacity limit does not dilute it, so for sets that no rule explains (e.g. mixed odd and even numbers) people keep their yeses closer to the examples' span than the base-ten model predicts, while its multiples-of-5 generalization stays the same.

## capacity_limited_evidence_integration  — rank 3, 56.6 ± 19.7 nats behind the best (2.9× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -2140.2

People infer the hidden concept by Bayesian learning over mathematical rules (with separate prior plausibilities for even, odd and the other kinds of rule) and numerical intervals, but they have a limited capacity for integrating examples: the examples of a set compete for the same limited working memory, so each additional example adds less evidence than the one before, and the total evidence grows more slowly than the number of examples. ## approximate_magnitude_target_encoding  — rank 4, 57.4 ± 19.8 nats behind the best (2.9× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -2141.0

This predicts that targets just outside a rule but numerically close to members — especially large targets — get more yeses than an exact-identity learner allows (the rule-violating near-target leak), while small targets are judged nearly exactly.

## selective_attention_exemplar_similarity  — rank 22, 357.8 ± 32.3 nats behind the best (11.1× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -2441.4

So when the examples share a category, targets breaking it are judged dissimilar even if numerically close, but when the examples share nothing (e.g. mixed parity) attention falls entirely on magnitude and yeses follow numerical proximity to the examples.

## spread_scaled_distance_gradient  — rank 23, 680.0 ± 45.4 nats behind the best (15.0× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -2763.5

People judge a target by its numerical closeness to the nearest example shown, with willingness to say yes falling off exponentially (a Shepard generalization gradient) with the distance between the target and that example on the 1-100 number line. The breadth of this gradient is not fixed but is set by how dispersed the examples are: tightly clustered examples produce a narrow gradient, widely scattered examples a broad one, so the spread of the examples tells people how far from them the program is likely to reach.
