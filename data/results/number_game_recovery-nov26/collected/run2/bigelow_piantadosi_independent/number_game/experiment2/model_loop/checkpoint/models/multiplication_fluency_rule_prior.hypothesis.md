Refinement of `capacity_limited_evidence_integration`: people do the same capacity-limited Bayesian learning over rules (separate prior plausibilities for even, odd and the other kinds of rule) and numerical ranges, but a "multiples of m" rule is entertained less readily the larger m is, because checking divisibility by a larger number is less fluent mental arithmetic (multiples of 3 or 4 come to mind easily, multiples of 7, 9, 11 or 12 hardly do). The single change is a fitted fluency gradient that lowers each multiples rule's prior plausibility as a power of its base. The disagreement with the best model lives in sets that fit multiples of a large base (e.g. 42, 35, 21, 84 or 36, 45, 54, 81): the best model generalizes them to the rule's distant members (91, 98, 90, 99), whereas this model predicts fewer yeses there and more yeses to non-member numbers inside the examples' span, which are carried by ranges.
