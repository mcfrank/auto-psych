Refinement of `narrow_range_prior_capacity_limited`: people do the same Bayesian learning over rules (separate prior plausibilities for even, odd and the other kinds of rule) and numerical ranges, with a prior expectation that the program's range is narrow, but the examples on display interfere with one another in working memory, so every additional example degrades the encoding of *all* the examples by a fixed proportion. The single change is replacing the power-law dilution of evidence with this multiplicative interference: total evidence first rises with set size and can then fall once the display overloads memory, so two-example sets are judged most conservatively and three- and four-example sets more generously, as the critique's two-example dip shows (a power-law dilution can only make evidence grow monotonically).
