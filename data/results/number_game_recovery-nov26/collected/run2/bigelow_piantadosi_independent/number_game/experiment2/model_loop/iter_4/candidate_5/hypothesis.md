Refinement of `parity_conflict_decision_veto`: people infer the hidden concept by capacity-limited Bayesian learning over rules (multiples of 5 as its own base-ten kind) and numerical ranges, and a target whose parity matches none of the examples triggers an automatic parity conflict that, on a fixed fraction of such trials, overrides the inferred concept with a "no". The single change is grafting rule-specific familiarity (from `rule_familiarity_within_kinds`) onto the concept-learning stage, so that within a kind some rules (e.g. multiples of 11, powers of 10) come to mind readily and others (e.g. multiples of 7, "contains the digit 1") hardly at all. The claim is that the decision-stage parity veto is a real effect and not a by-product of miscalibrated rule priors: even with well-calibrated rules, opposite-parity targets in all-odd or all-even sets (e.g. odd numbers inside the span of 20, 32, 92) get fewer yeses than the inferred concept implies.
