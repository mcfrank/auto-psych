Refinement of `capacity_limited_evidence_integration`: people do the same Bayesian learning over rules (separate prior plausibilities for even, odd and the other kinds of rule) and numerical ranges, but their limited capacity is a fixed span of two examples rather than a smooth power-law dilution — the first two examples of a set are each integrated in full, and only examples beyond that span are integrated partially (a fitted fraction of an example's evidence each). The single change is replacing the incumbent's gradual n^-gamma discount with this two-item span, predicting that two-example sets are judged as conservatively as an ideal learner would (fewer yeses), while three- and four-example sets gain little extra evidence and so generalize relatively broadly — the two-example dip the critique reports.
