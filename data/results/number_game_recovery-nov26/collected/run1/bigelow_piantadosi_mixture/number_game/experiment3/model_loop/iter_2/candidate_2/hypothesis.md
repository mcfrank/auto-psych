People treat additional examples as partly redundant rather than as independent evidence: the support that several examples lend a candidate concept grows with diminishing returns in the number of examples (as a fitted power of the count), instead of adding up example by example as the size principle assumes. So as examples accumulate, people narrow their guess toward small, specific concepts (tight ranges, "powers of 2") more slowly than a normative learner, and keep endorsing targets outside the examples' span after three or four clustered examples nearly as much as after two — while otherwise forming and keeping a single working guess as the current best learner does.
