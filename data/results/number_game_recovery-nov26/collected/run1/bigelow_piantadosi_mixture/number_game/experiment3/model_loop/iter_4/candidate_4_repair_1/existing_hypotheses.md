## noisy_criterion_belief_threshold  — rank 17, 71.0 ± 9.0 nats behind the best (7.9× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -3191.6

People infer the concept as the simplest current sticky-guess learner does, but they do not answer by probability matching: they compare how strongly they believe the target belongs against a fixed internal criterion of how sure they must be, and that comparison is perturbed by trial-to-trial noise in the felt belief, so they say "yes" with a probability that rises smoothly (logistically) with belief strength around the criterion. ## fan_retrieval_member_similarity  — no comparison row

People do not infer the program's concept; they retrieve familiar number categories from memory — rules such as "even", "powers of 2" or "ends in 6", and stretches of the number line — each stored as the list of its member numbers. Each example cues the categories that store it, with a memory fan effect: a cue's strength is diluted by how many members the category stores, and an example a category does not store counts only as a stray number. The target is then endorsed by how similar it is, on the number line, to the nearest stored member of each retrieved category (similarity falling off exponentially at a fitted rate), so near-misses just outside a range or beside a rule's members are partly endorsed, and because all the examples cue memory together, their order does not matter (no anchoring on the first example).

## intuitive_statistician_range_estimate  — no comparison row

People do not generalise along the number line by weighing every possible range the program might use; when they think the program produces numbers from a range, they estimate that range like an intuitive statistician, from the smallest and largest examples seen, extending it beyond each extreme by a margin that is a fitted base amount plus a fitted share of the examples' spread per example (as in estimating a population's range from a sample), and re-estimating it from all examples as each new one arrives. Because the estimate depends only on the extremes and the number of examples, range-based generalisation is symmetric about the examples and independent of their order (no extra extension on the first example's side), while rule guesses ("powers of 2", "ends in 6") are formed and kept as in the current sticky single-guess learner.
