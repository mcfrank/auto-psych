Refinement of the best model, gamma_width_distance_hesitant_guess. People infer a single sticky working concept guess (a rule, or a numerical range with a gamma-shaped width prior) by Bayesian concept learning from the first example, with digit rules salient once two examples share a digit, coincidences triggering a rethink, fitted trust, and misfits written off more readily the more examples the guess explains. They then hesitate to extend that guess to a target in proportion to its similarity to the nearest example. The one change is the similarity metric: closeness is judged by ratio on a logarithmically compressed mental number line, not by raw difference. Willingness falls off smoothly (Gaussian-like) with the log-ratio distance at a fitted reach. So the same numerical gap is a small step among large numbers (90 after 80) but a long leap among small ones (12 after 2), and far rule members of small examples are discounted most.
