People's space of candidate concepts includes, besides single rules and numerical ranges, "either-or" last-digit concepts — numbers ending in one of two digits, such as "ends in 2 or 7" (82, 67, 72) or "ends in 1 or 5" — which they entertain as written-form rules with their own fitted prior plausibility, shared equally among the 45 such pairs. When the examples end in just two different digits, people can therefore generalise to far-away numbers ending in either digit (62 or 17 after 82, 67, 72) instead of falling back on a range or writing an example off as a stray; otherwise they learn as the current best single-sticky-guess learner does.
