# Existing hypotheses

Each model below is ONE cognitive hypothesis, with how it stands on the current data by ELPD-LOO (best first). `elpd_diff ± dse` is a model's deficit against the best and the standard error of that difference (with the responses to each stimulus pair counted together, as they are correlated): within about 2·dse the two are statistically tied on this data; beyond it the model has lost. "PSIS-LOO unreliable" means the estimate itself is untrustworthy (too many high-Pareto-k trials), not that the model is bad. Propose a hypothesis that is genuinely different from these, or a refinement of a single one of them — never a combination of several.

## gamma_width_range_prior_guess  — rank 0, the best model on this data, ELPD-LOO -3123.8

Refinement of the incumbent evidence_graded_misfit_tolerance (a single working concept guess — a rule or a numerical range — formed from the first example by Bayesian concept learning, kept with fitted stickiness, digit rules discounted and salient only once two examples share the digit, a newly noticed coincidence triggering a rethink, fitted trust, and a contradicting example written off as a stray more readily the more earlier examples the guess explains). The one change: people's prior sense of how wide the program's range of numbers is does not have the fixed Erlang form the incumbent assumes, but a gamma form whose shape is fitted, so how implausible very narrow ranges hugging the examples are, relative to how fast very broad ranges become implausible, is set by the data. With a shape below the incumbent's, tight ranges around clustered examples are more readily entertained while broad ranges stay plausible longer, so people generalise sharply right around clustered examples but with a longer tail out to distant numbers.

## distance_hesitant_concept_extension  — rank 1, 0.3 ± 2.7 nats behind the best (0.1× dse: statistically tied with the best), ELPD-LOO -3124.1

People infer the program's concept as the current best learner does (a single sticky working guess — a rule or a numerical range — formed from the first example, with digit rules salient once two examples share a digit, coincidences triggering a rethink, and misfits written off more readily the more evidence the guess has), but they hesitate to extrapolate: even when the target belongs to their guessed concept, their willingness to say the program would produce it falls off smoothly (Gaussian-like) with how far the target lies on the number line from the nearest example they have seen, at a fitted reach. ## evidence_graded_misfit_tolerance  — rank 2, 2.1 ± 2.1 nats behind the best (1.0× dse: statistically tied with the best), ELPD-LOO -3125.9

Refinement of the incumbent misfit_tolerant_coincidence_rethink (a single working concept guess — a rule or a numerical range — formed from the first example by Bayesian concept learning, kept with fitted stickiness while later examples fit it, digit rules discounted and salient only once two examples share the digit, a newly noticed coincidence triggering a rethink, fitted trust, and a contradicting example sometimes written off as a stray). The one change: how readily people write off a contradicting example grows with how much evidence their guess already has — each earlier example the guess explains is an independent reason to keep it, so the chance of dismissing the misfit is one minus the chance that none of those confirmations holds (the same fitted tolerance per confirming example, zero for a guess that explains nothing). So "ends in 7" survives an outlier 8 more often after 77, 17, 37 than after 77, 17, and a misfit late in a long set is shrugged off more than one early in a short set. (To keep the fit tractable, the incumbent's power-law preference for where a range lies on the number line, which earlier rounds found the data barely constrain, is fixed at neutral.)

## misfit_tolerant_coincidence_rethink  — rank 3, 3.1 ± 2.3 nats behind the best (1.3× dse: statistically tied with the best), ELPD-LOO -3126.9

Refinement of the incumbent digit_coincidence_noticing_guess (a single working concept guess — a rule or a numerical range — formed from the first example by Bayesian concept learning, kept with fitted stickiness while later examples fit it, digit rules discounted and made salient only once a digit is noticed as shared by at least two examples, fitted trust in the examples). ## coincidence_salience_guess_revision  — rank 8, 8.2 ± 3.1 nats behind the best (2.6× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -3132.0

Refinement of coincidence_gated_all_rules_guess (a single sticky working guess — a rule or a numerical range — formed from the first example by Bayesian concept learning, with rules made salient only once two examples share them). The one change: noticing a coincidence is itself a trigger for rethinking — when a new example makes a rule shared by two examples for the first time, people abandon their current guess and reconsider the concept from all examples (even if the guess still fits) with a probability equal to how compelling the newly noticed rule is given the examples so far, with no extra free parameter. ## compressed_line_category_exemplars  — rank 11, 52.9 ± 11.8 nats behind the best (4.5× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -3176.7

People do not infer the program's concept; they retrieve familiar number categories (rules such as "even" or "powers of 2", and ranges bounded by multiples of five) stored in memory as lists of their member numbers, activating each category by how similar the examples are to its stored members, and endorse the target by its similarity to the nearest member of the activated categories. The comparison is made on a compressed mental number line (compression fitted), so the same numerical gap counts as less dissimilar among large numbers than among small ones: generalisation from large examples (e.g. 82, 92) spreads further than from small ones (e.g. 2, 12), and small categories crowded among small numbers are matched more sharply.

## noisy_criterion_belief_threshold  — rank 12, 67.8 ± 8.7 nats behind the best (7.8× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -3191.6

People infer the concept as the simplest current sticky-guess learner does, but they do not answer by probability matching: they compare how strongly they believe the target belongs against a fixed internal criterion of how sure they must be, and that comparison is perturbed by trial-to-trial noise in the felt belief, so they say "yes" with a probability that rises smoothly (logistically) with belief strength around the criterion. This noisy criterion produces both a ceiling below 1 for certain members (the examples themselves) and a floor above 0 for clear non-members, and makes moderately believed targets fall clearly on one side of the criterion rather than being endorsed in proportion to belief.

## weak_sampling_range_guess  — rank 13, 77.1 ± 15.0 nats behind the best (5.1× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -3200.9

Refinement of evidence_graded_misfit_tolerance (a single working concept guess, either a rule or a numerical range, formed from the first example by Bayesian concept learning; kept with fitted stickiness; digit rules salient only once two examples share the digit; a noticed coincidence triggering a rethink; fitted trust; misfits written off more readily the more examples the guess already explains). The one change: people apply the size principle only to rules, never to ranges. They think of a range as a region the program's numbers merely fall in, not as a pool the examples were drawn from, so a range that covers the examples gets no extra credit for being tight, and only their prior sense of a typical range width decides how far they generalise. Where this disagrees most with the best model: for clustered examples (24,35; 68,58; 83,77), and more so the more examples there are, it predicts that targets well outside the examples' span are still endorsed, because generalisation does not tighten toward the span as examples accumulate. Range guesses also compete less with rules.

## justification_search_noisy_or  — rank 14, 868.4 ± 43.3 nats behind the best (20.0× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -3992.2

People do not weigh competing explanations against one another; they answer by searching memory for any familiar explanation (a rule such as "even" or "ends in 6", or a numerical range) that covers both the examples and the target, and say "yes" as soon as one comes to mind. Each explanation comes to mind with a chance that grows with how well it accounts for the examples on its own (how plausible it is and how surprising the examples would be without it), so a target is endorsed by the absolute evidence for explanations that include it, not by its share relative to better explanations that exclude it — e.g. after 16, 8, 2, 64, "even" can still come to mind and license 10, even though "powers of 2" fits far better.

## partial_recall_guess_resampling  — no comparison row

People learn the concept as the current best sequential learner does (a single working guess — a rule or a numerical range — formed from the first example, kept while later examples fit it, digit rules salient once two examples share a digit, coincidences triggering a rethink, and misfits written off more readily the more evidence the guess has), but their memory of the earlier examples is limited: when they drop their guess and rethink, they always use the example just shown but recall each earlier example only with a fitted probability, and choose the new guess from the examples they recall. So a rethink driven by a late example often lands on a concept that fits that example and only some of the earlier ones (the classic "resample from what you remember" hypothesis-testing process), and the working guess, not a full record of the examples, is what carries the past.

## sample_size_insensitive_guess  — no comparison row

People learn the program's concept as the current best learner does (a single sticky working guess — a rule or a numerical range — formed from the first example by Bayesian concept learning, with digit rules salient once two examples share a digit, a noticed coincidence triggering a rethink, and misfits written off more readily the more evidence the guess has), but with one classic distortion, insensitivity to sample size: they treat successive examples as partly redundant, so the evidence from several examples counts for less than the sum of each example's evidence, growing only as a fitted power of the number of examples seen. As a result, when they form or rethink a guess from several examples, the preference for the smallest concept consistent with them (and the penalty for a guess that misses an example) sharpens less with each added example than a normative learner's would, while generalisation from a single example is unchanged.
