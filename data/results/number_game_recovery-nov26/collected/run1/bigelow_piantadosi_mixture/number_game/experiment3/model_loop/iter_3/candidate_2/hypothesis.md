People infer the concept as the simple sequential learner does (a single sticky working guess — a rule or a numerical range — formed from the first example and revised as examples arrive), but when they pick that working guess they do not probability-match the plausibility of the candidate guesses: they choose by a Luce choice rule whose exponent is fitted, so with an exponent above one they mostly seize the single most plausible guess (maximizing), and with one below one they pick less plausible guesses more often than their plausibility warrants. This decision rule makes generalisation more all-or-none (or more diffuse) around the best explanation without changing how plausible each explanation is.
