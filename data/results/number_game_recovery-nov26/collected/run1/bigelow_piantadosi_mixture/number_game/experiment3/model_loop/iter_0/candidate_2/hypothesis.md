People do not infer the program's concept; they retrieve familiar number categories (rules such as "even" or "powers of 2", and ranges bounded by multiples of five) stored in memory as lists of their member numbers, activating each category by how similar the examples are to its stored members, and endorse the target by its similarity to the nearest member of the activated categories. The comparison is made on a compressed mental number line (compression fitted), so the same numerical gap counts as less dissimilar among large numbers than among small ones: generalisation from large examples (e.g. 82, 92) spreads further than from small ones (e.g. 2, 12), and small categories crowded among small numbers are matched more sharply.
