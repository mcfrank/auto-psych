# Existing hypotheses

Each model below is ONE cognitive hypothesis, with how it stands on the current data by ELPD-LOO (best first). `elpd_diff ± dse` is a model's deficit against the best and the standard error of that difference (with the responses to each stimulus pair counted together, as they are correlated): within about 2·dse the two are statistically tied on this data; beyond it the model has lost. "PSIS-LOO unreliable" means the estimate itself is untrustworthy (too many high-Pareto-k trials), not that the model is bad. Propose a hypothesis that is genuinely different from these, or a refinement of a single one of them — never a combination of several.

## misfit_tolerant_coincidence_rethink  — rank 0, the best model on this data, ELPD-LOO -3126.9

Refinement of the incumbent coincidence_triggered_guess_rethink (a single working concept guess — a rule or a numerical range — formed from the first example by Bayesian concept learning, kept with fitted stickiness while later examples fit it, digit rules discounted and made salient only once a digit is shared by two examples, a newly noticed coincidence triggering a rethink, fitted trust in the examples). The one change: a guess is not automatically abandoned when a new example contradicts it — people sometimes write the misfitting example off as a stray number and keep their guess, with a fitted tolerance. So after 77 and 17 a guess of "ends in 7" often survives the outlier 8, and targets fitting the early examples' concept are still endorsed despite a later example that breaks it.

## coincidence_triggered_guess_rethink  — rank 1, 0.3 ± 0.8 nats behind the best (0.4× dse: statistically tied with the best), ELPD-LOO -3127.2

Refinement of the incumbent digit_coincidence_noticing_guess (a single working concept guess — a rule or a numerical range — formed from the first example by Bayesian concept learning, kept with fitted stickiness while later examples fit it, digit rules discounted and made salient only once a digit is noticed as shared by at least two examples, fitted trust in the examples). The one change: noticing a coincidence is itself a trigger to rethink. When a new example makes people notice a digit it shares with an earlier example, they drop their current guess with a fitted probability and reconsider the concept from all the examples, even if that guess still fits. So after 46 then 86, a range guess formed from 46 that happens to contain 86 is often given up in favour of "ends in 6", and far numbers like 96 are endorsed more than the purely sticky learner predicts.

## compressed_concept_size_sticky_guess  — rank 2, 1.8 ± 1.4 nats behind the best (1.3× dse: statistically tied with the best), ELPD-LOO -3128.7

People learn the concept as in the current best model (a single sticky working guess — a rule or a numerical range — formed from the first example by Bayesian concept learning, with the size principle favouring small concepts), but with one distortion: they misjudge how many numbers a candidate concept contains, perceiving concept size on a compressed, numerosity-like scale relative to the whole range 1-100 (with the compression fitted). So they feel "powers of 2" or a narrow range is less small, and "even numbers" less large, than it really is, which weakens (or, if expanded, strengthens) the size principle's preference for specific concepts without changing how strongly a non-fitting example counts against a guess.

## graded_digit_salience_guess  — rank 3, 3.1 ± 1.2 nats behind the best (2.5× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -3130.0

Refinement of digit_coincidence_noticing_guess (a single working concept guess — a rule or a numerical range — formed from the first example by Bayesian concept learning, kept with fitted stickiness while later examples fit it, digit rules discounted, fitted trust in the examples, and rules about how numbers are written made salient only by a noticed shared digit). The one change: noticing a shared digit is not all-or-none at two examples but grows with every example that carries it — the reduced plausibility of a written-form rule ("ends in 6", "contains a 3") is divided among the examples that have its digit, so it is fully reduced with one such example, half as reduced with two, a third with three — making "ends in 6" a gradually more salient guess as more examples repeat the digit, rather than fully salient as soon as two do.

## coincidence_salience_guess_revision  — rank 4, 5.1 ± 1.7 nats behind the best (2.9× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -3132.0

Refinement of coincidence_gated_all_rules_guess (a single sticky working guess — a rule or a numerical range — formed from the first example by Bayesian concept learning, with rules made salient only once two examples share them). The one change: noticing a coincidence is itself a trigger for rethinking — when a new example makes a rule shared by two examples for the first time, people abandon their current guess and reconsider the concept from all examples (even if the guess still fits) with a probability equal to how compelling the newly noticed rule is given the examples so far, with no extra free parameter. So a striking pattern revealed by later examples overrides a guess formed from an unrepresentative first example, while a weak coincidence barely unsettles it, and order effects are weaker than pure stickiness predicts.

## coincidence_gated_all_rules_guess  — rank 5, 5.2 ± 1.7 nats behind the best (3.0× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -3132.1

Refinement of digit_coincidence_noticing_guess (a single working concept guess — a rule or a numerical range — formed from the first example by Bayesian concept learning and kept with fitted stickiness while later examples fit it, digit rules discounted, fitted trust in the examples, and digit rules made salient only once a digit is noticed as shared by at least two examples). The one change: the same coincidence-noticing gate applies to arithmetic rules too — "even", "multiples of 7" or "squares" becomes a salient candidate only once at least two of the examples seen so far share that property, and until then (always after a single example) its plausibility is reduced by its own fitted availability factor. So a lone example suggests a range of nearby numbers rather than a broad rule, people generalise less to far-away numbers from one example, and the first working guess is range-like until a shared property is noticed.

## coincidence_noticing_guess_vote  — rank 6, 6.1 ± 2.6 nats behind the best (2.3× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -3133.0

Refinement of digit_coincidence_noticing_guess (a single sticky working concept guess — a rule or a numerical range — formed from the first example by Bayesian concept learning, digit rules discounted and made salient only once a digit is noticed as shared by two examples, fitted trust in the examples). The one change, the component taken from sampled_guess_vote_threshold: people do not answer by consulting a single guess, but call to mind several guesses generated by this learner and say "yes" only if a sufficient share of them contain the target, with the effective number of guesses and the share required fitted. So the same belief maps onto a sharpened or softened, more liberal or more conservative yes rate — e.g. targets that only some plausible guesses contain (96 after 46 and 86) can be endorsed less, and near-certain members more, than probability matching predicts.
