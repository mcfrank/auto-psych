People do Bayesian concept learning over familiar mathematical rules and numerical ranges (size principle, a prior weight per rule, an Erlang prior on range width), but they can also entertain a rule confined to a region of the number line bounded by decades — e.g. "numbers ending in 3 between 10 and 50" or "even numbers in the twenties" — with a fitted share of each rule's prior going to such restricted versions. Because a restricted rule is smaller, the size principle favours it whenever the examples all lie in one region, so people extend a rule like "ends in d" or "multiples of m" to far-away numbers less than a pure-rule learner does.
