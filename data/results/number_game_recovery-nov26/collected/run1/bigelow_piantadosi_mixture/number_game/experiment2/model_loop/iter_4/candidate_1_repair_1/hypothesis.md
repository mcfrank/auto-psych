When a new example falls outside the numerical range people are currently guessing, they often do not rethink the concept from scratch: with a fitted probability they make the minimal revision, stretching their range just far enough to take in the new example (so a range around 46 becomes "46 up to 86" after 86 arrives), and only otherwise reconsider from all the examples. Because the stretched range depends on which range was guessed first and on the order in which examples arrive, the same examples in different orders produce different generalisations, and contradicted range guesses grow into ranges spanning the examples instead of giving way to rules like "ends in 6" (otherwise as in the current best sticky-guess learner with coincidence-noticing of digit rules, with ranges weighted only by their width).
