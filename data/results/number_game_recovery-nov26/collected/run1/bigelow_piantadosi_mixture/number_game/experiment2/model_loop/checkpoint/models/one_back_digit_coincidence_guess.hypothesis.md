People hold a single working guess about the concept (a rule or a numerical range), formed from the first example and kept while later examples fit it, and a rule about how numbers are written ("ends in 6", "contains the digit 3") becomes a salient guess only once they notice its digit as a coincidence. They notice such coincidences by a one-back working-memory comparison, setting each new example against the example shown just before it, so a shared digit is noticed only when two consecutive examples have it. So the order of the examples matters: "46, 86, 13" makes "ends in 6" salient and leads people to say yes to 96, whereas "46, 13, 86" does not, although both contain the same numbers.
