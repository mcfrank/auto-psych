People judge the target by its similarity to the examples, but on whichever single metric of number similarity the examples themselves cluster on most surprisingly: either closeness on the number line (with a graded, exponentially decaying gradient) or sharing the same remainder when divided by some k from 2 to 10 (same parity, same last digit, both multiples of 7, and so on). They attend to the metric on which the examples are more alike than random numbers would be, and say "yes" in proportion to how similar the target is to the nearest example on that metric — so scattered examples that share a residue (8, 80, 48) are generalised to far numbers with that residue, while examples that share no residue are generalised only to nearby numbers.
