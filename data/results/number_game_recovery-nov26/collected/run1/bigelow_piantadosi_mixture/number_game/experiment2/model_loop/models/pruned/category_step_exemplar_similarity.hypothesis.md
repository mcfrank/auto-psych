People do not infer a concept; they retrieve familiar number categories stored in memory as ordered lists of their members (odd numbers, powers of 2, numbers ending in 6, ranges bounded by multiples of five, ...), each activated by how similar the examples are to its stored members. They then endorse a target by its exemplar similarity to the nearest example measured along each activated category — how far through the category's member list they must step from an example to reach the target — so a member only a step or two away from an example (32 after 16 in the powers of 2) is endorsed readily while one many steps away (96 after a lone 46 in "ends in 6") is endorsed much less.
