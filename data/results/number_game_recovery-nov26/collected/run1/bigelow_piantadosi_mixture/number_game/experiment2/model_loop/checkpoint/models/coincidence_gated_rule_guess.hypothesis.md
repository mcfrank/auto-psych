People do not suspect a rule of any kind — arithmetic ("powers of 2", "multiples of 7", "squares") or written ("ends in 6") — until it is suggested by a coincidence: a rule becomes a salient candidate only once at least two of the examples seen so far satisfy it, and before that its plausibility is reduced by a fitted availability factor, so a lone example is generalised mostly as a numerical range around it. Otherwise they hold a single sticky working guess formed from the first example by Bayesian concept learning, as in the current best model (whose availability penalty applies only to digit rules). The sharpest disagreement lives in single-example sets and in sets where only one example fits an arithmetic rule: after a lone 16 the best model still endorses far-away 64, 4 or 32 as powers of 2, whereas this model predicts these far rule-consistent targets are rejected until a second example such as 8 makes the rule a noticed coincidence.
