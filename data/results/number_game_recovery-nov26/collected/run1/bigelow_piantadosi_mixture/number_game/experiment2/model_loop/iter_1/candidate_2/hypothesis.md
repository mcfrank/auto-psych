People's space of candidate concepts includes not only single rules (odd, multiples of 4, ends in 1, ...) and numerical ranges, but also disjunctions of two familiar rules ("ends in 1 or a square", "even or ends in 5"), which they entertain with a fitted, complexity-penalised prior share. When examples do not all fit one rule, people can explain them with such an "either-or" concept rather than writing off an example as a stray, and then endorse targets that belong to either of the two rules — while still holding a single sticky working guess formed from the first example, as in the current best model.
