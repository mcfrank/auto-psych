People's space of candidate concepts includes not only single rules (odd, multiples of 4, ends in 1, ...) and numerical ranges, but also "either-or" disjunctions of two familiar rules ("a square or ends in 1", "even or ends in 5"), entertained with a fitted, complexity-penalised prior share. When the examples do not all fit one rule, people can explain them with such a two-rule union instead of writing off an example as a stray, and then endorse targets that belong to either rule — while still holding a single sticky working guess formed from the first example, as in the current best model.
