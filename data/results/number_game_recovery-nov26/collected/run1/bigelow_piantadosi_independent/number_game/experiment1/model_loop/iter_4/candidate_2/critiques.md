# Critique of `log_compressed_interval_sampling`

7 of 8 evaluated test statistics show a significant discrepancy (p ≤ 0.05), over 1000 posterior-predictive replicates.

## Significant discrepancies (a better model should address these)

Raw two-sided p shown with a Benjamini-Hochberg FDR-adjusted q across this round's statistics. Prioritise discrepancies that survive the FDR (`q ≤ alpha`); a raw-only hit may be one of several screened at once.
- **outlier_example_neighborhood** — For sets of >=3 examples where exactly one example breaks the majority last digit, yes rate among non-example targets within 3 of that outlier that do not end in the majority digit; observed below null means humans discount the exceptional example (do not generalise around it) more than the model, which places it inside a covering interval, above null means they treat it as a genuine member more. (observed 0.45 vs null mean 0.667, z=-3.61, p=0.004, q=0.00639) [survives FDR]
- **any_shared_digit_far** — Among non-example targets more than 5 from the nearest example, yes rate for targets sharing any digit (ones or tens) with at least one example minus targets sharing none; observed above null means people use digit-level similarity to individual examples more than the model's whole-rule/interval hypotheses predict, below null means less. (observed 0.209 vs null mean 0.149, z=3.53, p=0.002, q=0.00639) [survives FDR]
- **near_gradient_1_vs_2to5** — Among non-example targets, yes rate at distance exactly 1 from the nearest example minus yes rate at distance 2-5; observed below null means humans accept immediate neighbours (which change last digit/parity) less than the interval model predicts, above null means a steeper proximity peak than the model. (observed -0.146 vs null mean -0.0522, z=-3.14, p=0.004, q=0.00639) [survives FDR]
- **above_vs_below_near** — Among non-example targets 1-10 from the nearest example and outside the examples' range, yes rate for targets above the max example minus targets below the min example; observed above null means people still extend upward more than the compressed-number-line model predicts, below null means the model now over-produces upward extension. (observed 0.0462 vs null mean -0.0787, z=3.10, p=0.00599, q=0.00799) [survives FDR]
- **inside_range_far** — Among non-example targets more than 5 from the nearest example, yes rate for targets inside the examples' [min,max] range minus targets outside it; observed above null means people fill in the spanned range more than the compressed-interval model predicts, below null means the model over-produces in-range filling. (observed 0.134 vs null mean 0.0852, z=2.76, p=0.004, q=0.00639) [survives FDR]
- **cell_unanimity** — Fraction of (set, target) cells with >=2 responses in which all responses agree; observed above null means responses are more deterministic/extreme per item than the model's graded posterior predicts, below null means more disagreement. (observed 0.62 vs null mean 0.557, z=2.58, p=0.004, q=0.00639) [survives FDR]
- **set_breadth_spread** — Standard deviation across example sets (with >=20 responses) of each set's mean yes rate; observed above null means sets differ in how broadly people generalise more than the model predicts (some near-zero, others very broad), below null means less. (observed 0.18 vs null mean 0.159, z=2.52, p=0.00999, q=0.0114) [survives FDR]
