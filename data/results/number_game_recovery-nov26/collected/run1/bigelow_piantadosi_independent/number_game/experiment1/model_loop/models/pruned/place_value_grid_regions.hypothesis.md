People do exception-tolerant, tempered Bayesian concept learning over arithmetic rules, but they represent magnitude on a decimal place-value grid (tens digit by ones digit) rather than on a continuous number line: their non-arithmetic concepts are rectangular regions of that grid — a range of tens digits crossed with a range of final digits, such as "the 40s to 90s ending in 2" or "the 20s and 30s" — weighed by the size principle. This disagrees most sharply with the current best (number-line interval) model on targets one step from an example across a decade boundary or a final-digit change (e.g. 26 or 28 for an outlier 27 among 92, 82, 42), which it predicts people reject more, and on distant targets sharing a tens or final digit with the examples, and filling the grid block the examples span, which it predicts people endorse more.
