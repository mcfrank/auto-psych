Refinement of `bigelow_piantadosi_mixture`: people do Bayesian concept learning over the same 45 arithmetic rules and all intervals of 1-100 (same prior), but they do not weigh the examples' evidence as an ideal learner would. The one change is that the size principle has a free strength: the evidence the examples give for each candidate concept is tempered (or sharpened) by a single exponent, so people may favour a broad rule such as "even numbers" over a tight interval around the examples more (or less) than strong sampling predicts — which governs how sharply they reject nearby numbers of the wrong parity and how far they extend a rule to distant numbers.
