# Critique of `graded_profile_with_neighborhoods`

6 of 8 evaluated test statistics show a significant discrepancy (p ≤ 0.05), over 1000 posterior-predictive replicates.

## Significant discrepancies (a better model should address these)

Raw two-sided p shown with a Benjamini-Hochberg FDR-adjusted q across this round's statistics. Prioritise discrepancies that survive the FDR (`q ≤ alpha`); a raw-only hit may be one of several screened at once.
- **above_vs_below_range** — Among non-example targets 1-10 from the nearest example and outside the examples' range, yes rate for targets above the max example minus targets below the min example; observed above null means people extend generalisation upward more than downward relative to the symmetric-distance model, below null means downward bias. (observed 0.0462 vs null mean -0.0857, z=3.16, p=0.004, q=0.0107) [survives FDR]
- **inside_range_far** — Among non-example targets more than 5 from the nearest example, yes rate for targets inside the examples' [min,max] range minus targets outside it; observed above null means people generalise over the interval spanned by the examples more than the model (which has no range/interval property), below null means less. (observed 0.134 vs null mean 0.0849, z=2.79, p=0.004, q=0.0107) [survives FDR]
- **cell_unanimity** — Fraction of (set, target) cells with >=2 responses in which all responses agree; observed above null means responses are more deterministic/extreme (closer to 0 or 1 per item) than the model's graded posterior predicts, below null means more disagreement. (observed 0.62 vs null mean 0.547, z=2.77, p=0.00799, q=0.0128) [survives FDR]
- **set_breadth_spread** — Standard deviation across example sets (with >=20 responses) of each set's mean yes rate; observed above null means sets differ in how broadly people generalise more than the model predicts (some sets near-zero, others very broad), below null means less. (observed 0.18 vs null mean 0.155, z=2.69, p=0.00599, q=0.012) [survives FDR]
- **consistent_last_digit_far** — For sets of >=2 examples that all share one last digit, yes rate among non-example targets more than 5 from every example that end in that same digit; observed above null means people adopt the crisp shared-final-digit rule and extend it to distant numbers more than the model's graded profile predicts, below null means less. (observed 0.978 vs null mean 0.855, z=2.68, p=0.004, q=0.0107) [survives FDR]
- **near_gradient_1_vs_2to5** — Among non-example targets, yes rate at distance exactly 1 from the nearest example minus yes rate at distance 2-5; observed above null means human generalisation is concentrated on immediate neighbours more steeply than the model's fitted neighbourhood width predicts, below null means flatter. (observed -0.146 vs null mean -0.0761, z=-2.39, p=0.014, q=0.0186) [survives FDR]
