People are unsure how the examples they see were chosen: either the program itself produced them (so a concept that fits them tightly is a far better explanation — the size principle), or someone picked numbers at random and the program merely confirmed they belong (so every concept containing the examples explains them equally well), and they weigh the two possibilities by how well each accounts for the examples, otherwise learning the same rules, moderate-width ranges and example-anchored rule-plus-neighbourhood concepts as the best model. This replaces the best model's uniform softening of the size principle with a mixture of two sampling beliefs, and it disagrees with it most sharply on how breadth depends on set size: a single example or a loose set leaves the random-pick belief competitive, so people extend broad rules and wide ranges to distant numbers much more there, whereas three or four examples that fit a narrow concept make the produced-by-the-program belief win, so people tighten generalisation sharply.
