People are unsure how the examples they see were chosen: either the program itself produced them (so a concept that fits them tightly explains them far better, the size principle), or someone picked numbers at random and the program only confirmed that they belong (so every concept containing the examples explains them equally well). They weigh the two possibilities by how well each accounts for the examples, and otherwise learn over the same rules, moderate-width ranges and example-anchored rule-plus-neighbourhood concepts as the best model. This replaces the best model's uniform softening of the size principle with a mixture of two beliefs about sampling, and it disagrees with that model most sharply on how breadth depends on set size: a single example or a loose set leaves the random-pick belief competitive, so people extend broad rules and wide ranges to distant numbers much more, whereas three or four examples that fit a narrow concept let the produced-by-the-program belief win, so people tighten generalisation sharply.
