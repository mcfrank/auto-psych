Refinement of `anchored_neighbourhood_side_channel`: people do the same tempered, exception-tolerant Bayesian concept learning (salient final-digit rules, moderate-width ranges on a compressed number line, and a rule plus a fuzzy neighbourhood around an off-pattern example, emitted through a side channel). The one change is how they judge the size of that exception neighbourhood: they measure it on a compressed mental number line, not on a linear scale. So the same numerical spread feels narrower, and is a priori more plausible, around a large outlier (say 87) than around a small one (say 7), with the degree of compression fitted. As a result people generalise further around large off-pattern examples, up to six steps away, than around small ones, where they stay close to the example itself.
