# Existing hypotheses

Each model below is ONE cognitive hypothesis, with how it stands on the current data by ELPD-LOO (best first). `elpd_diff ± dse` is a model's deficit against the best and the standard error of that difference (with the responses to each stimulus pair counted together, as they are correlated): within about 2·dse the two are statistically tied on this data; beyond it the model has lost. "PSIS-LOO unreliable" means the estimate itself is untrustworthy (too many high-Pareto-k trials), not that the model is bad. Propose a hypothesis that is genuinely different from these, or a refinement of a single one of them — never a combination of several.

## salient_contained_digit_rules  — rank 0, the best model on this data, ELPD-LOO -2598.1

Refinement of `gamma_shaped_range_width_prior`: people do the same tempered, exception-tolerant Bayesian concept learning (salient final-digit rules, ranges on a compressed number line with a gamma-shaped width prior, and rule-plus-neighbourhood exceptions anchored on an off-pattern example and emitted through a side channel), but rules about a digit the numeral contains ("has a 4 in it") are also perceptually salient. They are read straight off the written numerals, so they get their own fitted prior boost instead of starting no more likely than an arbitrary arithmetic rule. The one change is this contained-digit salience: when every example contains a common digit in different places, people extend that digit to distant numbers that contain it (especially numbers in that digit's decade, such as 47 after 34, 43, 74), more than the incumbent predicts (the critique's shared-contained-digit tens statistic).

## gamma_width_range_plus_exception  — rank 1, 18.3 ± 9.2 nats behind the best (2.0× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -2616.5

Refinement of `gamma_shaped_range_width_prior`: people do the same tempered, exception-tolerant Bayesian concept learning (salient final-digit rules, ranges sampled along a compressed number line under a gamma-shaped width prior that can favour tight ranges, and rule-plus-neighbourhood exceptions anchored on an off-pattern example and emitted through a side channel), but the main channel of such a "program plus exception" can also be a range of numbers, not only an arithmetic rule: "numbers from 20 to 35, and now and then something around 87". The one change is this range-plus-exception program (grafted from `range_plus_exception_side_channel`), taking the same share of prior belief, neighbourhood radii and side-channel rate as the rule version. So when most examples cluster on the number line and one lies far away, people fill in the cluster's (possibly tight) stretch and also endorse the outlier's close neighbours, while rejecting the empty gap between them, instead of falling back on one wide range or on a rule.

## gamma_shaped_range_width_prior  — rank 2, 24.6 ± 9.4 nats behind the best (2.6× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -2622.7

Refinement of `anchored_neighbourhood_side_channel`: people do the same tempered, exception-tolerant Bayesian concept learning (the same rules with salient final-digit rules, ranges sampled along a compressed number line, and rule-plus-neighbourhood exceptions anchored on an off-pattern example and emitted through a side channel), but their prior expectation of how wide a "range of numbers" program is does not have the fixed Erlang shape, which makes ranges of one or two numbers unlikely. Instead it is a gamma-shaped width prior whose shape is fitted, so it can favour tight ranges hugging an example while keeping a long tail of broad ones. The one change is this free shape of the range-width prior: if people find tight ranges plausible, they generalise from a lone example steeply to its close neighbours and drop off fast with distance, more than the incumbent allows (the critique's single-example distance gradient), while still extending broad rules to distant numbers.

## conservative_belief_reporting  — rank 3, 26.1 ± 9.8 nats behind the best (2.7× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -2624.3

People infer the program's concept just as the best Bayesian concept learner does, but they report their belief conservatively: when deciding whether to answer "yes", they hedge toward fifty-fifty, so their odds of saying yes are a compressed (less extreme) version of their belief's odds, by a fitted degree of conservatism. Targets they are fairly but not fully sure belong (or do not belong) are therefore endorsed (or rejected) less consistently than their belief implies, with no lapses and no overall lean toward yes or no.

## pattern_violation_anchored_exception  — rank 4, 27.7 ± 9.6 nats behind the best (2.9× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -2625.8

People read the examples in the order shown and only treat an example as an exception when it breaks a pattern that earlier examples have already set up: they do the same tempered, exception-tolerant Bayesian concept learning as the best model (salient final-digit rules, moderate-width ranges on a compressed number line, and "rule plus a fuzzy neighbourhood around an off-pattern example" programs emitted through a side channel), but the exception neighbourhood can be centred only on an off-rule example that comes after at least one example the rule covers. So an off-pattern example shown first, before any pattern exists, is never singled out as the program's exception, and people generalise to its close neighbours less than they would if the same outlier came later in the list.

## even_leaning_rule_prior  — rank 7, 39.3 ± 10.9 nats behind the best (3.6× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -2637.4

People expect a number-producing program to be "about" even numbers more readily than about odd ones: in their prior over candidate rules, a rule is more plausible the larger the share of its members that are even (so "even", "multiples of 4", "powers of 2", "ends in 6" start ahead of "odd", "ends in 7", "powers of 3" or "primes"), while otherwise doing the same tempered, exception-tolerant Bayesian concept learning as the best model (salient final-digit rules, moderate-width ranges on a compressed number line, and rule-plus-neighbourhood exceptions anchored on an off-pattern example and emitted through a side channel). Because ranges are parity-neutral, this even-leaning rule prior shows up wherever the examples leave both parities open — most clearly in sets mixing odd and even examples, where even-based rule-plus-exception concepts win and people endorse even non-example targets more than odd ones.

## anchored_neighbourhood_side_channel  — rank 8, 40.8 ± 10.1 nats behind the best (4.0× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -2638.9

Refinement of `rule_with_exception_side_channel`: people do the same tempered, exception-tolerant Bayesian concept learning over the same 45 rules (final-digit rules salient) and moderate-width ranges on a compressed number line, and they picture a "rule plus exception" program the same way — a main rule channel that, at a fitted rate, emits a member of its exception instead — but the exception is a small fuzzy neighbourhood (radius zero to three, smaller radii a priori likelier) centred on an example the rule fails to cover, rather than an arbitrary single number or a whole decade. The one change is this example-anchored neighbourhood as the side channel's exception (grafted from `example_anchored_exception_neighbourhood`): because a wider neighbourhood only dilutes the off-pattern example's own likelihood, not the rule-following examples', people readily posit a broad region around the outlier and endorse its close neighbours (falling off with distance) much more than off-rule neighbours of the majority examples — more strongly than when rule and neighbourhood are sampled as one pooled set.

## pedagogical_teacher_sampling  — rank 9, 40.9 ± 11.0 nats behind the best (3.7× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -2639.1

People assume the examples were picked by a helpful teacher who wanted them to guess the program, not drawn at random from it (pedagogical sampling): when they ask how well a candidate concept explains the examples, they expect a teacher of that concept to have chosen numbers that point unambiguously to it and to avoid numbers that also belong to many other concepts (such as 16 or 64, which are powers, squares and even). So an ambiguous "special" number among the examples counts as strong evidence for the rule it is special for and as weak evidence for a mere range around it, while unremarkable numbers favour ranges — which changes how broadly people generalise from each set, in a way no random-sampling learner can express.

## even_salient_pedagogical_teacher  — rank 10, 42.4 ± 11.8 nats behind the best (3.6× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -2640.5

Refinement of `pedagogical_teacher_sampling`: people do the same helpful-teacher concept learning (they expect a teacher to pick unambiguous examples) over the same rules, ranges and example-anchored rule-plus-neighbourhood exceptions, but "even numbers" is a more natural, more readily entertained concept than "odd numbers", so the even rule gets its own fitted prior boost on top of the shared parity/final-digit salience. The one change is this even-over-odd asymmetry in the rule prior: when a set mixes odd and even examples people lean towards "even, with an exception" and endorse even non-example targets more than odd ones (the critique's mixed-parity even-target bias), and they extend all-even sets to distant evens more readily than all-odd sets to distant odds.

## weak_sampling_doubt  — rank 11, 42.5 ± 11.0 nats behind the best (3.9× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -2640.6

People reason like the best Bayesian concept learner (tempered, exception-tolerant inference over salient final-digit and other rules, moderate-width ranges on a compressed number line, and rule-plus-neighbourhood exceptions around an off-pattern example emitted through a side channel), with one distortion: they are not fully convinced the examples were produced by the program itself, and give some fitted weight to the possibility that the numbers were picked arbitrarily and merely confirmed to belong. Under that "picked, not produced" reading, a concept's size carries no evidential cost, so people discount the size principle — most when the examples fit both small and large concepts — and generalise more broadly than an ideal strong-sampling learner, extending to large rules, wide ranges and wide regions around an outlier example.

## anchored_side_channel_neighbourhood  — rank 12, 42.8 ± 10.3 nats behind the best (4.2× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -2640.9

## parity_markedness_odd_cost  — rank 13, 42.8 ± 11.0 nats behind the best (3.9× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -2641.0

People code parity asymmetrically: "even" (divisible by two) is the default, unmarked concept, while "odd" is represented as its negation ("not divisible by two"), so considering it carries an extra cognitive cost that lowers its prior plausibility; otherwise they infer the program's concept as the best tempered, exception-tolerant Bayesian learner does (salient final-digit rules, moderate-width ranges, rule-plus-neighbourhood exceptions around an off-pattern example emitted through a side channel). Because of this cost, when a set mixes odd and even examples, "even, plus an exception around the odd one" beats "odd, plus an exception around the even one", so people endorse even targets more than odd ones, and they extend all-odd sets to distant odd numbers less readily than all-even sets to distant even numbers.

## rule_with_exception_side_channel  — rank 18, 60.7 ± 10.8 nats behind the best (5.6× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -2658.9

## nearest_example_radius_concepts  — rank 21, 494.3 ± 37.6 nats behind the best (13.1× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -3092.5

People's magnitude-based guesses about the program are similarity neighbourhoods around the examples, not stretches of the number line: besides arithmetic and digit rules (and rule-plus-exception concepts), the only numerical concept they consider is "numbers within some distance of one of the examples I saw", with smaller similarity radii a priori likelier, weighed by the same tempered, exception-tolerant size principle. So endorsement of a non-rule target falls off with its numerical distance to the nearest example, whatever lies between the examples: an empty gap between two distant examples is not filled in, while each example's own close neighbours are endorsed even when they lie outside the span of the set.

## isolation_weighted_exemplar_memory  — rank 22, 818.8 ± 50.1 nats behind the best (16.4× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -3417.0

People do not infer a concept: they store each example as a separate exemplar and say yes in proportion to the target's average similarity to the stored examples, where similarity falls off with distance on a compressed mental number line and with mismatches in final digit and in parity. Exemplars are not stored equally strongly — an example that is isolated, sharing its final digit with none of the other examples (the one odd-one-out in the set), is remembered more vividly (a von Restorff isolation effect), so it carries more weight in the comparison and people generalise to its neighbours more than to equally close neighbours of the conforming examples.

## salient_repdigit_concept  — no comparison row

People do the same tempered, exception-tolerant Bayesian concept learning as the best model (salient final-digit and contained-digit rules, ranges on a compressed number line, and rule-plus-neighbourhood exceptions around an off-pattern example), but their space of concepts also includes "numbers written with a repeated digit" (11, 22, 33, … 99). They read this visually striking pattern straight off the numerals and find it far more plausible a priori than an arbitrary arithmetic rule. So when the examples include doubled-digit numbers, people extend to other repdigits far away (such as 44 or 77) and endorse near neighbours that break the doubling less than the best model predicts.

## independent_feature_prototype  — no comparison row

People do not search a space of rules or ranges; they summarise the examples as a single category prototype — a central tendency with a spread on a compressed mental number line, plus how often each final digit and each parity occurred among the examples (shrunk toward an even spread by prior uncertainty) — and say "yes" in proportion to how typical the target is of that prototype relative to a number picked at random. Each feature is judged independently, so a target is endorsed if it lies within the examples' magnitude spread and shares their dominant parity and final digit, and the prototype tightens smoothly as more consistent examples accumulate.

## generative_production_odds_judgement  — no comparison row

People answer not by asking whether the target belongs to the concept they inferred, but by imagining the program running and judging how likely it is to produce that very number compared with a number picked at random; the larger this production-to-chance ratio, the more likely they say yes (their concept inference is the same tempered, exception-tolerant Bayesian learning as the best model). So a target from a small concept (a tight range around the examples, a sparse rule, the neighbourhood of an off-pattern example) is endorsed more than an equally certain member of a broad concept such as "even numbers", and people extend broad rules to distant numbers less than their membership belief alone implies.

## salient_decade_place_value_rules  — no comparison row

Refinement of `salient_contained_digit_rules`: people do the same tempered, exception-tolerant Bayesian concept learning (salient final-digit and contained-digit rules, ranges on a compressed number line with a gamma-shaped width prior, and rule-plus-neighbourhood exceptions anchored on an off-pattern example and emitted through a side channel), but they also read the tens digit of a numeral as a place-value category: "numbers in the forties" (tens digit 4) is a rule they entertain directly, with its own fitted salience, not only as one range among the thousands of possible stretches of the number line. The one change is this set of salient decade rules: when the examples share a tens digit (e.g. 42, 47, 45), people fill in exactly that decade, endorsing its members, even those far from any example, and rejecting numbers just across the decade boundary (39, 50) more sharply than a learner whose only magnitude concepts are ranges with arbitrary edges.

## individual_rule_vs_range_style  — no comparison row

Refinement of `salient_contained_digit_rules`: people do the same tempered, exception-tolerant Bayesian concept learning (salient final-digit and contained-digit rules, ranges on a compressed number line with a gamma-shaped width prior, and rule-plus-neighbourhood exceptions emitted through a side channel), but individuals differ stably in how readily they think of the hidden program as an arithmetic or digit rule rather than a range of magnitudes.
