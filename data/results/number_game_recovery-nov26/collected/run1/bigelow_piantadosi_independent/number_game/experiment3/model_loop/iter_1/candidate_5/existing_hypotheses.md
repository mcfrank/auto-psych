# Existing hypotheses

Each model below is ONE cognitive hypothesis, with how it stands on the current data by ELPD-LOO (best first). `elpd_diff ± dse` is a model's deficit against the best and the standard error of that difference (with the responses to each stimulus pair counted together, as they are correlated): within about 2·dse the two are statistically tied on this data; beyond it the model has lost. "PSIS-LOO unreliable" means the estimate itself is untrustworthy (too many high-Pareto-k trials), not that the model is bad. Propose a hypothesis that is genuinely different from these, or a refinement of a single one of them — never a combination of several.

## anchored_neighbourhood_side_channel  — rank 0, the best model on this data, ELPD-LOO -2638.9

Refinement of `rule_with_exception_side_channel`: people do the same tempered, exception-tolerant Bayesian concept learning over the same 45 rules (final-digit rules salient) and moderate-width ranges on a compressed number line, and they picture a "rule plus exception" program the same way — a main rule channel that, at a fitted rate, emits a member of its exception instead — but the exception is a small fuzzy neighbourhood (radius zero to three, smaller radii a priori likelier) centred on an example the rule fails to cover, rather than an arbitrary single number or a whole decade. The one change is this example-anchored neighbourhood as the side channel's exception (grafted from `example_anchored_exception_neighbourhood`): because a wider neighbourhood only dilutes the off-pattern example's own likelihood, not the rule-following examples', people readily posit a broad region around the outlier and endorse its close neighbours (falling off with distance) much more than off-rule neighbours of the majority examples — more strongly than when rule and neighbourhood are sampled as one pooled set.

## anchored_side_channel_neighbourhood  — rank 1, 2.0 ± 0.9 nats behind the best (2.3× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -2640.9

Refinement of example_anchored_exception_neighbourhood: people do the same tempered, exception-tolerant Bayesian concept learning over the same 45 rules (final-digit rules salient), moderate-width ranges on a compressed number line, and "rule plus a small fuzzy neighbourhood" concepts whose exception region is centred only on a shown example the rule fails to cover, but they picture such a program as a main rule with an occasional side-output: most of the time it emits a rule member and, at a fitted rate, a number from the exception neighbourhood, rather than drawing evenly from rule and neighbourhood pooled together (the two-channel sampling grafted from rule_with_exception_side_channel). ## example_anchored_exception_neighbourhood  — rank 2, 3.3 ± 1.3 nats behind the best (2.6× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -2642.2

Refinement of `rule_plus_exception_neighbourhood`: people do the same tempered, exception-tolerant Bayesian concept learning over the same 45 rules (final-digit rules salient), moderate-width ranges on a compressed number line, and "rule plus a small fuzzy neighbourhood" concepts (radius zero to three, smaller radii a priori likelier), but they build those exceptions bottom-up from what they saw: a rule's exception region is only ever centred on an example that the rule fails to cover ("ends in 2, and also numbers around 27" because 27 was shown), never at an arbitrary place on the number line, and these anchored variants share a fitted part of the rule's prior. The one change is this example-anchored proposal of exceptions, replacing the incumbent's uniform prior over every possible exception location: when one example breaks the others' pattern, the rule-plus-neighbourhood account gets far more belief and is centred squarely on the outlier, so people endorse the outlier's close neighbours, falling off with distance from it, much more than mere neighbours of the majority examples that break the rule (closing the critique's outlier-versus-majority neighbour contrast).

## rule_with_exception_side_channel  — rank 5, 20.0 ± 4.3 nats behind the best (4.7× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -2658.9

Refinement of `rule_plus_decade_exception`: people do the same tempered, exception-tolerant Bayesian concept learning over the same 45 rules (final-digit rules salient), moderate-width ranges on a compressed number line, and "rule plus exception" concepts (a rule plus one extra number, or plus that number's whole decade), but they believe such a program works as a main rule with an occasional side-output: most of the time it emits a rule member, and now and then (at a fitted rate) a member of its exception, instead of drawing uniformly from rule and exception pooled together. The one change is this two-channel sampling of exception concepts: a larger exception (a whole decade) no longer weakens the evidence the rule-following examples give for the rule, so when several examples share a rule and one breaks it, people readily credit the off-pattern example's whole decade and endorse its neighbours far more than its mere proximity to other examples would warrant, while still extending the rule to distant numbers and rejecting rule-violating numbers next to the majority examples.

## rule_plus_exception_neighbourhood  — rank 6, 27.2 ± 4.9 nats behind the best (5.6× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -2666.1

Refinement of `rule_plus_exception_concepts`: people do the same tempered, exception-tolerant Bayesian concept learning over the same 45 rules (final-digit rules salient), moderate-width ranges on a compressed number line, and "rule plus exception" concepts, but the exception they add to a rule is not a single isolated number — it is a small fuzzy region of the number line around it ("ends in 2, and also numbers around 27"), whose radius (zero, one, two … up to three steps) is a priori more likely the smaller it is, with the fall-off fitted. The one change is this neighbourhood-sized exception: when one example breaks the others' pattern (for instance, the one odd number among even ones), people still endorse it and still extend the rule to distant numbers, but they also endorse the off-pattern example's close neighbours more than the point-exception model predicts, as the critique reports.

## seen_final_digits_decade_exception  — rank 7, 41.9 ± 10.1 nats behind the best (4.1× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -2680.8

Refinement of `rule_plus_decade_exception`: people do the same tempered, exception-tolerant Bayesian concept learning over the same 45 rules (final-digit rules salient), moderate-width ranges on a compressed number line, and "rule plus exception" concepts (one extra number or its whole decade), but when the examples end in two to four different digits they also read a disjunctive final-digit rule straight off them — "ends in one of the digits I saw" (for 33, 17, 5, 9: ends in 3, 5, 7 or 9) — an example-cued concept as salient as a single final-digit rule, its prior scaled by a fitted factor. The one change is this seen-final-digits rule: because it is smaller than parity, the size principle favours it over "odd" or "even", so people extend the examples' endings to distant numbers while rejecting same-parity numbers whose final digit was never shown (e.g. 61, 71, 81 after 33, 17, 5, 9).

## isolation_weighted_exemplar_memory  — rank 8, 778.1 ± 51.7 nats behind the best (15.0× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -3417.0

People do not infer a concept: they store each example as a separate exemplar and say yes in proportion to the target's average similarity to the stored examples, where similarity falls off with distance on a compressed mental number line and with mismatches in final digit and in parity. Exemplars are not stored equally strongly — an example that is isolated, sharing its final digit with none of the other examples (the one odd-one-out in the set), is remembered more vividly (a von Restorff isolation effect), so it carries more weight in the comparison and people generalise to its neighbours more than to equally close neighbours of the conforming examples.
