Refinement of `erlang_width_compressed_intervals`: people do the same exception-tolerant, tempered Bayesian concept learning over the same 45 rules and all ranges of 1-100 (members sampled along a compressed number line, Erlang-shaped width prior), but they do not find every kind of rule equally plausible beforehand — whole families of rules differ in salience (parity, final-digit, contained-digit, multiples, powers, and special sets such as primes or squares), with the share of prior belief each family receives fitted and split equally among its members. The one change is this family-salience prior over rules, replacing the flat one: if parity and final-digit rules are especially salient, people extend a shared final digit to distant targets almost unanimously and reject opposite-parity neighbours of same-parity examples more firmly than the incumbent predicts.
