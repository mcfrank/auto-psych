Refinement of `rule_plus_exception_neighbourhood`: people consider the same "rule plus a small fuzzy exception region" concepts ("ends in 2, and also numbers around 27") alongside the same rules and ranges, learned by the same tempered, exception-tolerant Bayesian inference. What changes is how they picture such a program producing numbers. It is not drawing evenly from the rule and the region pooled together. It is a two-part program: it mostly outputs rule members and, a fitted share of the time, outputs a number from the exception region. The one change is this two-part sampling. A wider exception region therefore makes only the off-pattern example less expected, not the rule-following ones, so people endorse the off-pattern example's close neighbours (of either parity) much more than numbers that are merely close to a majority example, as the critique reports, while still extending the rule to distant numbers.
