People infer the program's concept the way an exception-tolerant, tempered Bayesian learner does (arithmetic and digit rules with salient final-digit rules, moderate-width ranges on a compressed number line), but they hold the number asked about only approximately: with a fitted probability they judge an adjacent number (one above or one below) instead of the target itself. Under this approximate-number confusion, a target next to any example "borrows" that example's endorsement, whatever its parity or rule membership. So people endorse the immediate neighbours of examples, including the off-pattern example's neighbours, more than concept membership implies, and a number sitting just beside a rule the examples share is endorsed more than distant numbers that break it.
