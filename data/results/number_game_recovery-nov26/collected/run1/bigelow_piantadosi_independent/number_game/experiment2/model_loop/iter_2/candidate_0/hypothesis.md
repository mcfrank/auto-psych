People do not infer one concept that explains all the examples together. They keep each example in memory as a separate exemplar and compare the target with each one in turn, judging how likely the two numbers are to fall in a common consequential region — a shared arithmetic or digit rule, or a stretch of the number line of moderate width, where smaller shared regions count for more — and their yes probability is the average of these pairwise similarities across the stored examples. Because every example generalises on its own and no example is ever discounted as an exception, people endorse numbers around an off-pattern example (e.g. neighbours of the one odd number in an even set) far more than a joint concept learner predicts, and more examples do not tighten generalisation.
