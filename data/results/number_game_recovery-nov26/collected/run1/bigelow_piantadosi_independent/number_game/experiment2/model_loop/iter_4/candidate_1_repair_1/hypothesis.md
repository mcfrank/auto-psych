People do not weigh all the examples at once; they hold one working guess about the concept and revise it example by example in the order shown (win-stay, lose-shift). After the first example they pick a concept (a rule or a moderate-width range) in proportion to how plausible it is; when the next example fits their current guess they keep it unexamined, and only when an example contradicts it do they (usually, at a fitted rate) drop it and re-pick a concept in light of all the examples seen so far. So early examples lock in broad concepts that later consistent examples never tighten, and the same examples in a different order lead to different generalisations: an off-pattern example forces a revision only if it arrives after a guess it contradicts.
