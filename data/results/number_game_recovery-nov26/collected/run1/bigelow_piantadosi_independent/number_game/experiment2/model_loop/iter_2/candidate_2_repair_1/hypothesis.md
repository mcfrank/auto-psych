People do the same exception-tolerant, tempered Bayesian concept learning over the same 45 rules (final-digit rules salient) and moderate-width ranges on a compressed number line, but they also notice regular spacing: their concept vocabulary contains "every m-th number" progressions that do not start at zero ("5, 11, 17, … — numbers that leave remainder 5 when divided by 6", for steps 3 to 12), which share a fitted part of the rules' prior. The one change is these equal-step progression concepts: when the examples are evenly spaced off the multiples (e.g. 53, 47, 41 or 34, 26 or 15, 11), people endorse distant numbers on the same step (35, 59, 65; 18, 42, 50) and reject the in-between same-parity numbers far more than the best model, which can only explain such sets by a range or a parity rule.
