People believe the hidden program may draw its outputs from more than one source, so they spontaneously split the examples into groups: when one example does not fit the others they can attribute it to a second, separate source (with a fitted prior readiness to posit such a split, weighed by how much better two sources explain the examples than one), and they infer a concept for each source — rules or numerical ranges, by the same exception-tolerant, tempered Bayesian concept learning with salient final-digit rules on a compressed number line. A target is endorsed if either source would produce it, so people generalise around an off-pattern example in its own right (saying yes to its numerical neighbours and its own rules) while still extending the majority's rule to distant numbers.
