People do exception-tolerant, tempered Bayesian concept learning over arithmetic and digit rules (final-digit rules salient) and moderate-width ranges on a compressed number line, and they can explain an example that breaks a rule's pattern as a "rule plus an exceptional patch" concept — but they picture that patch the way they picture any stretch of the number line: as a region of moderate width, not as a lone point. Their expectations about the patch's width are Erlang-shaped (tiny patches and isolated single numbers are a priori unlikely, with the typical width fitted), so when one example breaks the others' pattern people endorse its numerical neighbours about as readily as the off-pattern example itself, while still rejecting rule-violating neighbours of the majority examples and extending the rule to distant numbers.
