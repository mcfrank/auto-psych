People generate "rule plus exception" concepts bottom-up from the examples rather than holding them in their vocabulary in advance: while checking a candidate rule against the examples, an example that the rule fails to cover captures attention, and the learner then entertains the rule together with a small fuzzy region around that particular noticed example ("ends in 2, and also around 27"), giving such example-cued exception concepts a fitted, substantial share of the rule's belief instead of the tiny share an exception at an arbitrary number would get. Otherwise they do the same tempered, exception-tolerant Bayesian concept learning over the same rules (final-digit rules salient) and moderate-width ranges on a compressed number line, so when one example breaks the others' pattern people endorse its close neighbours of either parity much more than they endorse equally close rule-violating neighbours of the conforming examples, while for sets that all fit a rule no exception concepts arise at all.
