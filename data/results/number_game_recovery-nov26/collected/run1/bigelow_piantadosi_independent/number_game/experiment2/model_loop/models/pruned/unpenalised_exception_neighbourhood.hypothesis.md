People infer the hidden program's concept the way a tempered, exception-tolerant Bayesian learner does (the same 45 rules with salient final-digit rules, moderate-width ranges on a compressed number line, and "rule plus a fuzzy neighbourhood exception" concepts), with one distortion: they apply the size principle only to the regular part of a concept and not to its exception. When one example breaks the others' pattern, they explain it with a fuzzy region around it but do not count the region's other numbers against how well the concept explains the examples. As a result they generalise freely to the off-pattern example's close neighbours, nearly as much as to the example itself, while still rejecting rule-violating neighbours of the majority examples and extending the rule to distant numbers.
