Refinement of `threshold_concepts_erlang_interval`: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over number rules, bounded numeric ranges (with an Erlang prior on width) and one-sided magnitude thresholds (their own family), with a prior mass per concept family, but they do not apply the "suspicious coincidence" of the size principle to magnitude concepts as strongly as to arithmetic and digit rules. The one change is that, for ranges and thresholds only, how much an example's probability shrinks with the concept's width is governed by a learned exponent (one recovers the incumbent, zero means ranges are treated as mere filters the examples pass), so how tightly people's magnitude-based generalisation hugs the examples, relative to rule-based generalisation, is inferred from the data rather than fixed by the ideal learner.
