Refinement of `without_replacement_size_principle`, adding one component from `digit_sum_concepts_threshold_erlang`: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over number rules, bounded ranges (with an Erlang prior on width) and one-sided thresholds, with a prior mass per concept family and examples believed to be drawn from the concept without replacement, but their space of candidate programs also includes digit-sum concepts — "numbers whose digits add up to s" (e.g. 5, 14, 23, 32, 41, 50) — as a new family with its own inferred prior mass, shared equally among the 18 possible sums. The one change is this digit-sum family: because these concepts are tiny, examples that happen to share a digit sum are an especially suspicious coincidence when drawn without replacement, so people endorse other numbers with that digit sum, and reject neighbours with a different sum, more than the incumbent predicts.
