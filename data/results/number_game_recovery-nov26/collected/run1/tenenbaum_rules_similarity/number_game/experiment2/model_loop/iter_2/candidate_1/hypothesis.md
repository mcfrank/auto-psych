People assume the program's examples were chosen to be helpful, as a teacher would choose them, rather than drawn at random from its concept (rational pedagogy): a teacher holding a concept picks examples in proportion to how strongly each one, on its own, would lead a learner to that concept. People therefore judge each candidate rule or range by how likely such a teacher would be to have shown exactly these examples, so examples that would point equally well to many other concepts count as weak evidence, and a concept is favoured when the examples are the ones most diagnostic of it; they then say "yes" with the probability that the inferred concept contains the target.
