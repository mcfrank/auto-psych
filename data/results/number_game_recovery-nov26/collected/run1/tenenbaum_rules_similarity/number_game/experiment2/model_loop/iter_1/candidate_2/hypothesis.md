Refinement of `threshold_concepts_erlang_interval`: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over number rules, bounded numeric ranges (with an Erlang prior on width) and one-sided thresholds, with the size principle and a prior mass per concept family. The one change is that their space of candidate programs also includes digit-sum concepts — "numbers whose digits add up to s" (e.g. 5, 14, 23, 32, 41, 50 for s = 5) — as a new family with its own inferred prior mass, shared equally among the 18 possible sums. When the examples happen to share a digit sum (especially a single example with a small, rare sum), people endorse other numbers with that digit sum more, and neighbours with a different digit sum less, than the incumbent predicts.
