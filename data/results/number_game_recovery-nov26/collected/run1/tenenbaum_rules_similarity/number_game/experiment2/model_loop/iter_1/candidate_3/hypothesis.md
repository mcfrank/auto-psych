Refinement of `threshold_concepts_erlang_interval`: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over number rules, bounded numeric ranges (with an Erlang prior on width) and one-sided magnitude thresholds (their own family), with the size principle and a prior mass per concept family, but they do not treat "prime numbers" as just one more member of a shared "special sets" family with squares, cubes and powers. The one change is that primes form their own concept family with a separately inferred prior mass, so examples such as 3, 7 or 53, 2 can be read as "primes" strongly enough that people endorse other primes (13, 29, 5, 11) and reject nearby composites, independently of how readily they read 81, 25 as "squares" or 8, 64 as "powers".
