Refinement of `round_number_range_boundaries`, adding one component from `digit_sum_concepts_threshold_erlang`: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over number rules, bounded ranges (Erlang width prior, with a learned round-number bias on ranges whose boundaries fall on multiples of ten) and one-sided thresholds, with a prior mass per concept family and examples drawn without replacement. The one change is that their space of candidate programs also includes digit-sum concepts — "numbers whose digits add up to s" (e.g. 5, 14, 23, 32, 41, 50) — as a new family with its own inferred prior mass, shared equally among the 18 possible sums. Because these concepts are tiny, examples that share a digit sum are a suspicious coincidence, so people endorse other numbers with that sum and reject neighbours with a different sum more than the incumbent predicts, while the round-number bias still shapes where magnitude-based "yes" answers stop.
