People do not infer a program; they judge a target by its similarity to the examples, where two numbers are similar to the degree that they fall together in familiar number categories (parity, multiples, final or contained digit, primes, squares, powers, magnitude ranges and thresholds), small distinctive shared categories counting more than broad ones. The similarities are combined conjunctively, as a geometric mean over the examples, so a target must resemble every example: being very dissimilar to any single example drags "yes" down sharply, unlike an average in which closeness to one example is enough. The willingness to say "yes" rises smoothly (logistically) with this geometric-mean similarity on a log scale.
