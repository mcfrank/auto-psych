People do Bayesian concept learning over number rules and one-sided thresholds as in the best model, but they do not represent a magnitude-based program as a sharp-edged range; they represent it as a graded prototype — a typical value on the number line with a spread — that produces numbers mostly near its centre and fewer the further away they lie. A target then belongs to such a concept to the degree it is close to the prototype's centre, so magnitude generalisation falls off smoothly with distance from the examples instead of dropping at a boundary.
