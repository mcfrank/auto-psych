People do not search the whole space of possible programs on every trial: each kind of program (parity, special sets such as primes, squares and powers, multiples, final digit, contained digit, exception rules, bounded ranges, one-sided thresholds) comes to mind only with its own probability, and people do Bayesian concept learning with the size principle only over the kinds that came to mind, giving each considered kind an equal share of their prior. When the kind of program that best fits the examples is not thought of, they fall back on whatever kind they did consider, so "yes" is the average, over which kinds came to mind, of the probability that the inferred program produces the target. How available each kind is to recall replaces a fixed prior plausibility per kind.
