Refinement of `without_replacement_primes_family`, adding one component from `digit_sum_concepts_threshold_erlang`: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over number rules (with "prime numbers" as its own family), bounded ranges (with an Erlang prior on width) and one-sided thresholds, with a prior mass per concept family, and they believe the program never repeats itself, so examples are drawn from the concept without replacement. The one change is that their space of candidate programs also includes digit-sum concepts — "numbers whose digits add up to s" (e.g. 5, 14, 23, 32, 41, 50) — as a new family with its own inferred prior mass, shared equally among the 18 possible sums. When the examples happen to share a digit sum, people endorse other numbers with that sum and reject neighbours with a different sum more than the parent predicts, and this works independently of how readily they read prime-like sets as "primes".
