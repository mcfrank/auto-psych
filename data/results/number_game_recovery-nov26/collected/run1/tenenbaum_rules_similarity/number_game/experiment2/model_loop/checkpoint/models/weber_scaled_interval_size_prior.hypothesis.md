Refinement of `erlang_interval_size_family_prior`: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over rules and numeric intervals, with the size principle, a prior mass per concept family (exception rules in their own family) and an Erlang prior over interval size, but they judge how big a range is relative to its magnitude (Weber's law) rather than in absolute units. The one change is that the Erlang prior is placed on the interval's width divided by a learned power of its location on the number line, so a range of ten numbers around 85 counts as "narrower" than one around 8; people therefore generalise to wider bands of nearby numbers after large examples than after small ones, with the strength of this magnitude scaling inferred from the data (zero recovers the incumbent).
