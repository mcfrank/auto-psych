Refinement of `powers_own_family_erlang_interval`: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over rules and numeric intervals, with the size principle, a prior mass per concept family (exception rules and powers of m each in their own family) and an Erlang prior over interval width, but they do not treat "prime numbers" as the same kind of program as squares and cubes. The one change is that primes form their own concept family with a separately inferred prior mass, so how readily people read examples such as 3, 7 or 53, 2 as "primes" (endorsing 29 or 59 and rejecting nearby composites) is no longer tied to how readily they read 81, 25 as "squares".
