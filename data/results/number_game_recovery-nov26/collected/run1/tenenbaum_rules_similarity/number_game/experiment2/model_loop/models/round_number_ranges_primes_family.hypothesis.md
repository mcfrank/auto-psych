Refinement of `round_number_range_boundaries`, adding one component from `without_replacement_primes_family`: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over number rules, bounded ranges (Erlang width prior, with a learned round-number bias on ranges whose boundaries fall on multiples of ten) and one-sided thresholds, with a prior mass per concept family and examples drawn without replacement. The one change is that "prime numbers" is no longer lumped with squares, cubes and powers in a shared special-sets family but forms its own family with a separately inferred prior mass, so prime-like sets (e.g. 3, 7 or 53, 2) are read as "primes" — people endorse other primes and reject nearby composites — independently of how plausible squares or powers are, while the round-number bias still shapes where magnitude-based "yes" answers stop.
