Refinement of `exception_rule_family_prior_2`: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over rules (with a prior mass per concept family, the two exception rules in their own family) and numeric intervals with the size principle, but they do not treat every interval of 1-100 as equally likely a priori — they expect a magnitude-based concept to span a characteristic, moderate range, so their prior over intervals follows an Erlang distribution over interval width with an inferred typical size (Tenenbaum's interval prior). The one change is this size-dependent interval prior: it penalises both the hairline intervals that hug the examples and the very broad ones, which changes how far people generalise by magnitude beyond the examples (e.g. to targets 10-20 away from a single example) relative to the incumbent's flat interval prior.
