Refinement of `exception_rule_family_prior_2`: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over rules and intervals, with the size principle and a separate prior mass per concept family (the exception rules in their own family), but they do not regard "powers of a base" (2, 4, 8, 16…; 3, 9, 27…; 8, 64) as the same kind of concept as the classic named sets primes, squares and cubes. The one change is splitting the "special sets" family in two — power sequences and the named sets primes/squares/cubes — each with its own inferred prior mass, so people can readily generalise along a power sequence while finding a "primes" (or "squares") explanation less natural, which mainly lowers endorsement of other primes after examples such as 2, 3, 5, 7 or 11, 13, 17.
