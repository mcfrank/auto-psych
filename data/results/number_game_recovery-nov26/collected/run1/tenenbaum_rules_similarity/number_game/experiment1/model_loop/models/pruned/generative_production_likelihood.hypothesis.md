People do not answer by asking whether the target belongs to the concept they have inferred; they answer by how readily they can imagine the program actually producing that target next — the probability of the target as the program's next output, which is high for targets in small, tight concepts (a lone powers-of-2 value, a narrow interval around clustered examples) and low for targets that belong only to broad concepts (all even numbers, a wide interval) even when membership is certain. Their yes rate rises smoothly with this predicted production probability, relative to what a program emitting numbers at random would give.
