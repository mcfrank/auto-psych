# Critique of `bigelow_piantadosi_mixture`

7 of 8 evaluated test statistics show a significant discrepancy (p ≤ 0.05), over 1000 posterior-predictive replicates.

## Significant discrepancies (a better model should address these)

Raw two-sided p shown with a Benjamini-Hochberg FDR-adjusted q across this round's statistics. Prioritise discrepancies that survive the FDR (`q ≤ alpha`); a raw-only hit may be one of several screened at once.
- **near_example_yes_rate** — Yes rate for targets within 1-3 of some example but not equal to any example; observed below the null mean means the model over-produces "yes" for numerically adjacent targets (too much interval mass), above means it under-produces similarity-based yes. (observed 0.235 vs null mean 0.437, z=-10.50, p=0.002, q=0.00266) [survives FDR]
- **parity_mismatch_yes_rate** — Yes rate for targets whose parity differs from that of every example, among sets whose examples all share one parity; observed above the null mean means the model under-produces "yes" for parity-violating targets (too sharp an odd/even rule). (observed 0.179 vs null mean 0.32, z=-8.89, p=0.002, q=0.00266) [survives FDR]
- **shared_last_digit_set_offdigit_yes** — In sets of >=2 examples that all end in the same digit, the yes rate for targets that do NOT end in that digit; observed above the null mean means the model's "ends in d" rule is too sharp and under-produces yes for non-matching targets. (observed 0.05 vs null mean 0.159, z=-7.23, p=0.002, q=0.00266) [survives FDR]
- **parity_match_gap** — Among sets of >=2 examples all of one parity, yes rate for same-parity targets minus yes rate for opposite-parity targets; observed below the null mean means the model exaggerates how much humans generalise along parity. (observed 0.159 vs null mean 0.0214, z=6.94, p=0.002, q=0.00266) [survives FDR]
- **trial_index_slope** — OLS slope of rating on trial_index scaled per 100 trials; a nonzero observed slope far from the null (which should be ~0 since the model has no time dependence) means humans' yes rate drifts over the session in a way the model cannot produce. (observed -0.0026 vs null mean -0.0285, z=3.74, p=0.002, q=0.00266) [survives FDR]
- **out_of_range_minus_in_range** — Yes rate for targets outside [min, max] of the examples minus yes rate for targets inside that range (sets with >=2 examples); observed above the null mean means the model over-weights interval/magnitude generalisation relative to humans (or under-generalises beyond the range). (observed -0.0922 vs null mean -0.0369, z=-2.80, p=0.002, q=0.00266) [survives FDR]
- **set_size_yes_gap** — Yes rate on 1-2-example sets minus yes rate on 4-example sets (excluding targets that are examples); observed below the null mean means the model's size principle shrinks generalisation with more examples more strongly than humans do (and above means less strongly). (observed 0.055 vs null mean -0.00664, z=2.44, p=0.012, q=0.0137) [survives FDR]
