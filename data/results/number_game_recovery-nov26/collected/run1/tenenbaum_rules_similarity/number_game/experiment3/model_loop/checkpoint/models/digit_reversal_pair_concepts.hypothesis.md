People infer the hidden program as a Bayesian concept learner over equally plausible familiar number rules, digit-sum rules and numeric ranges of a typical width, with the size principle. Their space of programs also contains "digit-reversal pair" programs: a program that produces only a two-digit number and its mirror image (e.g. 38 and 83, or 47 and 74). These programs are a kind of their own, with a prior plausibility inferred from the data. Because each one contains just two numbers, a set that holds both a number and its reversal is a strong suspicious coincidence, so people endorse the pair and reject other numbers more than the current models predict. Seeing only one member of a pair barely changes how readily people endorse its reversal.
