People infer the hidden program as a Bayesian concept learner over number rules (parity, primes, squares, powers, multiples, final and contained digits, digit sums) and numeric ranges of a typical width, with the size principle, but they take every example as a genuine output of the program and almost never write one off as a random slip. Instead, an example that breaks the rule the others share is explained by a disjunctive program joining two simple rules ("ends in 2 or ends in 7", "squares or multiples of 5"), a kind of program with its own prior plausibility, so after a set such as 92, 82, 27, 42 people endorse targets that satisfy either rule rather than only the majority rule.
