People infer the hidden program as a Bayesian concept learner over number rules (parity, special sets, multiples, final or contained digits, exception rules, digit sums) and numeric ranges of a typical width, but they read the examples one at a time and form an impression of what *kind* of program it is (a rule of some type, or a range) from the first example, which the later examples then revise by a different amount than an ideal learner would: later examples fully decide which program within a kind is meant, but the weight they get in deciding the kind itself is inferred from the data (less than full weight means sticking with the first impression; more than full weight means discounting it). So for multi-example sets, how much belief ends up on ranges versus rules (e.g. "a band around 16" versus "powers of 2" after 16, 8, 64) depends on which example came first, while single-example sets behave as in the ideal learner.
