People infer the hidden program as a Bayesian concept learner over number rules (including digit-sum rules with a graded prior over the sum) and numeric ranges, but they do not regard a range as equally plausible wherever it sits on the number line: they expect a magnitude-based program to live in a particular region (lower or higher numbers), so a range's prior plausibility rises or falls steadily with where its middle lies, with the direction and strength inferred from the data. The sharpest disagreement with the best model is in the direction of magnitude generalisation: after a single example or a tight cluster in mid-range, this model predicts asymmetric "yes" answers, more for targets on the favoured side of the examples than equally distant targets on the other side, whereas the best model generalises symmetrically.
