People do not infer a hidden rule; they abstract a single prototype of the example set — its typical magnitude and spread on the number line, and the profile of familiar number properties (parity, multiples, final and contained digits, primes, squares, powers) the examples tend to have — and say "yes" to a target in proportion to its similarity to that prototype. Attention to a property grows with how consistently the examples share it, and rarer, more distinctive properties count more, so a target is rejected mainly for lacking properties that all the examples have in common or for lying far from the examples' typical magnitude relative to their spread.
