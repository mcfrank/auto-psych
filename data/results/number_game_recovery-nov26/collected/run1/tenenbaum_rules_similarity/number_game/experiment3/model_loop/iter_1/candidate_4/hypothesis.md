Refinement of `graded_prior_digit_sum_concepts`, adding one component from `round_number_range_boundaries`: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over number rules, digit-sum rules (with a prior over sums that changes smoothly with the size of the sum) and numeric ranges (Erlang width prior, outputs clustered toward the range's middle), with the size principle and a prior mass per concept family. The one change is that multiples of ten act as reference points when people judge how plausible a range is a priori: a range whose lower or upper edge falls exactly on a multiple of ten is judged more or less likely than an otherwise identical range, with the direction and strength of the bias inferred from the data. This shifts where magnitude-based "yes" answers stop near a round number (e.g. whether 40 is endorsed after examples in the low thirties), while digit-sum and other rule-based generalisation are unchanged.
