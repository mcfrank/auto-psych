Refinement of `graded_prior_digit_sum_concepts`: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over number rules, digit-sum rules whose prior plausibility changes smoothly with the size of the sum, and numeric ranges with a preference for moderate-to-broad widths, the size principle and a prior plausibility per concept family. The one change is that final-digit rules ("numbers ending in d") are not equally natural either: within that family, how plausible a rule is a priori changes smoothly with the final digit, with direction and strength inferred from the data. So after examples that share a final digit, how readily people generalise to other numbers ending in that digit depends on which digit it is. (Range outputs are taken as uniform within the range: the incumbent's centre-clustering was inferred to be essentially zero, and dropping it makes the model fast enough to fit.)
