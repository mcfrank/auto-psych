Refinement of `graded_final_digit_digit_sum_concepts`, adding one component from `round_number_range_boundaries`. People do the same Bayesian concept learning as before: number rules with a prior plausibility per family, digit-sum rules and final-digit rules whose prior plausibility changes smoothly with the sum or digit, and numeric ranges with an Erlang width prior and the size principle. The one change is that multiples of ten act as reference points when people judge how plausible a range is a priori, so a range whose lower or upper edge falls exactly on a multiple of ten is judged more or less likely than an otherwise identical range, with direction and strength inferred from the data. This decides whether magnitude-based "yes" answers stop just before or exactly at a nearby multiple of ten (e.g. whether 40 is endorsed after examples in the low thirties).
