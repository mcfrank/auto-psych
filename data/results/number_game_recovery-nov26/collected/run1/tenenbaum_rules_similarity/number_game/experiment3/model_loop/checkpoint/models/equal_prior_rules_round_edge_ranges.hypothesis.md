Refinement of `equal_prior_familiar_rules_digit_sums`, adding one component from `round_number_range_boundaries`: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over equally plausible familiar number rules, digit-sum rules (with a prior that changes smoothly with the sum) and numeric ranges (Erlang width prior, the size principle). The one change is that multiples of ten act as reference points when people judge how plausible a range is a priori: a range whose lower or upper edge falls exactly on a multiple of ten is judged more or less likely than an otherwise identical range, with the direction and strength inferred from the data. So the magnitude-based "yes" answers stop just before, or exactly at, a nearby multiple of ten (for example, whether 40 is endorsed after examples in the low thirties). Range outputs are taken as uniform within the range, because the parent's centre-clustering was inferred to be essentially zero.
