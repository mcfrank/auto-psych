People do not infer a rule; they store the examples as exemplars and judge a target by its average similarity to them, where two numbers are represented by their multiplicative make-up (prime factorisation) as well as their magnitude: similarity decays exponentially with an attention-weighted sum of how far apart the numbers are on the number line and how many prime factors they do not share (the size of their least common multiple relative to their greatest common divisor). So a target is endorsed when it is close in size to the examples or built from the same factors (64 after 16 and 8, 42 after 14 and 28), and rejected when it is a near neighbour with unrelated factors (17 after 16).
