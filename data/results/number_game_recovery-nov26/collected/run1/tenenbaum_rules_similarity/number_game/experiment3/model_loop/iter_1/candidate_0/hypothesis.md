People infer the hidden program as a Bayesian concept learner over number rules (including digit-sum rules) and numeric ranges, but when they judge a target they test it positively: they imagine the target as one more output of the program and let it count, with a partial weight inferred from the data, as evidence for the programs that would produce it. So at the moment of judgement, programs that contain the target gain belief — the more so the smaller they are — and targets that fit a tight concept are endorsed more than the examples alone justify, while targets that fit only broad concepts gain little.
