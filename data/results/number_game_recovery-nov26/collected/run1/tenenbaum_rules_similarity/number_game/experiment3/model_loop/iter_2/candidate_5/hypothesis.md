Refinement of `round_edge_ranges_digit_sums` (Bayesian concept learning over number rules, digit-sum rules and centre-weighted numeric ranges with an Erlang width prior, the size principle, and a learned prior bias on ranges whose edges fall on multiples of ten). The one change is that multiples of five ("half-decade" numbers such as 25, 35, 65) are reference points in their own right, separate from multiples of ten: a range whose lower or upper edge falls on a multiple of five that is not a multiple of ten is judged more or less plausible a priori by its own bias, with direction and strength inferred from the data. So where people stop saying "yes" around a cluster of examples is set by a graded set of number-line landmarks, tens and fives, each with its own pull, not by multiples of ten alone (e.g. after examples in the low 30s, whether 35 is endorsed as the range's edge is a separate question from whether 40 is).
