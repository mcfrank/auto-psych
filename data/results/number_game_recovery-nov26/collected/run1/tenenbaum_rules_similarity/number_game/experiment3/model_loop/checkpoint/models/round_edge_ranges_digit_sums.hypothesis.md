Refinement of `centre_weighted_ranges_digit_sums`, adding one component from `round_number_range_boundaries`: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over number rules, digit-sum rules ("numbers whose digits add up to s", one family with an equal share per sum) and numeric ranges (Erlang width prior, outputs clustered toward the range's middle), with the size principle and a prior mass per concept family. The one change is that multiples of ten act as reference points when people judge how plausible a range is a priori. A range whose lower or upper edge falls exactly on a multiple of ten is judged more or less likely than an otherwise identical range, with the direction and strength inferred from the data. So the digit-sum coincidence decides which rule-based numbers are endorsed, while the round-number bias decides whether magnitude-based "yes" answers stop just before or exactly at a nearby multiple of ten (e.g. whether 40 is endorsed after examples in the low thirties).
