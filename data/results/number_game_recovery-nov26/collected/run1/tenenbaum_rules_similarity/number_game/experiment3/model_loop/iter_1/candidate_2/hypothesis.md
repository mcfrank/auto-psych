People infer the hidden program as a Bayesian concept learner over number rules (including digit-sum rules) and numeric ranges, as in the best current model, but when the examples share a common divisor they also consider "multiples of n" for any larger number n (13 up to 50, e.g. 13, 26, 39, 52 or 28, 56, 84), not just the familiar multiples of 3 to 12, as a family of programs with its own prior plausibility. Because such programs contain only a handful of numbers, examples that are all multiples of an unusual number (e.g. 13, 91 or 28, 84) are a suspicious coincidence, so people endorse other multiples of that number and reject nearby non-multiples more than the best model predicts.
