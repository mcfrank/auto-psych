People infer the hidden program as a Bayesian concept learner over number rules (including digit-sum rules) and numeric ranges, as in the best current model, but their space of programs also includes "multiples of n" for unusual, larger divisors n (13 up to 50, e.g. 13, 26, 39, 52 or 28, 56, 84), a family of programs judged a priori less plausible than the familiar multiples of 3 to 12 by an amount inferred from the data. Because such programs contain only a handful of numbers, examples that are all multiples of an unusual number (e.g. 13, 91 or 28, 84) are a suspicious coincidence, so people endorse other multiples of that number and reject nearby non-multiples more than the best model predicts.
