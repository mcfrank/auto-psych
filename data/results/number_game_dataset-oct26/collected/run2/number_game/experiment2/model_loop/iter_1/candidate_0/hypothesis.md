People judge a target by its recency-weighted exponential similarity to the remembered examples, where psychological distance is a weighted sum of mismatches in size, final digit, parity and shared factors, but their generalization gradient broadens as the session goes on: with repeated exposure to the task, every mismatch counts for proportionally less, so the same distance yields more similarity late in the session than early. This predicts a drift toward "yes" over the session that is concentrated on targets dissimilar to the examples, while targets that closely match the examples are endorsed about equally early and late.
