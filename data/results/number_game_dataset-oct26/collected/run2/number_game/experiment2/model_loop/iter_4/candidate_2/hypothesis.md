People judge how close a target is in size to a set of examples on a yardstick calibrated by the examples themselves: the distance by which a target falls outside the stretch the examples span is felt relative to how widely the examples are spread, so after tightly clustered examples (e.g. 41, 43, 44) a number ten steps away feels far, whereas after widely scattered examples (e.g. 12, 47, 83) the same ten steps feel negligible. The size-similarity gradient around a set therefore flattens as the examples' spread grows, making scattered sets generalized far more broadly beyond their span than tight ones (the too-compressed between-set yes-rate spread in the critique); everything else — recency-weighted similarity on final digit, parity and shared factor with coherence-stretched attention, the "multiples of g" and "ends in d" rules, the drifting criterion and the order-directed momentum of the incumbent — is unchanged.
