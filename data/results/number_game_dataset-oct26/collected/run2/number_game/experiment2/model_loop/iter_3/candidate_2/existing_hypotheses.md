# Existing hypotheses

Each model below is ONE cognitive hypothesis, with how it stands on the current data by ELPD-LOO (best first). `elpd_diff ± dse` is a model's deficit against the best and the standard error of that difference (with the responses to each stimulus pair counted together, as they are correlated): within about 2·dse the two are statistically tied on this data; beyond it the model has lost. "PSIS-LOO unreliable" means the estimate itself is untrustworthy (too many high-Pareto-k trials), not that the model is bad. Propose a hypothesis that is genuinely different from these, or a refinement of a single one of them — never a combination of several.

## span_filling_size_exemplar  — rank 0, the best model on this data, ELPD-LOO -3105.9

Refinement of drifting_criterion_dual_route_exemplar: people still judge a target by its recency-weighted exponential similarity to the remembered examples on final digit, parity and graded shared factor (with coherence-stretched attention), still consult the "multiples of g" and "ends in d" rules as separate evidence, and still relax their criterion over the session. The single change is how closeness in size is judged: people treat the examples as marking out a filled-in stretch of the number line, so a target's size mismatch is how far it falls outside the span from the smallest to the largest example (zero anywhere inside it), not its distance from each example. The disagreement with the best model lives inside widely spaced sets — after 10, 90 the target 50 is as close in size as 10 itself and is endorsed far more often, so widely spread sets are generalized much more broadly than tight ones (the too-compressed between-set yes-rate spread in the critique).

## set_size_scaled_magnitude_gradient  — rank 1, 0.5 ± 3.7 nats behind the best (0.1× dse: statistically tied with the best), ELPD-LOO -3106.4

Refinement of drifting_criterion_dual_route_exemplar: people still judge a target by its recency-weighted exponential similarity to the remembered examples (size, final digit, parity, graded shared factor, attention stretched by how much the examples resemble one another), still weigh the abstracted "multiples of g" and "ends in d" rules as separate evidence, and still relax their yes-criterion over the session. The single change is that how broadly people generalize in size around each example depends on how many examples the program has shown: they take a larger sample as evidence that the program ranges more widely, so the size gradient around each example widens with the number of examples (a single example is generalized narrowly in size, three or four examples broadly), the reverse of a size-principle narrowing. This changes how generously whole sets are generalized relative to one another, addressing the critique's between-set yes-rate spread.

## independent_retrieval_exemplar_search  — rank 15, 71.0 ± 17.1 nats behind the best (4.1× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -3176.9

Refinement of graded_factor_coherence_tuned_exemplar: people still compare the target with each remembered example by exponential similarity (size, final digit, parity, graded shared factor), with attention stretched toward the respects in which the examples resemble one another. The single change is the decision process. People do not add up familiarity and threshold it. They search memory example by example: each example is recalled (less often the longer ago it was shown) and independently accepts the target with probability equal to its similarity, and they answer "yes" as soon as any recalled example accepts it (otherwise they guess "yes" at a low base rate). So one highly similar example is enough for "yes", and every additional example is one more chance to accept, so yes rates rise with set size.

## shared_property_coincidence_rule  — rank 16, 202.9 ± 25.2 nats behind the best (8.0× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -3308.8

People read the examples for the arithmetic properties they all have in common (same parity, same final digit, same decade, being multiples of the same number) and treat each shared property as evidence for a rule in proportion to how surprising it would be for that many examples to share it by coincidence. They then say "yes" to a target according to the surprise-weighted balance of shared properties it has versus lacks, discounted only by how far the target falls outside the range the examples span — no similarity to individual examples is computed.

## memoryless_win_stay_lose_shift_rules  — rank 17, 249.9 ± 28.0 nats behind the best (8.9× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -3355.8

People test one candidate rule at a time while reading the examples in order, in a win-stay, lose-shift fashion: the first example suggests a single rule (a final digit, a multiple of some number, odd or even, or a range of sizes, smaller rules being favoured), they keep that rule as long as each new example fits it, and when an example contradicts it they discard it and pick a fresh rule suggested by that contradicting example alone, without re-checking the earlier ones. They then answer "yes" if the target obeys the rule they ended up holding, so the final judgement is driven by the last examples that forced a switch and by whichever rule survived from the start, and the same examples shown in a different order lead to different generalisations.

## factorized_rule_and_range_inference  — rank 18, 325.5 ± 31.8 nats behind the best (10.2× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -3431.5

People are Bayesian concept learners who think the program's numbers obey both an arithmetic rule (a step pattern such as "even", "multiples of 3" or "ends in 7", or no pattern at all) and a numeric range, each inferred from the examples with the size principle, but — as a resource-saving distortion of joint inference — they work out the rule and the range as two separate, independent questions and say "yes" only to the extent that the target passes both. So a target is endorsed when it fits the inferred step pattern AND lies within the inferred range: far-away numbers sharing the examples' final digit or factor are rejected, as are nearby numbers that break the pattern, and the yes-probability is the product of the two separate judgements.

## log_scale_magnitude_range_inference  — rank 19, 369.5 ± 34.8 nats behind the best (10.6× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -3475.4

People do not look for arithmetic rules or compare the target with each example; they infer the range of magnitudes the program produces, reasoning about size on a compressed, logarithmic mental number line. They favour the narrowest range (in ratio terms) that covers all the examples, more strongly the more examples there are, and say "yes" in proportion to their belief that the target falls inside that range — so generalization extends further above the largest example than below the smallest (equal ratios span more numbers upward), and tightly clustered sets are generalized narrowly while widely spread sets are generalized broadly.
