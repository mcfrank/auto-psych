People judge a target by its plain recency-weighted similarity to the remembered examples (closeness in size, shared final digit, shared parity, graded shared factor) and answer from that familiarity, but on some fraction of trials they do not consult the examples at all and simply guess, answering "yes" at their own guessing rate. The single claim is a decision-level lapse-to-guessing process on top of simple exemplar similarity, so even targets totally unlike the examples get a fixed floor of "yes" answers and even near-identical targets a fixed ceiling of "no" answers, the same for every set and target.
