Refinement of coherence_tuned_attention_exemplar: people still judge a target by its recency-weighted similarity to the remembered examples, with attention to each dimension (size, final digit, parity, shared factor) stretched toward the respects in which the examples resemble one another. The single change is what the shared-factor dimension compares: when the examples all share a common divisor of 3 or more, people judge each target-example pair on whether the target is a multiple of that set-wide common factor, rather than on whether the two numbers happen to share any factor pairwise (which they still do for single examples or sets with no common factor). So a target that shares some other factor with an example (e.g. 9 after 12, 24, 36) no longer counts as similar on that dimension, predicting a sharper "multiples of g" gap than the incumbent, as the critique's common-factor rule gap shows.
