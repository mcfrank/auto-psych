## Hypotheses

- **coherence_sharpened_decision_exemplar**: Refinement of graded_factor_coherence_tuned_exemplar: people still judge a target by its recency-weighted exponential similarity to the remembered examples (size, final digit, parity, graded shared factor), with attention stretched toward the respects in which the examples resemble one another, but how much the examples resemble one another also sets how decisively people turn that similarity into an answer. The single change is that set coherence sharpens the decision rule: after a coherent set (examples alike in size, digit, parity or factors) people answer near-deterministically from similarity, endorsing similar targets and rejecting dissimilar ones almost always, whereas after an incoherent set or a single example their answers stay noisy, which makes set-level yes rates more extreme than the incumbent predicts (the between-set yes-rate spread in the critique) and sharpens the "ends in d" and "multiples of g" gaps for coherent sets.
- **shared_factor_rule_sharpened_exemplar**: Refinement of coherence_sharpened_decision_exemplar: people still judge a target by its recency-weighted exponential similarity to the remembered examples (size, final digit, parity, graded shared factor), with attention stretched toward the respects in which the examples resemble one another and set coherence sharpening how decisively that similarity becomes an answer, but when all the examples share a common factor of 3 or more they also notice that factor as an explicit "multiples of g" rule, as in shared_divisor_rule_abstraction, and lean toward "yes" for targets that are multiples of g and toward "no" for those that are not. The single change is this grafted rule-based push on the answer, whose strength grows with how unlikely such a shared factor would be by coincidence (a larger g and more examples), so it acts even on targets far in size from every example — closing the common-factor rule gap in the critique, where people generalize "multiples of g" more sharply than similarity alone predicts.
- **coherence_sharpened_set_factor_exemplar**: Refinement of coherence_sharpened_decision_exemplar: people still judge a target by its recency-weighted exponential similarity to the remembered examples (size, final digit, parity, graded shared factor), with attention stretched toward the respects in which the examples resemble one another and set coherence sharpening how decisively similarity is turned into an answer. The single change (the set-level factor from set_factor_tuned_attention_exemplar) is that when several examples share a common factor of 3 or more, people notice that factor and judge the factor part of similarity by whether the target is a multiple of the examples' shared factor (graded by how much of that factor the target shares), rather than by the factors it shares with each example separately — so after 12, 18, 30 the target 42 is close and 15 or 20 far, sharpening the "multiples of g" gap the critique reports as still too small.
- **independent_retrieval_exemplar_search**: Refinement of graded_factor_coherence_tuned_exemplar: people still compare the target with each remembered example by exponential similarity (size, final digit, parity, graded shared factor), with attention stretched toward the respects in which the examples resemble one another. The single change is the decision process. People do not add up familiarity and threshold it. They search memory example by example: each example is recalled (less often the longer ago it was shown) and independently accepts the target with probability equal to its similarity, and they answer "yes" as soon as any recalled example accepts it (otherwise they guess "yes" at a low base rate). So one highly similar example is enough for "yes", and every additional example is one more chance to accept, so yes rates rise with set size.
- **dual_route_shared_factor_rule_exemplar**: Refinement of dimension_specific_coherence_exemplar: people still judge a target by its recency-weighted exponential similarity to the remembered examples (size, final digit, parity, graded shared factor), with each dimension's attention stretched by its own sensitivity to how much the examples resemble one another, but when the examples all share a common factor of 3 or more they also abstract the explicit rule "multiples of that factor" and consult it alongside similarity when answering. The single change is this rule as a separate source of evidence at the decision, not a dimension of similarity: it raises "yes" for multiples and lowers it for non-multiples regardless of how far they are in size, in proportion to how unlikely the shared factor would be by coincidence (larger factors and more examples), so far-away multiples of g are endorsed and nearby non-multiples rejected, closing the common-factor rule gap that similarity alone leaves.
- **dimension_specific_coherence_exemplar**: Refinement of graded_factor_coherence_tuned_exemplar: people still judge a target by its recency-weighted exponential similarity to the remembered examples (size, final digit, parity, graded shared factor), with attention stretched toward the respects in which the examples resemble one another, but how strongly noticing a shared property stretches attention differs by kind of property rather than being one common sensitivity. The single change gives each dimension its own coherence sensitivity, so a conspicuous surface regularity such as every example ending in the same digit, or every example sharing a large factor, can make people generalize an "ends in d" or "multiples of g" rule far more sharply than shared size or parity does, closing the final-digit and common-factor rule gaps in the critique.
- **graded_factor_coherence_tuned_exemplar**: Refinement of graded_shared_factor_exemplar_similarity: people still judge a target by its recency-weighted exponential similarity to the remembered examples, with the number-theoretic part of similarity graded by how large a factor the target and each example share, but the attention they give each kind of similarity (size, final digit, parity, shared factor) is stretched toward the respects in which the examples resemble one another. The single change grafts this coherence-tuned attention (from coherence_tuned_attention_exemplar) onto the graded model, with the examples' factor coherence itself measured by the same graded shared-factor match, so sets whose examples share a large common factor make people generalize sharply to targets sharing that factor, closing the common-factor rule gap, while a single example leaves attention at baseline.
- **coherence_tuned_attention_exemplar**: People judge a target by its recency-weighted similarity to the remembered examples, but how much attention they give each kind of similarity (closeness in size, shared final digit, shared parity, shared factor) is learned from the examples themselves: while reading the set they notice the respects in which the examples resemble one another and stretch attention toward those dimensions. So when all examples share a factor, final digit or parity, targets matching every example on that property are endorsed far more than pairwise similarity with fixed attention predicts, while a single example gives no such cue and leaves attention at its baseline.
