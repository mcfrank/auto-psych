People judge a target by how well it fits the examples as a whole, conjunctively: rather than summing its similarity to each remembered example (so that closeness to any one example suffices), they ask whether the target fits every example, so its familiarity is set by its worst-fitting examples (a soft maximum of its psychological distances to the examples on size, final digit, parity and graded shared factor, weighted by recency). This disagrees most sharply with the best (additive, coherence-tuned) exemplar model on multi-example sets for targets that closely resemble one or two examples but not the others — e.g. a near neighbour of one example that lacks the factor or final digit all the examples share — which it predicts are rejected, so endorsements concentrate on targets sharing the set-wide property (the "multiples of g" gap) without any separate attention tuning.
