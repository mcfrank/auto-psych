Refinement of leading_digit_string_similarity (all else unchanged: recency-weighted similarity on size, final digit, parity, graded shared factor and leading written digit with coherence-stretched attention, the "multiples of g", "ends in d" and fixed-list concepts, the filled-in span, sequence momentum, zigzag breadth, the saturating criterion relaxation and the upward-count start boundary). The single change is that people hold a prior belief that a number-generating program does not produce the trivial bottom of the counting sequence: the smallest numbers (1 most of all, then 2 and 3, fading quickly above that) feel like degenerate non-outputs, so they are rejected more than their similarity to the examples predicts, whatever the set. So 1 and 2 get fewer "yes" answers than equally similar targets elsewhere on the number line, after sets of large numbers and of small numbers alike.
