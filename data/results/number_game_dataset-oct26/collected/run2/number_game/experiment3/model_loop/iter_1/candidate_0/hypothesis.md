People imagine the hidden program as a generator that makes each new number from one of the numbers it has already produced (most often its latest) by a single elementary arithmetic operation: repeating it, rescaling it by a ratio (small ratio changes being far likelier than large ones), jumping by tens so the final digit is kept, or moving to another number that shares a factor with it. They answer "yes" in proportion to how probable it is that such a program would produce the target, relative to an arbitrary number. Because rescaling is a matter of ratios, the same linear gap is far less likely among small numbers, and below the examples, than among large numbers and above them.
