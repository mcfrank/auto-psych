The single change is how size generalization beyond the examples is judged: as in normative Bayesian inference about the range of numbers the program produces, people judge how far a target lies beyond the examples relative to how wide a range the examples already cover plus a prior expectation of the program's range, so ten steps beyond 41, 44 feels far while ten steps beyond 12, 83 feels negligible — but with one distortion, sample-size neglect: they ignore how many examples were shown, so three or four examples do not narrow generalization more than two do, as the normative size principle would require. Widely spread sets are therefore generalized more broadly than tight ones, widening the set-to-set differences in yes-rate (the critique's set-level dispersion gap).
