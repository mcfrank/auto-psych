Refinement of sample_size_neglect_range_inference (all else unchanged: how far a target lies beyond the examples is judged relative to the range they cover plus a prior expected range, ignoring how many examples were shown, together with the recency-weighted similarity on final digit, parity and shared factor, the "multiples of g", "ends in d" and fixed-list concepts, sequence momentum, zigzag breadth and the saturating criterion relaxation). The single change, taken from upward_count_start_boundary, is that people use the order of the examples to infer where the program's range begins: when the examples go up overall (the last one shown is larger than the first), they take the first example as the program's starting point and treat any number below it as one the program never reached, so they reject it much more than range inference alone predicts, while sets that go down overall have only the soft, spread-scaled lower bound.
