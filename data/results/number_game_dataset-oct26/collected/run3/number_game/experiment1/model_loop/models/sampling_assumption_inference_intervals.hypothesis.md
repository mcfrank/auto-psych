Refinement of `size_principle_strength_intervals`: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over the 42 arithmetic rules and the midpoint-weighted intervals of 1-100, but instead of applying a fixed partial size principle to every example, they are uncertain whether the examples were deliberately sampled from the concept (strong sampling) or merely happen to be consistent with it (weak sampling), and they infer this from the examples themselves. The one change replaces the per-example size-principle strength with this set-level sampling-assumption inference (as in Navarro et al., 2012): a single example is weak evidence that sampling is strong, so people generalize broadly from one example, while each further example that tightly fits a small concept makes strong sampling — and hence narrow, size-principle generalization — more credible.
