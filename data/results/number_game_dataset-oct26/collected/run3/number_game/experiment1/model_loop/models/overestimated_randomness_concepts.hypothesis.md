People learn the program's concept by ideal Bayesian inference over arithmetic rules and numeric intervals with the size principle, but they believe the program is far less reliable than it is: they think a sizeable, inferred share of its outputs are arbitrary numbers unrelated to its rule. Because any single example might be such a random output, it rules out the concepts that do not contain it only weakly, so people generalize broadly — saying "yes" to many unrelated targets — after one example, and tighten only as consistent examples accumulate.
