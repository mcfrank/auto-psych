People do not induce a concept or measure closeness on the number line; they judge whether the target can be made from one of the examples by a short chain of simple mental operations on numbers (add or subtract one, add or subtract ten, double, halve, reverse the digits), so the fewer operations it takes to turn the examples into the target, the more likely they say "yes" (transformational similarity). This predicts "yes" to 32 after 16, to 61 after 16, and to 40 after 20 even though they are far away on the number line, while a numerically moderate jump that needs many steps (e.g. 47 after 20) is rejected.
