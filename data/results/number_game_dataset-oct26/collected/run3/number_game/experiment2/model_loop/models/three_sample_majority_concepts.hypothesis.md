People do the same conservative Bayesian concept learning as the best model (42 arithmetic rules and midpoint-weighted, decade-favoured ranges; evidence weight growing with set size; an inferred tolerance for stray examples; guessing that becomes rarer as more examples are shown), but, being limited in how much of that belief they can hold in mind, they do not read off the exact probability that the target belongs: they call to mind just three candidate concepts drawn from their belief and answer "yes" when at least two of them contain the target. This fixed three-sample majority vote predicts more all-or-none, consensual answers to each item than the graded posterior gives — targets the concept probably includes are endorsed more often, and ones it only possibly includes (e.g. nearby parity violators) are rejected more sharply.
