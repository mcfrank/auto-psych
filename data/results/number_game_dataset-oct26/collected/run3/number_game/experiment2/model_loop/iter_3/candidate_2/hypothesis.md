People do the same conservative Bayesian concept learning as the best model (42 arithmetic rules and midpoint-weighted, decade-favoured ranges; evidence weight growing with set size; an inferred tolerance for stray examples; guessing that becomes rarer as more examples are shown), but, being limited in how much of that belief they can hold in mind, they do not read off the exact probability that the target belongs: they call to mind a few candidate concepts drawn from their belief and answer by the majority verdict of those samples. The one distortion is this inferred number of mentally sampled concepts (one sample recovers probability matching), which, if people sample several, predicts more all-or-none, consensual answers to each item than the graded posterior gives — targets the concept probably includes are endorsed more often and ones it probably excludes (e.g. nearby parity violators) are rejected more sharply.
