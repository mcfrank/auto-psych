# Existing hypotheses

Each model below is ONE cognitive hypothesis, with how it stands on the current data by ELPD-LOO (best first). `elpd_diff ± dse` is a model's deficit against the best and the standard error of that difference (with the responses to each stimulus pair counted together, as they are correlated): within about 2·dse the two are statistically tied on this data; beyond it the model has lost. "PSIS-LOO unreliable" means the estimate itself is untrustworthy (too many high-Pareto-k trials), not that the model is bad. Propose a hypothesis that is genuinely different from these, or a refinement of a single one of them — never a combination of several.

## outlier_tolerant_rule_concepts  — rank 0, the best model on this data, ELPD-LOO -2796.5

Refinement of `evidence_scarce_guessing_concepts`: people do the same conservative Bayesian concept learning over the 42 arithmetic rules and midpoint-weighted, decade-favoured intervals of 1-100 (evidence weight growing with set size, guessing that becomes rarer as more examples are shown), but how readily they accept that an example could be a stray number the program produced outside its rule is their own inferred belief, rather than a fixed one-in-ten chance. The one change is this inferred tolerance for exceptions, which matters most for sets where all examples but one share a rule (e.g. 92, 82, 27, 42 or 71, 61, 26, 81): a tolerant learner keeps the near-fitting rule and says "yes" to far targets that fit it (52, 62; 11, 41), while an intolerant learner abandons the rule for a range spanning every example and rejects them.

## evidence_scarce_guessing_concepts  — rank 1, 9.5 ± 3.3 nats behind the best (2.9× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -2806.0

Refinement of `decade_aligned_range_concepts`: people do the same conservative Bayesian concept learning over the 42 arithmetic rules and midpoint-weighted, decade-favoured intervals of 1-100, with evidence weight growing with set size, but when the examples give them little to go on they often do not consult a concept at all and simply guess (with their own inferred leaning toward "yes"), and this guessing becomes rarer as more examples are shown (an inferred guessing rate for a single example and an inferred decline with set size). The one change is this evidence-dependent guessing, which predicts that after a single example people say "yes" fairly often even to targets no plausible concept contains (e.g. 94 or 100 after 7, 1 after 100), whereas after three or four examples such targets are almost always rejected — the excess single-example non-member "yes" rate the critique of the best model flags.

## family_weighted_evidence_concepts  — rank 2, 11.7 ± 5.0 nats behind the best (2.3× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -2808.3

Refinement of `evidence_scarce_guessing_concepts`: people do the same conservative Bayesian concept learning over the 42 arithmetic rules and midpoint-weighted, decade-favoured intervals of 1-100 (evidence weight growing with set size, guessing that becomes rarer as more examples are shown), but they do not weigh the examples' evidence equally for the two kinds of concept: how strongly a tight fit counts in a concept's favour (the size principle) differs between arithmetic rules and number ranges, by an inferred ratio. The one change is this family-specific evidence weight, which, if people discount the coincidence that examples cluster in a narrow range relative to the coincidence that they all fit a rule, predicts that sets whose examples share a property (e.g. all even, or all ending in 4) are explained by that rule rather than by a nearby range, so nearby targets lacking the property (e.g. 25 after 22, 26, 30) are rejected more sharply — the excess opposite-parity "yes" rate the critique of the incumbent flags.

## fuzzy_edged_range_concepts  — rank 3, 18.7 ± 8.9 nats behind the best (2.1× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -2815.3

Refinement of `session_growing_acquiescence_concepts`: people do the same conservative Bayesian concept learning over the 42 arithmetic rules and midpoint-weighted, decade-favouring intervals of 1-100 (evidence weight growing with set size, blind "yes" growing over the session), but they treat a number range as a vague category with fuzzy edges rather than a crisp one: when checking whether the target falls in a range they believe in, a target just outside its edges still counts as partly inside, with credit fading with distance beyond the edge at an inferred tolerance. The one change is this graded range membership at the decision stage (arithmetic rules stay crisp, and the examples are still explained by crisp ranges), which predicts broader, smoothly decaying generalization to near-but-outside targets — strongest after a single example, whose posterior concentrates on narrow ranges around it — the excess single-example generalization the incumbent's critique flags.

## session_growing_acquiescence_concepts  — rank 4, 19.6 ± 8.8 nats behind the best (2.2× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -2816.2

Refinement of `decade_aligned_range_concepts`: people do the same conservative Bayesian concept learning over the 42 arithmetic rules and midpoint-weighted, decade-favouring intervals of 1-100, with evidence weight growing with set size, but as the session wears on they increasingly answer "yes" without consulting their concept at all — a blind-"yes" lapse whose rate rises steadily with how far into the session the trial falls (an inferred starting rate and growth rate). The one change is this session-growing acquiescence, which predicts more "yes" late in the session than early, chiefly to targets the concept clearly excludes (where a blind "yes" makes the most difference), closing the session-liberalization discrepancy the incumbent's critique flags.

## decade_aligned_range_concepts  — rank 5, 30.6 ± 11.9 nats behind the best (2.6× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -2827.1

Refinement of `sample_size_growing_evidence_concepts`: people do the same conservative Bayesian concept learning over the 42 arithmetic rules and midpoint-weighted intervals of 1-100, with evidence weight growing with set size, but they think about number ranges in round decades, so a range whose edges fall on decade boundaries (such as 20-29 or 30-59) is a priori more plausible than an arbitrarily bounded one by an inferred bonus. The one change is this decade-aligned prior bonus on intervals, which predicts that a target just outside the examples' span is endorsed more when it lies in the same decade as an example (e.g. 29 after 23 and 25) than an equally near target across a decade boundary (e.g. 19), the decade-grouping discrepancy the critique of the best model flags.

## log_compressed_sampling_concepts  — rank 6, 32.6 ± 13.8 nats behind the best (2.4× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -2829.1

People do the same conservative Bayesian concept learning over arithmetic rules and number ranges as the best model (size principle, evidence weight growing with set size, decade-aligned ranges favoured), but they imagine the program's examples as drawn evenly along a compressed, roughly logarithmic mental number line rather than evenly over the integers, so a concept's small members are expected far more often than its large ones. The one change is this inferred compression of the sampling assumption, which predicts that a large example is weak evidence for a narrow range around it (generalization from 85 spreads much wider in absolute terms than from 5), and that a concept whose members are mostly large is not penalised for its size as much as one spanning small numbers.

## prior_informed_guessing_concepts  — rank 7, 42.3 ± 11.1 nats behind the best (3.8× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -2838.9

Refinement of `evidence_scarce_guessing_concepts`: people do the same conservative Bayesian concept learning over the 42 arithmetic rules and midpoint-weighted, decade-favoured intervals of 1-100 (evidence weight growing with set size), and they likewise often stop using the examples when there are few of them, less so as more are shown — but when they do, they do not guess "yes" at one blind rate for every target; they answer from their prior beliefs about what such programs produce, as if no examples had been shown. The one change is this prior-informed guessing, which predicts that guessed "yes" answers (most common after a single example) go mainly to targets that many a-priori plausible concepts contain — mid-range and even numbers — and rarely to targets few concepts contain (e.g. 1, 97, 100, odd non-primes near the ends), rather than equally to every non-member.

## log_compressed_range_concepts  — rank 8, 45.1 ± 12.7 nats behind the best (3.5× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -2841.6

Refinement of `decade_aligned_range_concepts`: people do the same conservative Bayesian concept learning (42 arithmetic rules plus midpoint-weighted, decade-favoured intervals, evidence weight growing with set size), but they judge how big a number range is on a compressed mental number line, where the distance from 80 to 90 feels smaller than the distance from 5 to 15. The one change is that the size principle measures an interval's size, and how likely it is to have produced each example, on this compressed scale (an inferred compression exponent; no compression recovers the incumbent), so generalization is scale-invariant: people extend a range around large examples (e.g. 85, 88) much further in absolute terms than around small ones (e.g. 5, 8) — the region, single and clustered examples at the two ends of 1-100, where it disagrees most with the incumbent's uniform-width generalization.

## decade_concepts_lapsing_learner  — rank 9, 70.6 ± 13.3 nats behind the best (5.3× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -2867.2

Refinement of `lapsing_growing_evidence_concepts`: people do the same conservative Bayesian concept learning (42 arithmetic rules plus midpoint-weighted intervals, evidence weight growing with set size, blind "yes"/"no" lapses), but they also treat the decades of the written number system (1-9, 10-19, ..., 90-99) as privileged, ready-made concepts with their own inferred prior share, over and above the same ranges' standing as ordinary intervals. The one change is this decade hypothesis family, which predicts that a nearby target outside the examples' range is endorsed more when it shares the examples' tens digit than when it crosses into the next decade — the same-decade bonus the incumbent's critique flags.

## session_drifting_decade_concepts  — rank 10, 72.1 ± 13.5 nats behind the best (5.3× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -2868.6

Refinement of `decade_concept_bayesian_learning`: people do the same conservative Bayesian concept learning over arithmetic rules, midpoint-weighted intervals and decade concepts (evidence weight growing with set size, blind "yes"/"no" lapses), but as the long session wears on they increasingly answer "yes" without consulting their concept, so the chance of a blind "yes" drifts with how far into the session the trial falls, at an inferred rate. The one change is this session-drifting yes-lapse rate, which predicts that targets the concept excludes are endorsed more often late in the session than early — the growing liberality the critique of the best model flags.

## decade_concept_bayesian_learning  — rank 11, 73.0 ± 13.5 nats behind the best (5.4× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -2869.6

People do the same conservative Bayesian concept learning as the best model (42 arithmetic rules and midpoint-weighted intervals of 1-100, evidence weight growing with set size, attention lapses), but they also hold decades — "numbers in the 20s", "numbers in the 60s" (same tens digit) — as named concepts with their own inferred prior share, split equally across the ten decades. Because a decade is a privileged, a-priori-likely range rather than one of thousands of arbitrary intervals, a target just outside the examples' range is endorsed more when it shares their tens decade than when an equally close target crosses a decade boundary.

## tversky_ratio_feature_similarity  — rank 12, 129.0 ± 35.5 nats behind the best (3.6× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -2925.6

People judge the target by its feature-based similarity to each example separately, in the sense of Tversky's ratio model: a number is a bundle of salient properties (odd/even, final digit, decade, digits it contains, prime, square, cube, power-of-k, multiple-of-k), and the target's similarity to an example is the share of their combined features they have in common, discounted more for the example's features the target lacks than for the target's own extra features (both discount weights inferred). The higher the target's average similarity to the examples, the more likely people say "yes" — so a target is rejected chiefly for lacking properties the examples have (e.g. the wrong parity), whatever its distance on the number line.

## fatigue_driven_guessing_concepts  — no comparison row

People do the same conservative Bayesian concept learning as the best model (42 arithmetic rules and midpoint-weighted, decade-favoured number ranges, evidence weight growing with set size, an inferred tolerance for stray examples), but the trials on which they skip their concept and simply guess (with their own leaning toward "yes") come from fatigue, not from a shortage of examples: the chance of guessing rises with how far into the session the trial falls, at an inferred rate, and does not depend on how many examples were shown. This predicts that excluded targets get more "yes" late in the session than early (the late-minus-early drift the best model misses), and — unlike the best model — that guessed "yes" answers to non-members are as common after four examples as after one at the same point in the session.

## primacy_anchored_exception_concepts  — no comparison row

Refinement of `outlier_tolerant_rule_concepts`: people do the same conservative Bayesian concept learning over the 42 arithmetic rules and midpoint-weighted, decade-favoured intervals of 1-100 (evidence weight growing with set size, an inferred tolerance for stray examples, guessing that becomes rarer as more examples are shown), but they read the examples in the order shown and take the first one as their anchor: the first example is always trusted as a genuine output of the program's rule, and only examples arriving after it can be dismissed as stray exceptions. The one change is this primacy-anchored exception tolerance, which predicts that a set whose odd-one-out comes later (e.g. 92, 82, 27, 42) keeps the rule the others share, whereas a set whose odd-one-out is the first example (e.g. 27, 92, 82, 42) forces every surviving concept to include that first number, so far targets fitting only the later examples' rule are rejected.

## span_relative_powerlaw_similarity  — no comparison row

People judge a target purely by its closeness on the number line to the span the examples cover: any target inside that span is judged fully similar, and a target outside it loses similarity by a power law in its distance beyond the nearest edge measured relative to the span's own width (plus an inferred baseline width, so a lone example still generalizes to its neighbours). The falloff grows steeper with every additional example, so a few spread-out examples license wide generalization while many tightly clustered ones license almost none; apart from occasional blind guesses, "yes" follows this relative-distance similarity.
