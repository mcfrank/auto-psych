People reason about the examples communicatively: they assume the examples were chosen by a helpful teacher who picks numbers that would make the intended concept easy to recognise, rather than sampled at random from it. A learner who assumes such pedagogical selection discounts a concept whose members are mostly ambiguous (shared with many other plausible concepts), because a helpful teacher of that concept would have shown its distinctive members instead; the degree of assumed helpfulness is inferred, and the conclusions drawn are otherwise those of conservative Bayesian concept learning over arithmetic rules and decade-favoured number ranges, with guessing when examples are few. This predicts sharper, more all-or-none generalization than random-sampling learning — e.g. sets whose examples all share a rule are read as deliberately signalling that rule, so nearby rule violators are rejected.
