Refinement of `outlier_tolerant_rule_concepts`: people do the same conservative Bayesian concept learning over the 42 arithmetic rules and midpoint-weighted, decade-favoured intervals of 1-100 (evidence weight growing with set size, an inferred tolerance for stray examples, guessing that becomes rarer as more examples are shown), but when they consult their concept they do not answer "yes" with exactly their degree of belief that the target belongs — they turn that belief into a more decisive answer, so a target they think fairly likely to belong is endorsed more often than its probability and one they doubt is rejected more often (an inferred decision sharpness; no sharpening recovers the incumbent). The one change is this sharpened belief-to-answer mapping, which predicts more all-or-none, consensual answers to the same item than probability matching allows — the excess within-item consensus the incumbent's critique flags — and sharper rejection of targets that fit only a weakly believed concept (e.g. opposite-parity targets after examples sharing a parity).
