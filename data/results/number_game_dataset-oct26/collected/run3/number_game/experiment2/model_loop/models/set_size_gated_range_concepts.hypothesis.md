Refinement of `session_growing_acquiescence_concepts`: people do the same conservative Bayesian concept learning over the 42 arithmetic rules and midpoint-weighted, decade-favouring number ranges (evidence weight growing with set size, a blind-"yes" rate that grows over the session), but how plausible they find a magnitude-range explanation at all depends on how many examples they see: a lone example has no spread to suggest a range, so with one example people reason almost entirely about what kind of number it is (odd/even, multiple, power, final digit), and range concepts gain prior credibility only as more examples arrive. The one change is this set-size-dependent prior share of rules versus ranges (an inferred baseline and slope), which predicts that after a single example people endorse far-away targets of the same kind (e.g. same parity) much more, and nearby targets of a different kind much less, than the incumbent predicts.
