Refinement of `outlier_tolerant_rule_concepts`: people do the same conservative Bayesian concept learning over the 42 arithmetic rules and midpoint-weighted, decade-favoured intervals of 1-100 (evidence weight growing with set size, guessing that becomes rarer as more examples are shown), but their belief that the program sometimes produces stray numbers outside its rule is learned from experience: after the first half of the long session, having seen many sets whose numbers fit no neat rule, they are more willing to treat an example as an exception (an inferred early tolerance and an inferred change in the second half). The one change is this experience-dependent tolerance for exceptions, which predicts that later in the session the examples rule out concepts less firmly, so people say "yes" more often to targets outside the tightest concept — chiefly to targets in broader rules or ranges that contain most of the examples, not uniformly to every number as a blind-"yes" lapse would.
