Refinement of `outlier_tolerant_rule_concepts`: people do the same conservative Bayesian concept learning over the 42 arithmetic rules and midpoint-weighted, decade-favoured intervals of 1-100 (inferred tolerance for stray examples, guessing that becomes rarer as more examples are shown), but how much weight they give the examples' evidence drifts over the long session: as they tire, they take the examples' tight fit to a small concept less seriously and their generalization relaxes toward broader rules and ranges (an inferred change in evidence weight from the first to the second half of the session). The one change is this session-drifting evidence weight, which predicts that the late-session rise in "yes" falls chiefly on targets just outside the tightest consistent concept but inside broader ones containing the examples (e.g. 30 after 22, 26; other evens after 16, 8, 64), not uniformly on every excluded number as a blind-"yes" lapse would, nor on numbers that contradict the examples as more exception tolerance would.
