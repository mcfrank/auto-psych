People learn the program's concept by conservative Bayesian concept learning over arithmetic rules and number ranges (as the best model does), but they also notice regular spacing between the examples and entertain "every d-th number starting from r" concepts — numbers leaving the same remainder r when divided by d, such as 2, 6, 10, 14, ... or 1, 4, 7, 10, ... — as a family of concepts with its own inferred prior share. Because such a progression is much smaller than the ranges or parity rules it overlaps, the size principle favours it whenever the examples share a common step, so people endorse far targets that continue the examples' spacing (e.g. 34 or 62 after 98, 54, 18) and reject nearby targets off the step, including opposite-parity ones, more sharply than a learner without these concepts.
