People do the same conservative Bayesian concept learning as the best model (42 arithmetic rules and midpoint-weighted, decade-favoured number ranges, evidence weight growing with set size, an inferred tolerance for stray examples), but the trials on which they skip their concept and simply guess (with their own leaning toward "yes") come from fatigue, not from a shortage of examples: the chance of guessing rises with how far into the session the trial falls, at an inferred rate, and does not depend on how many examples were shown. This predicts that excluded targets get more "yes" late in the session than early (the late-minus-early drift the best model misses), and — unlike the best model — that guessed "yes" answers to non-members are as common after four examples as after one at the same point in the session.
