Refinement of `evidence_scarce_guessing_concepts`: people do the same conservative Bayesian concept learning over the 42 arithmetic rules and midpoint-weighted, decade-favoured intervals of 1-100 (evidence weight growing with set size), and likewise often guess when few examples are shown, but when they do consult their concept they do not answer "yes" with probability equal to their belief that the target belongs — they turn that belief into a choice more decisively, so a target they think probably belongs is endorsed more often than its probability and one they think probably does not is endorsed less often (an inferred decision sharpness; no sharpening recovers the incumbent). The one change is this belief-to-choice sharpening, which predicts more consensual, all-or-none answers per item after two or more examples, and sharper rejection of nearby targets that break the examples' shared rule — the two discrepancies the critique of the incumbent flags.
