People do the same conservative Bayesian concept learning over arithmetic rules and decade-favoured number ranges as the simplest current model, but whether they consult that concept to answer depends on how sure they feel they have figured out the program: when their belief about the concept is spread over many candidate concepts they often give up on it and simply guess (with their own leaning toward "yes" or "no"), while when the examples point clearly to one concept they answer from it. The decision rule — guessing gated by the learner's own uncertainty about the concept, not by how many examples were shown — predicts that a set with an obvious rule (e.g. 16, 8, 64) yields sharp rejections of non-members such as opposite-parity targets, whereas an ambiguous set of the same size yields many guessed answers.
