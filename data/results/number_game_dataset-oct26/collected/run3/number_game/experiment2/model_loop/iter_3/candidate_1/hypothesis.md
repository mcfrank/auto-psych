Refinement of `outlier_tolerant_rule_concepts`: people do the same conservative Bayesian concept learning over the 42 arithmetic rules and midpoint-weighted, decade-favoured intervals of 1-100 (evidence weight growing with set size, guessing that becomes rarer as more examples are shown), but their belief that the program sometimes produces stray numbers outside its rule is learned from experience over the session: after seeing many sets whose numbers fit no neat rule, they grow steadily more willing to treat an example as an exception (an inferred starting tolerance and growth rate). The one change is this experience-growing tolerance for exceptions, which predicts that late in the session the examples rule out concepts less firmly, so generalization broadens and people say "yes" more often to targets outside the tightest concept — chiefly to targets in broader rules or ranges that contain most of the examples, not uniformly to every number as a blind-"yes" lapse would.
