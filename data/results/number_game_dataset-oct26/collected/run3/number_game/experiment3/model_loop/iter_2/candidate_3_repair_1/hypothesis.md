Refinement of `outlier_tolerant_rule_concepts`: people do the same conservative Bayesian concept learning (42 arithmetic rules, midpoint-weighted decade-favoured intervals, evidence weight growing with set size, an inferred tolerance for stray examples, guessing that becomes rarer as examples accumulate), but they can also believe a non-parity rule holds only within a round stretch of the number line — "primes from the 20s to the 40s", "multiples of 5 from 50 to 79" — a family of decade-bounded rules with its own inferred share of prior belief, split equally among them. The one change is this decade-bounded rule family: because a bounded rule is smaller than the whole rule, the size principle favours it whenever rule-sharing examples cluster, so people extend the rule to members near the examples but reject members far outside their span (e.g. fewer "yes" to 89 after 23, 29, 31) — the over-extension of prime sets to far primes the incumbent's critique flags.
