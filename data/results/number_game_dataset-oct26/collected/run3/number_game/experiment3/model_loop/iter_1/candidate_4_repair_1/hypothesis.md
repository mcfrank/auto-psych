Refinement of `outlier_tolerant_rule_concepts`: people do the same conservative Bayesian concept learning over the 42 arithmetic rules and midpoint-weighted intervals of 1-100 (evidence weight growing with set size, an inferred tolerance for stray examples, guessing that becomes rarer as more examples are shown), but they favour a number range for each of its edges that falls on a decade boundary (starting at 1, 10, 20, ... or ending at 9, 19, 29, ... 100), so a range like "23 to 29" or "20 to 25" is already a priori more plausible than "23 to 31", rather than only ranges with both edges on decade boundaries getting the bonus. The one change is this edge-by-edge decade preference, which predicts that a target just outside the examples' span is endorsed more when it shares the tens digit of the nearest example (29 after 23, 25) than when it lies equally close but across a decade boundary (21 after 31, 33 is favoured over 19 after 23, 25) — the excess same-decade generalization that the critique of the incumbent flags.
