People read the question "would the program produce this number?" literally, as a judgment of how likely the program is to output the target, not of whether the target belongs to the program's concept: after learning about the concept from the examples (the same conservative Bayesian learning over arithmetic rules and decade-favoured number ranges as the best model), they estimate how probable it is that the program would generate the target and say "yes" to the degree that this probability is as high as the probability of generating the examples they were shown. Because a small concept generates each of its members often, while a broad concept generates each member rarely, targets supported only by broad concepts are rejected even though those concepts include them: for example, in-range numbers lacking the examples' shared final digit, or far same-digit numbers once nearby ranges also explain the examples.
