People learn the program's concept by the same conservative Bayesian concept learning as the best model (arithmetic rules and decade-favoured number ranges, evidence weight growing with set size, an inferred tolerance for stray examples, guessing that becomes rarer as examples accumulate), but their space of concepts also contains bounded arithmetic progressions — "70, 80, 90, 100", "every third number from 12 to 30", "the odd numbers from 51 to 67" — evenly spaced runs with a step of 2 to 10 between a start and an end, held as a family with its own inferred share of prior belief, with smaller steps (or larger ones) favoured a priori by an inferred amount. Because such a run is far smaller than the matching range or unbounded rule, the size principle makes it win whenever the examples are evenly spaced, so people reject off-step targets inside the examples' span (e.g. 75 or 84 after 80, 70, 90, 100) much more sharply than a learner with only unbounded rules and plain ranges.
