People infer the program's concept by plain Bayesian concept learning (equally weighted arithmetic rules and equally likely number ranges, the size principle with an inferred evidence weight), but they answer by asking whether the target is *possible* rather than how probable it is: they call to mind a few candidate concepts drawn from their belief and say "yes" if any one of them contains the target, apart from occasional blind guesses. How many concepts they consider is inferred (one recovers probability matching); if several, targets covered by only a minority of plausible concepts (e.g. other evens after 16, 8, 64, or neighbours of a single example) are endorsed far more often than their posterior probability, while targets no plausible concept contains stay rejected.
