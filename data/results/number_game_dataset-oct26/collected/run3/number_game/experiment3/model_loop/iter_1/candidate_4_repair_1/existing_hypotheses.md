# Existing hypotheses

Each model below is ONE cognitive hypothesis, with how it stands on the current data by ELPD-LOO (best first). `elpd_diff ± dse` is a model's deficit against the best and the standard error of that difference (with the responses to each stimulus pair counted together, as they are correlated): within about 2·dse the two are statistically tied on this data; beyond it the model has lost. "PSIS-LOO unreliable" means the estimate itself is untrustworthy (too many high-Pareto-k trials), not that the model is bad. Propose a hypothesis that is genuinely different from these, or a refinement of a single one of them — never a combination of several.

## outlier_tolerant_rule_concepts  — rank 0, the best model on this data, ELPD-LOO -3867.6

Refinement of `evidence_scarce_guessing_concepts`: people do the same conservative Bayesian concept learning over the 42 arithmetic rules and midpoint-weighted, decade-favoured intervals of 1-100 (evidence weight growing with set size, guessing that becomes rarer as more examples are shown), but how readily they accept that an example could be a stray number the program produced outside its rule is their own inferred belief, rather than a fixed one-in-ten chance. The one change is this inferred tolerance for exceptions, which matters most for sets where all examples but one share a rule (e.g. 92, 82, 27, 42 or 71, 61, 26, 81): a tolerant learner keeps the near-fitting rule and says "yes" to far targets that fit it (52, 62; 11, 41), while an intolerant learner abandons the rule for a range spanning every example and rejects them.

## late_session_acquiescence_concepts  — rank 1, 1.7 ± 4.8 nats behind the best (0.3× dse: statistically tied with the best), ELPD-LOO -3869.3

Refinement of `outlier_tolerant_rule_concepts`: people do the same conservative Bayesian concept learning over the 42 arithmetic rules and midpoint-weighted, decade-favoured intervals of 1-100 (evidence weight growing with set size, an inferred tolerance for stray examples, guessing that becomes rarer as more examples are shown), but as the long session wears on they increasingly answer "yes" without consulting their concept at all, whatever the number of examples — a blind-"yes" acquiescence whose rate rises with how far into the session the trial falls. The one change is this session-growing acquiescence (the mechanism of `session_acquiescent_tolerant_concepts`, admitted earlier but dropped only because its fit ran too long, here computed exactly but far more cheaply), which predicts that targets the concept excludes are endorsed more late in the session than early, even after three or four examples where evidence-scarce guessing is rare — the late-minus-early "yes" drift the incumbent's critique flags.

## parity_banded_range_concepts  — rank 2, 8.5 ± 5.1 nats behind the best (1.7× dse: statistically tied with the best), ELPD-LOO -3876.1

People learn the program's concept by the same conservative Bayesian concept learning as the best model (arithmetic rules and decade-favoured number ranges, evidence weight growing with set size, an inferred tolerance for stray examples, guessing that becomes rarer as examples accumulate), but their space of concepts also contains parity-restricted ranges — "the even numbers from 20 to 40", "the odd numbers from 51 to 67" — held as a family with its own inferred share of prior belief, split equally among them. Because such a range has half the members of the plain range, the size principle favours it whenever several examples lie close together and share a parity, so people endorse same-parity targets inside and near the examples' span (e.g. 30, 36, 40 after 52, 22, 94) but reject nearby opposite-parity targets more sharply than a learner with only plain ranges and global parity rules.

## exemplar_averaged_concept_similarity  — rank 3, 181.6 ± 34.8 nats behind the best (5.2× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -4049.2

People do not infer one concept that explains all the examples together; they store each example separately and judge the target by its average similarity to the stored examples (exemplar generalization), where the similarity of the target to a single example is how readily that one number alone would generalize to the target — shared membership in familiar arithmetic categories and number ranges, with small, specific shared categories counting more than broad ones. Because each example is compared on its own, a target similar to just one example of a mixed set (e.g. 27 in 92, 82, 27, 42) earns partial "yes", and adding consistent examples does not sharpen generalization beyond what each example licenses alone.

## any_of_sampled_concepts_decision  — rank 4, 214.7 ± 36.7 nats behind the best (5.8× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -4082.4

People infer the program's concept by plain Bayesian concept learning (equally weighted arithmetic rules and equally likely number ranges, the size principle with an inferred evidence weight), but they answer by asking whether the target is *possible* rather than how probable it is: they call to mind a few candidate concepts drawn from their belief and say "yes" if any one of them contains the target, apart from occasional blind guesses. How many concepts they consider is inferred (one recovers probability matching); if several, targets covered by only a minority of plausible concepts (e.g. other evens after 16, 8, 64, or neighbours of a single example) are endorsed far more often than their posterior probability, while targets no plausible concept contains stay rejected.

## bounded_last_digit_run_concepts  — no comparison row

People learn the program's concept by the same conservative Bayesian concept learning as the best model (arithmetic rules and decade-favoured number ranges, evidence weight growing with set size, an inferred tolerance for stray examples, guessing that becomes rarer as examples accumulate), but their space of concepts also contains final-digit-restricted ranges — "the numbers ending in 2 from 22 to 62", "the numbers ending in 7 between 17 and 67" — held as a family with its own inferred share of prior belief, split equally among them. Because such a bounded digit run is far smaller than either the plain range or the unbounded final-digit rule, the size principle favours it whenever examples share a last digit, so people reject in-range targets with a different last digit more sharply, and extend the shared digit to targets far beyond the examples' span less readily, than a learner with only unbounded digit rules and plain ranges.
