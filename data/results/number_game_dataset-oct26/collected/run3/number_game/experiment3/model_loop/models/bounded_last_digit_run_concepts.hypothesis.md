People learn the program's concept by the same conservative Bayesian concept learning as the best model (arithmetic rules and decade-favoured number ranges, evidence weight growing with set size, an inferred tolerance for stray examples, guessing that becomes rarer as examples accumulate), but their space of concepts also contains final-digit-restricted ranges — "the numbers ending in 2 from 22 to 62", "the numbers ending in 7 between 17 and 67" — held as a family with its own inferred share of prior belief, split equally among them. Because such a bounded digit run is far smaller than either the plain range or the unbounded final-digit rule, the size principle favours it whenever examples share a last digit, so people reject in-range targets with a different last digit more sharply, and extend the shared digit to targets far beyond the examples' span less readily, than a learner with only unbounded digit rules and plain ranges.
