People first screen the target by how it looks when written down: if it differs in written form from every example — a different number of digits (e.g. a one-digit target after two-digit examples) or a different roundness (a target not ending in zero after examples that all end in zero, or vice versa) — they often reject it outright, without consulting the concept they have learned. Only targets that pass this surface-format screen are judged by the usual conservative Bayesian concept learning over arithmetic rules and decade-favoured number ranges (with an inferred tolerance for stray examples and guessing that becomes rarer as examples accumulate). This predicts that non-round targets after all-round sets (e.g. 35 after 20, 50, 70) and one-digit targets after high sets are rejected more often than any concept's membership implies, while targets matching the examples' written form are unaffected.
