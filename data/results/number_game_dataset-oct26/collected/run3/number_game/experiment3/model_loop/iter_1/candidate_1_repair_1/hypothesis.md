People learn the program's concept by the same conservative Bayesian concept learning as the best model (arithmetic rules and decade-favoured number ranges, evidence weight growing with set size, an inferred tolerance for stray examples, guessing that becomes rarer as examples accumulate), but when they check a target against a number range they believe in, they often read that range in whole decades: a range they think runs from 22 to 25 is taken to mean "the twenties", so any target sharing a decade with the range's ends counts as inside it, at an inferred rate. This decision-stage decade rounding disagrees most sharply with the best model on targets just outside the examples' span: it predicts "yes" to same-decade neighbours (28, 29 after 22, 25) far more often than to equally near targets across a decade boundary (19, 31), while leaving arithmetic-rule judgments unchanged.
