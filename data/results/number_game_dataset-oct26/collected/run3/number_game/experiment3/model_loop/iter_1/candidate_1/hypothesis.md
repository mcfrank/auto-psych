People do the same conservative Bayesian concept learning as the best model (arithmetic rules and decade-favoured number ranges, evidence weight growing with set size, an inferred tolerance for stray examples, guessing that becomes rarer as examples accumulate), but their concept space also contains bounded final-digit runs — "numbers ending in 4 from 24 to 64", "multiples of ten from 30 to 70" — held as a family with its own inferred share of prior belief, split equally among them. Because such a run is far smaller than the unbounded final-digit rule, the size principle favours it whenever the examples share a last digit, so this model disagrees most sharply with the best model on far targets with the examples' last digit (e.g. 94 or 4 after 34, 44, 54), which it predicts are endorsed much less often, while in-range targets with a different last digit are rejected even more firmly.
