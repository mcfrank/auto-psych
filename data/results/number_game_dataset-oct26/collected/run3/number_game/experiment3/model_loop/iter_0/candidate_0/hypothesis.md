People infer the program's concept the same way as the best current learner (conservative Bayesian learning over arithmetic rules and decade-favoured number ranges, with an inferred tolerance for stray examples and guessing when examples are few), but they answer by asking whether the target is *possible*, not how probable it is: they call to mind a few candidate concepts drawn from their belief and say "yes" if any one of them contains the target. How many concepts they consider is inferred (one recovers probability matching), so if people consider several, targets covered by a minority of plausible concepts (e.g. other evens after 16, 8, 64, or neighbours of a single example) are endorsed far more often than their posterior probability, while targets that no plausible concept contains stay rejected.
