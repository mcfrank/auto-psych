People infer the program's concept by plain Bayesian concept learning over arithmetic rules and number ranges (the size principle with an inferred evidence weight), but whether they answer from that belief depends on how sure they feel they have identified the program: the more their belief is spread across many candidate concepts, the more often they give up on it and simply guess (with their own leaning toward "yes"). This uncertainty-gated decision rule predicts sharp rejections of non-members after sets with an obvious rule (e.g. 16, 8, 64), but many guessed answers — including more "yes" to unshown numbers inside the span — after equally large patternless sets that only a broad, ambiguous family of ranges explains.
