People learn the program's concept by the same conservative Bayesian concept learning as the best model (arithmetic rules and decade-favoured number ranges, evidence weight growing with set size, an inferred tolerance for stray examples, guessing that becomes rarer as examples accumulate), but they trust a concept only near the evidence: even when the concept they believe in contains the target, the further the target lies outside the span of the examples, the less confident they are that the program's rule really reaches that far, so their endorsement fades steadily with distance beyond the nearest example. This local-extrapolation discount predicts that far targets fitting the examples' rule (e.g. primes 20 or more beyond a set of primes, or 13 after 93, 43, 83, 53) are endorsed less often than equally rule-fitting targets near or inside the examples' span, which no ideal learner, whose rule membership is the same everywhere, can produce.
