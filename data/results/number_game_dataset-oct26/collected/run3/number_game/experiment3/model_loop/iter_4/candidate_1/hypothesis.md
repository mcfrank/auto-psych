People do not infer a concept; they store each example separately and compare the target with each stored example, where the similarity of the target to one example is how readily that number alone would generalize to it (shared membership in familiar arithmetic categories and number ranges, with small, specific categories counting more). Unlike averaging, they demand that the target resemble every stored example: their judgment is dominated by the least similar example (a soft "weakest link" pooling whose strictness is inferred), so a target that resembles most examples but not one of them is rejected — e.g. non-round numbers such as 23 after 20, 40, 60 are near only one example and earn little "yes", while 50 resembles all of them through "multiples of ten".
