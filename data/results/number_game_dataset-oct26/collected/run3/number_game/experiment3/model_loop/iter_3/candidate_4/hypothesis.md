Refinement of `outlier_tolerant_rule_concepts`: people do the same conservative Bayesian concept learning over the 42 arithmetic rules and midpoint-weighted, decade-favoured intervals of 1-100 (evidence weight growing with set size, guessing that becomes rarer as more examples are shown), but their tolerance for stray examples is specific to the kind of concept: they readily accept that an example is an exception to an arithmetic rule, whereas a number range is defined by where the examples fall, so an example outside a range counts as strong evidence against it rather than as a forgivable stray. The one change is separate inferred stray-example tolerances for rules and for ranges (the incumbent shares one), which predicts that for patternless sets people rarely settle on a narrow range covering all but one example and instead generalize to the whole span of the examples, filling its gaps with "yes" more than the incumbent — the excess in-range "yes" for ruleless sets its critique flags — while near-fitting rules (52, 62 after 92, 82, 27, 42) are still kept.
