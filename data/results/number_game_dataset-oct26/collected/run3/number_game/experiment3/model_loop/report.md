## Hypotheses

- **outlier_tolerant_rule_concepts**: Refinement of `evidence_scarce_guessing_concepts`: people do the same conservative Bayesian concept learning over the 42 arithmetic rules and midpoint-weighted, decade-favoured intervals of 1-100 (evidence weight growing with set size, guessing that becomes rarer as more examples are shown), but how readily they accept that an example could be a stray number the program produced outside its rule is their own inferred belief, rather than a fixed one-in-ten chance. The one change is this inferred tolerance for exceptions, which matters most for sets where all examples but one share a rule (e.g. 92, 82, 27, 42 or 71, 61, 26, 81): a tolerant learner keeps the near-fitting rule and says "yes" to far targets that fit it (52, 62; 11, 41), while an intolerant learner abandons the rule for a range spanning every example and rejects them.
- **late_session_acquiescence_concepts**: Refinement of `outlier_tolerant_rule_concepts`: people do the same conservative Bayesian concept learning over the 42 arithmetic rules and midpoint-weighted, decade-favoured intervals of 1-100 (evidence weight growing with set size, an inferred tolerance for stray examples, guessing that becomes rarer as more examples are shown), but as the long session wears on they increasingly answer "yes" without consulting their concept at all, whatever the number of examples — a blind-"yes" acquiescence whose rate rises with how far into the session the trial falls. The one change is this session-growing acquiescence (the mechanism of `session_acquiescent_tolerant_concepts`, admitted earlier but dropped only because its fit ran too long, here computed exactly but far more cheaply), which predicts that targets the concept excludes are endorsed more late in the session than early, even after three or four examples where evidence-scarce guessing is rare — the late-minus-early "yes" drift the incumbent's critique flags.
- **width_preferring_range_concepts**: Refinement of `outlier_tolerant_rule_concepts`: people do the same conservative Bayesian concept learning over the 42 arithmetic rules and midpoint-weighted, decade-favoured number ranges (evidence weight growing with set size, an inferred tolerance for stray examples, guessing that becomes rarer as more examples are shown), but before seeing any examples they also hold a graded belief about how wide a program's number range tends to be — a smooth preference for broader (or narrower) ranges whose strength is inferred, where the incumbent treats every width alike. The one change is this width preference in the prior over ranges; if people expect a range-producing program to cover a broad stretch of numbers, then after a patternless set they rarely settle on a narrow range that leaves an example out and instead credit ranges spanning all the examples and more, filling the gaps between the examples with "yes" (e.g. 40 or 55 after 67, 31, 17, 61) more than the incumbent — the excess in-range "yes" for rule-less sets its critique flags — while sets with a clear rule are still explained by the rule.
- **rule_favoring_width_range_concepts**: Refinement of `width_preferring_range_concepts`: people do the same conservative Bayesian concept learning over the 42 arithmetic rules and midpoint-weighted, decade-favoured number ranges with an inferred preference over range width (evidence weight growing with set size, an inferred tolerance for stray examples, guessing that becomes rarer as more examples are shown), but they find it a stronger coincidence when the examples all fit an arithmetic rule than when they merely cluster within a range, so the size-principle evidence counts more for rules than for ranges by an inferred ratio (the family-specific evidence weight grafted from `family_weighted_evidence_concepts`). The one change is this rule-versus-range evidence ratio, which predicts that sets whose examples share a rule (e.g. 80, 70, 90, 100 — all multiples of ten) are explained by the rule rather than by a range spanning them, so in-span targets lacking the property (75, 85) are rejected almost always — the over-acceptance of non-round targets after all-round sets flagged in the incumbent's critique — while a broad range still explains patternless sets.
- **parity_banded_range_concepts**: People learn the program's concept by the same conservative Bayesian concept learning as the best model (arithmetic rules and decade-favoured number ranges, evidence weight growing with set size, an inferred tolerance for stray examples, guessing that becomes rarer as examples accumulate), but their space of concepts also contains parity-restricted ranges — "the even numbers from 20 to 40", "the odd numbers from 51 to 67" — held as a family with its own inferred share of prior belief, split equally among them. Because such a range has half the members of the plain range, the size principle favours it whenever several examples lie close together and share a parity, so people endorse same-parity targets inside and near the examples' span (e.g. 30, 36, 40 after 52, 22, 94) but reject nearby opposite-parity targets more sharply than a learner with only plain ranges and global parity rules.
- **parity_banded_session_acquiescence**: Refinement of `parity_banded_range_concepts`: people do the same conservative Bayesian concept learning over arithmetic rules, decade-favoured number ranges and parity-restricted ranges ("the odd numbers from 51 to 67"), with evidence weight growing with set size, an inferred tolerance for stray examples and guessing that becomes rarer as examples accumulate — but as the long session wears on they increasingly answer "yes" without consulting their concept at all, a blind-"yes" acquiescence whose rate rises with how far into the session the trial falls. The one change is this session-growing acquiescence (the component of `late_session_acquiescence_concepts`, grafted onto the parity-banded learner), which predicts that targets the concept excludes — including opposite-parity neighbours that parity-banded ranges reject sharply — are endorsed more late in the session than early for the same stimulus, the within-stimulus late-session "yes" drift the incumbent's critique flags.
- **single_exception_rule_concepts**: Refinement of `outlier_tolerant_rule_concepts`: people do the same conservative Bayesian concept learning over the 42 arithmetic rules and midpoint-weighted, decade-favoured intervals of 1-100 (evidence weight growing with set size, an inferred tolerance for a stray example, guessing that becomes rarer as more examples are shown), but they allow the program at most one exception per set: either every example came from the concept, or exactly one of them is a stray number, never two or more. The one change is this single-exception limit (replacing independent strays for each example), which stops a patternless set of three or four examples from being explained as a tight cluster plus several strays, so people credit a range spanning (nearly) all the examples and fill its gaps with "yes" — the excess in-range "yes" for patternless sets the incumbent's critique flags.
