# Existing hypotheses

Each model below is ONE cognitive hypothesis, with how it stands on the current data by ELPD-LOO (best first). `elpd_diff ± dse` is a model's deficit against the best and the standard error of that difference (with the responses to each stimulus pair counted together, as they are correlated): within about 2·dse the two are statistically tied on this data; beyond it the model has lost. "PSIS-LOO unreliable" means the estimate itself is untrustworthy (too many high-Pareto-k trials), not that the model is bad. Propose a hypothesis that is genuinely different from these, or a refinement of a single one of them — never a combination of several.

## outlier_tolerant_rule_concepts  — rank 0, the best model on this data, ELPD-LOO -3867.6

Refinement of `evidence_scarce_guessing_concepts`: people do the same conservative Bayesian concept learning over the 42 arithmetic rules and midpoint-weighted, decade-favoured intervals of 1-100 (evidence weight growing with set size, guessing that becomes rarer as more examples are shown), but how readily they accept that an example could be a stray number the program produced outside its rule is their own inferred belief, rather than a fixed one-in-ten chance. The one change is this inferred tolerance for exceptions, which matters most for sets where all examples but one share a rule (e.g. 92, 82, 27, 42 or 71, 61, 26, 81): a tolerant learner keeps the near-fitting rule and says "yes" to far targets that fit it (52, 62; 11, 41), while an intolerant learner abandons the rule for a range spanning every example and rejects them.

## parity_banded_range_concepts  — no comparison row

People learn the program's concept by the same conservative Bayesian concept learning as the best model (arithmetic rules and decade-favoured number ranges, evidence weight growing with set size, an inferred tolerance for stray examples, guessing that becomes rarer as examples accumulate), but their space of concepts also contains parity-restricted ranges — "the even numbers from 20 to 40", "the odd numbers from 51 to 67" — held as a family with its own inferred share of prior belief, split equally among them. Because such a range has half the members of the plain range, the size principle favours it whenever several examples lie close together and share a parity, so people endorse same-parity targets inside and near the examples' span (e.g. 30, 36, 40 after 52, 22, 94) but reject nearby opposite-parity targets more sharply than a learner with only plain ranges and global parity rules.

## exemplar_averaged_concept_similarity  — no comparison row

People do not infer one concept that explains all the examples together; they store each example separately and judge the target by its average similarity to the stored examples (exemplar generalization), where the similarity of the target to a single example is how readily that one number alone would generalize to the target — shared membership in familiar arithmetic categories and number ranges, with small, specific shared categories counting more than broad ones. Because each example is compared on its own, a target similar to just one example of a mixed set (e.g. 27 in 92, 82, 27, 42) earns partial "yes", and adding consistent examples does not sharpen generalization beyond what each example licenses alone.
