People do not store the individual examples or infer a rule; they abstract a single prototype of the set — its average magnitude and the proportion of examples having each arithmetic property (even, multiple of 3/4/5/10, same final digit, prime, square, power of 2) — and say "yes" to the degree that the target is similar to that prototype, with similarity decaying exponentially with the attention-weighted distance between the target's own properties and the prototype's averaged ones. So a property shared by every example acts as a strong expectation, a property split among the examples barely matters, and targets near the set's centre of magnitude (rather than near any one example) are favoured.
