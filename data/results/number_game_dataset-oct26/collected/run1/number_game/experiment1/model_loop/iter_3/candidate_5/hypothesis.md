A refinement of `shared_property_rule_matching`: people still note which arithmetic properties all the examples share (even, odd, multiple of 3/4/5/10, prime, square, power of 2, a common final digit) and say "yes" to the degree that the target has those same properties, regardless of its magnitude, but the one change is that these properties are not equally salient — each kind of property carries its own inferred weight, so whether the target matches a highly salient shared property (e.g. parity or being a power of 2) matters far more than whether it matches a weak one. This makes judgments sharper across items than equal-weight rule matching (the critique's excess item and set variance) and lets a rule such as "multiples of 10" or "powers of 2" decide the answer even when the target shares no final digit with the examples.
