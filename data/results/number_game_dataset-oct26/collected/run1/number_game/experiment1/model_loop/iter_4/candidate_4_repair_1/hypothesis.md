A refinement of `final_digit_similarity_gradient`: people still say "yes" in proportion to the target's average final-digit similarity to the examples (same digit most similar, same parity partly, opposite parity least), but the one change is that they also notice when all the examples are multiples of a common factor of 3 or more (their greatest common divisor) and give an extra, inferred boost toward "yes" to targets that are multiples of that same factor. This addresses the critique that, among parity-matched targets, people accept multiples of the examples' common divisor more than the final-digit model can represent (e.g. 36 after 12,24 or 48 after 16), so counting-by-a-factor generalization operates alongside, not instead of, digit resemblance.
