A refinement of `round_counting_privileged_factor` (final-digit similarity judged by its shortfall from the examples' mutual similarity, rule-pre-empted parity credit, rule-gated decade-band familiarity, and a separately inferred boost for round versus non-round common-factor rules, all kept). The one change is what the privileged round rule is: people do not count by the examples' exact greatest common factor when it is round (by 25s after 25, by 45s after 90, 45, by 100s after 100), but slot round examples into the overlearned counting sequence itself — if every example ends in 0 they think "counts by tens", and if every example ends in 0 or 5 they think "counts by fives". So after a lone 100, 30 or 50, every multiple of 10 (e.g. 10, 60) is accepted much more than the incumbent predicts, and after 25, 15 or 90, 45 every multiple of 5 is.
