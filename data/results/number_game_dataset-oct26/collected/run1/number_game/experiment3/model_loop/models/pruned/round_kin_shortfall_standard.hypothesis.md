A refinement of `shortfall_from_mutual_similarity` (final-digit similarity held to the standard of the examples' own mutual similarity, with only the target's shortfall below that standard counting against "yes", plus rule-pre-empted parity credit, the common-factor boost and rule-gated decade-band familiarity, all kept). The one change, a component taken from `round_digit_kinship_rule_gated`, is to the final-digit similarity metric that both the target and the standard are measured on: people treat the "round" digits 0 and 5 as kin, so a number ending in 0 is partly similar to one ending in 5 despite their opposite parity, with that kinship credit inferred. So after examples such as 5, 65 or 15, 39, 35, round targets like 50, 80, 90 or 100 fall far less short of the standard and are accepted much more than parity-based similarity predicts, while sets mixing 0- and 5-endings (e.g. 90, 100, 5) raise a higher mutual-similarity standard.
