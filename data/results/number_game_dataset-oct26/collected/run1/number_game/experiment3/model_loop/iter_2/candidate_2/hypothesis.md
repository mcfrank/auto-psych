People generalize as Bayesian concept learners, but over a small space of concepts built only from the number features they actually attend to — "ends in digit d", "even" or "odd", "multiples of k", a contiguous band of decades, or "any number at all" — weighing each concept consistent with all the examples by its prior plausibility and by the size principle (smaller concepts gain more with every additional example), and saying "yes" according to their posterior belief that the target belongs to the program's concept. So how broadly people generalize differs sharply between sets: sets that share a final digit or a large factor collapse onto that narrow concept, while sets with no shared digit, parity or factor leave only broad decade bands or "anything", and are accepted broadly.
