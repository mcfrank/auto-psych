People read two or more examples as points on a sequence the program steps through. They pick up the regular spacing between the examples (the largest step that fits every gap, e.g. 4 for 3, 7 or 15 for 98, 83) and accept any target that the same step reaches by counting up or down from the examples (11, 15, 19 after 3, 7; 68, 53 after 98, 83), even when that target shares no final digit or common factor with them. Other judgments follow the current best model: final-digit similarity measured against the examples' mutual similarity, rule-pre-empted parity credit, privileged round and other common-factor rules, rule-gated decade-band familiarity, counting up from single examples, and the size principle on a shared final digit.
