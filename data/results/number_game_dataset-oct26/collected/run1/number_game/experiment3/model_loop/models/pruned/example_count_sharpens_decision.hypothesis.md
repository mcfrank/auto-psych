A refinement of `shortfall_from_mutual_similarity` (final-digit similarity, with only the target's shortfall below the examples' mutual similarity counting against "yes", rule-pre-empted parity credit, the common-factor boost and rule-gated decade-band familiarity, all kept). The one change is to how decisively people act on that judgment: each additional example shown makes them more confident they know what the program does, so their whole judgment is sharpened by an inferred factor per extra example, and answers after three or four examples are closer to all-or-none (well-fitting targets accepted almost always, poor ones rejected almost always) than after a lone example, which leaves them uncertain and graded. This addresses the critique that people's answers to individual items are more unanimous than the incumbent's lukewarm probabilities predict.
