Among the programs people consider is a digit-reversal concept: the program writes the examples' digits backwards, so a number that is one of the examples with its two digits swapped (27 after 72, 83 after 38, 4 after 40) feels like something the program would produce. Such mirror targets get extra support toward "yes", by an inferred amount, beyond what their final digit, parity, magnitude or arithmetic relation to the examples would predict. All other judgments follow the current best model (final-digit similarity judged by its shortfall from the examples' mutual similarity, rule-pre-empted parity credit, privileged round common-factor rule, rule-gated decade-band familiarity, step-costed counting up from single examples, and the size principle on a shared final-digit rule).
