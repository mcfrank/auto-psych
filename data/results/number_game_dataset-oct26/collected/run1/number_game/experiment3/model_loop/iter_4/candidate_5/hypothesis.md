A refinement of `bayesian_digit_rule_veto` (shortfall from the examples' mutual final-digit similarity, rule-pre-empted parity credit, the privileged "even numbers" rule, separate round and non-round common-factor boosts, rule-gated decade-band familiarity, and the Bayesian "ends in digit d" belief that vetoes targets ending in another digit, all kept). The one change, a component taken from `counting_up_from_single_example`, is that people also count up mentally from each single example of 3 or more (x, 2x, 3x, ...), so a target reached this way gets extra support toward "yes" even when it is not a multiple of what all the examples share (e.g. 66 after 33, 3; 48 after 4, 16, 12), strongest for double an example and fading by an inferred cost per further counting step. The claim is that the saturating digit-rule veto and step-costed counting together explain judgments better than the incumbent's linear per-example digit penalty.
