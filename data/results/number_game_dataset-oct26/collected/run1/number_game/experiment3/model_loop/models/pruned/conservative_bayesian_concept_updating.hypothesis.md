People generalize as an ideal Bayesian concept learner whose candidate concepts are the familiar ways of describing numbers: "ends in digit d", "even" or "odd", "multiples of k", a band of consecutive decades, and "any number at all". Each concept is weighed by its prior plausibility and by how well it explains the examples, with smaller concepts favoured (the size principle). The one distortion is conservatism: people update less than Bayes' rule says, so each example counts as only an inferred fraction of a full random draw from the concept. As a result, broad concepts such as parity, wide decade bands or "any number" keep more belief after several examples than an ideal learner would leave them. Sets whose examples fit no small concept therefore stay broadly accepted, while sets that fit one tightly are judged sharply.
