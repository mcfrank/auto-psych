A refinement of `rule_gated_decade_familiarity` (final-digit similarity with rule-pre-empted parity credit, common-factor boost, and decade-band familiarity cut back when an explicit rule is found, all kept). The one change: when two or more examples show no detectable rule (mixed final digits and no common factor of 3 or more), people entertain, with an inferred probability, the hypothesis that the program simply produces any number at all; under that "anything goes" belief they say "yes" regardless of the target, so rule-less multi-example sets get a floor of acceptance that lifts most the targets that resemble the examples least (far, unmatched targets), while lone examples and rule-bearing sets keep the incumbent's judgments. This addresses the critique that people accept far, digit-unmatched targets of rule-less sets more than predicted and that generalization breadth differs between sets more than the model produces.
