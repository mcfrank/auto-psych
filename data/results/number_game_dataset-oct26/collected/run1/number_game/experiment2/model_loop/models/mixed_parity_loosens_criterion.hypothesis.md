A refinement of `final_digit_rule_suppresses_parity` (final-digit similarity with parity credit suppressed by a shared final digit, common-factor boost and decade-band familiarity all kept). The one change: whether the examples agree in parity sets how broad people think the program is — when the examples mix odd and even numbers, people conclude the program is not bound by any parity or digit rule and lower their criterion, saying "yes" more readily to every target, whereas examples that all share a parity signal a constrained program and keep the criterion strict. This addresses the critique that generalization breadth differs between sets more than the incumbent produces: mixed-parity sets (e.g. 92, 14, 20, 5) are accepted broadly while same-parity sets (e.g. 68, 58) are rejected almost everywhere outside their rule.
