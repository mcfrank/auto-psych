A refinement of `digit_similarity_with_common_factor`: people still say "yes" in proportion to the target's average final-digit similarity to the examples (same final digit most similar, same parity partly, opposite parity least), with an extra boost for multiples of the examples' common factor (3 or more). The one change is that people code magnitude coarsely by decade and treat the whole block of decades from the examples' lowest to their highest decade as territory the program covers: a target whose tens digit falls anywhere in that decade span (even in a decade no example occupies) gets the full magnitude boost, and the boost fades with each decade the target lies beyond the span's edge. This addresses the critique that people accept in-range targets more than out-of-range ones (interpolation the nearest-decade incumbent misses) while respecting decade boundaries just outside the span (29 after 31 is not treated as nearby).
