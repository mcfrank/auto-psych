A refinement of `nearest_decade_digit_common_factor`: people still say "yes" in proportion to the target's average final-digit similarity to the examples (same final digit most similar, same parity partly, opposite parity least), with an extra boost for multiples of the examples' common factor and a familiarity boost from the example whose decade is closest to the target's. The one change, a range-interpolation component taken from `common_factor_digit_range_similarity`, is that people also treat the numerical span from the smallest to the largest example as territory the program covers, so a target lying inside that span gets an extra push toward "yes" that fades smoothly with how far (in raw numerical distance, regardless of decade) the target lies beyond the span's edges. This addresses the critique that the incumbent under-produces interval generalization: targets inside the examples' range are accepted more than targets outside it even when they share no final digit with any example.
