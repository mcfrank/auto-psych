People divide attention between a number's magnitude (its decade) and its final digit, and that split is set by how many examples they have read: a single example offers no regularity among digits to notice, so people judge mostly by magnitude and readily accept other numbers in the example's decade, but each further example lets digit regularities be detected and draws attention away from magnitude toward the final digit. Apart from this set-size-driven attention shift, judgments follow the current best model (final-digit similarity with parity credit suppressed by a shared final digit, decade-band familiarity, and a common-factor boost).
