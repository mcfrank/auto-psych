A refinement of `decade_band_digit_common_factor`: people still judge the target by its final-digit similarity to the examples (same final digit most similar, same parity partly, opposite parity least), with a boost for multiples of the examples' common factor and full leading-digit familiarity anywhere in the band of decades the examples span, fading with each decade outside it. The one change is that support from similar examples has diminishing returns: the first resembling example raises the willingness to say "yes" most, and each further one adds less (a concave, power-law transform of the average similarity with an inferred exponent), rather than every resembling example adding an equal share. So a target resembling just one of several examples, such as a single final-digit match, is accepted more, relative to a target resembling all of them, than the linear-share model predicts.
