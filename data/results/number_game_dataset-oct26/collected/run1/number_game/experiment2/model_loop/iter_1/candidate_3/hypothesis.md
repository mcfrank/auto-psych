A refinement of `nearest_decade_digit_common_factor`: people still say "yes" in proportion to the target's average final-digit similarity to the examples (same final digit most similar, same parity partly, opposite parity least), with an extra boost for multiples of the examples' common factor and a leading-digit (decade) familiarity boost that decays with each decade of separation. The one change is that people code the examples' magnitudes coarsely by decade and treat the whole band of decades the examples span (from the lowest example's decade to the highest's) as covered, so the decade boost is full for any target whose decade lies inside that band and decays only with how many decades the target lies beyond it, rather than with its distance to the single nearest example's decade. This addresses the critiques that the incumbent under-predicts "yes" for targets inside the examples' range and over-predicts it for targets just across a decade boundary outside the examples.
