People judge the target by the simple final-digit similarity used in the simplest current model (same final digit most similar, same parity partly, opposite parity least), but they do not answer in graded proportion to it: they apply a firm criterion, answering "yes" almost deterministically when the target's average similarity to the examples clears it and "no" when it falls short, except on an occasional fraction of trials where attention lapses and they answer by a coin flip. This all-or-none decision rule with lapses predicts more polarized item-level yes rates than a graded similarity response.
