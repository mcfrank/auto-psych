Refinement of inflated_size_familiar_rule_prior: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over 42 rules (with a prior that rises as a power of a rule's size) and all ranges of 1-100, with tempered evidence, a re-weighted first example, a decision gain and shifted criterion, and they still perceive concepts as larger than they really are in the size principle. The one change is that this perceived-size inflation is not one shared constant: people inflate the size of arithmetic rules and of magnitude ranges by two separately inferred numbers of members, so the bias that blunts tiny rules ("powers of 8") can differ from the one that blunts narrow ranges (e.g. 14-18), letting generalization along the number line widen (or stay tight) independently of how strongly tiny rules lose to larger ones; equal inflations recover the incumbent.
