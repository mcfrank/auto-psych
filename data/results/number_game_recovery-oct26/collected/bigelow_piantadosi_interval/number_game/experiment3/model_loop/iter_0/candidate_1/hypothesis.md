People do the same Bayesian concept learning as the best model (weighted rules with a size-dependent prior and all ranges of 1-100, perceived concept sizes inflated by a constant, evidence tempered separately for rules and ranges, a stronger- or weaker-weighted first example, a decision gain and shifted criterion), but their space of concepts also contains disjunctions of two familiar rules — "ends in 2 or ends in 7", "multiples of 8 or multiples of 9", "powers of 2 or squares" — which together take an inferred, a-priori small share of the rule belief, each union as plausible as its two rules picked independently. When the examples are not all covered by one tight rule but are by a union of two (92, 82, 27, 42; 8, 24, 48, 63), people generalize to the other members of both component rules rather than writing the odd example off as noise or falling back on a broad range; a zero share recovers the best model.
