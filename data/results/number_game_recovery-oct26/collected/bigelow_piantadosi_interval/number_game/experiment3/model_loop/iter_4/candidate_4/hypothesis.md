Refinement of uneven_rule_familiarity_ends. People do the same Bayesian concept learning over 42 unevenly familiar rules and all ranges of 1-100, with inflated perceived concept sizes, tempered evidence, a re-weighted first and last example and a decision gain. The one change, taken from adaptive_breadth_criterion, is to the yes/no criterion. It is no longer fixed: people calibrate it to how broad the concept they have inferred is, that is, to what share of the numbers 1-100 they expect the program to produce. So the same belief that a target belongs earns more yeses after sets that point to a broad concept ("even numbers", a wide range) than after sets that point to a narrow one ("powers of 2", a tight cluster), or fewer if the inferred slope runs the other way. A slope of zero recovers the incumbent.
