People infer the program's concept by the same Bayesian concept learning as the best model, but they do not read off their degree of belief when answering: they bring to mind a small number of concepts drawn from that belief and say "yes" only if at least a set number of the imagined concepts contain the target (a sample-and-count threshold). Because the answer is a vote among a few mental samples, the yes rate rises as a different power of belief at its two ends — weakly believed targets are rejected far more steeply than strongly believed ones are doubted, or vice versa, depending on how many samples must agree — unlike a symmetric gain and criterion on the log-odds; one sample with one required vote recovers plain probability matching.
