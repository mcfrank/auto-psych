Refinement of inflated_size_familiar_rule_prior: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over 42 weighted rules (with a prior that rises as an inferred power of each rule's size) and all ranges of 1-100 (evidence tempered separately for rules and ranges, a stronger- or weaker-weighted first example, a decision gain and shifted criterion), and they still perceive concepts as larger than they really are in the size principle. The one change is that this perceived-size inflation is not a single constant shared by every kind of concept: people misjudge the size of an arithmetic rule ("powers of 4", "multiples of 8") by one inferred number of extra members and the size of a magnitude range (e.g. 60-70) by a separate one, so how much tiny rules lose their edge over larger rules is decoupled from how much narrow ranges lose their edge over wide ranges (e.g. strong broadening to "even numbers" after 64, 4 alongside tight magnitude generalization after 83, 38, 58, 68, or the reverse); equal inflations recover the incumbent.
