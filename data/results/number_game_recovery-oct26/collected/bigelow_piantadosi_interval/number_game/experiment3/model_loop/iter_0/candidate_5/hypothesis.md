Refinement of volatile_changepoint_inflated_size (statistically tied with the best model): people still read the examples one at a time, updating a Bayesian belief over 42 weighted rules (with a rule-size prior) and all ranges of 1-100 while allowing that the program may switch concept between outputs, with tempered rule and range evidence, first-example weighting, a decision gain and a shifted criterion, and they still perceive concepts as larger than they really are. The one change is that this perceived-size inflation is not one constant shared by all concepts: a magnitude range, a fuzzy-edged stretch of the number line, is inflated by its own inferred number of members, separate from the inflation of a discrete arithmetic rule. So how strongly a tight range around the examples beats a wide one (how far people generalize along the number line, e.g. 60 after 52, 57, 55) is decoupled from how strongly "powers of 2" beats "even numbers"; equal inflations recover volatile_changepoint_inflated_size.
