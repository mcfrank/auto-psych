Refinement of inflated_size_familiar_rule_prior: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over 42 weighted rules (whose prior rises as an inferred power of their size) and all ranges of 1-100, with evidence tempered separately for rules and ranges, a re-weighted first example, a decision gain and a shifted criterion, and they still perceive concepts as larger than they really are in the size principle. The one change is that this perceived-size inflation is not shared: arithmetic rules are open-ended in the mind ("powers of 2" or "ends in 4" go on past 100), so rules get their own inferred number of extra members, while bounded magnitude ranges, whose edges are given, get a separate (possibly near-zero) inflation. Rules can then lose most of the size-principle edge of tiny rules over larger familiar ones without loosening how sharply clustered examples pick out a narrow range. So after all-power-of-2 sets (64, 4; 8, 64; 2, 8; 16, 32, 2, 8) people generalize to "ends in 4", "multiples of 8" and other even numbers more, and to the remaining powers less, than the incumbent predicts, while range generalization stays tight. Equal inflations recover the incumbent.
