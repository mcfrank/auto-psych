People infer which concept the program uses just as the best model does (Bayesian weighing of familiar arithmetic rules and numeric ranges), but when they judge a target they do not add up the belief of every concept that contains it. Instead, as in single-category reasoning, they compare the best explanation that includes the target with the best explanation overall, softened by an inferred degree of focus on the leading concepts. So a target that only many individually weak concepts support (such as a nearby number covered by many ranges, none of them dominant) gets fewer yeses than averaged belief predicts, while a target inside the one leading concept keeps its yeses; with no extra focus the judgment is ordinary Bayesian averaging.
