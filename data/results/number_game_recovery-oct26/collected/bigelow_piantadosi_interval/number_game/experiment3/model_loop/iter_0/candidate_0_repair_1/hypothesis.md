People infer the program's concept by the same Bayesian weighing of rules and numeric ranges as the simplest current model, but they answer by a batch check rather than by reading off their belief: they bring to mind small batches of concepts drawn from that belief, accept a batch only if every concept in it contains the target, and say "yes" if any of a few batches passes. Strict batches make weakly believed targets rejected far more steeply than plain belief would suggest, while checking several batches rescues strongly believed targets, so the two ends of the belief-to-answer mapping have independent steepness — unlike a symmetric gain and criterion on the log-odds; one batch of one concept recovers probability matching.
