Refinement of inflated_size_familiar_rule_prior. People infer the program's concept exactly as in that model: Bayesian concept learning over 42 rules, with a prior that rises as a power of rule size, and all ranges of 1-100, with perceived sizes inflated by a constant, tempered evidence, a re-weighted first example, a decision gain and a shifted criterion. The one change is in how they then check a new target against a rule they believe in. For rules that can be read off the digits (even, odd, ends in or contains a digit, multiples of 5, 10 or 11, powers of 10), the check never fails. For rules that need arithmetic (powers, other multiples, squares, cubes, primes), the check succeeds only with an inferred probability. Targets that were shown as examples are always recognized.

So even people who believe "powers of 2" or "powers of 4" give fewer yeses to that rule's unseen members than to other even numbers or same-final-digit numbers (16 after 64, 4 or after 2, 8, compared with 14, 24 or 62). This addresses the critique that the incumbent generalizes too readily to the remaining powers of 2. A check success rate of one recovers the incumbent. Unlike the earlier fallible-verification proposal, which was never scored because it was too slow on a volatile base, failures here depend on whether the check needs calculation, not on the rule's size.
