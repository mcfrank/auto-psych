Refinement of uneven_rule_familiarity_ends (the incumbent): people do the same Bayesian concept learning over 42 unevenly familiar rules and all ranges of 1-100, with inflated perceived concept sizes, separately tempered rule and range evidence, re-weighted first and last examples, and a decision gain with a shifted criterion. The one change: an arithmetic rule only comes to mind when people can see a pattern shared across examples, so the share of belief they give rules rather than magnitude ranges grows with the number of examples shown (by an inferred slope on the log number of examples). After a single example people therefore reason mostly by magnitude and rarely generalize to distant numbers that merely share a rule with it (e.g. 64 after 16), while with three or four examples rules dominate; a zero slope recovers the incumbent.
