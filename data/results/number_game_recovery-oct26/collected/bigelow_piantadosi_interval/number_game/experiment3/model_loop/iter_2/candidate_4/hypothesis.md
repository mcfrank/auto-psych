Refinement of inflated_size_familiar_rule_prior: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over 42 weighted rules (with a prior that rises as a power of rule size) and all ranges of 1-100, with perceived concept sizes inflated by a constant, evidence tempered separately for rules and ranges, a re-weighted first example, a decision gain and a shifted criterion. The one change is to the size principle. People notice that the program never repeats a number within a set, so they treat each example as drawn from the concept's members that have not yet been shown, not from all its members. Each further example therefore counts as stronger evidence for a small concept that it is close to exhausting: "powers of 4" after 4, 16, 64, or a tight range around clustered examples such as 52, 55, 57. Broad concepts are barely affected. So the advantage of small concepts grows faster than in proportion to the number of examples. Like the size inflation, this effect matters more the more examples are shown, but it works in the opposite direction. It adds no free parameter, and sampling with replacement recovers the incumbent.
