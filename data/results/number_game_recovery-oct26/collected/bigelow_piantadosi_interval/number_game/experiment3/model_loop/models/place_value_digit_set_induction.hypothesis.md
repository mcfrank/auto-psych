People do not think about these numbers arithmetically or as magnitudes on a number line; they treat each number as a two-digit written string and infer which tens digits and which final digits the program is allowed to write, favouring the smallest digit sets that cover the examples (the size principle over a tens-digit-by-final-digit grid). A target is endorsed to the degree that both its tens digit and its final digit are believed allowed, so after 2, 8 people accept 62 or 88 (seen final digits) but reject 16, and after 64, 4 they accept 14, 24, 94 but not 16, generalizing by shared digits rather than by powers or multiples.
