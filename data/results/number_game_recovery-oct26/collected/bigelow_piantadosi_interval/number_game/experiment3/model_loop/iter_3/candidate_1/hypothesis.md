People judge how tightly a set of examples clusters on a mental number line that is partitioned into decades: each decade boundary (between 10 and 11, 20 and 21, ...) a stretch of numbers crosses adds an inferred amount of extra psychological distance, so a magnitude range that stays inside one decade feels tighter than an equally wide one that straddles a boundary. Inferring the program's concept otherwise as the best model does, the size principle therefore favours within-decade ranges around the examples, and people say yes more to nearby targets in the examples' own decade than to equally near targets just across a decade boundary; no extra distance recovers the best model.
