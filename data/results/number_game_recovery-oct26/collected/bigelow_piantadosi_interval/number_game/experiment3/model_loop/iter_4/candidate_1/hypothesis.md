People do not infer a bounded numeric range when they generalize by magnitude. Instead they abstract a prototype of the examples: a central number on the mental number line with a graded similarity gradient around it, whose location and breadth they fit to where the examples lie and how tightly they cluster. They endorse a target in proportion to its similarity to that prototype, with arithmetic rules ("powers of 2", "ends in 4") competing with the prototype as in the best model. So generalization fades smoothly with distance from the examples' centre rather than stopping at an edge, and an example far from the centre is itself endorsed less than one at the centre.
