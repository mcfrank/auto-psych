People infer the program's concept as the best model does (Bayesian weighing of weighted rules and numeric ranges, with inflated perceived concept sizes and a size-dependent rule prior), but when they check whether a new target belongs to a rule they believe in, the check can fail. Unseen members of small, sparse rules are often not recognised as members ("is 32 a power of 2?", "is 16 a power of 4?"), while members of large, dense rules are ("is 48 even?", "does 24 end in 4?"). Recognition rises with how many numbers the rule contains, and examples they were shown are always recognised. This disagrees most sharply with the best model on the unseen members of tiny rules after sets that fit them (16 or 32 after 8, 64; 16 after 64, 4 or 2, 8): even when people believe "powers of 2", they say yes to the remaining powers of 2 less often than to other even numbers. Ranges are checked exactly, and perfect recognition recovers the best model.
