Refinement of conservative_partial_belief_updating: people still read the examples one at a time and revise their belief conservatively. After the first example (Bayesian concept learning over 42 weighted rules with a size-dependent prior and all ranges of 1-100, with tempered evidence, a re-weighted first example, a decision gain and a shifted criterion), each later example moves their belief only an inferred fraction of the way from their previous belief toward the full Bayesian update on that example. The one change, taken from inflated_concept_size_principle, is that in the size principle people perceive every concept as larger than it really is by an inferred number of members. This shrinks the advantage of tiny rules ("powers of 2", "powers of 8") over larger consistent ones. One evidence weight serves rules and ranges alike, because their separate weights were estimated as nearly equal. Unlike the volatile-changepoint model, where recent examples count most, conservative updating lets the early examples dominate. So broad concepts consistent with the first example (e.g. "even numbers" after 16 first) keep belief after later examples single out a tiny rule. An update fraction of one approximately recovers the incumbent inflated_size_familiar_rule_prior, and inflation near zero recovers conservative_partial_belief_updating.
