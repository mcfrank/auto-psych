Refinement of serial_position_ends_inflated_size: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over 42 rules and all ranges of 1-100, with inflated perceived concept sizes, evidence tempered separately for rules and ranges, the first and last examples re-weighted, and a decision gain with a shifted criterion. The one change is to how familiar each rule seems before any examples: a rule's prior plausibility is a power of its size times an idiosyncratic familiarity factor, and all rules draw these factors from one shared spread whose width is inferred. The incumbent instead gave each rule its own weight under a fixed prior that pulls every weight toward the others. So people can treat a few rules as strongly familiar ("even numbers", "multiples of 10", "powers of 2") and many others as barely entertained, far more unevenly than the incumbent allows. A spread near zero makes familiarity depend on rule size alone.
