People do the same Bayesian concept learning as the best model (42 rules with a size-dependent prior and all ranges of 1-100, perceived concept sizes inflated by a constant, evidence tempered separately for rules and ranges, a re-weighted first example, a decision gain and shifted criterion), but with one distortion: when asked about a number they cannot help imagining it as one of the program's outputs, so the queried target itself counts as an extra piece of evidence about the concept, with an inferred weight (positive is a confirmation bias, negative a discounting of the probe). Because this evidence follows the size principle, it inflates (or deflates) yeses most for targets that fall in small, tight concepts, such as the remaining powers of 2 after 16, 8, 64 or a number inside a narrow cluster, and barely moves targets supported only by broad concepts like "even numbers" or wide ranges; a zero weight recovers the best model.
