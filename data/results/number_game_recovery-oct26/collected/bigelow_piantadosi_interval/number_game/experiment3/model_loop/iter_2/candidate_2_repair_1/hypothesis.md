People's prior belief over arithmetic rules is not a list of separately learned familiarities for each rule; it comes from a simple rule grammar: they first pick a kind of rule (powers of a number, multiples of a number, ends in a digit, contains a digit, primes, odd/even, squares, cubes), every kind equally likely, and then fill in its number or digit with no preference among the options. Everything else is the Bayesian concept learning of the best model (all ranges of 1-100, perceived concept sizes inflated by a constant, tempered evidence, a re-weighted first example, a decision gain and shifted criterion), so "powers of 2" is exactly as plausible beforehand as "powers of 7", and a one-of-a-kind rule ("primes", "squares") starts with far more belief than any single "multiples of n" or "ends in d" rule.
