Refinement of inflated_size_familiar_rule_prior: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over 42 weighted rules and all ranges of 1-100 (evidence tempered separately for rules and ranges, a stronger- or weaker-weighted first example, a rule prior rising as a power of rule size, a decision gain and shifted criterion), and in the size principle they still perceive every concept as larger than it is by an inferred constant number of members, so tiny rules like "powers of 2" or "powers of 4" lose much of their edge over "even numbers" and ranges. The one change, taken from volatile_concept_changepoint, is that they read the examples one at a time and believe the program may switch concept between outputs (with an inferred switch probability), so each new example's evidence partly overwrites the earlier ones rather than accumulating; together with the perceived-size inflation this predicts that after all-power-of-2 sets people say yes to the remaining powers of 2 even less, relative to other even numbers, than either source model predicts, and a switch probability of zero recovers inflated_size_familiar_rule_prior.
