Refinement of anchored_range_bounded_rules: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over 42 weighted rules, their range-bounded runs (e.g. "multiples of 10 from 70 to 100", or "powers of 2 up to 16") and all ranges of 1-100, with evidence tempered separately for arithmetic concepts and for plain ranges, and answer through the same decision gain and shifted criterion; the first example shown still anchors the inference. The one change is how it anchors: instead of a flat belief boost for every concept containing the first example, the first example is weighed as a stronger (or weaker) draw from the concept than later examples, by an inferred multiplier on its size-principle evidence, so the anchor pulls belief most toward the tightest concepts that contain it — a short bounded run around the first example rather than the whole rule — which makes people generalize less to rule members far from the examples (e.g. 64 after 2, 4, 8) than the unbounded-rule incumbent predicts.
