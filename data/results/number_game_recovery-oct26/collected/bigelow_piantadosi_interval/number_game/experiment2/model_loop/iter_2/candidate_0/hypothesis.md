People store each familiar number category (mathematical rules such as "powers of 2" or "multiples of 10", and numeric ranges) as the collection of its member numbers, and judge both the examples and the target by exemplar similarity to those stored members rather than by all-or-none membership: a number fits a category to the degree that it lies close on the number line to the category's nearest stored member, with the fit falling off exponentially at an inferred rate. Categories whose stored members closely match all the examples — diluted by how many members the category stores — are retrieved most strongly, and the target is endorsed in proportion to its similarity to the stored members of the retrieved categories, so near-misses (30 after 16, 8, 64; 51 just past a range of examples) receive graded partial yeses instead of none.
