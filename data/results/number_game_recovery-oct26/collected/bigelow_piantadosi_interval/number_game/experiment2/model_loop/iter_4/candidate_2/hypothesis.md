People do not represent magnitude concepts as crisp ranges with hard edges: a numeric range is a similarity neighbourhood on the mental number line, so a number belongs to it fully if it lies inside and only partially otherwise, with membership falling off exponentially with its numerical distance to the range's nearest edge at an inferred gradient. This graded similarity governs both how well a range explains the examples (a range can account for a nearby example lying just outside it, and its fringe counts toward its size) and how far people generalize from it, so targets just past the examples' span get yeses that fade smoothly with distance, while arithmetic rules are still judged by exact membership (everything else — weighted rules with a size-dependent prior, evidence tempered separately for rules and ranges, a stronger-weighted first example, decision gain and criterion — as in rule_size_prior_anchored_evidence, which a zero gradient width recovers).
