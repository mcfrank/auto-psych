People assume the program's examples were chosen by a helpful teacher rather than drawn at random from the concept: they reason that a teacher would pick, for each example, a member of the concept that most clearly points a naive learner toward that concept. So when inferring the concept they ask not just "does this concept contain the examples?" but "would someone who had this concept in mind have chosen these particular examples to show it?" — an inferred strength of this pedagogical reasoning makes examples that are diagnostic of a concept (rather than shared by many concepts) count as stronger evidence for it, and with zero strength people reason from ordinary random sampling.
