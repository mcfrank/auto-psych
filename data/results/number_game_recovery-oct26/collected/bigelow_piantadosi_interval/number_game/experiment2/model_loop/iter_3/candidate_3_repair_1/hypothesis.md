Refinement of rule_size_prior_anchored_evidence: people do the same Bayesian concept learning (42 rules with a size-dependent prior and all ranges of 1-100, evidence tempered separately for rules and ranges, a stronger-weighted first example, a decision gain and shifted criterion), but how often they believe the program emits a stray number that does not belong to its concept is their own inferred belief rather than a fixed 10%. The one change, taken from inferred_example_noise_belief, is this inferred stray-example rate: a high rate lets a tight rule survive an example that breaks it (e.g. "ends in 2" after 92, 82, 27, 42, or "powers of 2" after 16, 8, 2, 30) and flattens the size advantage of tiny concepts, while a low rate forces every example to be covered, so it changes which concept people infer mainly on sets containing an odd-one-out; a 10% rate recovers the incumbent.
