Refinement of split_rule_range_tempering: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over the 42 weighted rules and all intervals of 1-100 (size principle, 10% noisy examples, evidence tempered separately for rules and for ranges) and answer through the same decision gain and shifted criterion, but they do not consider all numeric ranges equally plausible before seeing any examples. The one change is that a range's prior belief rises or falls as an inferred power of its width, so people may favour broad magnitude ranges (or narrow ones) a priori, independently of how many examples they have seen — unlike evidence tempering, whose effect grows with set size; an exponent of zero recovers the refined model.
