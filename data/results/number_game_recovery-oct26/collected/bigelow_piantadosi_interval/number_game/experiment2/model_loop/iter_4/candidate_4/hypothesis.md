Refinement of volatile_concept_changepoint: people still read the examples one at a time and update their belief about the program's concept after each one, allowing with an inferred switch probability that the program has re-drawn its concept between outputs (weighted rules and all ranges of 1-100, evidence tempered separately for rules and ranges, a stronger- or weaker-weighted first example, a rule-size prior, decision gain and shifted criterion). The one change, taken from inflated_concept_size_principle, is that in the size principle they perceive every concept as larger than it really is by an inferred constant number of members, so tiny rules like "powers of 2" or "powers of 4" lose much of their per-example advantage over larger consistent concepts (even numbers, ranges); after all-power-of-2 sets people therefore generalize to the remaining powers of 2 less sharply relative to other even numbers than the incumbent predicts, and zero inflation recovers the incumbent.
