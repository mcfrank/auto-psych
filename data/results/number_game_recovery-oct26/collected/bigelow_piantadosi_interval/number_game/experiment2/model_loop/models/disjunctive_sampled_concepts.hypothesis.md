People do not consult a full, graded belief over all concepts; instead they bring to mind a small handful of candidate concepts (mathematical rules or numeric ranges), each recalled in proportion to how plausible it is given the examples (size principle, tempered evidence), and answer "yes" if the target fits any one of the concepts they recalled, occasionally answering at random instead. Because the check is disjunctive, a target supported by even a modestly plausible concept gets many yeses (the more concepts recalled, the more), while the yes rate saturates for well-supported targets — an asymmetric belief-to-answer mapping rather than a symmetric gain and criterion shift on the log-odds.
