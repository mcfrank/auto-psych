# Existing hypotheses

Each model below is ONE cognitive hypothesis, with how it stands on the current data by ELPD-LOO (best first). `elpd_diff ± dse` is a model's deficit against the best and the standard error of that difference (with the responses to each stimulus pair counted together, as they are correlated): within about 2·dse the two are statistically tied on this data; beyond it the model has lost. "PSIS-LOO unreliable" means the estimate itself is untrustworthy (too many high-Pareto-k trials), not that the model is bad. Propose a hypothesis that is genuinely different from these, or a refinement of a single one of them — never a combination of several.

## first_example_evidence_weighting  — rank 0, the best model on this data, ELPD-LOO -1967.1

Refinement of first_example_anchored_concepts: people still let the first example they see anchor their Bayesian concept learning over weighted rules and numeric ranges (with evidence tempered separately for rules and ranges, and a decision gain and shifted criterion), but the anchoring works through evidence rather than a flat bonus: the first example is weighed as a stronger (or weaker) draw from the concept than later examples, by an inferred multiplier on its size-principle evidence. The one change replaces the incumbent's equal boost to every concept containing the first example with this extra evidential weight, so the first example pulls belief most strongly toward small, specific concepts that contain it (e.g. "powers of 2" when 16 is shown first) and only weakly toward broad ones like "even numbers" or wide ranges, while concepts that must write it off as noise are penalised more.

## first_example_anchored_concepts  — rank 1, 2.7 ± 1.7 nats behind the best (1.5× dse: statistically tied with the best), ELPD-LOO -1969.8

People infer the program's concept by the same Bayesian weighing of mathematical rules and numeric ranges as the best model, but the first example they see anchors that inference: they treat it as the defining instance of the program, so every concept containing the first example gets extra belief, while concepts that would have to dismiss it as a stray number are disfavoured more than concepts that dismiss a later example. The same numbers shown in a different order therefore generalize differently — an odd-one-out shown first pulls belief toward concepts that include it, while the same odd-one-out shown last is readily written off.

## anchored_range_bounded_rules  — rank 2, 3.5 ± 2.1 nats behind the best (1.6× dse: statistically tied with the best), ELPD-LOO -1970.6

Refinement of range_bounded_rules_split_tempering: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over 42 weighted rules, their range-bounded runs (e.g. "multiples of 10 from 70 to 100") and all ranges of 1-100, with evidence tempered separately for arithmetic concepts and for plain ranges, and answer through the same decision gain and shifted criterion. The one change, taken from first_example_anchored_concepts, is that the first example shown anchors the inference: every concept that contains it (any rule, bounded run or range) gets an inferred boost in belief, while concepts that would have to write it off as a stray number are disfavoured, so the same numbers in a different order generalize differently and a tight, range-bounded rule built around the first example is favoured; zero boost recovers the refined model.

## split_rule_range_tempering  — rank 3, 4.4 ± 2.6 nats behind the best (1.7× dse: statistically tied with the best), ELPD-LOO -1971.6

Refinement of criterion_shifted_tempered_decision: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over the 42 weighted rules and all intervals of 1-100 (size principle, 10% noisy examples, tempered evidence) and answer through the same decision gain and shifted criterion, but they do not weigh the examples' evidence equally strongly for the two kinds of concept. The one change is that the size principle is tempered separately, by its own inferred weight, for mathematical rules and for numeric ranges: people may treat examples as strong, informative samples of an arithmetic rule (locking onto "powers of 2" after 16, 8, 64) while treating them as only weak evidence about where a magnitude range starts and stops (so range-based generalization stays broad), or vice versa; equal weights recover the incumbent.

## range_bounded_rules_split_tempering  — rank 4, 5.0 ± 3.0 nats behind the best (1.6× dse: statistically tied with the best), ELPD-LOO -1972.1

Refinement of range_bounded_rule_concepts: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over 42 weighted rules, their range-bounded versions (any unbroken run of a rule's members, e.g. "multiples of 10 from 70 to 100", taking an inferred share of each rule's prior) and all ranges of 1-100, and answer through the same decision gain and shifted criterion. The one change, taken from split_rule_range_tempering, is that the examples' evidence is tempered by separate inferred weights for arithmetic concepts (rules and their bounded runs) and for plain magnitude ranges, so people can lock onto a tight, possibly range-bounded rule from a few examples while still treating the same examples as weak evidence about where a plain range stops (equal weights recover range_bounded_rule_concepts).

## width_prior_split_tempering  — rank 5, 5.4 ± 2.7 nats behind the best (2.0× dse: statistically tied with the best), ELPD-LOO -1972.5

Refinement of split_rule_range_tempering: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over the 42 weighted rules and all intervals of 1-100 (size principle, 10% noisy examples, evidence tempered separately for rules and for ranges) and answer through the same decision gain and shifted criterion, but they do not consider all numeric ranges equally plausible before seeing any examples. The one change is that a range's prior belief rises or falls as an inferred power of its width, so people may favour broad magnitude ranges (or narrow ones) a priori, independently of how many examples they have seen — unlike evidence tempering, whose effect grows with set size; an exponent of zero recovers the refined model.

## parity_restricted_rules  — rank 6, 8.0 ± 4.1 nats behind the best (2.0× dse: statistically tied with the best), ELPD-LOO -1975.1

People do the same Bayesian concept learning over weighted mathematical rules and numeric ranges as the best models (size principle with tempered evidence, a decision gain and shifted criterion turning belief into a yes/no answer), but their space of rules also contains each rule restricted to odd or to even numbers — "odd multiples of 3", "even squares", "odd numbers containing a 7" — which take an inferred share of their parent rule's prior belief. Because such a parity-restricted rule is half the size, the size principle favours it whenever all the examples share a parity, so after 63, 27, 99, 33 people endorse other odd multiples of 3 but reject even ones like 18 or 24.

## criterion_shifted_tempered_decision  — rank 7, 8.6 ± 4.7 nats behind the best (1.8× dse: statistically tied with the best), ELPD-LOO -1975.7

Refinement of tempered_evidence_decision_gain: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over the 42 weighted rules and all intervals of 1-100 (size principle, 10% noisy examples, examples' evidence tempered by an inferred weight) and pass their belief through the same inferred decision gain on its log-odds, but their yes/no criterion is not centred on even odds. The one change, taken from response_bias_concept_learning, is an inferred response bias that shifts the decision criterion by a constant on the log-odds scale, so the gain decides how sharply people separate strong from weak beliefs while the bias decides whether they lean toward "yes" or "no" — e.g. a liberal criterion gives more yeses to targets with middling or weak belief without making yeses to the examples themselves less likely.

## round_landmark_interval_prior  — rank 8, 10.0 ± 4.6 nats behind the best (2.2× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -1977.2

People do the same Bayesian concept learning over weighted mathematical rules and numeric ranges as the best model (size principle with tempered evidence, a decision gain and criterion turning belief into a yes/no answer), but the ranges they entertain are not all equally natural: ranges whose boundaries fall on round decade landmarks (such as 1-50, 20-29, 61-100 or "the 80s") get extra prior belief, by an inferred factor, over ranges with arbitrary edges like 23-87. Generalization from examples therefore tends to stop at, or extend out to, the nearest round decade boundary rather than tracking the examples' exact span.

## stochastic_example_forgetting  — rank 9, 10.8 ± 4.7 nats behind the best (2.3× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -1978.0

People do the same Bayesian concept learning as the best model (weighted rules and all intervals of 1-100, size principle with tempered evidence, and a decision gain and criterion turning belief into a yes/no answer), but their memory of the examples is unreliable: by the time they judge a target, each example has been retained only with an inferred probability, and they infer the concept from whichever examples they still hold. Their belief is therefore an average over the possible remembered subsets of the set, so larger sets are sometimes judged as if from fewer examples (broader generalization) and an odd-one-out example is sometimes forgotten, letting the tight rule the other examples share (e.g. "ends in 2" after 92, 82, 27, 42) survive without being explained away as noise.

## range_bounded_rule_concepts  — rank 10, 11.4 ± 5.0 nats behind the best (2.3× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -1978.5

People do the same Bayesian concept learning as the best model (42 weighted rules and all intervals of 1-100, size principle with tempered evidence, a decision gain and response bias turning belief into a yes/no answer), but their rules can also be bounded to a range: besides "multiples of 10" they entertain "multiples of 10 from 70 to 100", i.e. any unbroken run of a rule's members, which takes an inferred share of each rule's prior belief. Because a range-bounded rule is smaller, the size principle favours it whenever the examples cluster, so this model disagrees most sharply with the best model on rule-consistent targets far outside the examples' range (e.g. 10 after 80, 70, 90, 100, or 6 after 96, 86, 36, 66), which it predicts far fewer yeses to, while keeping rule-violating targets inside the range at near-zero.

## criterion_shifted_redundant_examples  — rank 11, 27.1 ± 7.5 nats behind the best (3.6× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -1994.2

Refinement of criterion_shifted_tempered_decision: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over the 42 weighted rules and all intervals of 1-100 (size principle, 10% noisy examples, tempered evidence) and turn their belief into a yes/no answer through the same decision gain and shifted (biased) criterion on its log-odds, but they treat additional examples as partly redundant rather than as independent draws. The one change, taken from redundant_examples_decision_gain, is that the total evidence from a set grows as an inferred power of the number of examples instead of in proportion to it, so with sublinear growth one example already supports a fairly tight concept while three or four sharpen generalization less than strong sampling predicts, while the criterion shift separately sets the overall lean toward "yes" (an exponent of one recovers the incumbent).

## probability_threshold_decision  — rank 12, 34.9 ± 8.2 nats behind the best (4.2× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -2002.0

People infer the program's concept exactly as the simplest current model does (Bayesian weighing of weighted mathematical rules and numeric ranges, with tempered evidence), but they answer by comparing their degree of belief that the target belongs against a fixed certainty threshold on the plain probability scale, with comparison noise of constant size in probability units. Because the noise is constant in probability rather than in log-odds, all weakly believed targets (belief 1% or 15%) get nearly the same small floor of yeses and all strongly believed targets (85% or 99%) nearly the same ceiling, while answers change steeply only for beliefs near the threshold — unlike a gain and criterion on log-odds, which keep distinguishing 1% from 15%.

## strong_weak_sampling_mixture  — rank 14, 55.2 ± 12.2 nats behind the best (4.5× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -2022.4

Refinement of split_rule_range_tempering: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over the 42 weighted rules and all intervals of 1-100 (10% noisy examples), judge the evidence separately for rules and for ranges, and answer through the same decision gain and shifted criterion. The one change is how the size principle is weakened: instead of discounting every example's evidence by a power, people believe each example was either drawn from the concept itself or picked at random from 1-100 and merely found to fit it (a mixture of strong and weak sampling, with an inferred strong-sampling share for rules and another for ranges). The size penalty therefore saturates — tiny concepts are still sharply distinguished from each other, but a large concept consistent with the examples (e.g. "multiples of 8" after 8, 64) is barely penalised relative to a tiny one, so generalization to larger consistent rules and wide ranges stays strong.

## win_stay_lose_shift_criterion_shift  — rank 15, 81.8 ± 15.9 nats behind the best (5.1× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -2048.9

Refinement of win_stay_lose_shift_decision_gain: people still hold a single running guess about the concept (drawn from the tempered Bayesian posterior over weighted rules and intervals after the first example, kept while later examples fit it, redrawn from the current posterior when an example contradicts it) and pass their belief that this tracked concept contains the target through an inferred decision gain on its log-odds. The one change, taken from the incumbent criterion_shifted_tempered_decision, is an inferred response bias that shifts the yes/no criterion away from even odds by a constant on the log-odds scale, so a liberal (or conservative) criterion raises (or lowers) yeses to targets with middling or weak belief under the order-dependent guess without much changing yeses to the examples themselves.

## conjunctive_category_similarity  — rank 16, 728.7 ± 45.7 nats behind the best (15.9× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -2695.8

People judge a target by its similarity to each example separately, where two numbers are similar to the degree that they fall together in the same familiar number categories (mathematical rules or numeric ranges), with small, specific shared categories counting more than broad ones by an inferred similarity gradient. These per-example similarities combine conjunctively (multiplicatively): the target must resemble every example, so a single example it is dissimilar to sharply lowers the chance of a "yes", and the combined similarity is turned into a yes/no answer through a decision gain and criterion.
