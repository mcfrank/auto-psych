People do the same Bayesian concept learning as the best model (42 weighted rules and all intervals of 1-100, size principle with evidence tempered separately for rules and ranges, a decision gain and criterion shift turning belief into a yes/no answer), but they do not fully reset their belief between trials: the belief they act on is partly carried over from the previous trial, so their confidence that the current target belongs to the concept is pulled, by an inferred inertia weight, toward the confidence they had about the previous trial's target. This sequential assimilation makes consecutive answers in a session more alike than the stimuli alone warrant (e.g. a "no"-worthy target asked right after a near-certain "yes" gets more yeses); zero inertia recovers the best model.
