People do the same Bayesian concept learning as the best model (weighted rules with a size-dependent prior and all ranges of 1-100, evidence tempered separately for rules and ranges, a stronger-weighted first example, a decision gain and shifted criterion), but when they check whether the target falls inside a numeric range they believe in, they perceive the target's magnitude imprecisely, with noise that grows in proportion to the number's size (Weber's law). The one change is this scalar magnitude noise: the edges of believed ranges become fuzzy, so targets just outside a range get some yeses and targets just inside lose some, and this blurring is wider for large numbers (around 80-100) than for small ones (around 5-15), while membership in arithmetic rules is judged exactly; zero noise recovers the best model.
