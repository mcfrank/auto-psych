Refinement of `sequence_opens_bottom_ends_top`: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over arithmetic rules (primes excluding 1), every range of 1-100 and range-restricted rules, with a last-shown largest example marking a range's top and a first-shown smallest example marking its bottom. The one change: people also read the examples' final digits as a pattern when they differ, so "numbers ending in 3 or 7" (any pair of final digits) is a kind of rule in its own right with its own inferred prior share. So after 13,27 people say "yes" to 3 or 17 more than to nearby numbers that share no final digit with any example, which addresses the critique that people generalize by shared ones digits beyond what single-digit "ends in d" rules allow.
