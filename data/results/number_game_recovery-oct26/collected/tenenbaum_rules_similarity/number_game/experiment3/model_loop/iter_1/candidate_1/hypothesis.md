People do the same Bayesian concept learning as the best model (arithmetic rules with primes excluding 1, every range of 1-100, range-restricted rules, the last-shown largest example marking a range's top), but they perceive a familiar arithmetic rule such as "even numbers" or "multiples of 3" as a tighter class than its true count of members, so the size principle penalises such rules less, by an inferred degree, than it penalises ranges and range-restricted rules. The sharpest disagreement with the best model lives on rule-consistent targets far outside the examples' span after several examples sharing a broad rule (e.g. 12 or 94 after 62,68,74): with more examples the best model concentrates on a range hugging the examples and says "no", while this model keeps the broad rule alive and says "yes" to same-parity (or same-multiple) numbers well beyond the span.
