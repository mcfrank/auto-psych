People can hold only a limited number of examples in working memory while judging: when a set has more examples than their capacity (which varies from trial to trial, between two and four, with inferred probabilities), they keep the last example shown plus a random selection of the earlier ones and forget the rest. They then reason exactly as in the best model's Bayesian concept learning (arithmetic rules perceived as smaller than they are, ranges of 1-100 and range-restricted rules, a last-shown largest example marking a range's top) from only the examples they still hold, so sets of three or four examples tighten generalization less than an ideal learner predicts and a forgotten example is itself sometimes rejected, while one- and two-example sets are judged in full.
