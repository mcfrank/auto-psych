People learn the program's concept by Bayesian inference over arithmetic rules and numeric ranges, as in the best model, but with an inferred probability they believe a program that produces a range spreads its examples evenly across it (one example from each equal stretch of the range) instead of scattering them at random. A set of several evenly spaced examples is then far likelier under the range they span than under a rule or a random draw, while a range reaching well past the outermost examples, or a set with clumped examples, gets no such boost. The sharpest disagreement with the best model is on widely and evenly spaced sets of three or four examples (e.g. 20,35,68,92): this model says "yes" more readily to any number inside their span, whatever its parity or divisibility, and its range-based "yes" stops close to the outermost examples.
