People carry the concept they inferred on the previous trial into the current one (proactive interference): they learn the program's concept from the current examples by the best model's Bayesian concept learning, but with an inferred probability the concept they formed for the preceding trial's examples has not been cleared from working memory and is the one they consult when judging the current target. So a target is accepted more often when it fits the concept suggested by the previous trial's examples (e.g. "yes" to 64 after a powers-of-two set on the previous trial) and less often when it fits only the current set's concept, a dependence on trial order that no model of the current examples alone can express.
