Refinement of `uncertain_sampling_top_marking`: people do Bayesian concept learning over arithmetic rules (primes excluding 1) and ranges of 1-100, unsure whether the examples were drawn from the program's concept (strong sampling) or are merely numbers it accepts (weak sampling), so broad concepts such as "even numbers" keep a floor of support beyond the examples' span (the critique's parity gap), and they believe the program, with inferred odds, deliberately shows its range's top number. The one change, taken from `multi_example_sequence_top_marking`: the marked top is read from the order of presentation, so only a sequence of several examples that ends on its largest example blocks generalization above it (after 20,35,60 "yes" to 45 but not 62), while sets whose largest example comes earlier, and single examples, get no edge credit and generalize upward as freely as downward.
