Refinement of `sequence_opens_bottom_ends_top`: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over arithmetic rules (primes excluding 1), every range of 1-100 and range-restricted rules, and read the range's edges from the order of the demonstration: a sequence of several examples ending on its largest marks the range's top, and one opening on its smallest marks its bottom (with separately inferred odds). The one change, taken from `multi_example_sequence_top_marking`: a lone example is not a sequence and marks no edge, so it is treated as an ordinary random draw rather than as the range's top. So after a single example (e.g. 60) people say "yes" to 62 about as readily as to 58, while after 20,35,60 generalization still stops at 60 and drops below 20.
