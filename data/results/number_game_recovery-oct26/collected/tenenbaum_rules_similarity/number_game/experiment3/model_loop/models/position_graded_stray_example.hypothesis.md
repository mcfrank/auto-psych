Refinement of `sequence_opens_bottom_ends_top` by way of `stray_closing_example`: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over arithmetic rules (primes excluding 1), every range of 1-100 and range-restricted rules, with a last-shown largest example marking a range's top and a first-shown smallest marking its bottom, and they suspect that, with an inferred probability, one example in a set of several is a random number mixed into the demonstration rather than a member of the program's concept, and learn from the others alone. The one change from `stray_closing_example`: any example, not only the last, may be the stray, and how suspect each position is follows an inferred recency gradient (later examples more suspect when it is positive, all positions alike at zero). So an example that breaks the others' pattern is often rejected wherever it was shown (e.g. "no" to 72 after 18,8,72,98 or to 27 after 92,82,27,42), not only when it closes the sequence.
