People do not infer which concept produced the examples; they retrieve familiar number categories from memory (arithmetic properties such as "even numbers" or "multiples of 5", and stretches of the number line, with familiar kinds and moderately wide stretches retrieved more readily) by how similar each stored category is to the example set, judged by feature overlap (Tversky's ratio model): only categories containing every example are retrieved, and a category's similarity falls with the share of its members that the examples do not show, weighted by an inferred factor. People say "yes" to a target in proportion to the retrieval strength of the categories that contain it, apart from an inferred share of guessed answers. Because similarity depends on the proportion of members shown rather than on the joint probability of the examples, extra examples consistent with a small category do not tighten generalization the way the size principle would.
