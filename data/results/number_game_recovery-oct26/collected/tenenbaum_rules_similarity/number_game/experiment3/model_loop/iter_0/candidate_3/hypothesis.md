Refinement of `last_example_marks_range_top`: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over arithmetic rules (primes excluding 1), ranges of 1-100 and range-restricted rules, and believe that, with inferred odds, the program ends its demonstration by marking where its range stops at the top — but the one change is that they think the closing example lies at or just below the range's top rather than exactly on it, falling a few steps short with an inferred, geometrically decaying probability. So after a set whose largest example is shown last, the marking credit is shared by ranges ending at or a little above that example, and "yes" to targets just above the largest example (e.g. 62-64 after 20,35,60) falls off gradually instead of stopping dead at it, while sets whose largest example comes earlier still get no edge credit.
