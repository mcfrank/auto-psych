Refinement of `position_graded_stray_example`: people do Bayesian concept learning over arithmetic rules (primes excluding 1), every range of 1-100 and range-restricted rules, with a last-shown largest example marking a range's top and a first-shown smallest marking its bottom, and they suspect (with an inferred probability and recency gradient) that one example in a set of several is a random stray, learning from the others alone. The one change, grafted from `parity_representativeness_bias`: people judge how likely the examples they keep are under a concept by how representative they look, so a concept holding both odd and even numbers is expected to produce a parity-mixed sample and each further kept example repeating a parity shared by all of them costs it an inferred penalty, while parity-pure concepts pay nothing. So an example of the odd-one-out parity (27 in 92,82,27,42) is read as the stray more readily, and people then say "yes" to even numbers outside the span and "no" to that stray example more than either parent model predicts.
