People infer the program's concept by plain Bayesian concept learning over arithmetic rules (primes excluding 1) and numeric ranges, but the criterion they use to say "yes" is not fixed: it adapts to how many of the numbers 1-100 they believe the program produces. When the examples suggest a broad concept (e.g. "even numbers", a wide range) they shift how sure they must be that a target belongs before answering "yes" by an inferred amount, relative to sets that suggest a narrow concept (e.g. powers of 4), so the same membership belief leads to different answers depending on the expected breadth of the concept.
