Refinement of `last_example_marks_range_top`: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over arithmetic rules (primes excluding 1), every range of 1-100 and range-restricted rules, and believe that, with inferred odds, the program deliberately ends its demonstration on its range's top number. The one change: people read this marking from the sequence only when there is a sequence. When several examples are shown and the largest comes last, they credit ranges ending there, as the incumbent does. A lone example is not taken to mark anything, so it is treated as an ordinary random draw. So after a single example (e.g. 60) "yes" spreads about as far above it as below it (62 as readily as 58). After 20,35,60, generalization still stops at 60 while spreading below 20.
