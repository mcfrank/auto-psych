Refinement of `compressed_rule_extension`: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over arithmetic rules (primes excluding 1, perceived as smaller than they are), ranges of 1-100 and range-restricted rules, with the last-shown largest example marking a range's top. The one change, taken from `uncertain_sampling_assumption`, is that people are unsure how the examples were chosen. With an inferred probability they take the examples as drawn from the program's concept (the size principle). Otherwise they take them as merely numbers the program accepts, so every concept containing them keeps its prior plausibility. Their "yes" averages over both readings, so a third or fourth example tightens generalization beyond the examples' span less than the incumbent predicts, and broad concepts such as parity keep a floor of support.
