Refinement of `compressed_rule_extension` (Bayesian concept learning over arithmetic rules perceived as smaller than they are, ranges of 1-100 and range-restricted rules, with a last-shown largest example marking a range's top). The one change: people hold concepts defined by how numbers are written — "ends in 7", "contains a 3" — loosely, as surface patterns the program may follow for only some of its outputs, so under a digit rule they expect an inferred, larger share of the examples to be stray numbers than under any other concept. So a set where only some examples share a final digit (e.g. 32,57) still supports "ends in 7" and "ends in 2", and people say "yes" to the digit-mates of individual examples outside the examples' span (7, 17, 12), addressing the critique that people use ones-digit similarity beyond what all-examples digit rules allow.
