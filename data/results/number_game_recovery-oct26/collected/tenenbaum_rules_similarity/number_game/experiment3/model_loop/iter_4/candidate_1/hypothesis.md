People do not infer which concept produced the examples; they retrieve familiar number categories from memory (arithmetic properties such as "even numbers" or "multiples of 5", and stretches of the number line, with familiar kinds and moderate, decade-bounded stretches retrieved more readily) by how similar each stored category is to the example set, judged by feature-overlap (Tversky's ratio model): examples the category contains count for it, examples it lacks count against it, and members the examples do not show count against it by a smaller inferred weight. They say "yes" to a target in proportion to the retrieval strength of the categories that contain it. Because similarity depends on the proportion of shown members rather than on their joint probability, extra examples consistent with a small category do not tighten generalization the way the size principle would.
