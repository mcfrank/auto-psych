People do Bayesian concept learning as in the best model (arithmetic rules with primes excluding 1 perceived as smaller than they are, every range of 1-100 and range-restricted rules, the size principle, the last-shown largest example marking a range's top), but their expectation of how wide the program's range is gets anchored on how widely the examples are spread, so the spread is counted twice: once as evidence and again in the prior. The one distortion: the typical range width people expect grows (or shrinks) with the examples' span by an inferred power, so widely spread sets lead people to expect, and generalize to, ranges reaching further past the outermost examples than the ideal learner would, and tightly clustered or single examples to ranges hugging them.
