Refinement of `compressed_rule_extension`: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over arithmetic rules (perceived as smaller than they are), ranges of 1-100 and range-restricted rules, with a last-shown largest example marking a range's top. The one change is that people also read the opening of a sequence. When several examples are shown and the first one is the smallest, or the largest, they believe (with inferred odds) that the program deliberately started on that edge of its range and drew the rest at random. Ranges with that end at the first example then gain credit, more for wider ranges. So after 20,35,60 people say "no" to 17 more readily, and after 60,35,20 to 63, than after the same numbers in an order that does not open on an extreme.
