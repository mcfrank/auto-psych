People judge a number as similar to an example when it ends in the same digit, and they entertain "numbers that look like one of the examples by their last digit" as a candidate concept of its own: when the examples end in different digits (e.g. 23,47), the numbers ending in any of those digits (every number ending in 3 or 7) are a possible concept, weighed by its size and an inferred prior share alongside the best model's arithmetic rules, ranges (with the last-shown largest example marking the top and a first-shown smallest the bottom) and range-restricted rules. So people say "yes" to numbers far outside the examples' span that share an example's last digit (13 or 17 after 23,47) more than to neighbours that share none (15 or 18), even though no ends-in-d rule fits all the examples.
