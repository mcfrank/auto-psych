People infer the program's concept by plain Bayesian concept learning over arithmetic rules (primes excluding 1) and numeric ranges, but how consistently they act on their belief depends on how sure they are which concept the program has. When the examples point clearly to one concept (e.g. 2,4,8,16,32), people answer decisively, close to all-or-none from their membership belief. When many concepts remain plausible (e.g. a single example like 60), their answers become noisier and drift toward 50/50, with how strongly confusion about the concept degrades answer consistency inferred from the data.
