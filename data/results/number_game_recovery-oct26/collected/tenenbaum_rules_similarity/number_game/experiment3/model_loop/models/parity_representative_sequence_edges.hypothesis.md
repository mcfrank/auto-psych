Refinement of `sequence_opens_bottom_ends_top`: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over arithmetic rules (primes excluding 1), every range of 1-100 and range-restricted rules, with a last-shown largest example marking a range's top and a first-shown smallest example (in sets of several) marking its bottom. The one change, taken from `parity_representativeness_bias`: people judge how likely the examples are under a concept partly by how representative they look of it, so a concept holding both odd and even numbers (a range, multiples of 3, squares) is expected to produce a parity-mixed sample, and each further example that repeats a parity shared by the whole set costs it an inferred extra penalty, while parity-pure concepts (even numbers, odd numbers, multiples of 4) pay nothing. So after 22,46,68 people say "yes" to even numbers outside the span (8 or 90) more, and to odd numbers inside it less, than the incumbent, while the order-read edges still stop generalization above a last-shown maximum and below a first-shown minimum.
