Refinement of `sequence_opens_bottom_ends_top`: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over arithmetic rules (primes excluding 1), every range of 1-100 and range-restricted rules, with a last-shown largest example marking a range's top and a first-shown smallest example (in sets of several) marking its bottom. The one change: when people check whether a target falls inside a numeric range, they locate it on a noisy mental number line whose noise grows in proportion to the number's size (Weber's law, with an inferred Weber fraction), while membership in arithmetic rules is checked exactly. So range edges are blurred rather than sharp, and more so among large numbers: after 20,35,60 people sometimes say "yes" to 62 just above the marked top, and generalization spreads further past high examples (83,77 → 86) than past low ones (3,7 → 9).
