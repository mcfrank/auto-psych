Refinement of `parity_representativeness_bias`: people do Bayesian concept learning over arithmetic rules (primes excluding 1), every range of 1-100 and range-restricted rules, with a last-shown largest example marking a range's top, and they judge a set by how representative it looks — under a concept holding both odd and even numbers, each further same-parity example costs an inferred extra penalty, so all-even or all-odd sets push belief toward parity-pure concepts. The one change, grafted from `sequence_opens_bottom_ends_top`: people also read the opening of the sequence, so when several examples are shown and the smallest comes first they believe (with separately inferred odds) the program deliberately started at its range's bottom, and ranges starting there gain generative credit. So after 22,46,68 people say "yes" to even numbers far outside the span (8, 90) more than the best model, yet to odd neighbours just below 22 less than after 46,22,68.
