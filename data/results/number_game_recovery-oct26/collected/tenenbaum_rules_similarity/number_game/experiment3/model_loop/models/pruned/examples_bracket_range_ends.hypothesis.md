People believe the program often demonstrates a range by showing both of its ends: with some inferred odds, the smallest and the largest example are the range's bottom and top numbers (whatever order they are shown in) and any other examples are random draws. Otherwise they reason as in the best model's Bayesian concept learning (arithmetic rules perceived as smaller than they are, ranges of 1-100 and range-restricted rules, a last-shown largest example marking a range's top). So the range spanning exactly the examples is favoured far more than chance sampling implies, most of all for widely separated pairs. After 22,46 people say "yes" to odd numbers between the examples (e.g. 33) more, and "no" to neighbours just outside (21 or 47) more, than the best model predicts.
