People read the examples in order and suspect that the program's closing example may be a stray: with an inferred probability, they believe the last number shown (in a set of two or more) was a random number tacked onto the demonstration rather than a member of the program's concept. Otherwise they reason as in the best model (Bayesian concept learning over arithmetic rules, ranges and range-restricted rules, a last-shown largest example marking a range's top, a first-shown smallest marking its bottom), and under the stray reading they learn from the earlier examples alone. So the last-shown example is itself rejected more often than earlier ones (e.g. "no" to 100 after 31,19,49,100), and a closing example that breaks the earlier examples' pattern loosens generalization less than an ideal learner predicts.
