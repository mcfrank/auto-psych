Refinement of `decade_bounded_interval_prior`: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over 42 arithmetic rules and every interval of 1-100 (inferred rule weights and rule share, size principle with 10% random examples, Erlang prior over interval width, "yes" = posterior probability the concept contains the target) with a prior preference for ranges bounded by round decade numbers, but each end of a range is judged round or not on its own: a range gains the inferred round-boundary factor once for a round lower end (1, 11, 21, ...) and once more for a round upper end (10, 20, ..., 100), instead of only when both ends are round. The one change is this per-end preference, so people can stop generalizing at the decade edge on one side of the examples (e.g. "up to 50") while extending past it on the other, rather than favouring only whole-decade blocks.
