People do Bayesian concept learning over arithmetic rules and intervals of 1-100 (size principle, a width-based Erlang prior on intervals), but instead of reporting the posterior probability that the target belongs to the concept, they decide from a small number of hypotheses sampled from that posterior and go with the majority. This resource limit distorts the normative judgement by a single response-gain: posterior beliefs are pushed toward certain "yes"/"no" (few samples, majority rule) or compressed toward 50/50, with the degree of distortion inferred from the data.
