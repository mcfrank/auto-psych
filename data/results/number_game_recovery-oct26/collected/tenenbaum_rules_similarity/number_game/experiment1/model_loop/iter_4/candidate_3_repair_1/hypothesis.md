Refinement of `family_pooled_rule_prior`: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over 42 arithmetic rules and every interval of 1-100 (size principle with 10% random examples, Erlang prior over interval width, a preference for decade-bounded ranges, "yes" = posterior probability the concept contains the target, rules of one kind equally likely a priori), but parity is a kind of rule in its own right. The one change: "even numbers" and "odd numbers" form their own kind with their own inferred prior share, instead of being lumped with primes, squares and cubes as one "special sets" kind, so how readily people fall back on odd/even after examples like 3,7 or 66,78 is set apart from how readily they think of primes, squares or cubes.
