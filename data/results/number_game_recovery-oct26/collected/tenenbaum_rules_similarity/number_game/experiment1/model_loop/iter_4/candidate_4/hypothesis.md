Refinement of `family_pooled_rule_prior`: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over 42 arithmetic rules and every interval of 1-100 (a prior share for each kind of rule, size principle with 10% random examples, Erlang prior over interval width, a preference for decade-bounded ranges, "yes" = posterior probability the concept contains the target), but within a kind they do not treat all rules as equally likely: a rule's prior grows (or shrinks) with how many numbers it contains, by an inferred power. The one change is this size-graded within-kind prior — e.g. "multiples of 3" or "even numbers", which people meet constantly, are a priori more plausible than "multiples of 11" or "cubes" — so it partly offsets the size principle and makes people generalize to broader rules of the same kind more readily than the equal-split prior predicts.
