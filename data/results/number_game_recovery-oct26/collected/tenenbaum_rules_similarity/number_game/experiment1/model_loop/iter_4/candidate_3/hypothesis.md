Refinement of `family_pooled_rule_prior`: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over 42 arithmetic rules and every interval of 1-100 (a prior share for each kind of rule split equally within the kind, size principle with 10% random examples, Erlang prior over interval width, a preference for decade-bounded ranges, "yes" = posterior probability the concept contains the target). The one change: people do not treat the ends of the 1-100 scale as neutral range boundaries — a range that runs all the way to 1 or to 100 ("numbers up to 20", "numbers above 70") has its prior multiplied by an inferred factor, so when the examples lie near an end of the scale, how far people generalize toward that end depends on whether they avoid (or favour) ranges that hit the scale's edge, not just on the range's width.
