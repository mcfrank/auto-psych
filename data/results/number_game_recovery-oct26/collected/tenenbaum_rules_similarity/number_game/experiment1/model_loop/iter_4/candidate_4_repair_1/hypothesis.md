Refinement of `family_pooled_rule_prior`: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over 42 arithmetic rules and every interval of 1-100 (size principle with 10% random examples, Erlang prior over interval width, a preference for decade-bounded ranges, a prior share for each kind of rule split equally among that kind's rules, "yes" = posterior probability the concept contains the target). The one change is that people treat parity — "even numbers" and "odd numbers" — as a kind of rule of its own, with its own prior share, rather than lumping it with the special number sets (primes, squares, cubes). So "even numbers" no longer has to be exactly as plausible a priori as "primes" or "cubes", and how readily people jump from a few even examples to "all even numbers" is set separately from how readily they jump to squares or primes.
