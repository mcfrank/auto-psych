Refinement of erlang_interval_size_prior: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over 42 arithmetic rules and every interval of 1-100 (inferred rule weights and rule share, size principle with 10% random examples, "yes" = posterior probability the concept contains the target), but their prior over how wide a range is takes a Gamma shape whose shape is inferred, instead of the Erlang's fixed shape of two. The one change is this free shape: if people's width prior falls off from the narrowest ranges (shape near or below one) rather than peaking at a moderate width, they believe the range ends close to the outermost examples, so they say "yes" to numbers inside the examples' span but sharply less to numbers just beyond it.
