Refinement of `tempered_evidence_interval`: people do Bayesian concept learning over the same 42 arithmetic rules and every interval of 1-100 (inferred rule weights and rule share, size principle with 10% random examples, "yes" = posterior probability the concept contains the target), and they weigh the examples' evidence at an inferred strength, raising the size-principle likelihood to a power rather than using it at exactly full strength. The one change is the interval prior: instead of a Beta density over where an interval lies, intervals get an Erlang prior over their width (from `erlang_interval_size_prior`), so evidence strength and an expected range width are separated. The claim is that people over-weight the examples relative to the ideal learner (a power above one), which makes them commit to ranges that just cover the examples, giving a sharper drop in "yes" at the edge of the examples' span than the ideal learner predicts, as the critique of the best model reports.
