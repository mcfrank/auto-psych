People do Bayesian concept learning over arithmetic rules (primes excluding 1), ranges of 1-100 and range-restricted rules, and they believe the program, with some inferred odds, opens its sequence by showing the top number of its range and then draws the remaining examples at random — so the order of the examples matters: only the first example shown can mark the range's top. When the first example is also the largest (e.g. 83,77 or 60), ranges ending there gain credit that grows with their width and "yes" spreads downward but stops above it; when the largest example comes later (e.g. 77,83) it is read as an ordinary random draw, so the same examples in that order license more generalization above the largest example.
