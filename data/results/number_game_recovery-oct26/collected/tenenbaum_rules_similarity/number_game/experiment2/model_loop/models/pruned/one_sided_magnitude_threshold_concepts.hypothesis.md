People do Bayesian concept learning over arithmetic rules (primes excluding 1, rules of one kind equally likely) with the size principle, but they do not represent numeric ranges with two ends: the only magnitude concepts they entertain are one-sided thresholds — "numbers up to N" or "numbers N and above" (with round decade thresholds preferred and an inferred preference for upper bounds over lower bounds). So magnitude-based "yes" never stops on both sides of the examples: once people judge by size, they accept everything below the largest example (or everything above the smallest), however far away, rather than only the examples' neighbourhood.
