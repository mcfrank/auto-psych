Refinement of `prime_concept_excludes_one`: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over arithmetic rules (primes excluding 1), ranges of 1-100 and range-restricted rules, but they apply the size principle at full strength only to arithmetic rules and only partly to numeric ranges. The one change: when judging how well a range explains the examples, people treat the range as if it were wider than it is — its effective size lies between its true width and the whole 1-100 scale, by an inferred degree — so more examples tighten range-based generalization less than rule-based generalization, and clustered examples are explained by ranges (with "yes" spreading further across the examples' neighbourhood) more readily than strong sampling predicts.
