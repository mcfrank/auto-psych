Refinement of `upper_edge_marking_ranges`: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over arithmetic rules (primes excluding 1), every range of 1-100 and range-restricted rules, and they take the largest example as marking the top of the program's range — but the one change is the form of that marking: rather than a constant evidence bonus for any range ending at the largest example, people believe that, with an inferred probability, the program deliberately showed the top of its range and drew the other examples at random. Under this teacher story the largest example pays no size-principle penalty for a range that ends at it, so the advantage of a top-marked range grows with its width: "yes" spreads broadly downward from the largest example (most strongly after a single example, e.g. after 60 people accept 35 or 45 but reject 63), while generalization above it stays blocked.
