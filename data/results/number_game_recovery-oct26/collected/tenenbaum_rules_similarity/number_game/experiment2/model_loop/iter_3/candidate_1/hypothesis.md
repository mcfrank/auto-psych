People attend to the extreme examples: they treat the smallest and the largest example shown as samples drawn from the program's concept, but the examples lying between them are encoded only as confirming that the concept includes them, so they count as weaker evidence under the size principle by an inferred degree (zero: mere consistency; one: the ideal learner). Otherwise people do Bayesian concept learning over arithmetic rules (primes excluding 1, kind-level prior) and ranges of 1-100 (Erlang width prior, decade-boundary preference, the largest example marking the top of the range), so adding examples inside the span of the first two tightens generalization less than an ideal learner predicts, while examples that extend the span tighten it fully.
