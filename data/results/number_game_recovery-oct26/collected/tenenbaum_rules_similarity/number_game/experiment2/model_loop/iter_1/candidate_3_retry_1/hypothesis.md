Refinement of `prime_concept_excludes_one`: people do the same Bayesian concept learning (arithmetic rules with a kind-level prior, every range of 1-100 with an Erlang width prior and decade-boundary preference, range-restricted rules, size principle with 10% random examples, primes excluding 1, "yes" = posterior probability the concept contains the target), but the familiar named sets — primes, even numbers, odd numbers, squares and cubes — are not equally likely a priori. The one change: each named set gets its own inferred prior weight instead of sharing one "special sets" weight equally, so how readily people reach for "even/odd" versus "squares" or "primes" (e.g. after 3,7 or 1,4,9 or 31,17) reflects how familiar each concept is.
