People do not weigh all the examples jointly; they hold a single running guess about the concept and revise it one example at a time, in the order shown (win-stay, lose-shift). After the first example they adopt one concept drawn in proportion to its posterior plausibility (arithmetic rules with a kind-level prior, ranges with a width prior favouring decade-bounded ranges, size principle); each later example that their current guess contains leaves the guess unchanged, and only an example the guess fails to contain makes them draw a new guess from the posterior given all the examples seen so far. So a broad concept adopted early (e.g. "even numbers" or a wide range) survives later examples that would have narrowed an ideal learner's belief, and the same examples in a different order lead to different generalization.
