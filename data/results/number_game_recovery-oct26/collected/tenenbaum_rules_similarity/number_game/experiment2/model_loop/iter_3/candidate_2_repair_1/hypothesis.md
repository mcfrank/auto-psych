People do Bayesian concept learning over arithmetic rules (primes excluding 1, rules of one kind equally likely) and numeric ranges of 1-100 (a width prior favouring moderate, decade-bounded ranges), with the size principle, but they judge how big a range is on a compressed, logarithmic mental number line: a stretch among large numbers (e.g. 81-90) feels smaller than an equally long stretch among small numbers (e.g. 1-10), by an inferred degree of compression. Because the size principle rewards small concepts, examples among large numbers are explained by tighter ranges, so people generalize less far from high examples like 83,77 than from low examples like 3,7.
