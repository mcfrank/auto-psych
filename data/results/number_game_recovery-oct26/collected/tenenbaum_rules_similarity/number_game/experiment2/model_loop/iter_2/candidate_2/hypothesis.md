People do Bayesian concept learning over arithmetic rules (primes excluding 1, kind-level rule prior) and numeric ranges of 1-100 (width prior favouring moderate, decade-bounded ranges, the top of a range marked by the largest example, size principle with 10% random examples), but they can also think of the program's numbers as lying in two separate clusters — two equally wide stretches of the number line with a gap between them ("numbers near 10 and numbers near 80") — a kind of concept with its own inferred prior share and a width prior shared with ranges. The sharpest disagreement with the best model lives in the gap between widely separated examples (e.g. 45 after 10,80, or 35 after 5,65): the best model must cover such examples with one wide range or a rule and so says "yes" across the gap, whereas this model says "yes" near each example and "no" in the empty middle.
