Refinement of `upper_edge_marking_ranges`: people do the same Bayesian concept learning over arithmetic rules (primes excluding 1), every range of 1-100 and range-restricted rules, and they take the largest example as marking where the program's range stops at the top, but they apply the size principle only partly to numeric ranges. The one change, grafted from `range_discounted_size_principle`: when judging how well a range explains the examples, people treat it as wider than it is (its effective size lies between its true width and the whole 1-100 scale, by an inferred degree), so with more examples "yes" spreads further below and across the examples' neighbourhood than strong sampling predicts, while generalization above the largest example stays cut off by the upper-edge inference.
