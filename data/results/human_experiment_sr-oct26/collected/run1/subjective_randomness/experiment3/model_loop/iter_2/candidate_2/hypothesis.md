People differ in how they act on their impression of randomness along a single signed dimension of decisiveness: most people choose the sequence that looks more random to them with a personal strength, some barely follow their impression at all, and a few systematically pick the sequence that looks less random (reading the task in reverse, e.g. choosing the one that "looks like a random pattern someone made up"). This one personal signed decisiveness replaces separate personal guessing rates and personal switching-rate sensitivities, and it predicts that people's agreement with the majority choice spreads far wider — down to below one half for some people — than guessing alone allows; each sequence's impression is otherwise judged as in the current best model.
