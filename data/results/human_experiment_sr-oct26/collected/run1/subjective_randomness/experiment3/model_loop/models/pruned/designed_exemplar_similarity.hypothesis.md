People recognise a "designed" sequence not by checking separate structural features but by its similarity to a few remembered examples of designed sequences — a solid streak, strict alternation, repeated pairs (HHTT…), any short chunk repeated end to end (HHTHHT…, HTTHTT…), and two solid halves (HHHHTTTT) — with similarity falling off exponentially with the number of flips that differ, summed over the remembered examples. A sequence close to these exemplars looks made rather than random, on top of each person's statistical sense of a random coin (their own ideal switching rate and sensitivity, lenient toward over-alternation, personal weights on imbalance and the longest streak, a personal guessing rate); how strongly a person reacts to resemblance to designed exemplars is their own trait, so this one similarity mechanism replaces the final-streak, mirror, antisymmetry, run-variety and lopsided-stretch cues of the current best model and predicts that a near-miss of a designed pattern (one flip off strict alternation or off HHTTHHTT) is rejected even when it has none of those exact structures.
