People scan a sequence for its single most striking repeated chunk: the longest stretch of flips that appears again, unchanged, somewhere else in the same sequence (HTT in HTTHHTTH, HTHT in HTHTHTHT, HHT in THHTHHTT). One long recurring chunk makes the whole sequence look copied and so less random, by a shared weight on the length of that longest repeat (relative to the longest repeat the sequence's length allows). This repeat cue is added to the current best judgement (personal ideal switching rate and sensitivity with lenience toward over-alternation, personal weights on imbalance and the longest streak, shared weights on the final streak, mirror symmetry, run-length variety and the most lopsided stretch, personal weight on complement symmetry, and a personal guessing rate). It predicts that two sequences matching on switching, balance and streaks differ when one contains a long chunk that recurs and the other does not.
