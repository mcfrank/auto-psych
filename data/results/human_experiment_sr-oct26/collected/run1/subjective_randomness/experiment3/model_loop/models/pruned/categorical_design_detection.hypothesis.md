Spotting that a sequence was deliberately constructed is an all-or-none event, not a graded impression: on each trial a person either notices that a sequence is designed (an end streak, a palindrome, an antisymmetric layout, monotone run lengths, a lopsided stretch — the more of this structure, the likelier the notice) or does not, and a sequence caught as designed is rejected by a fixed extra amount on top of the graded comparison of how random the two look (personal ideal switching rate and sensitivity with lenience toward over-alternation, personal weights on imbalance and the longest streak, shared weights on the structural cues, and a personal guessing rate). How readily a person makes that categorical call is their own detection threshold, drawn from a population, so low-threshold people decisively reject anything structured while high-threshold people never do — predicting that people's agreement with the majority varies more than graded weights produce, concentrated on pairs where exactly one sequence is structured.
