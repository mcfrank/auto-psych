People keep a running tally of heads versus tails as they read a sequence and expect a fair coin to keep correcting itself: the tally should drift away from balance and then come back to even, again and again. A sequence whose tally returns to an even count often (relative to how many returns its length allows) looks self-correcting and so more random, while one whose tally wanders off and rarely or never comes back to balance looks biased — by a shared weight on the share of possible returns to balance, on top of the current best judgement (personal ideal switching rate and sensitivity with lenience toward over-alternation, personal weights on imbalance and the longest streak, a personal pattern-detection gain scaling the final streak, mirror symmetry, complement symmetry, run-length variety and most-lopsided-stretch cues, and a personal guessing rate). It predicts that two sequences with the same final counts, switching rate and range of the tally differ when one passes through balance more often along the way (HTTHHT, back to even after every second flip, should look more random than HHTTHT, which matches it on switches, counts and range but returns to balance only twice).
