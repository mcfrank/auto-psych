Refinement of `local_representativeness` (Kahneman & Tversky local representativeness): people judge a sequence random when it is locally balanced and irregular, but the alternation rate each person's mental prototype of randomness expects differs between people — some expect strong over-alternation, others expect streakier sequences — instead of one shared over-alternating prototype. The single change is that the prototype alternation rate becomes a per-participant parameter (free to lie below or above 0.5) drawn from a population distribution, addressing the critique that real individual differences in alternation preference are far larger than the single-population model produces.
