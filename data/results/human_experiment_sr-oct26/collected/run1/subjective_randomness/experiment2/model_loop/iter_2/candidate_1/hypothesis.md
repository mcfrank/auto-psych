People judge randomness by pattern evenness: they expect a random coin to produce every short pattern about equally often — heads and tails, each of the four flip pairs (HH, HT, TH, TT) and each of the flip triples — so a sequence looks non-random to the extent that a few short patterns dominate it (as in a streak, which is all HH, or strict alternation, which is all HT and TH), measured as how far the spread of its overlapping one-, two- and three-flip patterns falls short of the most even spread its length allows. How strongly this pattern unevenness puts a person off is their own trait, drawn from a population, and on some trials people guess at a personal lapse rate.
