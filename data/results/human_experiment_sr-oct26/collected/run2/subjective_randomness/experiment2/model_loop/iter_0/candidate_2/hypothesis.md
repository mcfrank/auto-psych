People compare the two sequences lexicographically (a semiorder): they first ask which one switches between H and T at a rate closer to their own ideal for a random coin, and act on that difference when it is noticeable; only when the two sequences are close on switching does a second comparison take over, and then they pick the one whose heads and tails are more evenly balanced. Because the balance cue is consulted only when the partner leaves the switching comparison undecided, the same sequence's balance matters beside one partner and is ignored beside another.
