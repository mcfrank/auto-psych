People judge randomness by how hard a sequence is to describe by copying: reading it from left to right, they mentally chunk it into the fewest pieces such that each new piece is either a single new flip or a copy of something already seen earlier in the sequence (Lempel–Ziv parsing), and a sequence that breaks into more such "new" pieces looks more random. Streaks and strict alternation (HTHTHTHT collapses to H, T and one long copy) and repeated units are cheap to describe and look non-random, while sequences full of varied local patterns resist copying and look random; people differ in how strongly this felt incompressibility drives their choice, and each has a small habitual lean toward clicking left or right.
