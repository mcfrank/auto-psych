People judge randomness by exemplar memory of both kinds of sequence: they hold remembered examples of random-looking coin sequences (balanced heads and tails, no streak longer than two, no visible repeating unit) and of designed sequences (strings built by repeating a short unit at least twice, such as HHHH, HTHT, HHTHHT, HHTTHHTT), and a new sequence looks random to the extent that it is more similar to the random exemplars than to the designed ones, similarity falling off exponentially with the number of flips that differ. People pick the sequence with the higher random-versus-designed similarity, differing only in how strongly that felt resemblance drives their choice.
