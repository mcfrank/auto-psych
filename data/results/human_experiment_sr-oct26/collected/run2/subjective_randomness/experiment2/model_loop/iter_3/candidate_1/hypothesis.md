People read a sequence flip by flip and, holding only the last two flips in working memory, predict each next flip from a personal belief about how a random coin behaves after a switch versus after a repeat (most expect a repeat to be followed by a switch, but a switch not always to be followed by another switch). A sequence looks random to the extent that its flips keep matching these two-flip-memory expectations (its probability under that subjective coin), so both long streaks (repeat after repeat) and strict alternation (switch after switch after switch) violate expectation, and people choose the sequence their own subjective coin finds more probable, with a small personal lean toward clicking left or right.
