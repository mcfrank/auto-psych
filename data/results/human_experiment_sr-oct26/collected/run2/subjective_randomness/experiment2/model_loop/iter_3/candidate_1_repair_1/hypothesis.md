People read a sequence flip by flip and, holding only the last two flips in working memory, predict each next flip from a belief about how a random coin behaves after a switch versus after a repeat (a repeat is expected to be followed by a switch, but a switch not necessarily by another switch). A sequence looks random to the extent that its flips keep matching these two-flip-memory expectations (its probability under that subjective coin), so both long streaks (repeat after repeat) and strict alternation (switch after switch) violate expectation, and people choose the sequence the subjective coin finds more probable, apart from a small personal lean toward clicking left or right.
