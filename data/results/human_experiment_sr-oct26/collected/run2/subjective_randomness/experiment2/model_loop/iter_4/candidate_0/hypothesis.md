People judge randomness with a subjective coin that remembers only its last two flips: they expect a random coin's next flip to depend on whether the previous two flips were a repeat or a switch (typically, a repeat should be followed by a switch, while a switch need not be followed by another switch), and a sequence looks random to the extent that it is probable under this two-flip-memory coin. Long streaks (repeat after repeat) and strict alternation (switch after switch) both violate these expectations, and people choose the sequence the subjective coin finds more probable, apart from a small personal lean toward clicking left or right.
