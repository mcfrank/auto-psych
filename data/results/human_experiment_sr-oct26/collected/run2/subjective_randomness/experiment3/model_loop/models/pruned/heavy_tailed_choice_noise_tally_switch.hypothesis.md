The decision rule, not the evidence, is what differs from the current models: people's moment-to-moment impression of which sequence is more random is corrupted by heavy-tailed noise — usually small, but occasionally a gross misreading of a pair (a skipped flip, a miscounted streak) — so the probability of choosing a sequence rises with the felt difference in randomness like a Cauchy rather than a logistic curve. Near-ties are decided sharply by small differences, yet even clear-cut pairs never reach full consensus, approaching it only slowly. The evidence is kept simple and shared (how close each sequence's running heads-minus-tails tally span is to an expected span and its switching rate to an ideal rate), plus a personal left/right lean.
