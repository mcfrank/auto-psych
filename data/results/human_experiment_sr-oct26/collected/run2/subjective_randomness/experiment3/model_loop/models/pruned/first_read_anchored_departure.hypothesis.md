The sequence read first (the left one) sets the anchor for the comparison: people register how far each sequence departs from what a fair coin should do (its running heads-minus-tails tally swinging wider or narrower than they expect, its switching rate away from an ideal rate), but a departure in the first-read sequence weighs by a different amount than the same departure in the second, because the second is judged relative to an impression already formed. The claim is this shared asymmetry between first- and second-read departures, so a pair's choice depends not only on the gap between the two sequences but on how non-random the pair is as a whole and on which sequence is read first; three-flip chunk variety, a penalty on rule-built sequences and a personal left/right lean enter as usual.
