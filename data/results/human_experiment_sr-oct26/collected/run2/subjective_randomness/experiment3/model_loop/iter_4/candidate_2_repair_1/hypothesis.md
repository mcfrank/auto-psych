People compare the two sequences by aligning them flip by flip (structural alignment), and how sharply they act on their impression of which looks more random depends on how alignable the pair is: when the two strings share most of their flips position by position, the few places where they differ stand out and decide the comparison sharply, whereas when the strings differ almost everywhere the comparison is holistic and closer to a guess (the direction is a single shared parameter, so the data can also say alignment hurts). The impression itself is the current best model's (tally span against a personal expected span, switching judged by closeness to a personal ideal rate, chunk variety and a rule-built penalty scaled by personal pattern sensitivity, a penalty on a terminal streak, and a personal left/right lean that is not scaled); the claim is the shared dependence of decisiveness on the pair's position-by-position dissimilarity, so the same felt difference in randomness yields stronger consensus for a near-identical pair than for a pair of unrelated strings.
