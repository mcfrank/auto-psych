People do not weigh the two sequences' impressions equally: they read the left sequence first, and its impression of randomness anchors the comparison, so the first-read sequence's impression counts by a different weight than the second's (primacy anchoring), rather than the choice resting on the plain difference between the two. The impression itself is the current best model's (running heads-minus-tails tally span against a personal expected span, switching against a personal ideal rate, three-flip chunk variety and a rule-built penalty scaled by personal pattern sensitivity, personal left/right lean); the claim is the shared first-read weighting, so the same pair is judged differently depending on which sequence is read first, and a sequence's chance of being chosen depends on how random its partner looks in absolute terms, not only on the gap between them.
