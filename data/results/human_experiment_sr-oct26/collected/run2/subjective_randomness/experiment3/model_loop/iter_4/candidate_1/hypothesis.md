People judge randomness by exemplar memory of short local glimpses: they read a sequence through a four-flip window (the whole sequence if shorter) and compare each glimpse with remembered examples of designed coin patterns — a streak (HHHH), strict alternation (HTHT) and a two-by-two block (HHTT and its shifts) — and with a diffuse memory of random glimpses that is equally similar to anything. Each glimpse is classed as random with the probability that the random memory wins against its summed similarity to the designed exemplars (similarity falling off exponentially with the number of mismatched flips, the alternation and block exemplars stored with one shared strength relative to the streak), and a sequence looks random by the share of its glimpses classed as random; people pick the sequence whose glimpses look random more often, differing in how decisively they act on this and in a small left/right lean.
