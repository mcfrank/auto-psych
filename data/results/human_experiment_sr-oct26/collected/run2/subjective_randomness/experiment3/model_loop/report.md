## Hypotheses

- **edge_streak_primacy_recency_pattern_rule**: Refinement of the incumbent `terminal_streak_recency_pattern_rule` (tally span judged against a personal expected span, switching by closeness to a personal ideal switching rate, chunk variety and a rule-built penalty scaled by a personal pattern sensitivity, a personal left/right lean, and a shared penalty on every flip of the final run beyond the first). The one change: the edges of a sequence are salient at both ends, not only at its end — the run a sequence opens on is read first and noticed just as the run it finishes on is read last, so a sequence that starts with a streak (HHHTHT…, TTHTHH…) also looks less random, every flip of its opening run beyond the first counting against it by its own shared opening-streak penalty. Of two sequences with the same number of switches and the same ending, people therefore pick the one that starts with a switch, which the incumbent, blind to where the opening switches fall, predicts they do not.
- **terminal_streak_recency_pattern_rule**: Refinement of the incumbent `person_switch_ideal_pattern_rule_built` (tally span judged against a personal expected span, switching judged by closeness to a personal ideal switching rate, chunk variety and a rule-built penalty scaled by a personal pattern sensitivity, a personal left/right lean). The one change: people weigh the end of a sequence, the flips they read last, more than its interior — a sequence that finishes on a streak (the coin "getting stuck" at the end, HTHHTT, THTHHH) looks less random, and every flip of the final run beyond the first counts against it, by a shared terminal-streak penalty. Of two sequences with the same number of switches, people therefore pick the one that ends with a switch, addressing the critique that among equal-switch pairs people choose the sequence ending with a switch more often than the incumbent, which is blind to where switches occur, predicts.
- **person_switch_ideal_pattern_rule_built**: Refinement of the incumbent `person_pattern_sensitivity_rule_built` (tally span judged against a personal expected span, switching judged by closeness to an ideal switching rate, chunk variety and a rule-built penalty scaled by a personal pattern sensitivity, a personal left/right lean). The one change: people disagree about how often a random coin should switch sides — each person holds their own ideal switching rate, drawn from a population (some expect near-even switching, others heavy alternation), instead of one rate shared by everyone. On pairs that differ mainly in switching, people therefore split in opposite directions rather than all leaning the same way, addressing the critique that people differ in how often they agree with the majority even on pairs where pattern structure cannot act.
- **local_alternation_switch_ideal_rule**: Refinement of `person_switch_ideal_pattern_rule_built` (tally span judged against a personal expected span, switching judged by closeness to a personal ideal switching rate, chunk variety and a rule-built penalty scaled by a personal pattern sensitivity, a personal left/right lean). The one change: people notice a local stretch of strict alternation (HTHT or THTH within any four consecutive flips) as a visible pattern in its own right, and every such alternating four-flip stretch counts against a sequence's randomness through a shared penalty scaled by the person's pattern sensitivity — even when the whole sequence is not rule-built and its overall switching rate is near their ideal. Of two sequences with the same number of switches, people therefore pick the one whose switches are not bunched into extended alternation, addressing the critique that among equal-switch pairs people choose the sequence with fewer alternating four-flip windows more often than the best model predicts.
- **person_decisiveness_rule_built_tally**: Refinement of the incumbent `rule_built_tally_switch_triplet` (tally span judged against a personal expected span, switching judged against an ideal rate with a person-specific weight, a reward for three-flip chunk variety, a penalty for rule-built sequences, a personal left/right lean). The one change: people differ in how decisively they act on their whole impression of randomness — each person has a single consistency (decisiveness) that scales all of their cues together (span, switching, chunk variety and rule-built penalty), replacing the incumbent's person-specific sensitivity on the tally span alone, so some people follow the consensus on almost every pair while others choose nearly at random. This addresses the critique (surviving FDR) that people differ in how often they agree with the majority more than the incumbent allows.
- **person_pattern_sensitivity_rule_built**: Refinement of the incumbent `rule_built_tally_switch_triplet` (running heads-minus-tails tally judged by closeness of its span to a personal expected span with person-specific, length-scaled sensitivity; switching judged by closeness to a shared ideal rate with person-specific weight; a reward for variety of three-flip chunks; a penalty on sequences built by a copying or mirror rule; a personal left/right lean). The one change: people differ in how much they attend to the pattern structure of a sequence — each person has their own pattern sensitivity, a single trait that scales both structural cues together (chunk variety and the rule-built penalty), so some people reliably reject recycled-chunk and rule-built sequences while others barely notice them and judge only by tally and switching. This addresses the critique (surviving FDR) that participants differ in how often they agree with the majority more than the incumbent allows, locating that heterogeneity in pattern perception rather than in decision noise or lapses.
- **streak_excess_pattern_rule_built**: Refinement of the incumbent `person_pattern_sensitivity_rule_built` (running heads-minus-tails tally judged by closeness of its span to a personal expected span with person-specific, length-scaled sensitivity; switching judged by closeness to an ideal rate with a person-specific weight; a reward for three-flip chunk variety and a penalty on rule-built sequences, both scaled by a personal pattern sensitivity; a personal left/right lean). The one change: people treat a long streak of identical flips as a salient sign of non-randomness in its own right — every flip of the longest run beyond two (HHH, HHHH, ...) counts against a sequence, in absolute flips rather than as a share of its length — a shared streak penalty taken from the pruned streak models (`periodic_ideal_absolute_streak_penalty`). Of two sequences with the same tally span, switching rate and chunk structure, people pick the one whose longest streak is shorter, including on pairs where pattern structure cannot act, where the incumbent predicts indifference.
- **terminal_run_pattern_rule_built**: Refinement of `streak_excess_pattern_rule_built` (tally span judged against a personal expected span with person-specific, length-scaled sensitivity; switching judged by closeness to an ideal rate with a person-specific weight; chunk variety and a rule-built penalty scaled by a personal pattern sensitivity; a personal left/right lean; and a shared penalty on streaks). The one change is where the streak is felt: people read a sequence left to right and judge it most by how it ends, so the streak that counts against randomness is the run the sequence finishes on — every repeated flip of its final run (…HH, …TTT) makes it look less random, while a sequence ending on a switch looks most random — replacing the penalty on the longest run anywhere. Of two sequences with equal switch counts, people therefore pick the one that ends with a switch, addressing the critique that people choose the sequence ending with a switch more often than the best model, which is blind to switch position, predicts.
