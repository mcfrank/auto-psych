People compare two coin sequences in working memory, and each person has their own memory span: a sequence no longer than their span is held whole and judged sharply, but once a sequence's length exceeds it, the flips no longer fit in mind and their impression of which sequence is more random fades toward a guess, more the further the length overshoots the span. The impression itself is the one the current models describe (how close the running heads-minus-tails tally's span and the switching rate are to what a fair coin should do, variety of three-flip chunks, a penalty for visibly rule-built sequences, and a personal left/right lean, which does not fade); the claim is the person-specific memory span, so people with short spans agree with the majority on short pairs but drift toward chance on long pairs whatever their pattern structure.
