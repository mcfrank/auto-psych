Refinement of the incumbent `terminal_streak_recency_pattern_rule` (tally span judged against a personal expected span, switching judged by closeness to a personal ideal switching rate, chunk variety and a rule-built penalty scaled by a personal pattern sensitivity, a shared penalty on the streak a sequence ends on, a personal left/right lean). The one change: people also notice a local stretch of strict alternation as a pattern in its own right — every four-flip stretch that zig-zags perfectly (HTHT or THTH) counts against a sequence's randomness, by a shared local-alternation penalty, even when its overall switching rate is the same as its partner's. Of two sequences with equal switch counts, people therefore pick the one whose switches are not bunched into an extended zig-zag, addressing the critique that among equal-switch pairs people choose the sequence with fewer strictly alternating four-flip windows more often than the incumbent predicts.
