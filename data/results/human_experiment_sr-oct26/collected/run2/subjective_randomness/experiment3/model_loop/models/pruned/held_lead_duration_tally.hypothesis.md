People read a sequence flip by flip while keeping a running tally of how far heads lead tails, and they expect a random coin's lead to keep changing hands: the tally should leave even and come back, again and again. A sequence in which one face stays ahead for a long unbroken stretch — the tally not getting back to even for many flips, even if the lead stays small (HHTHTHTH is one or two heads ahead throughout) — looks like a coin stuck in favour of one face and so less random, by a shared penalty on the longest stretch the lead is held without a return to even (as a share of the length). The rest of the impression is the current models' (tally span against a personal expected span, switching judged by closeness to an ideal rate with a personal, positive weight, chunk variety and a rule-built penalty scaled by a personal pattern sensitivity, the final-streak penalty, a personal left/right lean); the claim is that how long a lead is held, not only how far the tally swings, counts against randomness.
