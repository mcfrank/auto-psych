People judge a sequence by its single most off-pattern stretch of switching: they scan it in short four-flip stretches and register, for each stretch, how far its rate of switching sides is from their own ideal switching rate for a random coin, and the one stretch that departs most from that ideal — a local streak (no switches) or a local stretch of strict alternation (switch after switch) — alone decides how non-random the sequence looks; how the rest of the sequence switches does not count. Each person has their own ideal switching rate and their own decisiveness, plus a small habitual left/right lean, and picks the sequence whose worst stretch is closer to their ideal.
