People read a sequence flip by flip while keeping a running tally of how far heads lead tails (or tails lead heads), and judge randomness by how far that tally wanders from balance along the way: each person expects a fair coin's tally to drift away from even by some typical amount (scaled to how many flips have been read so far), and a sequence looks random to the extent that its average drift matches that personal expectation. A long streak makes the tally run away from balance (too much drift), while strict alternation pins it at even (too little drift), so the path of the tally — not the final count or the switch rate — decides the choice.
