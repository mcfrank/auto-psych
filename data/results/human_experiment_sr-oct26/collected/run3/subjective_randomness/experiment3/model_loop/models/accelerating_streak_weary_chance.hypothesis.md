Refinement of the incumbent `streak_weary_chance_pseudoflip` (people judge a sequence random to the extent their own second-order, streak-weary picture of a fair coin explains it better than the regular generators they suspect — a switch-biased coin, a heads-favoured trick coin and a repeating motif with slips — with pseudo-flip-diluted per-flip evidence, heavy-tailed signed person sensitivity and a side habit). The single change is in how their gambler's fallacy grows with streak length: the expectation that chance must switch does not rise steadily with each extra flip of a run but accelerates, so a run of three barely seems overdue while a run of five or six feels sharply improbable for a fair coin, and the first break of a long streak makes a sequence look far more random than the incumbent's linear belief allows. This addresses the critique that among long pairs where both sequences have at most two switches, people choose the more-switching sequence more often than the incumbent predicts.
