People judge randomness by keeping a running tally of heads minus tails as they read a sequence and summarising its path by two things only: how far the lead ever strays from balance (the largest lead either way) and how many times the tally comes back to a tie. A sequence looks random to the extent that this tally gist is a typical one for their own imagined fair coin (each person with their own belief about how often a fair coin switches), so the route the tally takes matters, not the final count: a sequence that drifts far and rarely returns to a tie, or one whose tally is pinned unusually tightly to balance for that person's coin, looks less random.
