People judge randomness as Bayesian inference — does their own second-order picture of a fair coin explain the sequence better than the regular ways it could have been made (a switch-biased coin, a heads-favoured trick coin, a short motif repeated with slips) — but with one resource limit: on each trial they do not weigh all those regular explanations together, they bring a single one to mind (sampled with the generators' prior weights, each person with their own readiness to think of a motif) and judge both sequences of the pair only against that one. So a sequence that is regular under just one explanation (for example a short alternation, which only a motif explains, or a lopsided pair of flips, which only a trick coin explains) is condemned only on the trials where that explanation happens to come to mind, making choices on such pairs less consistent than the normative average over explanations predicts — most visibly on very short pairs.
