Refinement of the incumbent `short_sequence_pseudoflip_dilution` (people judge a sequence random to the extent their own second-order model of a fair coin explains it better than the regular generators they suspect — a switch-biased coin, a heads-favoured trick coin and a repeating motif with slips — with the evidence weighed per flip of a pseudo-flip-padded length, heavy-tailed signed person sensitivity and a side habit). The single change is in how a person's quick impression of a sequence's switching grows with its number of switches: with diminishing returns (a fitted concave curve whose shape, not overall strength, is free; the incumbent's straight line is the special case), so the first one or two breaks of a streak make a sequence look much more random while each further switch adds less — addressing the FDR-surviving critique that in long, low-switch pairs people reward the first breaks of a streak more than the incumbent predicts.
