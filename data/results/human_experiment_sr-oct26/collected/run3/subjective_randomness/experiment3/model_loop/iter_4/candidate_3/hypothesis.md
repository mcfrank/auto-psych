Refinement of the incumbent `streak_weary_chance_pseudoflip` (people judge a sequence random to the extent their own second-order, streak-weary picture of a fair coin — one that expects a long run to break — explains it better than the regular generators they suspect: a switch-biased coin, a heads-favoured trick coin and a repeating motif with slips, with pseudo-flip-diluted per-flip evidence, heavy-tailed signed person sensitivity and a side habit). The single change is in how lopsided people imagine a trick coin to be: instead of a fixed, flat spread of possible rigging strengths, the spread is fitted, so people may picture trick coins as only mildly rigged (or as grossly rigged), which sets how much a modest heads/tails imbalance counts as evidence of a trick coin relative to switching. This addresses the critique that among length-8 pairs where the more-switching sequence is also the more lopsided, people choose the more-switching sequence more often than the incumbent predicts — they weigh heads/tails balance against switching less than its flat trick-coin prior implies.
