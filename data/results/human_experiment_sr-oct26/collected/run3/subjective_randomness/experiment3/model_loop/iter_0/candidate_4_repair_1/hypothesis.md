Refinement of the incumbent `gamblers_run_second_order_side_habit`: people still judge a sequence random to the extent their own second-order, gambler's-fallacy model of a fair coin explains it better than the regular generators they suspect (a switch-biased coin, a biased coin, a repeating short motif with slips, with each person's own motif suspicion), with person-specific sensitivity and side habit. The single change is that the person's quick impression of how often a sequence switches (judged against their own believed switch rate of chance) is weighed on its own length scale, separate from the Bayesian weighing of the regular explanations: the switch-count impression is discounted (or amplified) with sequence length by its own fitted power, so its pull relative to the pattern evidence differs between short and long pairs — addressing the critique that in very short pairs people prefer the more-switching sequence less than the incumbent's single length scaling predicts.
