Refinement of the incumbent `gamblers_second_order_person_lapse`: people still judge a sequence random to the extent their own second-order, gambler's-fallacy model of a fair coin explains it better than the regular generators they suspect (a switch-biased coin, a biased coin, a repeating short motif with slips, with each person's own motif suspicion), the evidence weighed per flip by a fitted power of length, with person-specific sensitivity and side habit. The single change is to what happens when a person does not follow their judgment: instead of picking a side at random, a person on a person-specific share of trials gives the response mapped the wrong way round — choosing the sequence that looks *less* random (confusing the instruction or the buttons) — and for a few participants this reversal is the rule rather than the exception, so they agree with the majority well below chance on clear-cut pairs, which a random lapse (which can only pull agreement towards one half) cannot produce; this addresses the FDR-surviving critique that participants differ in their rate of agreeing with the majority more than the lapse model produces.
