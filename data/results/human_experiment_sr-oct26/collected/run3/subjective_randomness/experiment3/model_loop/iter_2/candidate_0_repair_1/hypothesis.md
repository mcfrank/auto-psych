People read a sequence flip by flip into a limited first-in-first-out working-memory buffer: once the buffer is full, each new flip pushes out the oldest, so the randomness judgment is made only on the last few flips they still hold, with a buffer capacity that varies from trial to trial around a fitted typical span. On what they hold, they judge randomness as in the current best account — how much better their own second-order picture of a fair coin explains the remembered flips than the regular generators they suspect — so on trials where the buffer overflows, streaks, imbalances or patterns confined to the start of a long sequence are forgotten and do not count, while the same features at its end count fully.
