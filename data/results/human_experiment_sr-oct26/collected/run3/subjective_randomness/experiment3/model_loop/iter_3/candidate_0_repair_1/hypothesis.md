People do not judge the two sequences of a pair in isolation: the pair as a whole sets a shared reference — how ordinary the two look, on average, for a fair coin of that length (how typical their heads/tails balance and number of switches are) — and that reference sets how sharply the difference in randomness between them is felt. Beside an equally odd-looking partner (two streaky or lopsided sequences) the difference stands out by contrast and is acted on decisively, while between two ordinary-looking sequences the same difference is felt only weakly, so the same sequence is chosen more or less decisively depending on its partner; each sequence's randomness is otherwise judged as in the current best account.
