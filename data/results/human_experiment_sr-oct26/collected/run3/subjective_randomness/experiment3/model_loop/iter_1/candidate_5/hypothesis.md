Refinement of `attentive_lapse_heads_rigged_motif` (people judge a sequence random to the extent their own second-order model of a fair coin explains it better than the regular generators they suspect — a switch-biased coin, a heads-favoured trick coin and a repeating motif with slips — the evidence weighed per flip, with person-specific sensitivity and side habit, and a person-specific share of trials answered at random). The single change is that a person's departure from their own judgment is not capped at random guessing: each person's commitment to their randomness impression is drawn from a population that is mostly near full commitment but can fall below zero, so a few participants systematically pick the sequence that looks *less* random to everyone else (having reversed the question), agreeing with the majority well below chance — in the data four participants agree on only 22–41% of trials, which no lapse rate can produce — addressing the FDR-surviving critique that participants' agreement with the majority varies across people more than the lapse model produces.
