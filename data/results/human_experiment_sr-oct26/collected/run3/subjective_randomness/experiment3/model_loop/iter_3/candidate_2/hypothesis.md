The choice is made by a Thurstonian comparison: each sequence's felt randomness is read with Gaussian internal noise, and the person picks whichever noisy impression is larger. Choice probability therefore follows a normal (probit) curve of the evidence difference instead of a logistic one, so its tails are thinner: on clear-cut pairs, such as a long pure streak against a sequence with one or two breaks, choices become close to deterministic, while near-ties stay graded. The evidence itself is unchanged from the current best account (the person's own second-order picture of a fair coin weighed against a switch-biased coin, a heads-favoured trick coin and a repeating motif with slips, per flip with pseudo-flip dilution, with heavy-tailed signed person sensitivity and a side habit). This addresses the FDR-surviving critique that people pick the more-switching sequence in long low-switch pairs more decisively than the logistic model predicts.
