Refinement of `thurstonian_probit_decision` (people pick the sequence whose noisy felt randomness is larger — a probit comparison — where felt randomness is how much better their own second-order picture of a fair coin explains the sequence than the regular generators they suspect: a switch-biased coin, a heads-favoured trick coin and a repeating motif with slips, weighed per flip with pseudo-flip dilution, heavy-tailed signed person sensitivity and a side habit). The single change is in how lopsided people imagine a trick coin to be: instead of a fixed prior over its heads rate, how concentrated that prior is is fitted, so if people picture trick coins as grossly rigged, only an extreme heads/tails imbalance is explained away as rigging, and a moderately lopsided but frequently switching sequence still looks random. This addresses the critique that in long pairs where the more-switching sequence is also the more lopsided one, people choose the more-switching sequence more often than the best model predicts — they weigh heads/tails balance against switching less than it assumes.
