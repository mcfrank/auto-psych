People judge each sequence exactly as in the simplest current account (their own second-order model of a fair coin, with a person-specific believed switch rate, weighed against a switch-biased coin, a biased coin and a repeating short motif with slips, evidence per flip, person-specific sensitivity), but the decision rule has a person-specific lapse: on some share of trials, which differs from person to person, a participant does not judge at all and picks a side at random. This caps how often anyone agrees with the majority even on clear-cut pairs, so a subgroup of inattentive or near-random responders makes people's agreement with the majority vary more than graded sensitivity alone produces.
