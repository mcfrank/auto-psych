Refinement of `heads_rigged_second_order_side_habit` (people judge a sequence random to the extent their own second-order model of a fair coin explains it better than the regular generators they suspect — a switch-biased coin, a heads-favoured trick coin and a repeating motif with slips — the evidence weighed per flip, with each person's side habit deciding near-ties). The single change is that people also differ in how often they actually make the judgment: on a person-specific share of trials (drawn from a population, mostly small but large for some inattentive participants) a person does not compare the sequences at all and picks a side at random, so some participants agree with the majority far less than their graded sensitivity would imply even on clear-cut pairs — addressing the critique that participants' agreement with the majority varies across people more than the model produces.
