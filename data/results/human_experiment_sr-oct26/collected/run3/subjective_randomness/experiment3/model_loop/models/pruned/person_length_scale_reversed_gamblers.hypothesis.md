Refinement of `reversed_mapping_second_order_gamblers` (people judge a sequence random to the extent their own second-order, gambler's-fallacy model of a fair coin explains it better than the regular generators they suspect — a switch-biased coin, a biased coin, a repeating motif with slips, with their own motif suspicion and switch belief — with a side habit and a person-specific share of trials answered the wrong way round). The single change is that people differ in how they weigh evidence against sequence length: each person has their own length scaling, drawn from a population, so some judge the total evidence (long sequences feel decisive, two- and three-flip pairs barely matter) while others judge the evidence per flip (short pairs feel as clear-cut as long ones). This addresses the critique that people prefer the more-switching sequence in very short pairs less than one shared length scaling predicts, by letting that preference vary between people rather than across the population as a whole.
