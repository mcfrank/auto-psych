People's picture of a fair coin keeps a running tally of how far heads lead tails as they read a sequence, and they believe chance corrects that lead: whenever one face is ahead, they expect the next flip to favour the face that is behind, the more strongly the bigger the current lead. A sequence therefore looks random when its tally drifts and is promptly pulled back towards balance, and non-random when a lead keeps growing before it is corrected — so the path to the final count matters, and breaking a long streak (which corrects a large lead) makes a sequence look much more random. This self-correcting chance coin is weighed against the regular generators people suspect (a switch-biased coin, a heads-favoured trick coin and a repeating motif with slips) exactly as in the current best account, and replaces its run-length gambler's belief.
