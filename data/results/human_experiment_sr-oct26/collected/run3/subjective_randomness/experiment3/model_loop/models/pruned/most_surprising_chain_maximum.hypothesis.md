People judge how random a sequence looks by its single most striking stretch: the longest unbroken chain of repeats (a streak) or the longest unbroken chain of switches (strict alternation), whichever would be the more surprising to see in a sequence of that length from their own picture of a fair coin, which may believe a coin switches more (or less) than half the time. Only that one most improbable stretch counts — how many switches or how much balance the rest of the sequence has does not — so the first one or two breaks of a long streak, which cut its longest run sharply, make a sequence look much more random, and the sequence whose most striking stretch is less improbable is chosen, with people differing in decisiveness and side habit.
