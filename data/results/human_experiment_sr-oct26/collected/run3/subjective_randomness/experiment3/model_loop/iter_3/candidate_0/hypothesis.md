People do not judge the two sequences of a pair in isolation: the pair as a whole sets a shared reference — the gist of how ordinary the two look for a fair coin (how typical their heads/tails balance and number of switches are) — and that reference sets how sharply the difference between them is felt. Beside an equally odd-looking partner (two streaky or lopsided sequences) the differences in randomness stand out by contrast and are acted on decisively, while between two ordinary-looking sequences the same difference is felt only weakly, so the same sequence is chosen more or less decisively depending on its partner. Each sequence's randomness is otherwise judged as in the current best account.
