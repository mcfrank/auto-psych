People judge randomness by the single most striking patterned stretch in a sequence: they scan for the longest contiguous stretch that keeps copying one short motif — a streak (H, H, H…), strict alternation (HT, HT…), or a repeated three- or four-flip motif — and how many flips that one stretch "predicts" sets how non-random the whole sequence looks, whatever the rest of it does. Each kind of motif has its own salience (a run of copies of a streak need not be as damning as an equally long alternation), and the sequence whose most striking patterned stretch is weaker is chosen as more random.
