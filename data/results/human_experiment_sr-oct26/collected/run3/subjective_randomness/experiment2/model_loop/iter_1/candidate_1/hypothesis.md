People commit to a "more random" choice only when the two sequences differ clearly in their single gist cue, how often they switch between heads and tails (each person with their own signed preference for switching); when the difference in switch rate falls inside an indifference band, they feel no real preference and fall back on their own habitual response side instead of judging. Side habits therefore show up mainly on pairs with similar switch counts, which makes people's overall rates of choosing Left vary more than a judgment-only model allows.
