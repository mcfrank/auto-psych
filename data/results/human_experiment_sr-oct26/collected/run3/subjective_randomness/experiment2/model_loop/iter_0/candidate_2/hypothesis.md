People do not compare the two sequences' raw weight of evidence; each sequence is first turned into a felt verdict, "how likely is it that this one came from a fair coin rather than from some regular generator" (a fair coin believed to over-alternate, against a switch-biased coin, a biased coin and a repeating short motif), and the choice is driven by the difference between the two verdicts. Because verdicts saturate, the same difference in evidence is decisive when the two sequences are both ambiguous and barely matters when both clearly look random or both clearly look regular, so a sequence's pull on the choice depends on its partner.
