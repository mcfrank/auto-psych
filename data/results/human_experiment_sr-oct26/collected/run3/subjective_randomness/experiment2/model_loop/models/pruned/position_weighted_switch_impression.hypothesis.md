People judge a sequence random to the extent their own model of a fair coin (with a person-specific believed switch rate, and a second-order belief about switching twice in a row) explains it better than the regular generators (a switch-biased Markov coin, a biased coin, and a repeating short motif with slips, with each person's own suspicion of motifs), weighing the evidence per flip by a fitted power of length. The single new claim is about the order of reading: people read the sequence left to right and their felt sense of how often it switches is not position-blind — switches and repeats near the end of the sequence (recency) or near its start (primacy, fitted direction and strength) weigh more in that impression, so two sequences with the same switch count but with their streak at different places look differently random.
