People judge randomness as Bayesian inference — does their own picture of a fair coin (with a person-specific belief about how often it switches) explain the sequence better than regular generators (a switch-biased coin, a biased coin, a short motif repeated with slips) — but they read the sequence flip by flip with a leaky memory. The evidence each flip gives for chance over regularity fades as later flips arrive (or, if the fitted direction is reversed, early flips dominate as a first impression), so where in a sequence a streak, a switch or a pattern break occurs changes how random it looks, not only how many there are.
