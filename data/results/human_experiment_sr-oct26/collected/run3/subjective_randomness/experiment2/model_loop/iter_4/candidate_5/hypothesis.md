Refinement of `face_biased_second_order_motif`: people still judge a sequence random to the extent their own second-order model of a fair coin (a person-specific believed switch rate, shifted right after a switch by a shared amount) explains it better than the regular generators (a switch-biased Markov coin, a biased coin, and a repeating short motif with slips, with each person's own suspicion of motifs), the evidence weighed per flip by a fitted power of length. The single change is where the coin's label asymmetry lives: instead of believing a fair coin lands on tails more often overall, people believe chance repeats one face more readily than the other (fitted; the data suggest a run of tails is more expected of chance than an equally long run of heads), so a heads-heads repeat is stronger evidence against chance than a tails-tails repeat — locating the FDR-surviving critique (people choose the head-heavier sequence less often than a symmetric model predicts) in streaks of heads rather than in the head count itself.
