Refinement of the incumbent `length_normalised_chance_vs_motif`: people still judge a sequence random to the extent their own model of a fair coin explains it better than the regular generators (a switch-biased Markov coin, a biased coin, and a repeating short motif with occasional slips), with the generators' unknowns averaged out and the evidence weighed per flip. The single change is that the one distortion of the normative account — the believed switch rate of a fair coin — is held differently by each person, drawn from a population: most people believe chance over-alternates, but some believe chance is streaky, so they credit sequences with fewer switches as more random, addressing the critique that a sizeable minority of participants systematically prefer the sequence with fewer switches.
