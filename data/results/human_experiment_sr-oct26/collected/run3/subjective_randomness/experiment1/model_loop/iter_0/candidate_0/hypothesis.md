People judge randomness by reading each sequence flip by flip and checking how well every flip matches a gambler's-fallacy expectation: after a streak, they expect the coin to switch, increasingly so the longer the streak has run. A sequence looks random to the extent its flips conform to this negative-recency expectation, with the most recently read flips (near the end of the sequence) weighing more heavily, and people differ in how strongly this conformity drives their choice.
