Refinement of the incumbent `coincidence_decisive_exemplar` (partly averaged similarity to the examples on magnitude, final digit, asymmetrically salient parity and the set's common divisor, plus distance-free multiples, factor-kin and round-number routes, with suspicious coincidences making the yes/no decision more decisive), with one component grafted from `problem_size_multiple_retrieval`: a far multiple of an example feels like its kin only if the multiplication fact linking them is retrieved from memory, and such facts are harder to retrieve the larger the problem (the sum of the two factors, as in the arithmetic problem-size effect). So small facts give a clear yes (14 after "7", 64 after "16", 8 after "4"), while multiples resting on large facts (98 after "49", 86 after "43", 92 after "23", 54 or 60 after "3") get almost no route and a clear no. This targets the critique that single-example and incoherent sets get more all-or-none answers than the incumbent predicts, which leaves such far multiples at a graded maybe.
