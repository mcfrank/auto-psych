Refinement of the incumbent `coincidence_rule_violation_route` (partly averaged similarity to the stored examples on magnitude, final digit, parity and the set's common divisor, plus distance-free factor-kin, whole-set round and parity-preserving multiples routes, a fixed loss for breaking the set's coincidence rule, and a decision that grows more all-or-none after suspicious coincidences). The one change: a stored example only brings its multiples to mind if people know it as a factor, and a two-digit prime (13, 17, 23, 31, 43, 47) is an indivisible number that people never multiply in everyday arithmetic, so its multiples route is much weaker. After a lone "17", "43" or "23", far multiples such as 34, 51, 86 or 92 are therefore mostly rejected, while equally far multiples of one-digit or composite examples (14 after "7", 64 after "16", 99 after "33", 50 after "25") keep the full route and are mostly accepted.
