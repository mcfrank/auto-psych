People do not compare numbers on a few geometric dimensions; they represent each number as a bundle of familiar properties (odd or even, multiple of 3 to 12, square, cube, power of two, prime, final digit, decade) and judge a target by Tversky-style featural contrast with each example. Properties the target shares with an example raise its similarity, while properties only one of the two has lower it, each property weighted by how rare it is among 1-100. So 64 after "16" or 99 after "33" (many shared, rare properties) is endorsed at any distance, while a prime such as 43 or 17 lends almost nothing to its double. Pooled featural similarity, not set-level rule reading, drives the yes/no choice.
