People know a number's multiples only through its times-table factors: an example built from small factors (16, 22, 33, 7, 25) brings its multiples to mind as kin at any distance, but an example with a prime factor above 11 (17, 23, 43, 13, 47) is an arithmetic dead end, so its multiples (34, 92, 86, 78, 94) get little of the distance-free multiples route. They are judged by ordinary similarity instead. Everything else is as in the current best account: partly averaged similarity on magnitude, final digit, parity and common divisor; factor-kin and whole-set round routes; a decision made more decisive by suspicious coincidences; and support lost for breaking the set's coincidence rule.
