People judge a target by its partly averaged similarity to the examples (magnitude, final digit, parity, the set's common divisor, plus distance-free factor-kin, whole-set round and parity-preserving multiples routes) and turn it into a sense of how likely the target is to belong, which is sharper after sets with a suspicious coincidence, exactly as in the current best account. The one change is the decision rule: on each trial people either probability-match, saying yes about as often as the target seems to belong, or maximize, saying yes whenever it seems more likely in than out. Because how often people maximize does not depend on the set, targets that clearly fit or clearly fail get all-or-none answers after single examples and incoherent sets too, while the matching trials keep graded answers in between.
