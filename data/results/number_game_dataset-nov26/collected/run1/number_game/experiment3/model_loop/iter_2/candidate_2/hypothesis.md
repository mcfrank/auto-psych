Refinement of `coincidence_decisive_exemplar` (partly averaged similarity to the examples on magnitude, final digit, asymmetrically salient parity and the set's common divisor, plus distance-free multiples, factor-kin and round-number routes, with a decision that grows more decisive after suspicious coincidences). The one change: a target that stands in an explicit arithmetic relation to an example (it is a multiple of it, shares a factor of 3 or more with it, or is a round number next to a 0/5-ending example) is checked as a rule ("is it in the 7 times table?") rather than felt out by resemblance, so people answer it all-or-none even after single examples and incoherent sets. Similarity still decides which way the answer goes, but for such targets (64 after "16", 99 after "33", 86 after "43", 54 after "3", 72 after "24, 35") yes-rates sit nearer 0 or 1 than for targets with no arithmetic tie to any example.
