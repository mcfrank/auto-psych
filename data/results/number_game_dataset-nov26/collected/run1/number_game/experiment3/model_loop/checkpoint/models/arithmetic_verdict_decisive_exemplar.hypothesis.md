Refinement of `coincidence_decisive_exemplar`: people still judge a target by its partly averaged similarity to the examples (magnitude, final digit, asymmetrically salient parity, the set's common divisor, plus distance-free multiples, factor-kin and round-number routes), and a suspicious coincidence among the examples still makes their yes/no decision more decisive. The one change: when the target is an arithmetic relative of an example (a multiple of it, or sharing a factor of 3 or more with it), people decide by explicitly checking that relation ("is 98 a multiple of 7?"), and that verdict is more all-or-none than a similarity judgment. So even after single examples and incoherent sets, multiples and factor-kin get polarized answers (14 after "7" or 99 after "33" almost always accepted; 34 after "17" or 86 after "43" mostly rejected), while other targets stay graded.
