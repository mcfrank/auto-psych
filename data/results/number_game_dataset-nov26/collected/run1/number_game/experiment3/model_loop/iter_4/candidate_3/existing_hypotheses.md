# Existing hypotheses

Each model below is ONE cognitive hypothesis, with how it stands on the current data by ELPD-LOO (best first). `elpd_diff ± dse` is a model's deficit against the best and the standard error of that difference (with the responses to each stimulus pair counted together, as they are correlated): within about 2·dse the two are statistically tied on this data; beyond it the model has lost. "PSIS-LOO unreliable" means the estimate itself is untrustworthy (too many high-Pareto-k trials), not that the model is bad. Propose a hypothesis that is genuinely different from these, or a refinement of a single one of them — never a combination of several.

## coincidence_rule_violation_route  — rank 0, the best model on this data, ELPD-LOO -4083.0

Refinement of the incumbent `parity_preserving_decisive_route` (people judge a target by its partly averaged similarity to the examples on magnitude, final digit, parity and the set's common divisor, plus distance-free routes for factor-kin, whole-set round kinship and parity-preserving multiples, and a suspicious coincidence makes the yes/no decision more decisive). The one change: when two or more examples share a suspicious coincidence, people read its most specific form (a shared final digit, else all multiples of 5, else a shared parity) as a rule. A target that breaks that rule loses a fixed share of all its support, its arithmetic and round-number routes included, whatever its distance from the examples. So after "15, 85, 45" or "12, 14, 16", targets that break the rule (e.g. 33 after "12, 14, 16", or 40 after "15, 85, 45") are rejected more consistently than graded similarity allows, even when they are far away or factor-kin of an example. Single examples and incoherent sets are unaffected.

## match_or_maximize_decision  — rank 4, 37.9 ± 12.8 nats behind the best (3.0× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -4120.9

People judge a target by its partly averaged similarity to the examples (magnitude, final digit, parity, the set's common divisor, plus distance-free factor-kin, whole-set round and parity-preserving multiples routes) and turn it into a sense of how likely the target is to belong, which is sharper after sets with a suspicious coincidence, exactly as in the current best account. The one change is the decision rule: on each trial people either probability-match, saying yes about as often as the target seems to belong, or maximize, saying yes whenever it seems more likely in than out. ## sample_size_neglect_concepts  — rank 29, 225.7 ± 30.5 nats behind the best (7.4× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -4308.7

People infer the hidden program as a Bayesian concept learner would: they weigh candidate rules (odd/even, primes, squares, cubes, powers, multiples of a number, a shared final digit) and magnitude ranges by how well each explains the examples, favouring small concepts that would make the examples a suspicious coincidence (the size principle), and say yes according to how probable it is that the target belongs. The one distortion is sample-size neglect: people treat a set of several examples as only a little more informative than a single one, so the total evidence grows much more slowly with the number of examples than the size principle says. Four examples therefore narrow generalization far less than normative inference implies (e.g. "63, 27, 99, 33" still admits broad ranges and loose rules), while single-example generalization is unchanged.

## featural_contrast_exemplar  — rank 31, 503.0 ± 40.8 nats behind the best (12.3× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -4586.0

People do not compare numbers on a few geometric dimensions; they represent each number as a bundle of familiar properties (odd or even, multiple of 3 to 12, square, cube, power of two, prime, final digit, decade) and judge a target by Tversky-style featural contrast with each example. Properties the target shares with an example raise its similarity, while properties only one of the two has lower it, each property weighted by how rare it is among 1-100. So 64 after "16" or 99 after "33" (many shared, rare properties) is endorsed at any distance, while a prime such as 43 or 17 lends almost nothing to its double. Pooled featural similarity, not set-level rule reading, drives the yes/no choice.
