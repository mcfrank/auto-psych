Refinement of the incumbent `common_divisor_sharpened_exemplar`. People still judge a target by its summed similarity to the examples (magnitude, final digit, parity and a factor dimension), with generalization sharpened on every dimension when the examples share a suspicious coincidence. The one change is to how the factor dimension is read. People take the common divisor of the examples (for a single example, the example itself) as a source of candidate "multiples of k" rules, and they accept any factor of 3 or more of it as such a rule. A target loses similarity to every example only when it shares no factor of 3 or more with that common divisor. So after a single example such as "7", multiples of 7 keep full similarity and other numbers are penalized. After "90, 45", 30 and 60 escape the penalty because they share 15 with the divisor 45. After "8, 64", even multiples of 4 that are not multiples of 8 also escape it.
