Refinement of `common_divisor_rule_exemplar`. People still judge a target by its summed similarity to the examples (magnitude, final digit, parity and a factor dimension), sharpened on every dimension when the examples share a suspicious coincidence, and they still read the factor dimension at the level of the whole set, from the examples' common divisor (for a single example, that number itself). The one change is that the factor test is kinship, not strict divisibility. When the set's common divisor is 3 or more, a target loses similarity to every example only if it shares no factor of 3 or more with that divisor. So 30 and 60 still fit "90, 45", 25 fits "80, 70, 90, 100", and after the single example "7" its multiples 14, 49 and 98 are endorsed while 15 is not, whereas a target that shares only the factor 2, or nothing, is penalized.
