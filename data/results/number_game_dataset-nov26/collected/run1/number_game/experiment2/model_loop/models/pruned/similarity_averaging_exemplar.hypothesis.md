Refinement of the incumbent `common_divisor_sharpened_exemplar`. People still judge a target by its similarity to the examples (magnitude, final digit, parity, and the set's common divisor), sharpened on every dimension when the examples share a suspicious coincidence. The one change is how evidence from several examples is pooled: people do not add up the similarity to every example, they (partly) average it, so each further example dilutes rather than adds to a target's support, and a target is judged by how well it fits the examples on average rather than how many it resembles. This predicts that adding examples does not make dissimilar targets more acceptable, that a far target in a sparse pair like "25, 82" is not carried by summing two weak similarities, and that single examples generalize relatively more broadly than multi-example sets, with the degree of averaging (from pure summing to pure averaging) inferred from the data.
