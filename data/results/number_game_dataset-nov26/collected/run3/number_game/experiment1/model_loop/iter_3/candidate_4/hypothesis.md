Refinement of `parity_restricted_concepts`: people do the same tempered-evidence Bayesian concept learning over the 45 individually weighted rules, all intervals of 1-100, and their odd-only and even-only versions. The one change is that how strongly they apply the size principle depends on how the examples are shown. When three or more examples come in sorted order (ascending or descending), people take the list to have been picked systematically, not sampled at random from the concept. They then treat the examples as weaker evidence for the smallest consistent concept, by an inferred factor (a factor of zero recovers the incumbent). This predicts that the same digit-sharing examples (e.g. 62,32,22 vs 63,23,43) loosen the "ends in d" rule and earn more yeses for targets with other final digits when they are shown in sorted order than when they are shuffled.
