People do the same tempered Bayesian concept learning over the 45 individually weighted rules and all intervals of 1-100, but when asked whether the program would produce a target they do not ask "is it in the concept?" — they judge how probable it is that the program, sampling from its concept, would output that number next (so a number in a small concept seems far likelier to come out than one in a big concept), and say "yes" when that predicted chance is high enough relative to an internal criterion, with graded noise. This disagrees most sharply with the best model's membership readout on members of broad rules: rule members outside the examples' range (supported only by large rules such as "multiples of 3" or "odd") are endorsed much less than in-range members, which also get the high output probability of tight intervals around the examples, and members of very large rules (odd, even) are endorsed less than members of small ones.
