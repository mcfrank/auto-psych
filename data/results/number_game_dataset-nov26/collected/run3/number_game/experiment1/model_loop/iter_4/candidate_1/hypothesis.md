Refinement of `monotone_order_range_prior_shift`: people do the same tempered, serial-position-weighted Bayesian concept learning over the 45 weighted rules and all intervals of 1-100 (with the sorted-order shift toward ranges). The one change is that people think of a magnitude range on a compressed, roughly logarithmic mental number line. So they assume a range's examples are spread evenly along that compressed line, and they judge a range's size by its length on that line. The degree of compression is inferred; no compression recovers the original model. This predicts that generalization from a range is asymmetric: extending a range downward toward small numbers costs more than extending it upward by the same amount. So targets above the examples' range are endorsed more than targets equally far below it, and small examples count as tighter evidence than large ones.
