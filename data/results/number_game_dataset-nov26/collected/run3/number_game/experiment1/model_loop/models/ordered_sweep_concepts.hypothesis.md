People entertain one more kind of concept than the current models allow: an ordered sweep, in which the program runs through a range of numbers in increasing or decreasing order, so examples shown in sorted order are much more probable under a sweep than under a randomly sampled concept (by the number of possible orderings), and a sweep receives an inferred share of prior belief. All else is the parity-restricted tempered Bayesian concept learner; because sorted presentation shifts belief from the tight "ends in d" rule toward a sweep over the examples' magnitude range, people endorse in-range targets that break the shared digit more when the examples come in sorted order than when the same kind of set is shuffled.
