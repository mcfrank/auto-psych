I refine `graded_explaining_away_by_specificity`: people infer the concept by the same tempered, sample-size-neglecting Bayesian concept learning over rules, magnitude ranges and bounded rules, and when they compare the examples they set aside a broader rule implied by a more specific rule all the examples share, the more completely the more specific that shared rule is. The one change, a component taken from `range_evidence_with_guessing_lapse`: on some trials people do not consult their inferred concept at all and guess, saying "yes" at an inferred rate whatever the target is. So the floor of yeses to numbers that no plausible concept contains, and the occasional "no" to the examples themselves, come from guessing rather than from a prior favouring very wide ranges, letting the imagined ranges stay closer to the examples while same-parity numbers with a different final digit (63,23,43 → 31, 35) are still rejected.
