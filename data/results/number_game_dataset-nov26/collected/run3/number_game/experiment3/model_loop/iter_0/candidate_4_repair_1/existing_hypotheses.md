# Existing hypotheses

Each model below is ONE cognitive hypothesis, with how it stands on the current data by ELPD-LOO (best first). `elpd_diff ± dse` is a model's deficit against the best and the standard error of that difference (with the responses to each stimulus pair counted together, as they are correlated): within about 2·dse the two are statistically tied on this data; beyond it the model has lost. "PSIS-LOO unreliable" means the estimate itself is untrustworthy (too many high-Pareto-k trials), not that the model is bad. Propose a hypothesis that is genuinely different from these, or a refinement of a single one of them — never a combination of several.

## consistent_examples_bounded_rules  — rank 0, the best model on this data, ELPD-LOO -4163.9

Refinement of `comparison_dependent_concepts` (the same tempered, sample-size-neglecting Bayesian concept learning over the 45 weighted rules, size-weighted intervals, their odd/even versions and range-bounded rules, with digit rules and bounded rules discounted when only one example is shown). The one change takes that model's own premise further. A rule confined to the examples' span ("ends in 3 between 48 and 63") is built by comparing the examples and seeing that they share the rule, so people entertain it only when every example fits the rule. A bounded rule that one of the examples breaks is never considered, rather than kept with that example written off as a stray, and no parameter is added. People should then drop a rule that most examples share but one breaks, and fall back on the examples' range or on whole rules. In-range numbers lacking the majority pattern (48,63,53 → 49, 51) should be endorsed more than the incumbent predicts, while sets that a rule fits fully are unchanged.

## log_odds_response_bias  — no comparison row

People infer the program's concept exactly as the current best model says (tempered, sample-size-neglecting Bayesian concept learning over weighted rules, size-weighted ranges, their odd/even versions and fully consistent range-bounded rules), but their yes/no answer does not report that belief directly: they apply a fixed response criterion that shifts the odds of their belief by a constant amount toward "yes" (or "no"). Because a constant shift in odds moves uncertain beliefs much more than confident ones, targets people are unsure about, such as in-range numbers after examples that no rule fully explains, should be endorsed noticeably more (for a yes-bias) than the belief alone predicts, while examples and clear non-members barely change.

## round_number_range_concepts  — no comparison row

People think of magnitude ranges in round numbers: besides mathematical and digit rules and arbitrary ranges, they explicitly entertain the few ranges whose ends fall on decade boundaries ("21 to 50", "the 60s and 70s"), and because these round ranges are few, each is a priori far more plausible than an arbitrary range. So when no rule fits all the examples, or only two examples are shown, people generalize to the round range that covers the examples, endorsing numbers inside that decade-bounded span more, and numbers beyond its decade boundaries less, than learners with only rules and arbitrary ranges would.

## central_tendency_feature_prototype  — no comparison row

People do not infer a rule. They boil the examples down to one prototype, which records how often each familiar number property (odd/even, prime, square, cube, power, multiple of k, final digit, digit contained) occurs among the examples and where the examples sit on average on the number line. They say "yes" to a target according to its similarity to that prototype. A property counts toward the comparison more steeply the more consistently the examples share it, and the target loses similarity for the share of that weight that falls on properties it lacks and for its distance from the examples' central magnitude. This predicts that when no property is shared by every example, generalization rests on closeness to the examples' centre, so targets between the examples are endorsed more than equally related targets outside their span, while a property shared by all the examples dominates the judgment.

## stray_aware_consistent_concepts  — no comparison row

Refinement of `consistent_examples_bounded_rules`. People use the same tempered Bayesian concept learning over weighted rules, size-weighted ranges, their odd/even versions and bounded rules that every example must fit, and they still neglect sample size and discount comparison-based rules when shown one example. The one change: the share of examples people believe are random strays is inferred instead of fixed at 10%. If people expect few strays, a rule that one example breaks loses most of its belief. So for sets that no rule fits fully, people fall back on the range covering all the examples: they should endorse in-range numbers more and out-of-range numbers less than the incumbent predicts.
