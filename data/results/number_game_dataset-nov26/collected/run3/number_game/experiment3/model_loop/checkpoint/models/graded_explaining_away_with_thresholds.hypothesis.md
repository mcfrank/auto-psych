I refine `graded_explaining_away_with_guessing`: people infer the concept by tempered, sample-size-neglecting Bayesian concept learning over weighted rules, size-weighted magnitude ranges and bounded rules. They set aside a broader rule implied by a more specific rule all the examples share, more completely the more specific that shared rule is, and on some trials they guess "yes" at an inferred rate without consulting the concept. The one change, a component taken from `magnitude_threshold_concepts`, is that people also treat one-sided magnitude thresholds ("numbers above 40", "numbers below 30") as concepts in their own right, so a range running to the top or the bottom of 1-100 gains extra plausibility, by a separate inferred amount for "above" and "below". Because guessing now supplies the floor of yeses, a threshold boost should mean that people generalize from the examples toward one end of the number line, more toward larger numbers than smaller ones, while ranges otherwise stay close to the examples and same-parity numbers with a different final digit are still rejected after sets that share a final digit.
