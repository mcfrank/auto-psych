People evaluate a magnitude range ("numbers between 20 and 70") by eliminative consistency checking, not by statistical sampling: they ask only whether the range admits each example, and every admitted example counts as the same fixed amount of evidence however wide the range is, while mathematical and digit rules are still weighed by the size principle within the same tempered, sample-size-neglecting Bayesian concept learner (with one-sided thresholds, odd/even versions, bounded rules, lone-example discounts and explaining away). Because a wide range that covers all the examples is then not penalized for its width, examples spread across a wide span should be filled in as much as examples spread across a narrow one, and when no rule fits (as in mixed-parity sets) people should endorse targets inside the examples' span much more than targets outside it, with how far they extend beyond the span set by their prior over range sizes alone.
