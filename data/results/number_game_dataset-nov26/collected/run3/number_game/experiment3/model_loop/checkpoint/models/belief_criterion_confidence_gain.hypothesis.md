People infer the program's concept by the same simple tempered Bayesian concept learning as the simplest current model, but they do not answer "yes" with a probability equal to their belief (probability matching): they weigh how strongly they believe the concept contains the target against an internal criterion of how sure they must be, and this comparison is noisy, so their answers are more or less all-or-none than the belief itself by an inferred confidence gain on the log-odds of belief. Unlike a guessing lapse, which pulls every answer toward one fixed rate by the same proportion, this rule moves confident beliefs (examples, clear non-members) much less than moderate ones, so a gain below 1 leaves near-certain judgments close to certainty while it compresses uncertain ones toward the criterion, and a gain above 1 makes them more extreme.
