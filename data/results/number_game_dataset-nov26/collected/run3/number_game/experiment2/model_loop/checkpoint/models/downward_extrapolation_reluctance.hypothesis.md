People infer the program's concept from the examples as the current best model does (tempered, sample-size-neglecting Bayesian concept learning over weighted rules, size-weighted ranges, their odd/even versions and range-bounded rules, with digit rules discounted for a lone example), but they treat the examples as where the program's output starts, and they think of it as producing numbers counting upward. Accepting a number smaller than every example therefore means extrapolating backward, which people trust less the farther the target lies below the smallest example: the chance of saying yes is the belief that the concept contains the target, discounted by an inferred rate for each step below the smallest example, while targets above or among the examples are not discounted. This predicts that a digit rule's members below the examples (48,78,38,98 → 8, 18; 97,77,87,47 → 7) are endorsed less than its members above them, and in general that people generalize less downward than upward.
