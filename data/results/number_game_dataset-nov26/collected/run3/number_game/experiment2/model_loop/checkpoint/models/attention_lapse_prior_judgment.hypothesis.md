People do not attend to the examples on every trial. They infer the hidden concept from the examples, using the same Bayesian concept learning as `digit_rules_need_comparison`. But on an inferred share of trials their attention lapses: they do not look at the examples and judge the target alone, answering "yes" with their prior belief that a program would produce that number. This predicts that examples and clear members of a confidently inferred rule are endorsed well short of certainty (a digit rule extended outside the examples' range gets about 0.85 yes, not 0.95). Non-members keep a target-dependent floor of yeses that is higher for numbers belonging to broad, a-priori plausible concepts.
