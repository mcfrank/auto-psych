Refinement of `digit_rules_need_comparison`: people learn concepts the same way. They do tempered, sample-size-neglecting Bayesian concept learning over the 45 weighted rules, size-weighted intervals, their odd/even versions and range-bounded rules, and they discount digit rules when only one example is shown. The one change, taken from `open_multiples_guessing_lapse`, is that people do not consult this concept on every trial. On an inferred share of trials they skip the inference and guess, answering "yes" at an inferred guessing rate whatever the target is (a lapse rate of 0 recovers the incumbent). This predicts that even clear members of a firmly inferred rule are endorsed well short of certainty: the shared final digit beyond the examples' range (59,49,99,69 → 9, 19; 97,77,87,47 → 7) gets about 0.8–0.9 yes, not 0.95. The examples themselves get only about 0.7–0.85, and clear non-members keep a small floor of yeses.
