Refinement of `comparison_dependent_concepts` (tempered, sample-size-neglecting Bayesian concept learning over the 45 weighted rules, size-weighted intervals, their odd/even versions and range-bounded rules, where concepts that can only be noticed by comparing examples, such as digit patterns and rules bounded to the examples' span, lose prior plausibility when only one example is shown). The one change is to apply that principle to every concept defined by a span of examples. A plain magnitude range ("numbers from 20 to 40") is fixed by where the examples lie relative to one another, so a lone example cannot suggest it either. When a single example is shown, ranges are discounted by the same inferred amount as digit and bounded rules, and no parameter is added. A lone example should then be read almost purely as an instance of an arithmetic rule: its far rule members (25 → 100, 66 → 99, 10 → 90) are endorsed more and non-members that lie close to it (6 → 9, 6 → 20, 1 → 27) less than the incumbent predicts, while sets of two or more examples are unchanged.
