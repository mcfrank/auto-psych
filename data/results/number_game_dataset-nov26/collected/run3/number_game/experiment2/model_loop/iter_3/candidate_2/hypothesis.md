People learn the program's concept by the same tempered Bayesian concept learning as the current best model: weighted mathematical/digit rules, magnitude ranges, odd/even versions and range-bounded rules, with sample-size neglect, and digit rules discounted after a lone example. They have one resource limit, in memory: on some trials, at an inferred rate, the examples are no longer available when the target is judged, so people answer from prior belief alone, saying "yes" as often as they would expect a program to produce that number before seeing any examples. This predicts that confident judgments fall short of certainty. Members of a clear digit rule outside the examples' range, and the examples themselves, get about 0.85 yes, not 0.95. Clear non-members keep a floor of yeses that is higher for a-priori plausible numbers (members of broad rules and wide ranges).
