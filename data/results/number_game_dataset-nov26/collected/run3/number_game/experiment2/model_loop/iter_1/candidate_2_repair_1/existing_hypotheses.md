# Existing hypotheses

Each model below is ONE cognitive hypothesis, with how it stands on the current data by ELPD-LOO (best first). `elpd_diff ± dse` is a model's deficit against the best and the standard error of that difference (with the responses to each stimulus pair counted together, as they are correlated): within about 2·dse the two are statistically tied on this data; beyond it the model has lost. "PSIS-LOO unreliable" means the estimate itself is untrustworthy (too many high-Pareto-k trials), not that the model is bad. Propose a hypothesis that is genuinely different from these, or a refinement of a single one of them — never a combination of several.

## range_size_prior_concepts  — rank 0, the best model on this data, ELPD-LOO -3157.5

Refinement of `range_bounded_parity_concepts`: people do the same tempered-evidence Bayesian concept learning over the 45 weighted rules, all intervals of 1-100, their odd-only and even-only versions, and range-bounded rules, but they do not think every magnitude range is equally plausible a priori — a tiny range such as "numbers from 10 to 12" seems an unlikely thing for a program to produce, so a range's prior plausibility grows (or shrinks) as an inferred power of its size (power 0 recovers the incumbent). The one change is this size-dependent prior on ranges; because the size principle favours tiny ranges most when there is only one example, a broad-range prior moves belief from tiny ranges around a lone example to the rules it belongs to, so people generalize from a single example (10 → 80, 11 → 55) much more than the incumbent allows, while several examples still pin down a tight concept.

## single_example_rule_reading  — rank 1, 13.5 ± 11.1 nats behind the best (1.2× dse: statistically tied with the best), ELPD-LOO -3171.1

Refinement of `parity_restricted_concepts` (people do tempered-evidence Bayesian concept learning over the 45 weighted rules, all intervals of 1-100, and their odd-only and even-only versions). The one change: a magnitude range is a concept defined by its endpoints, so a single example cannot suggest one — when only one example is shown, people read it as an instance of a mathematical or digit property, and prior belief shifts from range concepts to rules by an inferred amount (zero shift recovers the original model). This predicts that from a lone example people endorse the members of the rules that contain it broadly (11 → 22, 55, 99; 10 → 60, 90) rather than confining belief to a tight range around the example, while judgments about sets of two or more examples are unchanged.

## sample_size_neglect_evidence  — rank 2, 14.3 ± 9.6 nats behind the best (1.5× dse: statistically tied with the best), ELPD-LOO -3171.8

People do the same tempered Bayesian concept learning as the best model (45 weighted rules, all intervals, their odd/even versions and range-bounded rules), with one distortion: they neglect sample size, so the total weight they give the examples grows less than in proportion to how many examples they saw — the first example counts heavily, and each further example adds less. This predicts that a single example already pins down its most salient rule (e.g. 10 → multiples of 10), producing many yeses for that rule's members, while adding more examples sharpens generalization far less than an ideal learner would.

## weak_sampling_hypothesis_elimination  — rank 3, 24.9 ± 16.8 nats behind the best (1.5× dse: statistically tied with the best), ELPD-LOO -3182.4

People learn the hidden concept by eliminating hypotheses rather than by weighing how typical the examples are: they keep every rule (one of 45 mathematical or digit rules, each with its own prior plausibility) or range of numbers that is consistent with the examples, treating an example that does not fit only as a possible stray, and they answer "yes" with their total belief in the surviving concepts that contain the target. Because there is no size principle (weak sampling), a small concept gains nothing over a broad one that also fits, so people generalize broadly — especially from a single example — and adding consistent examples does not sharpen belief toward the smallest rule.

## range_bounded_parity_concepts  — rank 4, 26.5 ± 5.7 nats behind the best (4.6× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -3184.0

Refinement of `parity_restricted_concepts`: people do the same tempered-evidence Bayesian concept learning over the 45 weighted rules, all intervals of 1-100, and their odd-only and even-only versions. The one change is a single added component, taken from `bounded_range_rule_learning`: people can also restrict a rule to a range of numbers that covers the examples (e.g. "multiples of 3 between 20 and 100"), and these range-bounded rules get an inferred share of the prior for rules (share 0 recovers `parity_restricted_concepts`). A bounded rule is smaller than the full rule, so the size principle favours it. This predicts that people endorse members of the examples' rule that lie outside the examples' min-max range less than members inside it, and that this gap is larger than the rule/interval mixture allows (63,27,99,33 → 3 below 45).

## extrapolation_verification_failure  — rank 5, 104.2 ± 22.3 nats behind the best (4.7× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -3261.7

People infer the hidden concept by the same tempered, serial-position-weighted Bayesian concept learning as the best model (45 weighted rules and all intervals, with sorted lists shifting belief toward ranges), but to answer they must mentally verify that the target follows from the examples, and this extrapolation becomes unreliable beyond the span of numbers they have actually seen: the farther the target lies outside the examples' min–max range, the more likely the check fails and they answer "no", at an inferred rate per unit of distance. This predicts that members of a broad arithmetic rule (e.g. multiples of 3 after 63, 27, 99, 33) are endorsed less outside the examples' range than inside it, more steeply than belief in the concept alone implies, while inside-range judgments are unchanged.

## inattentive_guess_lapse  — no comparison row

People infer the hidden concept by simple hypothesis elimination — they keep every mathematical/digit rule (each with its own prior plausibility) or range of numbers consistent with the examples, with no size principle — but on a fixed, inferred share of trials they do not consult that belief at all and instead answer by an inattentive guess with their own inferred bias toward "yes". This decision-level lapse predicts that even the examples themselves are endorsed well short of certainty (about 70%), and that targets the belief firmly rejects still collect a floor of yeses set by the guessing bias, compressing all judgments toward that bias by the same proportion.
