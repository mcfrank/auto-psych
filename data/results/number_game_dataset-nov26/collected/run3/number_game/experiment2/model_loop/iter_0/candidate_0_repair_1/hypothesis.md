People do the same tempered Bayesian concept learning as the best model (45 weighted rules, all intervals, their odd/even versions and range-bounded rules), with one distortion: they neglect sample size, so the total weight they give the examples grows less than in proportion to how many examples they saw — the first example counts heavily, and each further example adds less. This predicts that a single example already pins down its most salient rule (e.g. 10 → multiples of 10), producing many yeses for that rule's members, while adding more examples sharpens generalization far less than an ideal learner would.
