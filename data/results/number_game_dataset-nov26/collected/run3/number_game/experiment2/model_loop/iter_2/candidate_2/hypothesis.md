People infer the hidden concept from the examples by the same Bayesian concept learning as the current best model: weighted mathematical/digit rules, magnitude ranges, their odd/even versions and range-bounded rules, with sample-size neglect. On some trials, at an inferred rate, they do not use the examples at all. On those trials they answer from prior belief alone, saying "yes" as often as they think a program would produce the target before seeing any examples. This disagrees most sharply with the best model in two places. First, the examples themselves are accepted well short of certainty. Second, numbers outside a tight concept (e.g. non-matching digits after 63, 23, 43) keep a floor of yeses that is higher for a-priori plausible numbers, such as members of broad rules like even numbers or multiples of 10, and lower for idiosyncratic ones.
