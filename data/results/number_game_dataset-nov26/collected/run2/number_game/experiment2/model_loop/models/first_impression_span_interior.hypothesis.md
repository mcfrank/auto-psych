Refinement of `first_impression_anchoring` by one change: when two or more examples are shown, people also take "a number lying between the smallest and the largest example" as a candidate property of the program (the range the examples fill in), with its own inferred salience, overshadowed like the other non-digit properties by a visible shared final digit. Everything else is unchanged (the first impression's contradicted properties keep a fraction of their weight, graded magnitude band, progression concept, seed multiples, the last example's lingering digit), so on multi-example sets targets strictly inside the examples' range are endorsed more than targets just outside it even when they share no digit, parity or seed-multiple with the examples, closing the inrange_nonshared_rate critique.
