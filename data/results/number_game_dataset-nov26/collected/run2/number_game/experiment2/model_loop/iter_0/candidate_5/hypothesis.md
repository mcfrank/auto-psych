Refinement of `seed_multiple_salience_overlap`. I kept its account: people say "yes" in proportion to the salience-weighted share of the examples' shared property profile that the target also has, and they treat "multiples of the smallest example" as a salient property when that example divides all the others. The one change is grafted from `final_digit_overshadowing_overlap`: when several examples visibly share a final digit, that pattern overshadows every other shared property (the seed multiple, parity, other multiples, the magnitude bounds, and so on), and those properties all lose an inferred fraction of their salience. As a result, the seed-multiple account predicts strong digit-based generalization on shared-final-digit sets (28, 98 -> 18, 38 endorsed; 14, 56 rejected) while keeping generalization along multiples of the seed for sets like 7, 63.
