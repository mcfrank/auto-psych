# Existing hypotheses

Each model below is ONE cognitive hypothesis, with how it stands on the current data by ELPD-LOO (best first). `elpd_diff ± dse` is a model's deficit against the best and the standard error of that difference (with the responses to each stimulus pair counted together, as they are correlated): within about 2·dse the two are statistically tied on this data; beyond it the model has lost. "PSIS-LOO unreliable" means the estimate itself is untrustworthy (too many high-Pareto-k trials), not that the model is bad. Propose a hypothesis that is genuinely different from these, or a refinement of a single one of them — never a combination of several.

## progression_band_first_digit_primacy  — rank 0, the best model on this data, ELPD-LOO -3160.0

Refinement of `progression_graded_magnitude_band` (the incumbent) by one grafted component, taken from `graded_band_first_digit_primacy`: when several examples are shown without a shared final digit, the final digit of the FIRST example also lingers as a candidate pattern ("ends in 3"), with its own inferred salience, because the first example anchors people's initial guess before the later examples arrive. Everything else is the incumbent's judgment unchanged (salience-weighted share of the examples' shared profile, with the graded magnitude band, the "start at the smallest and add the gap" progression concept, multiples of the seed, final-digit overshadowing and the last example's lingering digit), so targets sharing the first-shown example's final digit are endorsed more than targets sharing no example's final digit, closing the first_example_digit_lift critique the incumbent fails.

## first_impression_anchoring  — rank 1, 0.0 ± 5.2 nats behind the best (0.0× dse: statistically tied with the best), ELPD-LOO -3160.1

People read the examples in order and form their first guess about the program from the first example alone, entertaining every salient property it has (its parity, the numbers it is a multiple of, its digits, its final digit). Later examples should rule out the first example's properties they lack, but people adjust insufficiently from this first impression: contradicted first-example properties keep a fraction of their weight, so targets that share properties with the first-shown example — not only its final digit — are endorsed more than the examples' common profile alone implies, and the same examples in a different order are generalized differently.

## coincidence_gated_first_digit_primacy  — rank 2, 4.1 ± 9.6 nats behind the best (0.4× dse: statistically tied with the best), ELPD-LOO -3164.1

Refinement of `coincidence_gated_pattern_belief` by one grafted component, taken from `graded_band_first_digit_primacy`: when people believe the program follows a rule, the final digit of the FIRST example shown also lingers as a candidate pattern ("ends in 3"), with its own inferred salience, because the first example anchors their initial guess before the later examples arrive. Everything else is unchanged: people first judge from how striking the examples' agreement is whether the program is patterned at all (a single example or broadly shared properties leave them suspecting it is random, so they lean toward "yes"), and otherwise say "yes" by the salience-weighted share of the examples' shared profile the target has, the last example's lingering digit included. So on multi-example sets without a shared final digit, targets sharing the first-shown example's final digit are endorsed more than targets sharing no example's final digit, which is the first_example_digit_lift discrepancy that the best model misses.

## coincidence_tilted_overlap  — rank 3, 8.1 ± 5.9 nats behind the best (1.4× dse: statistically tied with the best), ELPD-LOO -3168.1

People weigh each pattern the examples share by how much of a coincidence that sharing would be. A pattern that covers few numbers (powers of 3, multiples of 11, squares, a narrow magnitude bound) carries more weight than a broad one (even, contains a 4), and this tilt toward the rarer patterns grows with each example beyond the first that confirms them. A single example therefore carries no coincidence at all. Otherwise "yes" follows the share of that weighted profile the target has, as in the current best account. So the more examples share a narrow pattern, the more firmly people reject targets that lack it, even when those targets keep the broad shared properties.

## progression_graded_magnitude_band  — rank 4, 8.2 ± 5.8 nats behind the best (1.4× dse: statistically tied with the best), ELPD-LOO -3168.2

Refinement of `gap_step_progression_overlap` by one change, grafted from `graded_magnitude_band_overlap`: people do not treat "at least as large as the smallest example" and "no larger than the largest example" as all-or-none tests, but give a target magnitude credit that falls off smoothly (a bell-shaped gradient of inferred width) with its distance below the smallest or above the largest example. Everything else is unchanged — the salience-weighted share of the examples' shared profile, including the "start at the smallest and add the gap" progression concept, multiples of the seed, final-digit overshadowing and the last example's lingering digit — so the change predicts broader, distance-graded generalization from a single example (near targets endorsed more than far ones), which the incumbent misses.

## specificity_weighted_overlap  — rank 5, 9.9 ± 5.9 nats behind the best (1.7× dse: statistically tied with the best), ELPD-LOO -3169.9

People weigh each property the examples share the way a normative Bayesian learner would, by the size principle — a property that picks out few numbers (powers of 2, cubes, multiples of 11) is a more suspicious coincidence and so counts for more than a broad one (odd, contains a 3) — but they apply it only partially, discounting the narrowness of a property by an inferred power below the normative one (a conservative mis-weighting of specificity). "Yes" then follows the specificity-weighted share of the examples' shared property profile the target has (with the current best account's progression, seed-multiple, graded magnitude, overshadowing and lingering-digit properties), so a lone example with narrow properties (64) is generalized more narrowly than a nondescript one (37), making breadth vary from seed to seed.

## coincidence_gated_pattern_belief  — rank 7, 13.4 ± 11.3 nats behind the best (1.2× dse: statistically tied with the best), ELPD-LOO -3173.4

People first ask whether the examples look patterned at all, or could be numbers the program produced at random. They decide this by coincidence detection: the more striking the agreement among the examples (several examples all falling in a small category such as "powers of 2" or "ends in 8"), the more sure they are that the program follows a rule. A single example, or several that share only broad properties, gives no such evidence, so people suspect the program produces almost any number and lean toward "yes" for every target. When they do believe in a rule, they judge the target as the current best account does, by the salience-weighted share of the examples' shared property profile it has.

## single_example_magnitude_neighborhood  — rank 8, 15.5 ± 10.8 nats behind the best (1.4× dse: statistically tied with the best), ELPD-LOO -3175.5

When the program has shown only a single example, that number's magnitude itself becomes one of the candidate properties people consider ("numbers about this size"), and a target earns credit for it that falls off smoothly with its distance from the example, in both directions. Otherwise "yes" follows the salience-weighted share of the examples' shared property profile the target has, as in the current best model (arithmetic-progression concept, seed multiples, final-digit overshadowing, the last example's lingering digit). The disagreement with the best model lives in one-example sets: targets near the lone example, above or below it and even when they break its parity or digit properties, are endorsed more, and far targets above it are no longer favored just for passing the "at least as large" bound.

## gap_step_progression_overlap  — rank 11, 17.9 ± 8.6 nats behind the best (2.1× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -3177.9

People's space of candidate concepts includes an arithmetic-progression concept that the current models lack. When the examples are evenly spaced (e.g. 8 and 16, or 2 and 8), people entertain "start at the smallest example and keep adding the gap between the examples" (8, 16, 24, 32, ...; 2, 8, 14, 20, ...) as one more property of the set, with its own inferred prior salience. Otherwise they judge the target as before, by the salience-weighted share of the examples' shared property profile it has, so targets on the upward continuation of the progression are endorsed more even when they break other shared properties such as being a power of 2.

## graded_magnitude_band_overlap  — rank 12, 20.3 ± 7.9 nats behind the best (2.6× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -3180.3

Refinement of `seed_overshadowing_last_digit_recency` by one change to its magnitude property. People do not treat "at least as large as the smallest example" and "no larger than the largest example" as all-or-none tests. They judge how close in magnitude the target is to the range the examples span, and that magnitude similarity falls off smoothly (a broad, bell-shaped gradient) with the target's distance below the smallest or above the largest example. So a target just outside the examples keeps nearly full magnitude credit, and only far-away targets lose it. Everything else is unchanged: "yes" follows the salience-weighted share of the examples' shared profile the target has, including the seed-multiple property, final-digit overshadowing and the lingering last-example digit. The main consequence is broader generalization from a single example, where every non-example target lies outside the examples' "range".

## inattentive_guessing_lapse  — rank 20, 81.3 ± 14.3 nats behind the best (5.7× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -3241.3

People judge each target the way the current best account says: by the salience-weighted share of the examples' shared property profile the target has. That profile includes multiples of the smallest example when it divides the others, a shared final digit that overshadows other properties, and the last example's lingering final digit. The new claim is about the decision rule. On some fraction of trials, which the model infers, people do not consult that judgment at all and answer by guessing, saying "yes" at their own inferred guessing rate. This puts a floor under endorsement of targets that clearly do not fit and a ceiling under targets that clearly fit, and the same floor and ceiling apply to every set.

## seed_multiple_salience_overlap  — rank 23, 139.0 ± 22.4 nats behind the best (6.2× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -3299.0

People notice a relation between the examples themselves that the property-overlap models ignore: whether the smallest example divides every other example (7 and 63, 9 and 81, 8 and 64). When it does, they read that smallest number as the program's seed and take "multiples of the seed" as a salient property of the set, with its own salience. As in the overlap account, they still say "yes" in proportion to the salience-weighted share of the examples' common properties that the target also has, so a multiple of the seed is endorsed even when it breaks other shared properties such as parity (e.g. 14 and 28 for 7, 63).

## property_exemplar_similarity  — rank 24, 173.7 ± 25.3 nats behind the best (6.9× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -3333.8

People do not infer a concept from the examples; they store each example shown as a separate exemplar, described by its mathematical properties (parity, primality, squares/cubes, powers, multiples, base-ten multiples, final digit, contained digits) and its magnitude, and judge a target by its average similarity to the stored examples. Similarity to each example falls off exponentially with the attention-weighted number of properties on which the target and that example differ plus their numerical distance, and the more similar the target is to the examples on average, the more likely a "yes". So every example counts equally whatever its position, a target sharing a property with only some examples (e.g. the first example's final digit) still gains, and a single example is generalized to numbers close in magnitude.

## leaky_memory_example_elimination  — rank 25, 174.2 ± 20.6 nats behind the best (8.5× dse: distinguishable from the best — it has lost on this data), ELPD-LOO -3334.3

People generalize like a weak-sampling Bayesian learner over salient number properties (the incumbent's salience-weighted overlap, with a visible shared final digit overshadowing other properties), but with one resource limit: working memory for the examples fades, so each earlier example is only remembered — and so only rules out the properties it lacks — with a probability that falls with how many examples were shown after it. Properties of the most recent example that an earlier, partly forgotten example contradicts therefore keep some weight, so targets resembling the last-shown example (e.g. sharing its final digit) are endorsed more than targets resembling the first.
