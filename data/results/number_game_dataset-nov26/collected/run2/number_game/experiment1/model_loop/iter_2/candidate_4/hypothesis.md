Refinement of `family_salience_feature_overlap`: people still note every salient mathematical property the examples all share and say "yes" in proportion to the salience-weighted fraction of that shared profile the target also has, with no size principle. The one change is that multiples of five and ten (the round numbers of base-ten counting) form their own family of property with their own salience, separate from multiples of other numbers (3, 4, 6, 7, 8, 9, 11, 12), so when examples share both (e.g. 90 and 45 are multiples of 5 and of 9) people generalize along the round-number property far more than along the other multiple.
