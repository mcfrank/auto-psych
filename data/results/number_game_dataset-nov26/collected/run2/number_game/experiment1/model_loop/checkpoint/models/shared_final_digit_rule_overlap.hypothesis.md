Refinement of `one_sided_magnitude_bound_overlap`: people still note every salient property all the examples share (each family with its own salience, base-ten multiples their own family, two one-sided magnitude bounds, no size principle) and say "yes" in proportion to the salience-weighted share of that profile the target also has. The one change is that a final digit shared by two or more examples is not just one diluted property in that profile. People read it as a self-evident rule ("they all end in 8"), so a target that ends in that digit gets an extra, independent boost toward "yes". That boost does not shrink when the examples also happen to share other, coincidental properties (e.g. 28 and 98 both being multiples of 7).
