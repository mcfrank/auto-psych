Refinement of `one_sided_magnitude_bound_overlap`: people still note every salient property all the examples share (each property family with its own salience, base-ten multiples their own family, two one-sided magnitude bounds, no size principle) and judge a target by the salience-weighted share of that profile it also has. The one change is that how decisively they act on that share depends on how salient the examples' common pattern is: when the examples share a highly noticeable property (such as a common final digit or being powers of the same number), a target that fits the profile is endorsed much more confidently than one that fully fits a profile made only of weak, unremarkable properties (such as merely being even), so the pull toward "yes" grows with the total salience of the shared profile rather than with the matched share alone.
