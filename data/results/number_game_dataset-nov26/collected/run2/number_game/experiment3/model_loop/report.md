## Hypotheses

- **neighbourhood_doubt_three_example_memory**: Refinement of the incumbent `parity_doubt_three_example_memory` by one change to its rule-ambiguity doubt, taken from `magnitude_bounded_ambiguity_doubt`: when the several examples people hold in mind fit so many rules that they give up on rule-based judgment, the loosely patterned program they fall back on keeps the examples' magnitude neighbourhood as well as their shared parity, so the fallback "yes" fades smoothly (with its own inferred width) with the target's distance below the smallest or above the largest remembered example. Everything else is the incumbent unchanged (the three-example working-memory limit averaged over which example was lost, the salience-weighted share of the shared profile with the interval similarity, and lone-example doubt that keeps only parity), so rule-ambiguous multi-example sets such as 64, 4 or 96, 48 are endorsed broadly for same-parity targets near and between the examples but much less for same-parity targets far beyond them. (The one significant critique, a lower yes rate early in the session, depends on trial position, which a stimulus-only model cannot represent.)
- **parity_doubt_three_example_memory**: Refinement of the incumbent `parity_bounded_ambiguity_doubt` by one grafted component, taken from `working_memory_three_example_limit`: people can hold only three examples in mind, so when four are shown, on an inferred share of trials one example has dropped out of memory and the whole judgment — the salience-weighted share of the shared profile, how many rules the examples fit (and so how often people give up and answer flatly), and the parity kept in that fallback — is made from the three remembered examples, averaged over which one was lost. Because three examples share more coincidental properties and fit more rules than four, four-example sets are judged against a richer profile, so targets fitting the full set's profile are endorsed less, and sometimes more broadly, than a reasoner using all four would; sets of one to three examples are judged exactly as by the incumbent.
- **output_dispersion_crowding_veto**: People expect the program's outputs to be spread out over the number line, the way they expect a random-looking generator not to repeat itself, so a target lying right next to a number the program has already produced (within a few units of an example, such as 43 after 42) seems an unlikely further output and is vetoed on an inferred share of trials, a veto that fades quickly with distance from the nearest example. Apart from this crowding veto, judgment is that of the current best account (the salience-weighted share of the examples' shared profile with the interval similarity, parity-keeping rule-ambiguity doubt, and the three-example memory limit), so targets crowding an example — even inside the examples' range — are endorsed less than equally fitting targets a little further away.
- **common_step_grid_concept**: People's space of candidate concepts includes a "common grid" concept the current models lack: when several examples are shown, they notice when every gap between the examples is a multiple of one step of 3 or more (7, 22, 37 all sit on a grid of step 15 starting at 7; 12, 48, 36 on a grid of step 12), even when the examples are unevenly spaced, and entertain "numbers on that grid, in either direction" as a candidate property. Its prior salience is an inferred multiple of the arithmetic-progression concept's salience, and like other non-digit properties it is overshadowed by a visible shared final digit. Otherwise the judgment is that of the current best account, so targets lying on the examples' common grid, inside or outside their range, are endorsed more than the strictly shared profile implies.
- **magnitude_bounded_ambiguity_doubt**: Refinement of `exception_tolerant_parity_doubt` by one change to its rule-ambiguity doubt: when several examples fit so many rules that people give up on rule-based judgment, their fallback is not "any number of the right parity" but "numbers of the right parity around where the examples fall" — the loosely patterned program they then imagine keeps the examples' magnitude neighbourhood as well as their shared parity, so the fallback "yes" fades smoothly (with its own inferred width) with the target's distance below the smallest or above the largest example. Everything else (exception-tolerant salience-weighted share of the shared profile with interval similarity, and the lone-example doubt that keeps only parity) is unchanged, so rule-ambiguous multi-example sets such as 64, 4 or 96, 48 are endorsed broadly for same-parity targets near and between the examples but much less for same-parity targets far beyond them.
- **exception_tolerant_parity_doubt**: Refinement of the incumbent `parity_bounded_ambiguity_doubt` by one grafted component, taken from `single_exception_tolerance`: people allow that one of the examples may be an odd one out (a slip or noise in the program's output), so when three or four examples are shown, a property that every example but one has (three of 12, 48, 36, 7 are even; two of 48, 63, 53 end in 3) is still entertained as a possible rule, at an inferred fraction of the weight it would carry if all examples shared it. Everything else is the incumbent unchanged — the salience-weighted share of the shared profile with the interval similarity, and rule-ambiguity doubt at every set size whose fallback "yes" keeps the examples' shared parity — so on three- and four-example sets, targets carrying an almost-shared property are endorsed more than the strictly shared profile implies, while one- and two-example sets are judged exactly as before.
- **working_memory_ambiguity_doubt**: Refinement of `working_memory_three_example_limit` by one grafted component, taken from `rule_ambiguity_doubt_all_sizes`: the doubt that makes people give up on rule-based judgment and answer at a flat rate is not limited to lone examples but operates at every set size, more often the more candidate rules all the examples fit together (its own base rate for multi-example sets). Everything else is the working-memory model unchanged: people hold at most three examples in mind, so on an inferred share of four-example trials one example has dropped out and the target is judged against the richer profile the remaining three share. So rule-ambiguous sets (64, 4) are endorsed broadly and flatly at any size, while four-example sets are generalized more narrowly than their full shared profile implies.
- **parity_bounded_ambiguity_doubt**: Refinement of `rule_ambiguity_doubt_all_sizes` by one grafted component, taken from `parity_preserving_random_doubt` and applied at every set size. When the examples fit so many candidate rules that people give up on rule-based judgment (whether one example or several are shown), they do not answer completely flatly: they keep the parity that all the examples share as the one default constraint on a loosely patterned program. Their fallback "yes" goes to targets of that parity at a flat rate and much less often to opposite-parity targets. So rule-ambiguous even sets such as 64, 4 or 96, 48 are endorsed broadly for even targets but not for odd ones, while rule-based judgment (the salience-weighted share of the shared profile with the interval similarity) is unchanged.
