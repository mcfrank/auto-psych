When several examples are shown, people try to reconstruct how the program generated them step by step, and one operation they readily try is repeated multiplication: if every example lies on one chain of powers of a common base (4 and 64; 9 and 81; 2, 8, 16), they read the program as "keep multiplying by the base" and take the other links of that chain (16 for 4, 64; 27 and 3 for 9, 81) as numbers it produces, a concept with its own salience that a shared final digit does not overshadow, because it describes how the numbers were made rather than how they look. Otherwise the judgment is that of the current best account (the salience-weighted share of the examples' shared profile, the in-span interval similarity, and the richness-dependent doubt on lone examples), so the missing links of a power chain are endorsed far more than the share of the examples' surface profile alone predicts.
