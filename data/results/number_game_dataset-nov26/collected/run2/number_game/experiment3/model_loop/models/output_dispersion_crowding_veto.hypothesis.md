People expect the program's outputs to be spread out over the number line, the way they expect a random-looking generator not to repeat itself, so a target lying right next to a number the program has already produced (within a few units of an example, such as 43 after 42) seems an unlikely further output and is vetoed on an inferred share of trials, a veto that fades quickly with distance from the nearest example. Apart from this crowding veto, judgment is that of the current best account (the salience-weighted share of the examples' shared profile with the interval similarity, parity-keeping rule-ambiguity doubt, and the three-example memory limit), so targets crowding an example — even inside the examples' range — are endorsed less than equally fitting targets a little further away.
