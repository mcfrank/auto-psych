Refinement of the incumbent `span_interval_similarity` by one change. When the program has shown only a single example, people often cannot rule out that it produces numbers more or less at random. On an inferred share of one-example trials they set the example's properties aside and answer "yes" at their own inferred rate, which is the same for every target. On the remaining trials, and on every multi-example trial, they judge exactly as the incumbent does (the salience-weighted share of the examples' shared profile, with the interval similarity). So one-example sets are endorsed more often, and the endorsement depends less on the target's distance and properties than the overlap alone predicts.
