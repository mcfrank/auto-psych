Refinement of the incumbent `parity_doubt_three_example_memory` by one change to its rule-ambiguity doubt, taken from `magnitude_bounded_ambiguity_doubt`: when the several examples people hold in mind fit so many rules that they give up on rule-based judgment, the loosely patterned program they fall back on keeps the examples' magnitude neighbourhood as well as their shared parity, so the fallback "yes" fades smoothly (with its own inferred width) with the target's distance below the smallest or above the largest remembered example. Everything else is the incumbent unchanged (the three-example working-memory limit averaged over which example was lost, the salience-weighted share of the shared profile with the interval similarity, and lone-example doubt that keeps only parity), so rule-ambiguous multi-example sets such as 64, 4 or 96, 48 are endorsed broadly for same-parity targets near and between the examples but much less for same-parity targets far beyond them. (The one significant critique, a lower yes rate early in the session, depends on trial position, which a stimulus-only model cannot represent.)
