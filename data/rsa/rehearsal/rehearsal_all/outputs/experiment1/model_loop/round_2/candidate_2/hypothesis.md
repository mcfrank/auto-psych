Human listeners have limited visual attention to the display during reference resolution. Rather than assuming words are interpreted uniformly across all candidate objects, the listener allocates capacity-limited visual attention across the display based on visual salience (contextual uniqueness, feature complexity, and color contrast), which directly gates literal semantic grounding. Communicative speakers anticipate that unattended distractors will fail to capture the listener's attention, and pragmatic listeners invert this attention-grounded speaker to resolve referring expressions.
