People expect communicative referents to be visually salient according to their informational surprisal, assigning higher prior probability to objects possessing rare features or unexpected feature absences. Observers evaluate the total Shannon information content of each candidate referent relative to the display, anticipating that speakers are more likely to select items with rare, distinctive visual characteristics. Pragmatic listeners integrate this feature-surprisal prior with recursive communicative reasoning to interpret referring expressions and make spontaneous referent choices on uninformative trials.
