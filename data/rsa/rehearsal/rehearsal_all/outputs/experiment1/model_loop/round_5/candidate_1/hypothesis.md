Speakers and listeners adhere to Horn's division of pragmatic labor, matching the contextual markedness of a referring expression to the visual complexity of the intended referent: broad, unmarked words that apply widely across the scene are directed to simple, feature-sparse objects, while narrow, marked words are reserved for complex objects. On displays where a broad word is shared between a simple one-feature item and a complex three-feature item, this mechanism predicts choosing the simple item to match unmarked expression with unmarked referent, sharply disagreeing with the current best model whose complexity prior favors the feature-rich item.
