Listeners in reference games do not passively probability-match their beliefs when clicking an object. Instead, people treat referent selection as a discrete utility-maximizing decision over their pragmatic and prior beliefs, choosing referents via a softmax decision rule governed by an internal decision rationality. When decision rationality exceeds one, comprehenders act decisively by amplifying the most probable referent over less likely alternatives; when it is below one, choices hedge toward indifference.
