People evaluate candidate referents through an evaluative attribute lens, where semantic features and chromatic color represent positive visual endowments. Communicators adjust their preference for attribute-rich referents based on their evaluative goal, favoring attribute-rich items under positive framing ('favorite'), inverting to favor attribute-poor items under negative framing ('least favorite'), and maintaining a baseline positivity bias on neutral trials. Pragmatic listeners integrate this evaluative prior with recursive communicative reasoning to interpret referring expressions and spontaneous choices.
