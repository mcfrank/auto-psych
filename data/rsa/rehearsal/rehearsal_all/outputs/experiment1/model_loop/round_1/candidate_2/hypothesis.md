Speakers in reference games avoid using referring expressions that deprive competitor referents of their sole identifying labels, choosing candidate words based on how exclusively competitors rely on them. Pragmatic listeners invert this reliance-sensitive speaker, expecting words shared with feature-sparse objects to refer to those sparse competitors rather than feature-rich referents that possess alternative descriptive options. On displays where an ambiguous word applies to a competitor with no other features versus one with multiple descriptive alternatives, this mechanism predicts choosing the multi-feature competitor's alternative word, sharply disagreeing with feature-salience models that prioritize visually complex referents.
