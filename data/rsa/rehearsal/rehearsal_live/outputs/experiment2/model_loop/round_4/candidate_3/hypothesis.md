Listeners interpret referring expressions by modeling a communicative speaker who produces words with contrastive focus, selecting descriptive features specifically to resolve minimal contrast pairs in the visual scene where a competitor shares the referent's other features but lacks the named one. When candidate referents satisfy the uttered expression, listeners favor the object whose extraneous features contrast minimally with a competitor lacking the word, inferring that the speaker produced the feature to resolve that contextual contrast rather than describe an uncontrasted object. On trials where no informative expression is heard, listener expectations reflect baseline communicative contrast potential, expecting speakers to target referents that possess distinctive visual contrast over redundant or featureless items.
