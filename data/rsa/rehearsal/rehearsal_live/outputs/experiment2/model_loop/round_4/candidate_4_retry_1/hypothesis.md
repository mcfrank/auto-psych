Attenuated solitary perceptual oddity listener with twin contrast, visual color contrast, attenuated evaluative valence framing, and feature economy. Listeners interpret referring expressions by integrating visual color contrast, attenuated evaluative valence framing, and twin-contrast solitary oddity pop-out with a feature economy heuristic that penalizes extraneous unmentioned properties during semantic comprehension. When an informative referring expression is produced, listeners favor candidate referents with the fewest total visual features while continuing to weight candidate referents by their chromatic coloration and attenuated evaluative attitude; on uninformative prior displays without an informative expression, choices track baseline solitary oddity contrasting with twins, chromatic coloration, and unconstrained evaluative attitude.

### Refinement details relative to twin_color_attenuated_valence_listener
- **Single stated change**: Extended twin_color_attenuated_valence_listener by incorporating the feature economy heuristic from fewest_features_listener into semantic utterance comprehension.
- **Why it should fit better**: In visual reference games where multiple candidate referents satisfy the literal semantics of an uttered word (such as classic scalar implicature displays), listeners systematically prefer the minimal target referent over an over-specified competitor possessing extraneous unmentioned features. The source model (twin_color_attenuated_valence_listener) lacked an extraneous feature penalty during utterance interpretation, predicting an uninformative equal probability split among semantically matching referents of identical coloration; incorporating the feature economy heuristic penalizes candidate referents in proportion to their total feature count during utterance comprehension, aligning predictions with human pragmatic preferences for minimal, concise referents while preserving twin-contrast solitary oddity pop-out, visual color contrast weighting, and attenuated evaluative valence modulation.
