Listeners interpret referring expressions by engaging in depth-2 recursive Theory of Mind, modeling a sophisticated communicative speaker who anticipates a pragmatic listener capable of first-order scalar implicature. On a display containing an identical twin pair and a solitary singleton with an additional distinguishing feature, hearing the shared feature leads listeners to decisively favor the twin pair over the singleton—sharply reversing the predictions of the current best model, which mistakenly favors the singleton due to solitary oddity pop-out—because a recursive speaker intending the singleton would have chosen its unique label. On uninformative prior trials without an informative word, listener expectations reflect that speakers prefer to communicate about descriptive, feature-rich exemplars.
