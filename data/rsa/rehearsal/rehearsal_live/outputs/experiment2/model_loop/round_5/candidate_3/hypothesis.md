Listeners interpret referring expressions by modeling a communicative speaker whose prior choice of an intended referent is driven by contextual typicality, grounded in prototype theory. Candidate referents are evaluated by their similarity to the contextual prototype formed by the central tendency of visual features across display competitors, with typical exemplars serving as accessible communicative defaults. Pragmatic listeners invert this typicality-guided speaker via Bayes' rule, combining the contextual prototype prior with communicative informativeness.
