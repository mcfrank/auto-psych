Listeners interpret referring expressions by modeling a communicative speaker who chooses intended referents according to their visual feature surprisal, expecting speakers to target objects possessing rare features that deviate from the context ensemble. On uninformative trials where no informative word is uttered, listener expectations directly favor objects possessing high feature surprisal over common or featureless alternatives. When an informative referring expression is produced, pragmatic listeners invert this speaker using Bayes' rule, integrating the feature surprisal prior with linguistic applicability.
