Listeners interpret referring expressions by modeling a speaker who chooses utterances to maximize expected communicative success under a bounded linear payoff, rather than logarithmic information surprisal. On a display containing an identical twin pair and a solitary singleton with an additional distinguishing feature, hearing a shared feature leads listeners to favor the twin pair over the singleton, because an intended singleton would have been named with its unique feature; this sharply reverses the predictions of the twin-contrast solitary oddity listener, which mistakenly favors the singleton as an odd-man-out. When no informative expression is heard, listener expectations reflect that speakers prefer to target referents whose features permit unambiguous communicative success over incommunicable duplicates.
