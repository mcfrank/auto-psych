Listeners interpret referring expressions by modeling a communicative speaker who reasons about a perceptually grounded literal listener, rather than an abstract semantics-only agent. Bottom-up visual distinctiveness biases literal attentional selection toward prominent referents; communicative speakers anticipate this perceptual bias when selecting referring expressions; and pragmatic listeners invert this visually grounded speaker. On uninformative prior trials without an informative word, listener choices track continuous visual distinctiveness directly.
