Listeners expect speakers to communicate about referents possessing greater contextual information content, assuming a prior over objects proportional to total feature surprisal (Shannon self-information) where rarer display features carry higher communicative relevance. In pragmatic comprehension, listeners invert an informative speaker using this feature surprisal prior, naturally resolving scalar implicatures while dampening over-penalization of complex referents. On uninformative trials without an informative word, listener choices follow contextual feature surprisal directly.
