Listeners interpret referring expressions by integrating a solitary perceptual oddity heuristic with contextual familiarization base rates. When evaluating candidate referents, listeners favor solitary singletons contrasting against identical background duplicates, while additionally favoring referents that were encountered more frequently during prior familiarization. On uninformative prior trials without an informative word, choices follow both solitary oddity pop-out and familiarization frequency; when an informative referring expression is heard, listeners choose among semantically matching referents weighted by both solitary oddity and familiarization frequency.

### Refinement details relative to solitary_oddity_heuristic_listener
- **Single stated change**: Extended solitary_oddity_heuristic_listener by incorporating contextual familiarization base-rate weighting. The source model treated all referents as having equal a priori base rates even when prior experience established strong frequency asymmetries; incorporating familiarization base rates allows listeners to weight candidate referents by their observed exposure frequencies in both prior expectations and utterance comprehension, while preserving solitary oddity pop-out on scenes with duplicate items.
- **Recursion depth**: Unchanged at depth 0 (`choice_probs` evaluates `L_heur` directly without recursive speaker simulation).
- **Parameters added or removed**: Added parameter `w_base` (prior Normal(0.0, 2.0)); retained parameter `beta` (prior Normal(0.0, 2.0)) and parameter `lapse` (prior Beta(1.0, 9.0)).
