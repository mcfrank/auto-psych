Listeners resolve referring expressions through pragmatic inference, but their decision rule incorporates a salience-structured lapse rather than uniform random clicking. When top-down attention lapses during utterance comprehension, clicks are captured by bottom-up visual pop-out (favoring solitary visual singletons over background duplicates), naturally producing attenuated oddity effects without separate communicative weights. On uninformative prior trials where top-down linguistic guidance is absent, choices are guided directly by bottom-up visual salience.
