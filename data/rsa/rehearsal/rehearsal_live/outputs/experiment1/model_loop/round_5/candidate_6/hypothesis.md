Attenuated solitary perceptual oddity listener with twin contrast, evaluative valence framing, and familiarization base rates. Listeners interpret referring expressions by integrating a solitary perceptual oddity heuristic with contextual familiarization base rates and evaluative valence framing, where bottom-up visual pop-out operates exclusively when a solitary singleton contrasts against an identical twin pair rather than against majority triplets or larger background groups. When evaluating candidate referents, listeners favor solitary singletons contrasting with twins while additionally weighting candidates by their observed familiarization base rates from prior exposure and modulating their expectations of referent feature complexity according to the speaker's evaluative framing attitude (favoring feature-rich exemplars for positive framing and sparse exemplars for negative framing).

### Refinement details relative to twin_contrast_solitary_valence_listener
- **Single stated change**: Extended twin_contrast_solitary_valence_listener by incorporating contextual familiarization base-rate weighting from r4_c5 (and base_rate_solitary_oddity_listener). The source model treated all referents as having equal a priori baseline rates whenever no twin contrast was present, ignoring empirical exposure frequencies established in familiarization phases; incorporating familiarization base rates allows listeners to weight candidate referents by their observed exposure frequency in both prior expectations and utterance comprehension, while preserving twin-contrast solitary oddity pop-out on duplicate displays and evaluative valence modulation across framed contexts.
- **Why it should fit better**: In visual reference games with pre-exposure familiarization trials, participants develop strong prior expectations favoring frequently encountered objects over rare ones. The source model lacked any familiarization mechanism, predicting uniform baseline distributions across distinct objects even under asymmetric exposure base rates; weighting referents by observed familiarization base rates captures these empirical exposure biases across both prior and utterance trials while retaining solitary oddity pop-out when a singleton contrasts with a twin pair and evaluative framing sensitivity when attitudes are expressed.
- **Recursion depth**: Unchanged at depth 0 (`choice_probs` evaluates `L_heur` directly without recursive speaker simulation).
- **Parameters added or removed**: Added parameter `w_base` (prior `Normal(0.0, 2.0)`); retained parameter `beta_prior` (prior `Normal(0.0, 2.0)`), parameter `beta_utt` (prior `Normal(0.0, 2.0)`), parameter `w_valence` (prior `Normal(0.0, 2.0)`), and parameter `lapse` (prior `Beta(1.0, 9.0)`).

In `L_heur`, added parameter `w_base`, parameter `base_rate: ...`, and updated the referent choice weighting expression to `at(lex, u, r) * exp(beta_utt * vec(is_singleton, r) + w_base * vec(base_rate, r) + vec(valence_bias, r))`.
