Listeners interpret referring expressions by evaluating the Bayesian diagnosticity of the uttered word for each candidate referent against alternative objects in the visual context. An uttered feature provides strong evidence for a referent when a speaker would be likely to produce it for that object but unlikely to produce it for other objects in the display, naturally resolving ambiguity in favor of referents with fewer extraneous competitors. On uninformative trials where no informative word is heard, listeners distribute their expectations according to the expected communicative diagnosticity of each object's features.
