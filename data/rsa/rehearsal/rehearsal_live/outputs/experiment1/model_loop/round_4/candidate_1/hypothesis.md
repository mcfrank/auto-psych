Listeners interpret referring expressions by reasoning about a speaker whose communicative goal addresses an informative aspect of the referent rather than its exhaustive identity. When hearing an expression, listeners jointly infer what descriptive aspect the speaker sought to convey and which candidate referent motivated that goal, expecting speakers to choose distinctive, diagnostic aspects of an object over non-diagnostic shared properties. On uninformative trials where no informative word is uttered, listeners expect speakers to target referents possessing more distinctive communicative aspects.
