Listeners in reference games differ in their depth of communicative reasoning: the population comprises a mixture of literal listeners, who select referents according to literal semantic truth conditions, and pragmatic listeners, who invert a recursive mental model of an informative speaker. On trials with an informative referring expression, aggregate choices reflect this population mixture of literal and pragmatic listener strategies, defaulting to uniform baseline expectations when no informative expression is heard. A lapse rate captures unguided random clicking.
