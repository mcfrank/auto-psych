We refine the depth-1 salience listener by incorporating the speaker's evaluative attitude into the referent prior, so that listeners expect speakers describing a favorite object to favor feature-rich referents and speakers describing a least-favorite object to favor simpler referents. By conditioning referent expectations on both communicative valence and perceptual base rates (feature count and familiarization), the listener captures how affective framing shifts referent choice beyond visual salience alone.
