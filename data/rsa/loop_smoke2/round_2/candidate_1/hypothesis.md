Listeners expect speakers to refer to the most distinctive object in a scene, weighting referents by their contextual feature contrast (average dissimilarity to all other objects in the display). On twin displays where all objects share the same number of features but one is a unique singleton and two are identical twins, this contrast salience mechanism strongly favors the singleton, whereas the current best model predicts equal choice probabilities because feature counts are identical. This shared contrast prior guides prior object expectations and pragmatic disambiguation of informative utterances.
