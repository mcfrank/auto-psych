Listeners evaluate referents through a contextual distinctiveness prior: rather than viewing features in isolation, an object's visual salience is determined by how rare its features are across the visual scene. Features shared by multiple objects form a redundant visual background, whereas rare or unshared features produce perceptual pop-out that captures attention. Pragmatic listeners use this scene-relative distinctiveness prior to guide referent selection both when interpreting an utterance and on uninformative prior trials.
