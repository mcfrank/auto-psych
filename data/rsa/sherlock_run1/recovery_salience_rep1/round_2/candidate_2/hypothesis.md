Resource-limited listeners focus visual attention primarily on candidate referents matching the heard utterance, attenuating the attentional weight of non-candidate objects in the visual display. When simulating what a speaker would have said, the listener's mental model evaluates the communicative informativeness of alternative utterances against this attention-weighted display representation, discounting irrelevant distractors. Consequently, non-candidate objects exert diminished influence on the perceived ambiguity of alternative expressions, dampening the asymmetric preference between candidate referents on complex displays.
