Listeners interpret referential descriptions using a mutual exclusivity constraint rather than recursive theory-of-mind mentalizing: when hearing a shared feature, they penalize candidate referents in proportion to the number of unique, unambiguous distinguishing labels available for that object in the visual scene. On symmetric complex displays where candidate referents share the heard word and their alternative features are also shared with other objects rather than unique, mutual exclusivity predicts an equal choice split because neither candidate possesses an exclusive alternative name. This sharply contradicts the depth-2 rational speech act model, which predicts an asymmetric preference driven by recursive speaker vocabulary trade-offs.
