Listeners interpret utterances via a grammatical exhaustivity heuristic rather than recursive speaker mentalizing: hearing a feature asserts that feature and penalizes candidate objects for each additional unmentioned feature they possess. On simple implicature displays, this prefers the minimal target over a multi-feature foil, matching classic pragmatic choices. On complex displays where candidates share the heard word and have equal numbers of unmentioned features (such as Mayn & Demberg's target complex display), exhaustivity predicts an even split between candidates, disagreeing sharply with rsa_l2 which predicts a large asymmetry driven by depth-2 speaker alternative vocabularies.
