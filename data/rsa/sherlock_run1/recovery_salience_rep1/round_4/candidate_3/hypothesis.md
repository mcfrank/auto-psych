Listeners interpret referential expressions by modeling a contrastive speaker whose communicative choices are driven by the principle of contrast. When describing a target referent, speakers prioritize utterances that differentiate the target from competitors sharing its other features, gaining greater communicative value when eliminating similar contrast foils than irrelevant distractors. Pragmatic listeners invert this contrastive speaker, favoring candidate referents for which the heard utterance serves a diagnostic contrastive function.
