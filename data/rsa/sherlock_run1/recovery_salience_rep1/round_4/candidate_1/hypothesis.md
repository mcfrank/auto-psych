Listeners interpret referential descriptions by reasoning about the speaker's communicative goal or question under discussion: rather than assuming the speaker aims to single out the object's complete identity, listeners model a speaker who chooses an utterance to informatively convey a specific feature or aspect of the intended referent. Because simpler objects with fewer extraneous features offer fewer competing communicative aspects to describe, hearing a shared feature provides stronger evidence that the speaker intended the focused, simpler referent, while tempering exaggerated pragmatic inferences on complex displays where candidate referents share multiple features.
