Listeners evaluate referents through a typicality prior grounded in Eleanor Rosch's prototype theory: before hearing an informative description (and on uninformative prior trials), listeners expect the speaker to refer to the prototypical object in the visual scene, quantified by family resemblance (how extensively an object's features are shared across the visual display). Prototypical exemplars with greater feature overlap serve as cognitive anchors, biasing prior expectations toward central category members.
