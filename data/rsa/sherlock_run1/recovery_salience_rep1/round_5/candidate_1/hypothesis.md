Listeners interpret referential expressions through an ensemble grouping prior grounded in Gestalt similarity: objects that share visual features with other items in the display are perceptually grouped into a collective background ensemble and discounted, whereas isolated objects that share fewer features stand out as prominent individuals. On uninformative displays where multiple objects possess extraneous features but only one shares its features with competitors, this mechanism favors the visually isolated object over the grouped competitor, directly reversing the predictions of prior_lapse_focal_listener which penalizes total feature count in isolation. Pragmatic listeners invert a communicative speaker while maintaining this perceptual grouping prior over referents.
