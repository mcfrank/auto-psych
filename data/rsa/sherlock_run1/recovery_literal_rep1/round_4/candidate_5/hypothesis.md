We refine softmax_belief_salience_listener by hypothesizing that a speaker's communicative valence modulates referent salience: listeners expect speakers describing a favorite referent to prefer feature-rich objects, but this preference diminishes or reverses when describing a least-favorite referent, incorporating the valence-salience mechanism from valence_salience_listener. Because communicative valence shifts prior expectations shared in descriptive speaker mentalization as well as bottom-up visual attention during attentional lapses, this better accounts for affective framing across reference displays. Relative to softmax_belief_salience_listener, recursion depth remains unchanged at L1, one parameter w_valence is added with prior Normal(0.0, 1.0), no parameters are removed, and the feature count term in the salience prior is scaled by adding w_valence multiplied by display valence.
