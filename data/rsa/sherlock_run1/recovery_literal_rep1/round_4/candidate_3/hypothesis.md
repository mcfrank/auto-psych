Listeners interpret referring expressions through bi-directional associative resonance between the heard word and candidate objects in the scene. Upon hearing an utterance, referent selection is driven by the joint resonance of forward cue validity—how specifically the word singles out an object across the display—and backward category validity—how strongly the object evokes that word among its own features. Candidate objects with high bi-directional resonance are selected, while attentional lapses default to bottom-up perceptual salience.
