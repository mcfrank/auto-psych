Speakers in reference games are satisficing communicators who balance informativeness against cognitive effort, treating partially informative words as acceptable alternatives rather than strictly optimizing communicative utility. When evaluating candidate utterances, a satisficing speaker exhibits diminishing marginal utility for informativeness, producing ambiguous shared labels with substantial probability even when unique labels exist. The pragmatic listener inverts this satisficing speaker, attributing the mention of a shared label to communicative sufficiency rather than evidence against an unmentioned feature.
