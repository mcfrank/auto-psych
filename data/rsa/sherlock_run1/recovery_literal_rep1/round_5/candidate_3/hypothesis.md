Listeners expect speakers to choose referents that can be successfully communicated under the constraints of the reference game. Before hearing a referring expression, a listener evaluates each candidate object's communicative referability—the maximum discriminability among the available labels that literally describe it in the visual context—rather than raw visual complexity. The listener inverts a descriptive speaker using this referability prior over intended referents, with attentional lapses defaulting to this baseline communicative expectation.
