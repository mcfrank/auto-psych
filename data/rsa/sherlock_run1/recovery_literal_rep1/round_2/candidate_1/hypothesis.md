Listeners interpret utterances by expecting speakers to refer to contextually distinctive objects, reflecting an isolation effect where features that are rare or unique in the display capture attention over widely shared features. Before hearing an informative utterance, an object's prior salience is determined by the contextual distinctiveness of its features (the sum of feature specificities across the visual scene) rather than raw feature count. The listener inverts a descriptive speaker who samples among the true features of the intended referent, using this distinctiveness prior over objects.
