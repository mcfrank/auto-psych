Listeners interpret referring expressions with limited visual attention to the display, focusing cognitive resources on candidate objects that match the heard utterance while discounting non-matching distractors. When evaluating a speaker's communicative choices, the listener simulates listener comprehension over this attentional representation of the scene, dampening inferences that rely on distant non-matching foils. This attentional bottleneck prevents spurious scalar implicatures in complex asymmetric displays while preserving classic pragmatic reasoning between attended candidates.
