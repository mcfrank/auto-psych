Listeners select referents using a softmax decision rule over candidate objects rather than probability matching, with attentional lapses defaulting to perceptual salience rather than uniform guessing. During deliberate choice, the listener evaluates communicative evidence by inverting a descriptive speaker and exponentiates candidate posterior probabilities, concentrating selections on the most probable referent. When cognitive attention lapses, choices fall back on the bottom-up visual salience of the objects shaped by feature complexity and familiarity.
