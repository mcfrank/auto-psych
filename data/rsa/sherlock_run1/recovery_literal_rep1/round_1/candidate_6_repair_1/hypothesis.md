We refine naive_speaker_listener by hypothesizing that when speakers produce a descriptive label for an intended referent, they incur an utterance production cost that penalizes more specific or discriminating features, making them less likely to produce rare labels. The pragmatic listener inverts this cost-sensitive naive production speaker rather than assuming equal probability across true words. Relative to naive_speaker_listener, recursion depth remains at L1, one parameter c_feature is added with prior Normal(0.0, 1.0), no parameters are removed, and the speaker's true-utterance weights are discounted by an utterance cost inversely proportional to feature extension.
