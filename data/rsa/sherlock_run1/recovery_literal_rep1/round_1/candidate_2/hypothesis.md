Listeners interpret utterances by directly inverting a naive production speaker who samples among the true features of the intended object, rather than mentalizing a recursive speaker who simulates listener comprehension. On asymmetric displays where a referent shares a feature with a distractor but possesses an unmentioned unique feature, this naive production model predicts equal choice between ambiguous referents because the speaker does not reason about alternative words. This sharply contrasts with standard pragmatic models, which infer that the speaker actively avoided the unique word and therefore penalize the uniquely identifiable referent.
