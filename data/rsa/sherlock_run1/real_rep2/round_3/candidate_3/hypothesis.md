People choose referents by evaluating pragmatic posterior beliefs through a softmax decision rule rather than linear probability matching or posterior sampling. In reference games, a listener treats the subjective probability that each candidate referent was intended as its expected communicative value, selecting an object with probability governed by a decision rationality parameter that softly maximizes over these beliefs. This mechanism sharpens choices toward the most probable referent while naturally producing a baseline trembling-hand choice rate for unlikely or false alternatives.
