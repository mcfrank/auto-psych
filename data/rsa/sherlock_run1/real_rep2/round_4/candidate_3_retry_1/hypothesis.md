People interpret referring expressions through statistical preemption: when an unspecific word applies to multiple candidate referents, listeners penalize a referent in proportion to whether the speaker bypassed a more specific, unambiguous alternative word that applies to it. Instead of performing recursive social simulation of a speaker, listeners evaluate direct cue competition among words, discounting candidate referents whose distinctive features were left unmentioned. On prior displays without an informative description, people choose objects according to their perceptual distinctiveness and familiarization.
