People interpret referential descriptions using graded truth values rather than binary logic, treating words as more faithful descriptions of objects with fewer extraneous features. A listener hearing a single-feature word perceives it as a prototypical match for an object defined solely by that feature, but as a diluted, less complete match for an object with competing features. Pragmatic listeners invert a speaker who communicates with these graded semantic representations, sharpening preference for minimal, prototypical exemplars.
