Listeners interpret referential descriptions by maintaining a multimodal salience prior that unifies perceptual pop-out and evaluative framing. Before hearing an informative utterance, listeners assign higher prior prominence to objects that contrast perceptually with the display (an object in chromatic color amidst grayscale competitors) and objects that align with the speaker's evaluative framing (feature-rich objects under positive framing and feature-sparse objects under negative framing), defaulting to baseline feature complexity in neutral contexts. Pragmatic listeners invert a communicative speaker who shares this common-ground prominence distribution across recursive reasoning depths.
