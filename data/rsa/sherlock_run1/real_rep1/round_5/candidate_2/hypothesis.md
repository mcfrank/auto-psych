People interpret referential descriptions by inverting a satisficing speaker who prunes alternative words whose informativeness falls short of the best available descriptor for the intended referent. Rather than exhaustively weighing every true description, speakers penalize inferior descriptors in proportion to their relative deficit compared to the optimal label available for that object. Pragmatic listeners invert this bounded speaker, recognizing that a speaker will only consider an ambiguous, shared descriptor if the intended referent lacked a clearly superior alternative.
