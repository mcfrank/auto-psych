Listeners interpret referential descriptions by modeling a speaker whose communicative goal is to convey an aspect or feature of the target object rather than its full identity. Hearing a descriptive term, listeners jointly infer the speaker's intended referent and communicative goal, recognizing that an ambiguous descriptor is more likely when an object offers fewer alternative features to talk about. Marginalizing over this communicative goal guides listener choices toward objects for which the heard feature constitutes the primary or solitary descriptive aspect.
