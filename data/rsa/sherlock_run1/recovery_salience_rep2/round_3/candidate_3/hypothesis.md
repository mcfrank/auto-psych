Listeners do not choose by matching probabilities to their posterior beliefs; instead, they apply a soft-maximizing decision rule over their posterior probabilities, amplifying differences between candidate referents when selecting an object to click. When resolving an ambiguous utterance, recursive pragmatic inference informs the listener's subjective beliefs, but an internal decision determinism sharpens these beliefs into action, concentrating choices on the most probable referent more decisively than normative probability matching predicts.
