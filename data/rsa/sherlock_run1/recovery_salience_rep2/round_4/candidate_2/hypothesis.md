Listeners in the population differ in their depth of recursive social reasoning, forming a mixture of distinct cognitive types. While a small fraction of individuals act as literal responders who simply choose among objects satisfying the semantic truth conditions of the utterance, pragmatic reasoners are divided between first-order listeners who simulate a speaker communicating to a literal agent and second-order listeners who anticipate pragmatic inferences. Aggregate choices reflect a population mixture over these discrete reasoning depths, with uninformative speech producing uniform guessing across all types.
