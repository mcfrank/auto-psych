Listeners interpret referring expressions by reasoning about the speaker's communicative goal under a question under discussion, assuming the speaker chooses to be informative about a distinctive visual aspect of the target rather than identifying the unique object. When an object possesses multiple features, the speaker selects which aspect to communicate according to how distinctive that feature is in the context, diluting the chance of mentioning shared features for complex objects. By inverting this goal-directed speaker, the listener infers that an utterance naming a shared feature was intended for the simpler object where no more distinctive aspect was available.
