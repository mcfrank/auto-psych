Listeners interpret referring expressions under a perceptual salience prior governed by visual feature rarity across the context, expecting speakers to refer to objects possessing infrequent or unique features that pop out against the background. On displays where objects have identical feature counts but unequal feature frequencies (such as an asymmetric display where one object possesses a rare singleton feature while the other objects share a common feature), this mechanism predicts a strong preference for the singleton object, whereas the current best model predicts an equiprobable split.
