Listeners interpret referring expressions by Bayesian integration of the speaker's communicative likelihood with an empirical prior over referents formed during familiarization, rather than assuming all objects are equally likely a priori. On displays with skewed familiarization base rates (such as a 3-object display where one object appeared 7/9 of the time and the others 1/9, tested in Experiment 5), this mechanism disagrees most sharply with the uniform-prior incumbent (rsa_l2) by strongly favoring the frequent referent—predicting ~78% choice on prior queries where rsa_l2 predicts an even 33% split, and heavily biasing interpretations of ambiguous words. When no informative utterance is heard, listeners choose referents in proportion to this familiarization prior, defaulting to uniform choice only when no familiarization phase occurred.
