When choosing referents in a reference game, listeners turn their probabilistic beliefs into choices through an inverted S-shaped probability weighting function that overweights small probabilities and underweights large probabilities. Rather than matching undistorted Bayesian posterior beliefs, listeners' referent selections reflect subjective decision weights that flatten the contrast between target and competitor referents. This subjective probability distortion directly captures systematic conservatism in human referent choice while keeping communicative inference simple.
