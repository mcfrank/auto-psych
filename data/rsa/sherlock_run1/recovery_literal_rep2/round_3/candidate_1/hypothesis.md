Listeners evaluate candidate referents according to their value-driven visual prominence, where an object's intrinsic prominence reflects its perceptual richness (distinctive features, vivid color, and familiarity). Speaker framing sets the evaluative polarity of this prominence: positive framing ('favorite') amplifies the preference for prominent referents, negative framing ('least favorite') inverts the evaluative polarity to favor plain or degraded referents, and neutral framing applies a default positive prominence baseline.
