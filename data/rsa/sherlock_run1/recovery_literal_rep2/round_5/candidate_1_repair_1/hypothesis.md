Listeners evaluate candidate referents through referential coverage: they prefer matching objects whose overall communicative identity is most completely captured by the uttered word, penalizing referents whose other unmentioned features are distinctive rather than ubiquitous in the scene. On a display where one matching object has two features with an unmentioned unique feature and a competitor has three features with two unmentioned ubiquitous background features, the current best model prefers the two-feature object, whereas the referential coverage mechanism predicts listeners choose the three-feature object because its identity is more comprehensively covered by the uttered word.
