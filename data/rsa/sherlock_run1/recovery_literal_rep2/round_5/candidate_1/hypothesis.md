Listeners interpret referring expressions through a principle of contextual prototypicality rather than feature economy: upon hearing an expression, they prefer candidate referents that maximize family resemblance with other objects in the visual scene, treating feature-rich, interconnected exemplars as central category prototypes rather than sparse outliers. On a display where one matching object has two features and shares an extraneous feature with the context while a competitor has only the uttered feature, the current best model favors the minimal single-feature object, whereas the prototypicality mechanism predicts listeners choose the shared-feature object instead.
