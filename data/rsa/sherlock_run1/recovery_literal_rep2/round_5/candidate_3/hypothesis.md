Listeners evaluate candidate referents through contextual typicality, expecting speakers to refer to the prototypical exemplar of the visual ensemble. Grounded in prototype theory, an object's typicality reflects its feature overlap with the other objects in the display: objects that share features widely with competitors are typical exemplars, whereas objects possessing idiosyncratic or unshared features are atypical outliers. Before hearing an utterance, listeners expect the speaker to refer to the prototypical object; upon hearing a referring expression, listeners integrate this contextual typicality prior with the literal meaning of the word, favoring typical exemplars over atypical competitors that carry extraneous unshared features.
