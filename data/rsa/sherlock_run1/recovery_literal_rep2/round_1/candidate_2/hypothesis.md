People's prior expectations over referents are governed by contextual feature rarity: an object is salient to the extent that its features are rare and informative within the visual scene, with features shared across fewer competitors contributing more strongly to salience. On the complex reference display where two objects have equal feature counts but one possesses a uniquely distinguishing feature, the current shared-prior model assigns them equal prior probability and predicts listeners choose the shared-feature object, whereas the contextual rarity mechanism predicts listeners choose the unique-feature competitor instead.
