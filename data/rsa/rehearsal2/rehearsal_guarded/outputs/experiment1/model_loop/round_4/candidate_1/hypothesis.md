Listeners in reference games differ in communicative sophistication, forming a population mixture of literal and pragmatic reasoners rather than a homogeneous cognitive type. Literal listeners interpret referring expressions purely through semantic truth conditions, selecting uniformly among matching objects without modeling speaker intent, whereas pragmatic listeners perform recursive social reasoning about speaker informativeness. On uninformative trials without a descriptive word, both listener types share a common perceptual bias favoring visually unique singletons over duplicated objects.
