Speakers choose referring expressions using a fast-and-frugal take-the-best heuristic, identifying the target's single most contextually discriminative feature and penalizing any candidate word by how far its specificity falls below that maximal cue, rather than integrating over all omitted features or competitor confusion. Pragmatic listeners invert this take-the-best speaker, expecting words to pick out referents for which the heard descriptor was the best available feature rather than a compromised sub-optimal choice. On a display where one candidate has a superior unique feature alongside the heard word while another candidate possesses multiple cluttered background features that never exceed the heard word's specificity, the take-the-best listener chooses the cluttered referent because the heard word remains its optimal cue, whereas the current best model rejects it due to accumulated omission penalties over extraneous features.
