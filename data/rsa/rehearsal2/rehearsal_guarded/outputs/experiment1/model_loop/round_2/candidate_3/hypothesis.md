Listeners interpret referring expressions under uncertainty about which visual feature of the display an uttered word picks out, rather than assuming words map deterministically to features. When hearing a word, listeners reason that a speaker chooses an intended feature to informatively identify a target referent, but may transmit that feature using an alternative word due to lexical ambiguity or communication noise. Pragmatic listeners invert this process by jointly inferring the target referent and integrating over their uncertainty about the word-to-feature mapping.
