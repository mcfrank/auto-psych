Listeners interpret referring expressions by reasoning about the speaker's communicative goal or question under discussion: rather than attempting to uniquely pinpoint the referent's complete scene identity, speakers aim to be informative about a specific visual aspect (feature) of the target object. When selecting which aspect of a referent to communicate, speakers favor features that are contextually informative and distinctive in the display, and express that chosen aspect with a descriptive word. Pragmatic listeners invert this process by jointly inferring the intended referent and the speaker's communicative goal, expecting a heard feature to pick out objects for which that feature was the most contextually relevant aspect to highlight.
