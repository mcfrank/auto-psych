Listeners interpret referring expressions by reasoning about visual salience grounded in perceptual crowding, expecting speakers to refer to items that are perceptually isolated from other scene objects rather than items embedded in dense visual clusters. Grounded in Shepard's exponential similarity law, each candidate referent suffers perceptual crowding proportional to its summed visual feature similarity with competing scene items, suppressing its visual prominence. Pragmatic listeners invert a speaker who shares this common-ground visual isolation prior, spontaneously favoring uncrowded items on uninformative trials and integrating visual isolation with recursive pragmatic reasoning upon hearing a descriptive word.
