Listeners interpret referring expressions by inverting a speaker who actively chooses descriptions to establish contrast against visual competitors that lack the named feature, rather than merely evaluating target recovery or distractor overlap. When multiple objects in a display share features, speakers evaluate how effectively candidate words differentiate the target from minimally different alternatives in the scene, and listeners reason recursively at depth 2 about this contrast-sensitive speaker. On uninformative trials with no informative word, choices are governed directly by visual pop-out and feature complexity biases.

Refining rsa_l2_singleton_feat_color_valence_l0 by incorporating contrast-maximizing referring expression utility from contrastive_speaker: simulated speakers at both reasoning tiers evaluate candidate words not only by their informativeness to simulated listeners, but also by adding a communicative utility bonus when a word differentiates the intended referent from minimally different competitors that lack that feature. This improves fit on displays containing partially overlapping items, capturing human speakers' preference for contrastive specification that pure target recovery overlooks.

- Parameters added: w_contrast (Normal(0.0, 1.0)), weighting the contrast utility bonus for expressions that resolve contrast against competitors lacking that feature.
