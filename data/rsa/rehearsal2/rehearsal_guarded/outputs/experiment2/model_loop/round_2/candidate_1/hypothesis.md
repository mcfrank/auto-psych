Listeners interpret referring expressions by reasoning that speakers choose what to talk about based on an object's communicative expressibility—how uniquely and informatively that object can be identified in the scene. Rather than assuming speakers choose referents uniformly, listeners model a speaker who selects intended referents in proportion to their maximum recovery probability under the available vocabulary, favoring uniquely nameable objects over ambiguous or featureless items. On uninformative trials with no descriptive word, choices directly reflect this communicative expressibility expectation alongside visual singleton salience.
