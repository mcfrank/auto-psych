When an intended referent can be uniquely named in the display, speakers reliably produce that distinguishing descriptor to ensure communicative recovery. When an intended referent cannot be uniquely named, the speaker faces an expressive dilemma where no word guarantees recovery and falls back on selecting among imperfect descriptors with a distinct dilemma choice rationality. Pragmatic listeners invert this two-regime speaker, recognizing that ambiguous expressions indicate unnameable referents facing communicative compromise rather than uniquely nameable referents whose distinguishing descriptors were withheld.
