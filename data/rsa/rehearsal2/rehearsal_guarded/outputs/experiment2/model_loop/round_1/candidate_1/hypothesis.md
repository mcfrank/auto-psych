Listeners interpret referring expressions by evaluating the weight of evidence each word provides in favor of the target over the scene distractors. Rather than engaging in nested recursive Theory of Mind, listeners invert a speaker who chooses descriptions proportional to their diagnostic log-odds—the degree to which the named feature singles out the intended referent rather than alternative objects in the context. On uninformative trials without a distinguishing word, choices default to visual singleton salience, favoring contextually unique objects over duplicated items.
