Speakers in reference games are boundedly rational satisficers who select referring expressions meeting an aspiration threshold of communicative recovery rather than maximizing informativeness. When an expression is sufficiently clear to reach this threshold (such as an ambiguous word that narrows the candidates down to an acceptable set), the speaker produces it without paying the cognitive cost of searching for a uniquely distinguishing alternative. Pragmatic listeners invert this satisficing speaker, recognizing that ambiguous words do not rule out feature-rich competitors that also satisfy the threshold, thereby moderating excessive scalar implicatures on minimal displays and preserving competitor choices.
