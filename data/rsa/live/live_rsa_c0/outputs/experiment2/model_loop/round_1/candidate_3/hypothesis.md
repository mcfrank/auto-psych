Speakers in reference games operate under bounded cognitive consideration, evaluating communicative alternatives probabilistically rather than exhaustively. When describing a referent with multiple features, speakers consider distinguishing alternative expressions with a bounded retrieval rate, occasionally producing an unconsidered true feature without communicative comparison. Pragmatic listeners invert this bounded-consideration speaker, recognizing that hearing a shared word does not rule out feature-rich competitors whose unique distinguishing words may have failed to enter the speaker's consideration set.
