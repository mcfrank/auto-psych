Listeners in reference games belong to a heterogeneous population composed of distinct cognitive types: literal listeners who choose among semantically valid referents based on contextual category typicality and visual feature complexity without computing speaker intentionality, and pragmatic listeners who invert an informative speaker to derive scalar implicatures. The overall communicative choice distribution reflects this population mixture over listener types under capacity-limited executive inhibitory control, naturally moderating population-level scalar implicatures on minimal displays and elevating choices of feature-rich objects under ambiguous expressions while maintaining robust pragmatic target identification across complex visual displays.
