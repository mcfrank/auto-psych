Listeners interpret referring expressions by reasoning about a speaker with uncertain communicative goals: referential identification versus informative aspect attribution. Under the identification goal, the speaker selects words to single out the referent's identity among display alternatives, generating scalar implicatures. Under the aspect goal, the speaker selects words to be informative about a descriptive aspect of the referent—evaluating true features by their contextual specificity—without attempting to distinguish the referent from other candidates. Pragmatic listeners invert this goal-directed speaker: hearing an ambiguous or shared expression leads listeners to infer that the speaker was communicating an informative aspect rather than uniquely identifying the referent, naturally softening scalar exclusion and elevating selections of feature-rich candidates on multi-match and ubiquitous displays.
