Listeners interpret referring expressions by combining goal-directed communicative inference with a feature-structured lapse process: when cognitive attention or communicative reasoning lapses, listeners do not choose uniformly at random across the visual display, but instead select an object in proportion to its bottom-up visual feature complexity. This feature-driven decision error naturally accounts for non-matching distractor selections and elevated choices of feature-rich candidates, while dampening excessive singleton choices on uninformative displays where duplicate objects possess greater feature richness.
