Listeners interpret referring expressions under capacity-limited visual attention that is divided among competing candidate referents in the visual scene. When an utterance is referentially ambiguous and applies to multiple matching candidates, the attentional capacity available for counterfactual pragmatic mentalizing is divided across the candidate set, causing pragmatic scalar exclusion to attenuate with candidate load while choices become increasingly guided by visual salience and feature complexity. On unambiguous pop-out displays with a single matching candidate, undivided attention ensures full pragmatic verification, whereas multi-match displays dilute attention and soften excessive scalar inferences.
