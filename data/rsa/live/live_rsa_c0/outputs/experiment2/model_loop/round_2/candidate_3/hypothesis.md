Listeners interpret referring expressions under lexical feature uncertainty: rather than assuming an error-free mapping from words to visual features, listeners are uncertain which contextual feature dimension a speaker's word denotes. When hearing a word, listeners treat it as primarily denoting its literal feature but consider with non-zero probability that it denotes other contextual visual features present in the scene. Because feature-rich referents instantiate more visual features that could be intended under feature uncertainty, they receive elevated semantic applicability, softening excessive scalar exclusion, explaining preferences for maximal-feature objects on ambiguous displays, and naturally accounting for non-matching distractor intrusions.
