Listeners interpret referring expressions with an a priori expectation that speakers refer to contextually typical objects embodying the scene's family resemblance or central tendency. Before hearing an informative word (and during referent selection), listeners evaluate each referent by its category typicality—the average feature overlap with other context objects—scaling smoothly across display sizes and redundant feature overlaps. This typicality prior tempers excessive singleton preferences on uninformative trials, elevates choices of duplicated candidates and feature-rich items under ubiquitous expressions, and moderates aggressive scalar exclusion.
