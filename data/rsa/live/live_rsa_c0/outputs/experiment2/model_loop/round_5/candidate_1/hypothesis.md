Speakers in reference games adhere to Gricean clarity by incurring a cognitive ambiguity cost when producing referentially ambiguous expressions, penalizing words in proportion to the number of unintended context objects they describe. When describing an object, speakers strongly favor unambiguous or distinguishing expressions over ambiguous shared words, producing shared words only when communicating referents that lack distinguishing alternatives. Pragmatic listeners invert this ambiguity-averse speaker under capacity-limited executive attention: hearing an ambiguous word provides strong evidence for referents that lacked clean alternatives, elevating choices of multi-feature pragmatic targets over all-feature competitors.
