Listeners interpret referring expressions with an a priori expectation that speakers select communicative targets based on referential resolvability: the degree to which an intended referent can be successfully singled out from the visual context given a single-word budget. Before hearing an informative word and during referent selection, listeners evaluate each referent by its communicative resolvability—the maximum recovery precision attainable across its true descriptive features—anticipating that speakers avoid intending referents that cannot be cleanly identified in context. Pragmatic listeners invert this resolvability-guided speaker, preventing feature-rich competitors from overpowering minimal pragmatic targets under shared words and properly calibrating choices of full duplicates on uninformative trials.
