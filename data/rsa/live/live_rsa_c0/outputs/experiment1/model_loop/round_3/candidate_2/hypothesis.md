Listeners interpret referring expressions by combining pure communicative inference with a salience-biased softmax decision rule that trades communicative evidence against visual feature complexity. Rather than attributing perceptual preferences to the speaker as a common-knowledge prior, the listener evaluates candidate referents at the decision stage by combining the log-evidence of referent recovery with an intrinsic preference for distinctive, feature-rich objects. This decision rule naturally moderates extreme scalar implicatures on ambiguous and ubiquitous displays while capturing strong preferences for salient referents on uninformative trials.
