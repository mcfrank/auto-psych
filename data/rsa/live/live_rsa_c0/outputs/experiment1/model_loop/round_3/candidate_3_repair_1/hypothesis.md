Listeners interpret referring expressions by reasoning about a speaker with uncertain communicative goals: either referential identification (informing a pragmatic listener who draws scalar inferences) or descriptive aspect attribution (informing a literal listener about the object's visual features). Under the aspect goal, the speaker selects expressions based on literal descriptive informativeness rather than recursive scalar contrast, moderating scalar exclusion and explaining why feature-rich referents retain substantial choice probability on ubiquitous and scalar displays.
