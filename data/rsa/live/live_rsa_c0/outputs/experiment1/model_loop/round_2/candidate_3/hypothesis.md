Listeners interpret referring expressions under uncertainty about which visual feature a speaker's word picks out. Rather than assuming an error-free mapping from words to visual features, pragmatic listeners reason that a speaker selects an informative descriptive feature of the intended referent but transmits an expression subject to feature confusion among available contextual alternatives. This feature uncertainty naturally produces non-zero choices of feature-rich foil objects, tempers overly extreme scalar exclusion inferences, and preserves literal accuracy on simple unambiguous displays.
