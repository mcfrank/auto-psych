Listeners interpret referring expressions under capacity-limited visual attention across the display, balancing top-down pragmatic reasoning against bottom-up visual attentional capture. When an utterance is heard, bounded attentional resources are allocated based on display size and visual salience: under higher visual load in larger displays, attentional dispersion occasionally causes bottom-up attentional capture by salient objects without full communicative verification, whereas unambiguous pop-out displays ensure near-perfect target identification. This attentional bottleneck moderates extreme scalar exclusion, allowing feature-rich referents to retain substantial choice probability while naturally explaining foil selections in multi-object scenes.
