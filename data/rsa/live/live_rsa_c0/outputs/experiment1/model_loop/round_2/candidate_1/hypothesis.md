Listeners expect speakers to talk about contextually informative objects, forming an a priori expectation over referents based on feature surprisal where rare or unique visual features confer high informativeness while ubiquitous features carry none. When interpreting referring expressions or anticipating referents on uninformative trials, listeners integrate this feature informativeness prior with communicative reasoning, expecting speakers to target entities with distinctive visual information. This naturally moderates excessive scalar exclusion and feature-rich omission across varied display sizes without ad-hoc cue weighting.
