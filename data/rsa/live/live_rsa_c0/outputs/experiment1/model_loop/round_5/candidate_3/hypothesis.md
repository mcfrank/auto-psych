Listeners interpret referring expressions under capacity-limited executive inhibitory control, balancing goal-directed pragmatic inference against distractor interference. When an utterance is referentially ambiguous, the cognitive load of resolving competing candidates depletes executive control, causing inhibitory suppression of non-matching contextual distractors to degrade proportionally to candidate competition. Visually salient distractors—especially solitary contrastive foils—escape inhibition and capture choices under high candidate load, whereas unambiguous pop-out expressions preserve near-perfect target identification.
