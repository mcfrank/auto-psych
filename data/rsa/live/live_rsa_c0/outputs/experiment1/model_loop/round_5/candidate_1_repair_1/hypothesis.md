Speakers in reference games consider an outside option to abstain from speaking when all available expressions for an intended referent are ambiguous, preferring silence over producing an uninformative referring expression. Pragmatic listeners invert this choice: knowing that an ambiguous word would frequently have been replaced by silence had the speaker intended an object lacking distinguishing features, listeners infer that hearing an ambiguous word is less diagnostic of sparse targets. This naturally moderates excessive scalar implicatures on two-object displays while elevating choices of feature-rich objects on ubiquitous utterance displays.
