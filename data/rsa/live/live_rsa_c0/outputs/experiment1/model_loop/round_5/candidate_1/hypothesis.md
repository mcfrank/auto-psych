Speakers in reference games consider an outside option to abstain from speaking (silence) when all available true expressions for an intended referent yield low communicative utility, rather than being forced to produce an uninformative utterance. Pragmatic listeners invert this choice: knowing that an ambiguous or ubiquitous word would frequently have been replaced by silence had the speaker intended a sparse referent with no other options, listeners infer that an uttered ambiguous word is less diagnostic of sparse targets. This moderates overly aggressive scalar implicatures on two-object displays, lowers intermediate referent over-prediction, and elevates choices of feature-rich objects on ubiquitous utterance displays.
