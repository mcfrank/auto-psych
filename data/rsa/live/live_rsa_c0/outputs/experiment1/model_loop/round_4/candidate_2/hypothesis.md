Listeners interpret referring expressions under capacity-limited visual feature binding, where visual features can undergo illusory conjunction and migrate to neighboring objects with probability proportional to their scene-wide prevalence. When hearing a word that applies to almost all objects in the display, the high contextual prevalence of that feature causes occasional perceptual migration to the solitary foil, resolving the under-prediction of non-matching foil choices while preserving literal precision on pop-out displays. Across larger displays and redundant feature overlaps, this prevalence-dependent perceptual scaling naturally softens scalar implicatures and elevates choices of feature-rich referents.
