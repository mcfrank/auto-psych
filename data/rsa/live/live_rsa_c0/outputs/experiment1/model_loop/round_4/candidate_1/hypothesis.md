Listeners in reference games belong to a heterogeneous population composed of distinct cognitive types: literal listeners who choose among semantically valid referents based on visual salience without computing speaker intentionality, and pragmatic listeners who invert an informative speaker to derive scalar implicatures. The propensity to engage in recursive pragmatic reasoning is capacity-limited and declines under higher visual load in larger displays, where listeners increasingly rely on literal semantics. The observed choice distribution reflects this load-dependent mixture over listener types, moderating population-level scalar implicatures and elevating choices of feature-rich objects under ambiguous expressions while maintaining strict semantic accuracy.
