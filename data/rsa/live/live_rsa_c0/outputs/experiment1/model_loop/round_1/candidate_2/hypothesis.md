Speakers in reference games choose words by maximizing their expected probability of communicative success—the linear likelihood of correct referent recovery in a coordination game—rather than information-theoretic surprisal. Because success probability is inherently bounded between zero and one, the communicative penalty for an ambiguous or shared word does not compound with logarithmic severity, leading pragmatic listeners to maintain substantial choice probability for feature-rich referents rather than drawing extreme scalar exclusion inferences. This mechanism disagrees most sharply with standard models on displays with ubiquitous features shared across all objects, where it correctly predicts that listeners frequently choose feature-rich objects instead of near-exclusively selecting sparse scalar targets.
