Listeners in reference games belong to a heterogeneous population composed of two distinct cognitive types: parsimonious scalar listeners who invert an informative speaker to select the minimal referent possessing the fewest extraneous features, and prototypical maximizing listeners who select the most feature-complete exemplar matching the heard expression. Because evaluative choices in the population are divided between minimal scalar targets and maximal category exemplars, intermediate-feature candidates falling in the interior of the candidate set are systematically avoided by both types, directly resolving the over-prediction of intermediate candidates while explaining why humans frequently select feature-rich competitors under ambiguous expressions.
