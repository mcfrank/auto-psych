Speakers and listeners in reference games evaluate referring expressions under graded semantics where a word's truth value reflects its descriptive coverage of the intended referent rather than a binary truth condition. Because a single-word utterance cannot articulate all features of a complex object, its semantic applicability to a candidate referent diminishes as the object possesses more extraneous unmentioned features, rewarding referents for which the word provides an exhaustive description and discounting overloaded competitors. Pragmatic listeners invert this coverage-sensitive speaker under capacity-limited executive attention, naturally elevating choices of multi-feature pragmatic targets over all-feature competitors while preserving literal precision on simple displays.
