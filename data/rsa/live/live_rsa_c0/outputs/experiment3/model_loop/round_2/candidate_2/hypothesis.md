Listeners interpret referring expressions through resource-bounded approximate inference using a finite sample of mental particles rather than exhaustive Bayesian normalization. When hearing an utterance, the listener samples candidate referents from semantic attention and evaluates the speaker's communicative likelihood pairwise only when distinct competing candidates are co-sampled into working memory. With a small sample budget, identical candidates are frequently sampled without counterfactual comparison, which naturally attenuates extreme scalar implicatures on minimal displays while preserving sharp target discrimination when alternatives are co-evaluated.
