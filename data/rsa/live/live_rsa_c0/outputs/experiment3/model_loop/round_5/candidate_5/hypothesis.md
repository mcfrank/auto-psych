Listeners interpret referring expressions through boundary polarization with avoidable ambiguity avoidance: when a referring expression matches multiple candidate referents that vary in feature complexity, evaluative attention anchors on the range extrema—the minimal pragmatic target and the maximal category prototype—while candidates located in the interior of the feature range suffer cognitive suppression due to lack of boundary contrast. When evaluating alternative referring expressions, listeners expect that speakers adhere to Gricean clarity by penalizing ambiguous expressions specifically when the ambiguity is avoidable—that is, when the intended referent possessed an alternative distinguishing expression that would have uniquely identified it in context. Consequently, competitors that possessed uniquely distinguishing alternatives are decisively precluded under shared expressions, while minimal pragmatic targets and multi-feature candidates lacking distinguishing options are strongly favored.

Refining boundary_polarization_listener by incorporating the avoidable ambiguity avoidance component from avoidable_ambiguity_speaker: speakers in reference games adhere to Gricean clarity by penalizing referentially ambiguous expressions specifically when the ambiguity is avoidable—that is, when the intended referent possessed an alternative distinguishing expression that would have uniquely identified it in context. Pragmatic listeners invert this avoidable-ambiguity-sensitive speaker under boundary polarization, recognizing that hearing an ambiguous word provides strong evidence for referents that lacked distinguishing alternatives.
