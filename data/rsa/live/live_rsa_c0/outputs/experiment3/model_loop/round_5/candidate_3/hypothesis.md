Listeners interpret referring expressions under capacity-limited visual attention that groups identical display objects into perceptual ensembles. Rather than individuating redundant duplicates as independent competing referents, visual attention allocates resources across distinct perceptual groups, and listeners reason about a speaker whose referring expressions target perceptual types rather than distinguishing identical spatial tokens. This ensemble grouping prevents spurious disambiguation penalties against duplicate-matching expressions, properly elevates choices of full duplicates on uninformative mumble trials, and moderates excessive scalar exclusion of duplicate competitors in multi-candidate displays.
