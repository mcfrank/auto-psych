Listeners interpret referring expressions by evaluating candidate referents within a contrastive feature hierarchy under Clark's Principle of Contrast—penalizing subsumed intermediate candidates whose visual features are strictly nested within a more complete competitor—while evaluating contextual category typicality through exemplar-based psychological similarity across the visual scene. Before hearing an informative word and during referent selection, each referent's contextual typicality is determined by its average exponential-decay psychological similarity to all other scene exemplars under the Generalized Context Model rather than additive feature overlap. This exemplar similarity formulation properly rewards identical candidates and central scene exemplars without artificially compounding with total feature count, directly mitigating excessive singleton preferences on uninformative mumble trials (mumble_singleton_choice_rate), elevating choices of full duplicates (mumble_full_duplicate_choice_rate) and duplicate-matching competitors (three_object_target_vs_duplicate_competitor_rate), and calibrating pragmatic choices across multi-object displays.
