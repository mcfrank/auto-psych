Speakers in reference games recognize that identical duplicate objects in a visual scene cannot be uniquely disambiguated by any single referring expression. When intending an object that has an identical duplicate in context, speakers experience communicative indifference between viable true descriptions rather than hyper-optimizing between degrees of partial ambiguity, producing shared and distinguishing words with equal ease. Pragmatic listeners invert this duplicate-indifferent speaker under capacity-limited executive attention: because speakers intending a duplicate candidate do not avoid broader shared words, hearing a shared expression does not rule out duplicate competitors, properly elevating choices of duplicate-matching competitors.
