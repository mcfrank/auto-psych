Listeners interpret referring expressions under Gestalt perceptual grouping across identical visual candidates: when duplicate objects appear in a visual scene, observers group identical tokens into a single perceptual type rather than independent spatial individuals. Before hearing an informative word and during referent resolution, listeners expect speakers to choose communicative referents at the level of distinct visual types, allocating a prior over types that is shared equally among identical tokens rather than compounding with spatial token numerosity. This eliminates artificial singleton inflation on uninformative mumble trials, properly calibrates choices between unique targets and duplicated competitors in both word and mumble conditions, and applies the discourse prior in a principled place at the pragmatic decision tier without recursive over-compounding.
