Listeners interpret referring expressions through boundary polarization with exemplar-based category typicality: when a referring expression matches multiple candidate referents that vary in feature complexity, evaluative attention anchors on the range extrema—the minimal pragmatic target and the maximal category prototype—while candidates located in the interior of the feature range suffer cognitive suppression due to lack of boundary contrast. Before hearing an informative word and during referent selection, contextual category typicality is determined by each referent's average exponential-decay psychological similarity to all other scene exemplars under the Generalized Context Model rather than additive feature overlap. This exemplar similarity formulation properly rewards identical candidates and central scene exemplars without artificially compounding with total feature count, directly mitigating excessive singleton preferences on uninformative mumble trials and calibrating selections when identical or duplicate-matching competitors are present.

Refining boundary_polarization_listener by replacing contextual category typicality from additive feature overlap with exemplar-based psychological similarity under the Generalized Context Model from exemplar_typicality_listener: listeners evaluate contextual typicality as an object's average exponential-decay similarity to all other scene exemplars across the visual display rather than an unnormalized sum of shared feature fractions. This directly rectifies the observed under-selection of duplicate-matching competitors in three-object displays (three_object_target_vs_duplicate_competitor_rate), the under-selection of full duplicates on uninformative mumble trials (mumble_full_duplicate_choice_rate), and the excessive singleton bias (mumble_singleton_choice_rate), while fully preserving range-frequency boundary polarization, capacity-limited executive attention, competitor confusion avoidance, and contrastive outlier pop-out.
