Speakers in reference games choose referring expressions to maximize global communicative information gain—the Kullback-Leibler divergence between the listener's prior belief and updated belief across the visual scene—alongside referent recovery. When communicating a feature-rich referent, speakers strongly favor distinguishing expressions that decisively resolve scene-wide uncertainty over ambiguous expressions that convey little information. Pragmatic listeners invert this information-gain speaker under capacity-limited executive attention, recognizing that hearing an ambiguous word provides evidence for referents that lacked high-information alternatives.
