Speakers in reference games choose referring expressions to maximize global communicative information gain—the reduction of listener uncertainty across the entire visual scene—alongside referent recovery. When communicating an intended referent, speakers evaluate alternatives probabilistically with bounded consideration, strongly favoring distinguishing expressions that resolve scene-wide uncertainty over ambiguous expressions. Pragmatic listeners invert this information-gain speaker under capacity-limited executive inhibitory control, recognizing that hearing an ambiguous word provides evidence for referents that lacked high-information alternatives while distinguishing features that isolate a competitor decisively rule out that competitor, elevating choices of multi-feature pragmatic targets over all-feature competitors.
