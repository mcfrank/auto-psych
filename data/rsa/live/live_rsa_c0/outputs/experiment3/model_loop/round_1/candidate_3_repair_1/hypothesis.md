Speakers in reference games adhere to Gricean clarity by penalizing referentially ambiguous expressions specifically when the ambiguity is avoidable—that is, when the intended referent possessed an alternative distinguishing expression that would have uniquely identified it in context. When an intended referent cannot be uniquely singled out because all its descriptive features are shared, producing an ambiguous expression is a communicative necessity and incurs no avoidable ambiguity penalty. Pragmatic listeners invert this avoidable-ambiguity-sensitive speaker under capacity-limited executive attention: hearing an ambiguous word provides strong evidence for referents that lacked distinguishing alternatives, resolving scalar under-prediction of multi-feature pragmatic targets over all-feature competitors.
