Listeners in reference games do not strictly probability-match their communicative beliefs when clicking an object; instead, they select referents using a soft-maximizing decision policy governed by decision precision. Rather than choosing an object in direct proportion to its posterior belief, listeners apply a power-law decision rule (a softmax over log-posterior beliefs) that sharpens high-confidence candidates and suppresses intermediate competitors. This decision rule naturally reduces the selection of intermediate-feature candidates in multi-candidate displays while elevating choices of multi-feature pragmatic targets.
