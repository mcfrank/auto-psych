Listeners interpret referring expressions by reasoning about a speaker with uncertain communicative goals: referential identification versus characteristic aspect attribution. Under the identification goal, the speaker selects expressions to uniquely single out the intended referent among display alternatives, whereas under the aspect goal, the speaker communicates a defining visual property of the intended referent by prioritizing words that maximize the joint product of cue validity and category validity for that object. Pragmatic listeners jointly infer the intended referent while reasoning about the speaker's communicative goal under capacity-limited executive attention, resolving scalar under-prediction of multi-feature targets, suppressing intermediate competitors, and elevating choices of feature-rich candidates.
