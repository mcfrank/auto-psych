Listeners interpret referring expressions by reasoning about a speaker with uncertain communicative goals: referential identification versus descriptive aspect recovery. Under the identification goal, the speaker chooses expressions to uniquely single out the intended referent, whereas under the aspect goal, the speaker communicates to maximize the listener's recovery of the referent's visual features and attributes, evaluating expressions by their expected feature overlap with the listener's interpretation. Pragmatic listeners jointly infer the intended referent while reasoning about the speaker's communicative goal under capacity-limited executive attention, resolving scalar under-prediction of multi-feature targets, suppressing intermediate competitors, and properly calibrating choices of duplicate competitors.
