Listeners interpret referring expressions using a non-Bayesian mutual exclusivity heuristic: when hearing a word that applies to multiple candidate objects, listeners penalize candidates that possess alternative distinguishing names in the visual scene, assuming that each object has a unique primary descriptor. Candidates with strong or distinctive alternative features are preemptively excluded, while candidates for which the heard word is the only or primary available descriptor are preferred. On uninformative prior trials without an informative word, listeners spontaneously choose the object with the greatest communicative naming affordance.
