When communicating about an intended referent, candidate utterances compete for selection in the speaker's lexicon. Referents with multiple applicable words induce lexical competition and selection interference, attenuating the speaker's communicative precision and increasing the production of shared or redundant expressions. Pragmatic listeners invert this competition-bounded speaker at depth 2, naturally softening recursive preemption penalties against multi-featured competitor referents while unprompted expectations follow visual salience.
