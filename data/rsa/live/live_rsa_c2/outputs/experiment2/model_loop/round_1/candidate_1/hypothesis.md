When interpreting referring expressions in visual reference games, listeners reason under perceptual feature-binding noise (Feature Integration Theory): features present in the scene are susceptible to illusory conjunctions, erroneously binding to candidate objects that physically lack them. The rate of illusory conjunctions is not uniform across distractors, but scales with an object's visual feature complexity because feature-dense objects draw focal attention and provide receptive perceptual scaffolding for migrant features. Pragmatic listeners invert speakers within this perceptually grounded environment, naturally explaining why literal semantic violations fall overwhelmingly on visually complex non-matching objects rather than uniform random distractors.
