When resolving referring expressions in visual reference games, listeners reason under perceptual feature-binding noise (Feature Integration Theory): free-floating features active in working memory are prone to illusory conjunctions, erroneously binding to distractor objects that physically lack them. The susceptibility to illusory conjunctions scales with a distractor's visual feature complexity, as feature-dense objects capture ambient spatial attention and provide receptive perceptual scaffolding for migrant attributes. Pragmatic listeners invert informative speakers while accounting for this perceptual vulnerability, naturally explaining why literal semantic violations fall overwhelmingly on visually complex non-matching objects rather than uniformly across distractors.
