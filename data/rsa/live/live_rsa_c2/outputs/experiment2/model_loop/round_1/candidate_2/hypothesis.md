Listeners gate their pragmatic inferential capacity by the contextual discriminative contrast of the heard utterance: words that eliminate non-matching distractors license confident counterfactual preemption, whereas words that are true of all objects in the scene convey zero discriminative contrast, leading listeners to attenuate pragmatic preemption and rely on default visual expectations. This directly resolves the severe overprediction of pragmatic targets in two-object subset displays and ambiguous-word singleton displays where the heard label rules out no candidates in context. This mechanism disagrees most sharply with bounded_endpoint_prev_redundancy_l2 on two-object scalar displays containing a minimal target and a superset competitor, predicting substantially softer competitor preemption and elevated competitor choices because the shared word has zero contextual contrast.
