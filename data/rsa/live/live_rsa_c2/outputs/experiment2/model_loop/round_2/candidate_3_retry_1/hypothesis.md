When interpreting referring expressions in visual reference games, listeners reason with contextual lexical uncertainty about which feature in the display the speaker's word denotes. Rather than rigidly binding the heard word to a single attribute, listeners consider alternative features present in the visual scene in proportion to their contextual co-occurrence with the mentioned feature on candidate objects. Pragmatic listeners marginalize over these candidate feature interpretations using recursive Theory of Mind, naturally softening preemption penalties against multi-featured competitor referents and accounting for choices of feature-complex objects.
