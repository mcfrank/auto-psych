In visual reference games, listeners structure their default expectations around contextual prototype deviance: before hearing an informative word, observers automatically extract the scene's contextual prototype (the average feature profile across display objects) and expect speakers to refer to atypical, distinctive exemplars that diverge from this contextual average rather than unremarkable, prototypical items that blend into the ensemble. Pragmatic listeners integrate this prototype-deviance prior into reference resolution at depth 2, naturally explaining why strictly intermediate-complexity objects are strongly disfavored on both unprompted and word trials while atypical baselines and feature-rich exemplars attract referential expectations. Across varying display sizes and redundant feature distributions, this deviance metric smoothly discounts objects that match the prevailing contextual norm while concentrating communicative expectations on polar and unique exemplars.
