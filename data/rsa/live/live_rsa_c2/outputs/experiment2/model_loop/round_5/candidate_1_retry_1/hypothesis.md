When formulating referring expressions in visual reference games, speakers balance communicative informativeness against the cognitive verification cost of using distinctive attributes: to deploy an exclusive or distinguishing label, the speaker must visually search and verify that context distractors do not possess that attribute. This verification effort scales with the utterance's contextual discriminative contrast (the proportion of context distractors ruled out), prompting speakers to frequently produce accessible, shared category labels even when an exclusive distinguishing feature is available. Pragmatic listeners invert this verification-bounded speaker via recursive Theory of Mind, naturally softening counterfactual preemption in subset displays and elevating simpler targets in cross-cutting contexts.
