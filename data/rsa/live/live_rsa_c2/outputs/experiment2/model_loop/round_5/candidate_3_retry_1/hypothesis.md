In visual reference games, human working memory cannot simultaneously evaluate and normalize across all display candidates; instead, listeners interpret referring expressions through Decision by Sampling, evaluating candidate referents via pairwise comparisons against a comparator sampled from the visual scene according to perceptual salience. In each pairwise tournament, the listener evaluates how convincingly the heard expression describes the candidate relative to the sampled competitor under bounded inferential precision. This mechanism naturally softens recursive preemption penalties against multi-featured competitor referents in scalar subset contexts while visual salience occasionally draws comparisons to prominent non-matching distractors, accounting for false-feature choices on complex objects.
