When choosing an object in a visual reference game, listeners do not match choice frequencies linearly to posterior probabilities (probability matching); instead, they turn referential beliefs into action via a softmax decision rule governed by response sensitivity (the Luce choice axiom). Under cognitive load, listeners exhibit conservative probability compression, softening extreme Bayesian preemption penalties and elevating competitor choices in subset-superset displays while preserving communicative contrast. Default referential expectations and attentional lapses are guided by visual feature salience.
