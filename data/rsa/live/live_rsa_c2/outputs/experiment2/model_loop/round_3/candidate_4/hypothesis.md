We refine feature_uncertainty_salience_lapse_l2 by incorporating contextual end-point salience from endpoint_salience_l2 into the shared visual referent prior. In visual reference games, listeners structure their default expectations around contextual extremity: perceptual attention spontaneously anchors at the structural boundaries of the scene's feature complexity distribution (the minimal baseline object and the maximal exemplar), while intermediate objects that fall strictly between these poles are disfavored as unremarkable non-targets. Pragmatic listeners invert informative speakers under this extremity prior alongside contextual lexical feature uncertainty and salience-captured attentional lapses, directly rectifying the source model's systematic overprediction of strictly intermediate-complexity objects on both unprompted mumble trials and matching word trials. Across varying display sizes from 2x2 to 4x4, this mechanism naturally preserves polarity between the simplest baseline anchors and maximally complex exemplars while discounting intermediate distractors.
