When interpreting referring expressions in visual reference games, listeners reason about multi-goal speakers who may communicate either to uniquely identify the referent or to inform about a specific attribute aspect of the object (an aspect question under discussion). Under an aspect goal, speakers evaluate how informatively an utterance communicates the object's features rather than attempting to contrast the object against context distractors, producing applicable labels for multi-featured objects even when unmentioned distinguishing features are present. Pragmatic listeners invert this multi-goal speaker via joint recursive Theory of Mind, dampening scalar preemption penalties against multi-featured competitor referents while preserving target identification when an identity goal is inferred.
