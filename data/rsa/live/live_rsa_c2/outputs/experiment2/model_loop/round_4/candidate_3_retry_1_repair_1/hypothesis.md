In rational speech act theory, listeners ground literal comprehension in the Size Principle of generative communication: the probability that a speaker produces any specific feature of an object scales inversely with the object's total visual feature complexity. Pragmatic listeners invert informative speakers under this generative semantic foundation, systematically favoring candidates with fewer total features in cross-cutting contexts while maintaining substantial credence for competitor referents in subset displays. This mechanism disagrees most sharply with the current best model on cross-cutting displays where candidate objects match the uttered word but vary in extraneous feature count, predicting a robust preference for the simpler candidate rather than dividing choices evenly.
