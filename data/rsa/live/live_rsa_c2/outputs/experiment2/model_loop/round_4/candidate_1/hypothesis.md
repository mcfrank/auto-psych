Listeners in reference games are cognitively heterogeneous across strategic reasoning depths, forming a cognitive hierarchy population mixture of literal comprehenders (Level-0), first-order pragmatic reasoners (Level-1), and second-order pragmatic reasoners (Level-2). When interpreting a referring expression, Level-0 listeners resolve words by literal semantic applicability weighted by visual salience, Level-1 listeners invert a literal speaker to compute standard scalar implicatures, and Level-2 listeners invert a strategic speaker to resolve higher-order communicative ambiguities. Across displays of varying sizes and feature overlaps, this reasoning-depth distribution naturally accounts for bounded target preferences in scalar subset contexts and heterogeneous choices in complex reference games without assuming uniform strategic capacity.
