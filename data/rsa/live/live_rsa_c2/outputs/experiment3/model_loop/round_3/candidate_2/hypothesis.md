When choosing an object in a visual reference game, listeners' motor responses are subject to perceptual confusability with visually similar objects in the display. Governed by Shepard's Universal Law of Generalization, the probability of mistakenly clicking a distractor instead of the intended referent decays exponentially with their visual feature distance. Listeners translate referential beliefs into action through this similarity-based confusion kernel, which naturally directs choice errors and slips toward objects that share visual features with the intended referent.
