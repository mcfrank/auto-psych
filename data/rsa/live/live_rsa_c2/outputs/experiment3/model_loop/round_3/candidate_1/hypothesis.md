Listeners interpret referring expressions using a non-Bayesian mutual exclusivity heuristic rather than recursive Theory of Mind. When hearing a word, listeners choose among matching candidate objects by penalizing candidates in proportion to the cumulative contextual specificity of their unmentioned alternative features, as each alternative label competes under mutual exclusivity to serve as the object's primary description. On unprompted trials without an informative word, default choices follow bottom-up visual referent salience.
