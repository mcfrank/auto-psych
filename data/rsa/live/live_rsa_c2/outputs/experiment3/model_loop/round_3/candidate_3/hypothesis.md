When interpreting referring expressions, listeners reason that speakers may communicate to highlight an attribute aspect of an object rather than solely to establish its unique individual identity. Under an aspect goal, speakers produce applicable features without contrastively avoiding words shared with context distractors, with production probability scaling inversely with the referent's total feature complexity. Pragmatic listeners invert this multi-goal speaker via recursive Theory of Mind at depth 2, naturally dampening recursive preemption in subset displays and favoring simpler matching candidates in cross-cutting contexts.
