In visual reference games, human perceptual attention does not scale linearly with visual feature complexity. Under Feature Integration Theory and psychophysical power laws of visual conspicuity, visual features combine superlinearly: feature conjunctions create accelerating bottom-up salience, establishing the maximal visual exemplar as the dominant figure in the scene while leaving intermediate items unremarkable and empty objects as the baseline ground. Pragmatic listeners and speakers incorporate this convex feature salience into their shared referent prior, directly explaining why people strongly prefer maximal exemplars over empty baselines on mumble trials, avoid intermediate objects across contexts, and direct attentional lapses toward visually complex distractors.
