When interpreting referring expressions in visual reference games, listeners exhibit base-rate neglect: the arrival of diagnostic communicative evidence (the speaker's utterance) causes listeners to discount default perceptual base rates (visual salience) in favor of the speaker's communicative likelihood. On unprompted trials without an informative word, choices are guided entirely by the perceptual prior, whereas on word trials, listeners attenuate the perceptual prior, naturally favoring simpler referents in cross-cutting contexts and disfavoring intermediate distractors.
