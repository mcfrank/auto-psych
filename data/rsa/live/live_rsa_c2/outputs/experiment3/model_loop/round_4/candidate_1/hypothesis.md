Listeners in visual reference games are cognitively heterogeneous, forming a population mixture of parsimonious (simplicity-seeking) and strategic (pragmatic) reasoners. Parsimonious listeners resolve referring expressions under the principle of cognitive economy (the Size Principle of generative communication), choosing among matching candidate referents by penalizing extraneous unmentioned features in proportion to an object's overall visual complexity. In contrast, strategic listeners engage in recursive Theory of Mind to counterfactually preempt competitors, explaining why human populations reliably favor simpler candidates in cross-cutting displays while dampening extreme preemption in scalar subset contexts.
