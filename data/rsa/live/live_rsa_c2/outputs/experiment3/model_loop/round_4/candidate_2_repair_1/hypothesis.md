When formulating referring expressions in visual reference games, speakers are subject to an attentional encoding bottleneck: rather than exhaustively registering every visual attribute of an object, speakers probabilistically encode features into their active referring plan with an attentional registration probability, occasionally producing a shared category label because alternative distinguishing features were not attended. Pragmatic listeners invert this attention-bounded speaker via recursive Theory of Mind at depth 2, which softens recursive preemption in subset displays and favors simpler matching candidates in cross-cutting contexts because the probability of failing to register distinguishing attributes decreases with each additional feature.
