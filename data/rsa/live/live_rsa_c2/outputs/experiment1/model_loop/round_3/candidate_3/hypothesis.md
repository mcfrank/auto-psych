When interpreting referring expressions, listeners reason that speakers may communicate to highlight a specific feature aspect of the referent (answering a feature-level question under discussion) rather than solely to uniquely pick out its individual identity. Pragmatic listeners invert this multi-goal speaker by jointly inferring the intended referent and the speaker's communicative goal, expecting an aspect-directed speaker to describe matching attributes regardless of unmentioned distinguishing features. This joint inference dampens recursive preemption penalties against overspecified competitor objects in scalar contexts while preserving referential contrast when the speaker's goal is unique identification.
