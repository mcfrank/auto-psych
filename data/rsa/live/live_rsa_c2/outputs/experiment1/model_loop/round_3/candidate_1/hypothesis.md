Listeners interpret referring expressions using a non-Bayesian fewest-features simplicity heuristic rather than recursive Theory of Mind. When hearing a word that applies to multiple candidate referents, listeners choose among matching objects by favoring the candidate with the fewest total features, penalizing candidates in proportion to their extraneous unmentioned features. On unprompted trials without an informative word, choices are guided by visual salience, favoring feature-rich exemplars and perceptual singletons.
