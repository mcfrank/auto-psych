Listeners interpret referring expressions under feature-reference uncertainty, reasoning that a speaker's word primarily denotes its nominal visual feature but carries residual uncertainty about which feature in the display was intended. When resolving reference, listeners marginalize over candidate visual features, which scales naturally across display sizes and elevates the communicative viability of overspecified competitor objects that possess multiple candidate attributes. On uninformative trials without a word, listeners rely on a shared visual perceptual prior favoring unique, distinct, and feature-rich objects.
