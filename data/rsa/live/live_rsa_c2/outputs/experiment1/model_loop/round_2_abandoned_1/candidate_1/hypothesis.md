Listeners interpret referring expressions by modeling speakers who share an object prior governed by contextual extremity salience: human attention before hearing a word anchors on the polar extremes of the visual scene's feature complexity distribution—the maximal figure (the most feature-rich object) and the minimal ground (the baseline contrast anchor). Objects with strictly intermediate feature counts lack both the maximal communicative richness of the figure and the baseline clarity of the ground, and are therefore strongly discounted. Depth-2 pragmatic listeners invert this extremity-anchored speaker to resolve referring expressions and form prior expectations on uninformative trials.
