Listeners are resource-limited reasoners whose pragmatic reference resolution is constrained by a finite information-processing capacity rather than unbounded Bayesian inversion. When inverting a speaker's communicative likelihood, capacity-limited listeners update their beliefs conservatively, dampening the extreme preemption penalty that standard recursive models impose against candidate referents with unmentioned distinguishing features. This bounded inference naturally explains why listeners maintain substantial credence for overspecified competitor objects across scalar reference games, while unprompted choices remain governed by visual salience.
