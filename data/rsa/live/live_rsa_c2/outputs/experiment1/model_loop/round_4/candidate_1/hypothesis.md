Listeners in reference games are cognitively heterogeneous, forming a population mixture of literal and pragmatic reasoners. A literal subpopulation resolves ambiguous referring expressions by selecting among matching candidate referents according to perceptual visual salience without computing counterfactual speaker alternatives, while a pragmatic subpopulation performs recursive social reasoning to preempt overspecified competitors. This population structure naturally sustains competitor choices in scalar contexts and reconciles human preference rates without requiring inflated lapse noise or distorted speaker rationality.
