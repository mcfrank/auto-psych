In rational speech act theory, literal listeners do not interpret expressions in a perceptual vacuum, but integrate linguistic semantics with common-ground perceptual priors over candidate referents. When deciding what to say, speakers anticipate this perceptual grounding, knowing that visually prominent, feature-rich objects already attract baseline listener attention and therefore do not require exclusive distinguishing labels to achieve successful reference. Depth-2 pragmatic listeners invert this grounded speaker, resolving the systematic overprediction of minimal target choices on scalar trials and aligning reference resolution with visual salience.
