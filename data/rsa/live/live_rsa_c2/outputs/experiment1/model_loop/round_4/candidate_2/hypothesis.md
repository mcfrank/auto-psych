In visual reference games, speakers are not compelled to utter an ambiguous expression when all available words fail to clearly identify the intended referent, but instead have an outside option to remain silent. When every true label for an object is ambiguous, the speaker frequently withholds speech rather than communicating unreliably; conversely, when a distinguishing feature is present, the speaker readily speaks. Depth-2 pragmatic listeners invert this strategic silence speaker, reasoning that hearing an ambiguous label is weaker evidence for a minimally featured target because that speaker would often remain silent, naturally softening recursive preemption penalties against competitor objects.
