People interpret referring expressions under soft truth semantics, reasoning that linguistic features are subject to baseline semantic noise and perceptual categorization error rather than rigid Boolean truth values. At the foundational literal level, words apply strongly to matching objects but retain a small baseline applicability to non-matching objects, leading pragmatic speakers to occasionally commit semantic slips and depth-2 listeners to maintain non-zero credence for false-feature objects. This softens recursive preemption penalties across displays of all sizes, naturally explaining why listeners sometimes select objects that lack the heard feature and choose overspecified competitors more often than standard RSA predicts.
