Listeners interpret referring expressions with word-to-feature lexical uncertainty: rather than binding a heard word rigidly to a single attribute, they reason that the speaker's word denotes its matching feature with high probability but may denote an alternative feature present in the visual scene with a baseline uncertainty rate. Depth-2 pragmatic listeners invert speakers who anticipate this lexical uncertainty, so candidate referents with multiple distinguishing attributes remain viable under alternative feature interpretations, softening recursive preemption penalties against overspecified competitors and naturally explaining low-rate false-feature choices. On uninformative trials without an informative word, default expectations remain governed by visual salience.
