When interpreting referring expressions, listeners are resource-limited reasoners whose pragmatic inference is constrained by the perceptual load of the visual scene. In simple displays with few items, low perceptual load leaves ample attentional bandwidth for counterfactual reasoning, producing sharp preemption of competitors. In visually crowded displays with more objects and feature conjunctions, high perceptual load depletes visual attention, systematically attenuating pragmatic inferential precision and softening preemption penalties against competitor referents.
