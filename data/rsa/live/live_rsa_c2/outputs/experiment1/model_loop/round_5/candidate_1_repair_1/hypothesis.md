When an intended referent cannot be uniquely identified in context because all applicable words are ambiguous or duplicated across objects, speakers fall back to choosing among true expressions with dampened informativeness rather than aggressively avoiding shared labels. Depth-2 pragmatic listeners invert this fallback speaker, recognizing that hearing an ambiguous word does not rule out unidentifiable or duplicated competitors. On uninformative trials without an informative word, choices follow visual referent salience.
