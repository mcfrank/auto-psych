When communicating about an object that cannot be uniquely named in context because all applicable words are ambiguous, speakers frequently take an outside option—such as withholding speech or using an unmodelled descriptive gesture—rather than uttering an unreliably ambiguous label, while readily speaking when an exclusive distinguishing feature is available. Pragmatic listeners invert this ambiguity-escaping speaker at depth 2, reasoning that hearing an ambiguous label is weaker evidence for a minimally featured target because that speaker would often take the outside option, naturally elevating choices for competitor referents that possess distinguishing features or redundant attributes. On uninformative trials with no informative word, choices follow visual referent salience.
