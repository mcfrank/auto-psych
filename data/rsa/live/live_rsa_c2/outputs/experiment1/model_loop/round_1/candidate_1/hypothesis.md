Speakers in reference games do not globally optimize communicative precision; rather, they are boundedly rational satisficers who evaluate candidate utterances against an aspiration threshold. Once a true referring expression meets the satisficing threshold of informativeness, it is considered acceptable, yielding diminishing marginal returns for further uniqueness and allowing ambiguous or redundant expressions to be produced for complex objects. Depth-2 listeners invert this satisficing speaker to interpret referring expressions, maintaining substantial credence for overspecified competitor referents.
