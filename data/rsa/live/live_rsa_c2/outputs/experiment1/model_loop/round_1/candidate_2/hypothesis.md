Listeners interpret referring expressions by modeling speakers who discount redundant communicative alternatives based on their contextual feature overlap across objects in the scene. Rather than treating each overlapping or co-extensive feature as an independent communicative competitor that multiplies the preemption penalty against shared words, a rational speaker groups correlated features into shared communicative strategies. This mechanism diverges most sharply from standard RSA on displays where an overspecified competitor possesses multiple redundant distinguishing features, preventing the artificial collapse of competitor probability and correctly predicting substantial competitor choices.
