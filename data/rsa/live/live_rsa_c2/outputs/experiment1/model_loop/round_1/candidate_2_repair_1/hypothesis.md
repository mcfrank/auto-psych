Speakers in reference games exhibit a contextual feature prevalence bias, producing labels that describe more objects in the visual scene more readily than rare, context-specific features. Pragmatic listeners invert this prevalence-biased speaker at depth 2, expecting speakers to use prevalent labels even when describing objects that possess more specific distinguishing attributes. This mechanism diverges most sharply from standard RSA on scalar reference games where a shared word is true of both a minimal target and an overspecified competitor, preventing the artificial collapse of competitor probability and correctly predicting substantial competitor choices.
