Speakers communicate under dual communicative goals: either to uniquely identify an individual referent in the visual scene, or to be informative about the referent's visual feature aspect. Under the feature aspect goal, single-word descriptors are evaluated by how exhaustively they describe the referent's visual features without omission, penalizing objects with excess unmentioned visual features relative to the minimal matching exemplar. A pragmatic listener maintains uncertainty over the speaker's communicative goal and jointly inverts the speaker across both referents and goals, naturally elevating minimal-feature targets on redundant-feature trials and suppressing intermediate competitors.
