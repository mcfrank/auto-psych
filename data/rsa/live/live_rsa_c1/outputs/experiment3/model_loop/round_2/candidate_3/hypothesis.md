Listeners interpret utterances under graded semantic applicability: rather than treating words as rigid binary filters, they recognize that a descriptor's truth-conditional applicability diminishes with an object's excess unmentioned visual features relative to the context's minimal matching exemplar. When hearing a descriptive label, foundational literal interpretation favors the minimal defining exemplar that possesses no extraneous features, while penalizing complex and intermediate competitors in proportion to their descriptive dilution. A rational pragmatic listener inverts a communicative speaker operating over this graded lexicon, directly suppressing intermediate competitor choices and elevating minimal-feature target selection on ambiguous and redundant-feature trials.
