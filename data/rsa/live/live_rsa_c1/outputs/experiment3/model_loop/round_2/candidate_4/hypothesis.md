In communicative reference, choosing an ambiguous descriptor when a more discriminative descriptor is available incurs a discriminative opportunity cost proportional to that deficit. Refining contrastive_extremity_noisy_speaker by incorporating the discriminative deficit component from discriminative_deficit_speaker: communicative speakers evaluate message informativeness through perceptual confusability decaying with visual feature distance, but penalize descriptors that rule out fewer scene competitors than the referent's most discriminative available descriptor. This discriminative deficit penalty directly curbs the model's overprediction of intermediate-feature competitors and boosts minimal-feature target choices on redundant-feature trials and across display sizes, while preserving visual endpoint extremity expectations and stochastic execution noise.
