Listeners evaluate candidate referents prior to communication through contextual atypicality, forming expectations based on an object's departure from the visual scene prototype established by the display's feature distribution. Because prototypical objects blend into the background as unremarkable norms, communicative attention favors atypical items that deviate from the scene average, with visual search prioritizing positive feature departures over bare feature absences. Pragmatic listeners invert a communicative speaker under this atypicality-grounded prior, directly explaining why intermediate-complexity objects are disfavored across uninformative mumble trials and ambiguous utterances.
