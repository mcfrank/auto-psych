Pragmatic listeners operate under bounded visual attention across the display, reasoning that communicative speakers must compete for an attentive listener's gaze whose visual attention is drawn asymmetrically to visual complexity extremes rather than distributed uniformly across candidate objects. Because communicative confusability between referents is scaled by the visual attention commanded by competing items in the scene, intermediate-complexity competitors suffer attentional compression and communicative discounting, mitigating their over-selection on ambiguous trials. On uninformative mumble trials where no descriptive word is spoken, choices directly follow this capacity-limited visual attention distribution across the display.
