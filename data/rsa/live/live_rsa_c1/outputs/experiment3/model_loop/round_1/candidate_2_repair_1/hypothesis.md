In communicative reference games, speakers evaluate utterances through Gricean specificity deficit: choosing an under-informative descriptor when a more specific, distinguishing feature is available incurs a communicative penalty proportional to the loss in specificity. Pragmatic listeners invert this specificity-sensitive speaker, inferring that an ambiguous descriptor strongly implicates an intended referent that lacks any more specific alternative, rather than a competitor that possessed a more discriminating descriptive option. This mechanism disagrees most sharply with standard RSA and similarity-contrast models on displays with redundant features and intermediate competitors (such as a shared word matching one-feature, two-feature, and three-feature objects), where penalizing under-specific alternatives sharply suppresses intermediate competitor choices and elevates minimal-target preference.
