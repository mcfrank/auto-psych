Speakers evaluate candidate descriptors through relative discriminative deficit: when referring to an object, choosing a descriptor that rules out fewer scene competitors than the referent's most discriminative available descriptor incurs an opportunity cost proportional to that deficit. Pragmatic listeners invert this efficiency-seeking speaker, recognizing that a speaker describing a multi-featured competitor avoids ambiguous shared descriptors when a more discriminative descriptor is available. This mechanism sharpens pragmatic inferences on ambiguous trials with redundant features and across display sizes, while dampening choices of intermediate-complexity competitors.
