Listeners in visual reference games are cognitively heterogeneous, dividing into contrastive and exhaustive communicative listener types. Contrastive listeners assume speakers aim for referential discrimination and invert an informative speaker who minimizes perceptual confusability across scene competitors, whereas exhaustive listeners assume speakers aim for feature characterization and invert an informative speaker who penalizes excess unmentioned features relative to minimal matching exemplars. Observed choices reflect a population mixture of these two listener types, directly elevating minimal-feature target selection on redundant-feature trials and curbing intermediate competitor choices across display sizes.
