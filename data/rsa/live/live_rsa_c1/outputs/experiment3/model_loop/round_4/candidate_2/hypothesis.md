Speakers and listeners communicate by evaluating referring expressions through mutual validity—the joint product of cue validity (how specifically a descriptor isolates the referent amidst perceptual confusability from scene competitors) and category validity (how exhaustively the descriptor characterizes the referent's visual profile without omitting defining visual features). Pragmatic listeners invert this mutual-validity speaker under stochastic articulation noise, naturally favoring minimal-feature targets that maximize category validity, penalizing intermediate competitors whose extraneous features dilute descriptive exhaustivity, and scaling referential choices across display sizes.
