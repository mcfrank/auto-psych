In communicative reference, speakers pursuing a referent identification goal incur a discriminative opportunity cost when choosing an under-informative descriptor if a more discriminating descriptor is available for that referent in the visual scene, while speakers pursuing a feature aspect goal evaluate descriptors by how exhaustively they describe the referent's visual features without omission. Refining feature_aspect_qud_listener by incorporating the discriminative deficit component from discriminative_deficit_speaker: a pragmatic listener maintains uncertainty over whether the speaker aims for discriminative referent identification or feature aspect description, naturally elevating minimal-feature targets on redundant-feature trials and improving target choice scaling across display sizes, while curbing intermediate competitor choices.
