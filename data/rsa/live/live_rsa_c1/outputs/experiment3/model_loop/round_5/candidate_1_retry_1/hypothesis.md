In communicative reference games, speakers evaluate referring expressions through contrastive competitor elimination: choosing an ambiguous descriptor incurs an opportunity cost whenever the speaker could have chosen an alternative descriptor that eliminated a confusable scene competitor. Pragmatic listeners invert this elimination-seeking speaker under stochastic articulation noise and visual scene expectations, inferring that hearing an ambiguous descriptor strongly points toward a candidate referent that lacked any alternative descriptor capable of eliminating its competitors. This contrastive elimination mechanism naturally suppresses intermediate-feature competitors, elevates minimal-feature target selection on redundant-feature trials, and properly scales pragmatic target choices from two-object to four-object scenes.
