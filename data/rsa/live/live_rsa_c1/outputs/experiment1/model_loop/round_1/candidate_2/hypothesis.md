Speakers communicate voluntarily and consider an outside alternative of remaining silent when every available descriptor is ambiguous or uninformative. Listeners invert this voluntary speaker, inferring that hearing an ambiguous word does not compel a minimally-featured referent because that speaker could have remained silent. This mechanism disagrees most sharply with standard RSA on displays where an utterance applies to all objects or where a large feature gap exists, preventing listeners from overwhelmingly concentrating probability on the minimal-feature object.
