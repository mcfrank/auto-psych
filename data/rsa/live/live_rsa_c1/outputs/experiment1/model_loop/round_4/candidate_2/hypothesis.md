Speakers choose utterances through a nested decision process that groups functionally substitute descriptors into communicative tiers rather than treating every co-extensional word as an independent alternative. Consequently, having multiple distinguishing descriptors distributes the speaker's choice among those words without multiplying the overall drive to avoid an applicable shared descriptor. Pragmatic listeners invert this nested speaker, maintaining moderate expectations for multi-featured competitors when hearing an ambiguous word rather than excessively ruling them out.
