In communicative reference, speakers evaluate potential utterances not against an undifferentiated background of all scene objects, but through perceptual confusability: communicative interference decays with visual feature distance, so sharing a descriptor with a distant, visually distinct competitor incurs minimal penalty compared to sharing it with an adjacent neighbor. A rational pragmatic listener inverts this similarity-contrast speaker, recognizing that a speaker describing a multi-featured referent freely uses a shared descriptor when the competing matching object is distant in feature space. This mechanism disagrees most sharply with standard RSA on displays with large feature gaps between a minimal target and a multi-featured competitor (such as hearing a shared feature for a one-feature object competing against a four-feature object), preventing listeners from overwhelmingly eliminating the complex competitor.
