Listeners have limited visual attention across the display, so even foundational literal interpretation is biased toward visually salient objects rather than uniform across truth-conditional matches. Communicative speakers anticipate an attentive listener whose gaze is naturally drawn to visually distinctive and feature-rich objects, making speakers more willing to use shared descriptors for complex referents. Pragmatic listeners in turn do not excessively eliminate feature-rich competitor objects when hearing an ambiguous utterance.
