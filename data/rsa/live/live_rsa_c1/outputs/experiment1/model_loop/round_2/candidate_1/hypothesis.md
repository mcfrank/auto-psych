Listeners expect speakers to talk about objects that afford high communicative success, establishing an internal prior over referents proportional to how easily and discriminatively each object can be identified using the available vocabulary. In the absence of an informative utterance, choices favor feature-rich or distinctively marked objects that offer clear descriptive pathways over featureless or redundantly identical competitors that cannot be effectively communicated. Pragmatic listeners invert this affordance-grounded speaker upon hearing an utterance, balancing message informativeness against the prior communicative viability of each candidate referent.
