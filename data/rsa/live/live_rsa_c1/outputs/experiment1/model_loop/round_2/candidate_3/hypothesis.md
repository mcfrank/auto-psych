Listeners interpret utterances under lexical grounding uncertainty: rather than assuming a rigid, deterministic mapping between spoken words and visual feature dimensions, they maintain uncertainty about which feature dimension a word picks out in the visual scene. A rational speaker chooses an informative feature true of the referent, but the listener interprets the heard word through an uncertain grounding channel that permits the word to denote an alternative visual feature. This lexical grounding uncertainty prevents listeners from categorically ruling out complex competitor objects when hearing an ambiguous word, dampening over-confident target inferences in displays with large feature gaps or duplicated competitors.
