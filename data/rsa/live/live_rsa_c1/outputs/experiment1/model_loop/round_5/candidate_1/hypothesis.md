In communicative reference, candidate descriptors for a visually complex referent compete for lexical retrieval under feature crowding, where mutual inhibitory interference scales with the referent's total visual feature load. Descriptors that are contextually primed by appearing across multiple objects in the display resist this competitive inhibition, whereas idiosyncratic features unique to an individual object suffer retrieval interference as feature load grows. A rational pragmatic listener inverts this interference-burdened speaker, recognizing that a speaker describing a multi-featured referent frequently produces an accessible shared descriptor rather than retrieving crowded idiosyncratic alternatives.
