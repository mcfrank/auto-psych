Listeners evaluate candidate referents before hearing an utterance through endpoint extremity and feature-search asymmetry: visual attention and communicative expectations are anchored by the extreme endpoints of the complexity continuum—the maximally elaborated, feature-rich focal exemplar and the bare baseline contrast—while intermediate objects suffer perceptual compression. Furthermore, unique objects capture pre-attentive pop-out only when they possess visual features, ensuring unadorned baseline objects do not pop out merely because they lack duplicates. A pragmatic listener inverts a communicative speaker governed by these endpoint expectations, which cushions pragmatic likelihood ratios when hearing a shared descriptor and prevents excessive elimination of feature-rich competitors.
