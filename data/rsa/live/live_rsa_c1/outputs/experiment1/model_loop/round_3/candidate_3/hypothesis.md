Listeners understand that speakers are not always trying to uniquely identify an individual object in the display, but frequently communicate with the goal of highlighting or conveying an aspect of the referent (a feature-oriented question under discussion). Under an aspect-descriptive goal, speakers freely choose among the referent's true features without penalizing descriptors that are shared across other objects. A pragmatic listener maintains uncertainty over whether the speaker aims for referent identification or aspect description, preventing extreme elimination of multi-feature and duplicated competitor objects when hearing an ambiguous word.
