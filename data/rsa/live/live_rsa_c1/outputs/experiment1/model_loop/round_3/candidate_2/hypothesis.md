Listeners evaluate candidate referents through pragmatic counterfactual reasoning, but rather than strictly matching choice probabilities to posterior beliefs, they select their click via a bounded-precision softmax decision rule over beliefs. When choosing among semantically valid referents, response sensitivity determines how strongly differences in subjective belief translate into click probabilities, softening extreme pragmatic inferences and generating bounded, probabilistic choice. On uninformative trials, choices likewise reflect this bounded softmax response rule applied over prior perceptual and contextual expectations.
