Listeners interpret referring expressions under uncertainty about the speaker's communicative goal, reasoning that the speaker communicates either to identify a specific referent or to be informative about the referent's visual elaboration category. When aiming to communicate elaboration category, the speaker selects descriptors that maximize the listener's recovery of the referent's complexity tier, freely sharing descriptors across objects within the same complexity class while avoiding descriptors that cross complexity boundaries. Pragmatic listeners jointly infer the intended referent and the speaker's communicative goal upon hearing an utterance, while uninformative mumble trials reveal prior expectations anchored at the scene's complexity extremes.
