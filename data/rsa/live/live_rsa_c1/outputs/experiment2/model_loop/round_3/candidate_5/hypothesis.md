Speakers prioritize uniquely identifying descriptors that single out an intended referent, which pragmatically preempts the use of an ambiguous shared descriptor whenever a distinguishing feature is available. Pragmatic listeners invert this preemption-sensitive speaker under perceptual similarity-contrast and endpoint extremity, recognizing that hearing an ambiguous descriptor strongly implies the speaker intended a referent lacking any unique alternative label rather than a competitor for which a distinguishing feature could have been produced. This preemption mechanism directly resolves the incumbent model's underprediction of minimal-feature targets on redundant-feature trials and overprediction of complex competitors across large feature gaps, while preserving similarity-contrast reasoning and stochastic transmission noise.
