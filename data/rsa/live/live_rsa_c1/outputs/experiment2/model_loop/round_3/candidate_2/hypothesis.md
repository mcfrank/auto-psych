Referential choice execution is governed by visual search load: the probability of an attentional lapse or motor misselection scales with the number of visual distractors in the display rather than remaining constant across display sizes. In simple two-object displays without distractors, focused attention prevents non-pragmatic errors, while in larger displays each additional distractor increases the likelihood that visual attention slips into unconstrained guessing. This load-dependent decision rule directly explains why non-matching foil errors double in four-object displays and why pragmatic target choices scale positively across display sizes.
