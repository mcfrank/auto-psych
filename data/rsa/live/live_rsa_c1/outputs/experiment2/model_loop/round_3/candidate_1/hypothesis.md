In communicative reference, sharing an applicable descriptor with a competitor always incurs an irreducible baseline semantic interference that cannot be fully eliminated by perceptual feature distance. Communicative speakers evaluate message informativeness through perceptual confusability decaying with visual feature distance, but anchor competitor interference at a non-zero semantic baseline reflecting literal multiplicity in the visual scene. A rational pragmatic listener inverts this speaker, preventing the over-attribution of ambiguous shared descriptors to complex competitor objects across large visual feature gaps.
