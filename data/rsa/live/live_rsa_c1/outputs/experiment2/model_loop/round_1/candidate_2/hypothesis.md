Speakers evaluate communicative interference from competing objects based on competitor reliance: a competitor that possesses few alternative descriptive pathways relies heavily on the shared descriptor and exerts severe communicative interference, whereas a competitor with multiple alternative descriptors poses minimal threat of misidentification. A rational pragmatic listener inverts this reliance-sensitive speaker, recognizing that hearing an ambiguous descriptor strongly points toward the candidate referent that lacks alternative descriptive options. This mechanism disagrees most sharply with contrastive similarity models on displays with large feature gaps between a minimal target and a complex competitor, correctly preventing listeners from over-selecting the complex competitor.
