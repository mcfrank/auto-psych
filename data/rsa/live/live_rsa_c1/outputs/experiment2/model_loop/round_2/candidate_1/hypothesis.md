In communicative reference, listeners expect speakers to choose referents according to figural topic prominence, where communicative expectations and visual attention are grounded in visual feature presence rather than bare scene existence. Unadorned baseline objects serve as the perceptual ground rather than informative topics, so visual pop-out applies strictly to feature-bearing figures that possess distinguishing visual features. Furthermore, topic expectations scale with visual feature elaboration, concentrating communicative focus on the focal full exemplar and the baseline contrast, while intermediate-complexity objects suffer perceptual compression.
