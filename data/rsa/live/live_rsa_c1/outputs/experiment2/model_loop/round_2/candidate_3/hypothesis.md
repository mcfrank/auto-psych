Listeners interpret utterances under noisy truth-conditional semantics, reasoning that words do not function as rigid boolean filters but have soft, probabilistic applicability across objects. Even when a descriptor is literally absent from an object, there is a small baseline semantic compatibility that permits the word to denote non-matching referents. A rational pragmatic listener inverts a speaker operating under these soft truth values, naturally accounting for non-matching foil selections and tempering extreme pragmatic inferences across displays of varying sizes.
