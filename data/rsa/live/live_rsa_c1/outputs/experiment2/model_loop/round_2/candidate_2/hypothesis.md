When interpreting an informative word, listeners with bounded attention restrict their referential domain to the subset of candidate objects that satisfy the spoken descriptor. Rather than evaluating communicative contrast against the entire visual display—where non-matching background distractors dilute the speaker's alternative descriptive options—the resource-limited listener evaluates informativeness locally within this attended candidate set, with residual attention leaking to distractors in larger displays. In the absence of an informative descriptor on mumble trials, visual attention is unconstrained across the scene, and choices directly reflect perceptual prior expectations.
