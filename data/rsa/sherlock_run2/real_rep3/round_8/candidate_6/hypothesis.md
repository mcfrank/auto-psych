We refine crowding_discriminative_chromatic_l2 by incorporating competitor confusion aversion (taking the competitor confusion component from competitor_confusion_speaker) into the simulated speakers' communicative utility: speakers actively penalize referring expressions that are shared with visually confusable competitors in the context, in addition to incurring a communicative cost for ambiguous words when a uniquely distinguishing alternative was available. Pragmatic listeners invert this cost- and confusion-sensitive speaker across recursive reasoning levels, resolving ambiguous words and scalar implicatures while expecting speakers to choose distinguishing words when distractors are visually similar and colorful. Compared to crowding_discriminative_chromatic_l2, recursion depth remains at L2 (choice_probs calls L2), one parameter (w_confusion) is added, no parameters are removed (retaining alpha, cost, w_singleton, w_crowd, w_color, and lapse), the shared referent prior is unchanged, and the speaker choice utility in S1 and S2 subtracts the competitor confusion penalty alongside the discriminative alternative cost.
