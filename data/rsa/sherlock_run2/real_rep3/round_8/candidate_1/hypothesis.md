The population of listeners consists of two distinct cognitive types: pragmatic listeners who resolve referring expressions via recursive Theory-of-Mind reasoning about speaker informativeness, and visual perceptual listeners who select candidate referents that escape attentional crowding from shared feature overlap with other display items. Observed choices reflect a mixture over these two listener types, where pragmatic listeners resolve scalar implicatures through communicative reasoning while perceptual listeners bias reference resolution and uninformative prior displays toward referents that avoid visual crowding.
