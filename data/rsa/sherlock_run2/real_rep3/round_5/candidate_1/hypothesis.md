Speakers avoid choosing referring expressions that are shared with visually similar or confusable distractors in the display, penalizing candidate words by the degree of feature overlap with alternative referents that also satisfy the expression. Pragmatic listeners invert this confusion-averse speaker, expecting speakers to use ambiguous words only when distractors are visually distinct rather than near-identical clones. This disagrees sharply with depth-2 attentional crowding models on displays where an object has high overall visual crowding but possesses an unshared feature: the crowding model penalizes the object globally across all expressions, whereas the competitor-confusion speaker incurs zero penalty when producing the unshared word.
