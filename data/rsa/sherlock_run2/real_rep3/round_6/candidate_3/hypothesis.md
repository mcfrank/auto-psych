Listeners evaluate candidate referents along a unified dimension of visual elaboration, where both discrete features and chromatic color contribute positive ornamentation. The speaker's communicative framing sets an evaluative stance toward this elaboration: in neutral communication, listeners assume a default positive preference for ornamented referents; an explicit positive framing ('favorite') amplifies this preference, whereas a negative framing ('least favorite') inverts it toward plain, feature-sparse, and desaturated referents. Pragmatic listeners invert a speaker whose referential expectations reflect this evaluative utility, explaining both prior preferences and language-driven choices across framing conditions.
