Listeners interpret referring expressions using a non-Bayesian lexical preemption heuristic rather than recursive Theory of Mind. When hearing a word that applies to multiple referents, listeners choose among candidate objects possessing that feature by penalizing candidates that possess unmentioned distinguishing features that could have uniquely identified them in the context, favoring referents that lack an exclusive alternative label. On uninformative trials without an informative word, listeners choose uniformly among available objects.
