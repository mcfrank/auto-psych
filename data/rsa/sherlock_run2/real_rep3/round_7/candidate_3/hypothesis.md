Listeners interpret referring expressions by modeling speakers as having a communicative goal targeting a distinctive aspect of the intended referent rather than transmitting its token identity, choosing words to inform the listener about that aspect. Pragmatic listeners invert this generative process, jointly inferring the speaker's communicative goal and the intended referent upon hearing an utterance. On uninformative trials without a word, listeners expect speakers to refer to objects that afford distinctive communicative goals in the visual context.
