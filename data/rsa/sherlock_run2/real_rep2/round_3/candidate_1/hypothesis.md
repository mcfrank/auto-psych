People interpret referring expressions by attributing to the speaker a valence-aligned referent preference: speakers preferentially refer to objects with greater visual desirability—defined by feature richness and color—under positive or unmarked framing, but invert this preference under negative framing to refer to plain, unadorned objects. When an informative word is heard, listeners combine this evaluative prior with pragmatic reasoning about alternative descriptions, whereas on uninformative prior trials choices directly reflect the speaker's valence-aligned preference.
