Listeners interpret referring expressions through depth-2 pragmatic reasoning about speakers who evaluate graded semantic applicability while favoring referents that exhibit perceptual isolation from display distractors. Under perceptual isolation, visual pop-out is determined by an object's minimum feature distance to any other context object (nearest-neighbor contrast), so that singletons pop out against identical or near-identical distractors while objects on balanced multi-feature displays maintain equal prior salience. At the same time, graded semantic applicability penalizes candidate referents whose features are diluted by competing visual attributes, preventing the over-selection of cluttered items. On uninformative prior trials, listeners' expectations follow this perceptual isolation prior.

This model refines `distinctive_graded_l2` with a single change: replacing mean pairwise Hamming distance with perceptual isolation (minimum Hamming distance to another object in the display). While mean distance mistakenly penalizes target referents on balanced multi-feature displays whose items differ across multiple dimensions, nearest-neighbor isolation preserves singleton pop-out in twin displays while allowing depth-2 pragmatic implicatures and graded semantic applicability to operate cleanly on complex displays.
Differences from `distinctive_graded_l2`:
- Recursion depth: Remains 2 (`choice_probs` calls `L2`).
- Parameters added or removed: None (retains `alpha`, `w_distinct`, `gamma`, and `lapse`).
- Other terms: Computes object distinctiveness as minimum Hamming distance to any other context item (`jnp.min(pair_dist + eye, axis=1)`) rather than mean pairwise distance (`jnp.sum(pair_dist, axis=1) / denom`), where `eye = jnp.eye(n_obj) * 1e5` masks self-distance.
