Listeners interpret referring expressions using a non-Bayesian parsimony heuristic: rather than reasoning recursively about counterfactual utterances a speaker might have produced, they directly select among candidate objects matching the heard word, penalizing referents with extraneous unmentioned features. When hearing a word, listeners choose the candidate object with the fewest total features, treating an entity that possesses only the named attribute as the most parsimonious referent.
