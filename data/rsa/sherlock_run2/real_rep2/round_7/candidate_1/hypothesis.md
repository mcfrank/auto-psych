Listeners interpret referring expressions using a non-Bayesian perceptual isolation heuristic: rather than reasoning recursively about counterfactual utterances a speaker might have produced, they directly select among candidate objects that literally match the heard word, favoring referents that exhibit higher nearest-neighbor visual contrast (perceptual isolation) from distractors. When hearing an ambiguous word, listeners choose the matching candidate that is most isolated from display distractors, while on uninformative prior trials choices directly reflect this perceptual isolation distribution.
