Listeners interpret referring expressions through depth-4 pragmatic reasoning that jointly accounts for perceptual isolation, contextual feature surprisal, graded semantic applicability, and utterance extension costs. When resolving an ambiguous referring expression, listeners invert a speaker who anticipates a depth-3 pragmatic listener's interpretation, expecting speakers to refer preferentially to objects that exhibit nearest-neighbor visual contrast and rare distinctive features while penalizing feature dilution and overextended descriptions. On uninformative prior trials, listeners' expectations directly follow the integrated prior of perceptual isolation and feature surprisal.

This model refines `isolated_graded_costly_l2_2` with a single change: increasing the recursion depth from depth 2 to depth 4. While lower-order pragmatic reasoning successfully resolves basic scalar implicatures, complex multi-object displays with competing visual features, feature surprisal, and overextended descriptions require higher-order recursive coordination, allowing listeners to invert speakers who anticipate how sophisticated pragmatic listeners resolve embedded contextual alternatives.

Differences from `isolated_graded_costly_l2_2`:
- Recursion depth: `choice_probs` calls `L4` instead of `L2`.
- Parameters added or removed: None (retains `alpha`, `w_distinct`, `w_surprisal`, `gamma`, `cost_weight`, and `lapse`).
- Other terms: Adds the level-3 and level-4 pragmatic listeners `L3[u, r]` and `L4[u, r]`, where the level-4 listener inverts a level-4 speaker `S4` who evaluates communicative utility against `L3[u, r]` with utterance extension costs and perceptual isolation, while `L0`, `L1`, `L2`, `object_isolation`, `object_feature_surprisal`, `compute_graded_lex`, utterance extension costs, and the lapse mixture remain identical.
