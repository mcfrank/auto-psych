Listeners interpret referring expressions by attributing to the speaker an aspect-oriented communicative goal: rather than aiming to uniquely pinpoint the referent's complete identity, the speaker selects an utterance to inform the listener about a specific visual aspect or property of the target object. When an ambiguous referring expression is heard, the listener performs joint pragmatic inference over the speaker's latent communicative goal and intended referent, inferring both which visual aspect the speaker sought to convey and which object possesses that attribute. On uninformative prior trials, listeners guess uniformly among available objects.
