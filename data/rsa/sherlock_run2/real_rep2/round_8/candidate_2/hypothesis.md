Listeners interpret referring expressions through associative cue competition rather than recursive mental simulation: visual features of objects in the display compete as associative cues predicting candidate referents according to the Rescorla-Wagner learning equilibrium. When a referent possesses multiple attributes, unique diagnostic features overshadow and block shared features from predicting that entity, so hearing a shared word selectively retrieves the referent where that feature is unblocked by competing cues. On uninformative prior trials without an uttered word, listeners guess uniformly among available objects.
