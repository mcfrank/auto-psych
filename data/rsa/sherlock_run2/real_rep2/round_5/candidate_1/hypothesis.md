People interpret referring expressions by modeling a speaker who maximizes the Pointwise Mutual Information between candidate utterances and referents, choosing words that are strongly associated with the intended target relative to their baseline prevalence across the visual display. This mechanism disagrees most sharply with the current best model on displays featuring duplicate twin objects and a singleton when a feature shared across all three items is named: while the best model's perceptual isolation prior boosts the singleton to over 50% choice probability, mutual information predicts listeners will disfavor the singleton and select the twins (under 25% for the singleton), because a speaker intending the singleton had an exclusively unique descriptor available that provided far greater mutual information than the ambiguous word.
