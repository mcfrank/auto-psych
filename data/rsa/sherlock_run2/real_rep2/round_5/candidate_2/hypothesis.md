Speakers evaluate candidate utterances by balancing communicative informativeness against the opportunity cost (regret) relative to the most informative description available for the target referent. When choosing among true words, speakers incur a cost proportional to how much less informative the chosen word is compared to the target's best available alternative, so that ambiguous descriptions are heavily penalized for referents that possess an unambiguous label, but incur no penalty for referents that lack any better description. Pragmatic listeners invert this regret-sensitive speaker to resolve ambiguous referring expressions.
