Listeners interpret referring expressions by assuming speakers preferentially refer to objects that carry high contextual information content, assigning prior probability to candidate referents in proportion to the cumulative surprisal of their visual features (the contextual rarity of each feature in the display). When resolving an ambiguous referring expression, listeners invert a speaker whose referent choice is driven by this feature surprisal prior through recursive pragmatic reasoning. On uninformative prior trials without an informative word, listeners' expectations directly reflect this information-theoretic salience.
