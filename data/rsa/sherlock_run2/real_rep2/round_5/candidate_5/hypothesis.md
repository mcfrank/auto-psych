Listeners interpret referring expressions through depth-2 pragmatic reasoning that jointly weights perceptual isolation, graded semantic applicability, utterance extension costs, and prior familiarization experience. When resolving an ambiguous word, listeners invert a speaker who anticipates a pragmatic listener while expecting speakers to favor candidate referents that have higher nearest-neighbor perceptual contrast and higher prior familiarization base rates, discount candidate referents whose features are diluted by competing visual attributes, and evaluate communicative trade-offs against the contextual extension of shared features. On uninformative prior trials, listeners' expectations directly follow this integrated prior of perceptual isolation and familiarization history.

This model refines `isolated_graded_costly_l2` by incorporating familiarization base-rate sensitivity from `familiar_isolated_graded_l2` into the shared referent prior. While perceptual isolation captures visual pop-out, graded semantics discounts cluttered objects, and utterance extension costs prevent over-penalizing shared descriptions, participants also rely on exposure frequency when resolving ambiguous references and predicting referent choices a priori.

Differences from `isolated_graded_costly_l2`:
- Recursion depth: Remains 2 (`choice_probs` calls `L2`).
- Parameters added: `w_familiar` with prior `dist.Normal(0.0, 2.0)`. Parameters removed: None (retains `alpha`, `w_distinct`, `gamma`, `cost_weight`, and `lapse`).
- Other terms: In `choice_probs`, the object prior integrates familiarization base rates alongside perceptual isolation: `prior = softmax_prior(params["w_distinct"] * dist_vec + params["w_familiar"] * ctx.familiarization)`. All other components (`L0`, `L1`, `L2`, `object_isolation`, `compute_graded_lex`, utterance extension costs, and the lapse mixture) remain identical.
