People interpret referring expressions by modeling a speaker whose communicative goal is to convey an object's constituent visual aspects rather than uniquely pinpoint its identity. The speaker evaluates candidate words by the expected aspect agreement achieved under the listener's interpretation, preferentially choosing descriptions that accurately communicate the target's visual features even when candidates share overlapping attributes. Pragmatic listeners invert this aspect-oriented speaker through recursive pragmatic reasoning, capturing both scalar implicatures and shared-feature compatibility.
