Speakers evaluate candidate utterances by contrastive distractor elimination weighted by perceptual similarity: an utterance's communicative utility reflects the proportion of distractors it rules out, with each distractor weighted by its perceptual similarity to the intended target under Shepard distance decay. Because eliminating confusable, near-neighbor distractors is more critical than ruling out items that are already distinct on other dimensions, speakers prioritize features that differentiate the target from its most similar competitors. Listeners interpret referring expressions by inverting this similarity-weighted contrastive speaker, while guessing uniformly on uninformative prior trials.
