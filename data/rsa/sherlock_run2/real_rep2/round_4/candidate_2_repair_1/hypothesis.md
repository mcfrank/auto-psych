The participant population consists of a mixture of two distinct listener types: perceptual listeners who choose literally matching referents biased by visual pop-out and perceptual isolation, and pragmatic listeners who resolve ambiguous descriptions through counterfactual reasoning about alternative utterances. When interpreting a referring expression, aggregate choices reflect the latent proportions of these two cognitive strategies across the population, while on uninformative prior trials choices follow perceptual pop-out salience.
