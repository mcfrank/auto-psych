Listeners operate under limited visual attention across feature dimensions: hearing a referring expression directs the listener's attentional spotlight to the named visual attribute, causing an attentional bottleneck that attenuates awareness of unmentioned alternative features. When simulating what the speaker could have said, the listener perceives unmentioned alternative descriptions with reduced attentional availability, discounting the speaker's communicative pressure to use alternative words. On uninformative prior trials without an uttered word, visual attention is distributed uniformly across all features.
