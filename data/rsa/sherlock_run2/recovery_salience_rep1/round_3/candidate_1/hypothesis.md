Listeners infer the speaker's intended referent by modeling a speaker whose object choices are guided by referential prominence across visual and linguistic framing. An object's prominence increases when it stands out in visual color against grayscale competitors, while its feature complexity is evaluated relative to the communicative frame—favoring simpler, prototypical objects under neutral framing, but shifting toward multi-feature objects under negative evaluative framing ("least favorite"). When no informative utterance is heard, listeners choose directly according to this referential prominence prior.
