Listeners have limited visual attention across the display, selectively focusing on candidate referents compatible with the heard utterance while discounting unattended distractors in the visual background. When evaluating the speaker's communicative alternatives, the listener computes informativeness primarily over the attended candidates, so non-matching distractors exert attenuated influence on pragmatic inference. In the absence of an informative utterance, the listener allocates attention uniformly across the available objects.
