Listeners maintain an inductive prior over referents grounded in contextual feature distinctiveness, expecting communicators to target objects whose features are rare or unique within the display rather than shared across many items. Rather than evaluating features in isolation or assuming an equiprobable prior over referents, listeners weight candidate referents by their contextual distinctiveness relative to the surrounding scene. When interpreting an utterance or choosing in the absence of an informative message, listeners integrate this contextual distinctiveness prior with pragmatic speaker reasoning.
