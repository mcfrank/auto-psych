Listeners interpret referring expressions through referential contrast sets rather than recursive Theory-of-Mind mental simulation: when hearing a descriptive word, candidate referents that form a minimal contrastive pair with an object lacking that feature are favored, because the utterance serves a contextual contrastive function to distinguish that referent from its counterpart. On uninformative trials with no descriptive word, listeners guess uniformly among the objects. This mechanism disagrees most sharply with depth-2 RSA on displays containing a two-feature object, a one-feature object differing by the uttered word, and an isolated one-feature competitor matching the word: depth-2 RSA predicts a strong scalar implicature preference for the isolated competitor, whereas the contrast set listener favors the two-feature object because it forms a minimal contrastive pair with the distractor.
