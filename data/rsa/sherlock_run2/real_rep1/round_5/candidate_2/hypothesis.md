When choosing an utterance, speakers actively avoid communicative collision with competing referents in the visual context, penalizing a descriptive word in proportion to how dependent the competitor objects are on that same word given their own available feature alternatives. Pragmatic listeners invert this collision-sensitive speaker, reasoning that hearing an ambiguous word implies the intended referent could not safely use its alternative descriptors. On uninformative trials without a descriptive word, listeners guess uniformly among candidate referents.
