Listeners interpret referring expressions through polarity congruence between the communicative framing and the visual display: visual features and chromatic color endow objects with positive visual polarity against sparse or desaturated distractors, while communicative framing establishes the evaluative polarity of the speaker's referential goal. When referring to a favorite object or under neutral framing with default communicative positivity, speakers target referents whose positive visual polarity matches the positive frame; when referring to a least favorite object, this preference inverts to target referents whose negative visual polarity matches the negative frame. Pragmatic listeners invert this polarity-congruent speaker to resolve ambiguous descriptions, and rely directly on polarity congruence when evaluating referents without an informative description.
