Listeners turn probabilistic pragmatic beliefs into behavioral choices via a softmax decision rule rather than passive probability matching. When interpreting an utterance, the listener infers posterior beliefs over referents through simple communicative reasoning and then chooses an object with probability proportional to the exponentiated belief that it is the intended target. This bounded-rational decision rule exponentially amplifies confidence toward the most likely referent while naturally explaining baseline decision noise toward non-matching distractors without assuming uniform guessing.
