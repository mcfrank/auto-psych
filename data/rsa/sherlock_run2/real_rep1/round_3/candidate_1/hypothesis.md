Listeners interpret referring expressions by attributing to the speaker an evaluative salience goal, where objects with more visual features and color possess greater baseline appeal, but the communicative framing determines the polarity of the speaker's preference. When referring to a favorite object or under neutral framing, speakers prefer appealing, feature-rich and colored objects; when referring to a least favorite object, this preference inverts to target sparse, featureless objects. Pragmatic listeners invert this valence-modulated speaker to resolve ambiguous descriptions, and rely directly on valence-modulated evaluative salience when choosing without an informative description.
