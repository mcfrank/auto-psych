Listeners in reference games differ in whether they resolve referential expressions through communicative mental simulation or visual salience heuristics: one subpopulation acts as recursive depth-2 pragmatic listeners who reason about speaker informativeness, while another subpopulation acts as perceptual heuristic listeners who choose among matching referents in proportion to their contextual visual distinctiveness relative to the display. On uninformative trials without a descriptive word, communicative listeners guess uniformly while perceptual heuristic listeners choose the most visually distinctive object. Observed population choices reflect a latent mixture over these two distinct listener types.
