When interpreting a referring expression, listeners reason that the speaker is informative about an underlying question under discussion concerning a visual feature rather than the unique identity of the referent object. The speaker chooses an utterance to maximize the listener's probability of correctly answering that feature question, and the pragmatic listener jointly infers which feature question the speaker intended to address and which referent object was meant. On displays where a description is shared, this question-under-discussion reasoning resolves referential ambiguity by penalizing candidate objects whose alternative features would have prompted different communicative questions.
