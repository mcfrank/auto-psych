When interpreting referring expressions in multi-object scenes, listeners reason recursively about speakers whose communicative goal is contrastive: rather than communicating to identify an object globally across the entire scene, the speaker aims to eliminate a specific competing foil. At each level of communicative reasoning, the speaker chooses an utterance to resolve this pairwise contrast by distinguishing the intended referent from the selected foil. Pragmatic listeners infer the intended referent by marginalizing over the latent competing foils that motivated the speaker's contrastive goal.
