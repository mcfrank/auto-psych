When interpreting a referring expression, listeners have limited visual attention to the display: attention is concentrated on the candidate objects matching the heard description, while non-matching distractor objects receive attenuated attention. In evaluating how effectively alternative expressions would describe candidate objects, the listener reasons about a speaker communicating over this attention-weighted display rather than the full display. Consequently, competitor objects whose alternative descriptions are shared with non-matching distractors are perceived as more viable than full-attention models predict, because the distractors that undermine those alternatives are partially unattended.
