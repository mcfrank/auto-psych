The participant population is structured as a discrete mixture of two distinct cognitive listener types: literal semantic listeners who treat all candidate referents satisfying the heard word as equally plausible, and parsimonious listeners who select among matching referents by penalizing feature complexity according to descriptive parsimony. Observed choices on communicative trials reflect a population mixture over these two types parameterized by the proportion of parsimonious listeners, explaining why aggregate implicature rates remain bounded near chance across diverse displays. On prior trials with no informative word, all listeners choose uniformly among candidate objects, modulated only by familiarization base rates.
