The participant population is structured as a mixture of two distinct cognitive types: literal listeners who choose among truthful referents according to contextual and visual salience, and pragmatic listeners who invert a cost-sensitive communicative speaker to derive scalar implicatures. On trials with an informative utterance, observed choices reflect a population mixture over these two types parameterized by the proportion of pragmatic listeners. On prior trials with no informative word, all listeners fall back to contextual object salience driven by feature richness and familiarization base rates.
