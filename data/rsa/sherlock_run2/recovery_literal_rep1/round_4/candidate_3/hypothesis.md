Listeners interpret referential expressions by penalizing candidate referents according to the residual surprisal of their unmentioned features. When evaluating objects that satisfy the heard word, listeners discount referents that possess unmentioned features carrying high contextual information content (rarity across the display), reasoning that an informative speaker would not omit highly diagnostic attributes. When no informative word is heard, choices are guided uniformly across candidate referents, modulated by prior familiarization expectations.
