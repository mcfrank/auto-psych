Listeners have bounded attentional resources to generate and reason over unmentioned alternative utterances when interpreting a speaker. When simulating the speaker's utterance choice, a resource-limited listener discounts unmentioned alternative expressions relative to the focal heard word, reducing the speaker's perceived likelihood of having chosen an alternative descriptor. Under limited attention to alternatives, the listener relies predominantly on literal semantics and contextual salience, attenuating the pragmatic pressure of scalar implicature.
