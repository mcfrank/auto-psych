Listeners operate with bounded attentional capacity: upon hearing an utterance, they focus visual attention strictly on the described feature, penalizing candidate referents that carry superfluous, unmentioned visual features that create visual clutter. Rather than recursively simulating what alternative words a speaker might have produced, the listener uses a bounded-depth decision rule that favors the referent that parsimoniously matches the utterance with minimal extraneous features. On prior trials with no informative word, choices are guided by baseline contextual salience without a feature clutter penalty.
