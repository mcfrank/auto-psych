Listeners expect speakers to refer to objects that are communicatively referable: before hearing an informative word, listeners assign higher prior probability to objects possessing uniquely distinguishing features, reasoning that a speaker constrained to a single word prefers to discuss targets that can be communicated unambiguously. In pragmatic communication, this communicative referability prior shapes the listener's model of the speaker's intended topic, counteracting ambiguous competition when multiple referents share a descriptor.
