Listeners interpret referential expressions by evaluating descriptive specificity under contextual framing: when an utterance is heard, listeners select among matching referents by balancing the exhaustivity of the description against the referent's perceptual and affective prominence. In neutral contexts, referents possessing unmentioned excess features are penalized because the speaker omitted distinguishing descriptors; under negative framing ('least favorite'), this penalty inverts because excess features constitute additional undesirable attributes. Visual prominence (rendering in color rather than grayscale) enhances referent appeal across all contexts, while prior trials with no informative word are governed by framed visual salience.
