Listeners interpret referential expressions by inverting a framed, boundedly rational speaker whose referential goals reflect contextual framing and whose word choices balance semantic truthfulness with descriptive accessibility. Under affective framing, the speaker's prior target reflects emotional valence, amplifying the appeal of feature-rich objects when describing a favorite and discounting complexity when describing a least favorite, while visual prominence renders colored objects more salient across all contexts. Bounded listeners infer the speaker's intended referent from these framed communicative expectations, relying directly on framed visual salience when no informative word is heard.
