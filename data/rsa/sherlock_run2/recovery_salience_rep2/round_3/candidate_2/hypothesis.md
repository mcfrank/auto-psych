Listeners resolve referential ambiguity through a direct alternative-feature heuristic rather than recursive Theory-of-Mind speaker simulation, penalizing candidate referents in proportion to the specificity of their other visual features. When an ambiguous word is heard, candidate objects that possess distinguishing alternative descriptors are discounted because an intentional speaker had clearer labels available to identify them. On uninformative baseline trials without an informative utterance, listeners choose uniformly among the available objects.
