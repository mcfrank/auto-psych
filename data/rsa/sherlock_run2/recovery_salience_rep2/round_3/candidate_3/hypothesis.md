Listeners do not simply probability-match their subjective beliefs when selecting a referent; instead, they convert posterior beliefs into choices via power-law probability matching under the generalized matching law. A decision-precision exponent governs how sharply beliefs translate into action, allowing listeners to over-match by disproportionately favoring the most probable candidate referent when communicative evidence is decisive. On uninformative baseline displays, choices default to a uniform guess across all candidate objects.
