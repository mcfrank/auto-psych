Listeners evaluate candidate referents through a contextual salience prior that unifies perceptual pop-out and evaluative framing into a shared referent expectation. Referents that are visually highlighted in full colour against grayscale competitors, or whose feature complexity matches the speaker's affective framing (feature-rich objects when describing a favorite, feature-sparse objects when describing a least favorite), receive higher prior probability during recursive speaker simulation. On neutral displays without colour contrast or evaluative framing, this contextual salience prior reduces to a uniform distribution over referents.
