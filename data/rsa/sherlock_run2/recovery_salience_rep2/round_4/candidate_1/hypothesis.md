Listeners interpret referential expressions by reasoning about the speaker's communicative goal under discussion rather than assuming the speaker aims to specify the referent's complete identity. When communicating about an object, speakers select an aspect of that object to highlight—favoring distinctive visual features that are uncommon in the visual scene—and choose words that informatively convey that intended aspect to a literal listener. Listeners invert this generative process by jointly inferring the speaker's communicative goal and intended referent, naturally favoring referents whose distinctive features best explain the speaker's choice of topic.
