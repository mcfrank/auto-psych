In communicative reference games, participants exhibit cognitive heterogeneity in recursive Theory-of-Mind depth rather than reasoning at a single uniform level. The population is structured as a discrete mixture of first-order pragmatic listeners (who simulate a speaker reasoning about literal semantics) and second-order pragmatic listeners (who simulate a speaker reasoning about a pragmatic listener), with a mixture proportion parameter governing the relative prevalence of each type. On complex displays with overlapping features, this population mixture produces intermediate choice frequencies that reflect the coexistence of both strategic reasoning depths.
