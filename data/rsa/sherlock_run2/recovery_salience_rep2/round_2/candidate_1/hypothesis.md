Listeners evaluate candidate referents through a family-resemblance typicality prior, expecting speakers to refer to distinctive or atypical objects that minimize feature overlap with other entities in the visual display. This contextual typicality prior governs baseline expectations when no informative word is heard and combines with communicative likelihood during pragmatic Bayesian reference resolution. Rather than treating all referents as equally likely or judging them solely by their own individual feature count, listeners evaluate an object's contextual typicality by how many features it shares with competitor objects in the scene.
