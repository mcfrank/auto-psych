Listeners experience lexical uncertainty regarding which visual feature an utterance picks out, entertaining a small probability that the speaker is referring to an alternative feature present in the visual display. This feature uncertainty modulates the literal semantic applicability of words across candidate referents, conferring higher semantic robustness to referents that possess multiple visual features while still allowing pragmatic implicatures to emerge through recursive speaker reasoning. Baseline expectations on uninformative trials remain uniform in the absence of any informative lexical cue.
