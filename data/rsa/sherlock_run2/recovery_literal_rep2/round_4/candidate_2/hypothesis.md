Listeners in reference games comprise a heterogeneous population of distinct cognitive types rather than a monolithic cohort of identical reasoners. A proportion of the population acts as literal listeners who choose among matching referents purely on the basis of semantic truth, while the remaining proportion acts as depth-1 pragmatic listeners who invert an informative speaker to make scalar implicatures. Observed choices reflect this population mixture of literal and pragmatic listener types alongside baseline familiarization and general response lapses.
