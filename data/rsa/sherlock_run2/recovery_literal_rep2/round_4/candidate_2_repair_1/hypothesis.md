Listeners in reference games comprise a heterogeneous population of distinct cognitive types rather than a monolithic cohort of identical reasoners. A subpopulation of literal listeners selects among truth-conditionally matching referents guided by perceptual feature salience, while pragmatic listeners invert an informative speaker sharing this salience prior to draw scalar implicatures. Observed choices reflect this population mixture of literal and pragmatic listener types across both communicative and prior contexts alongside baseline familiarization.
