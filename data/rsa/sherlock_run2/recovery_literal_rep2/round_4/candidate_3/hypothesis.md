Listeners interpret referring expressions by evaluating how representative or diagnostic each candidate referent is for the spoken description, rather than recursively simulating counterfactual speakers or applying additive feature penalties. When an utterance is heard, listeners judge each matching referent by the likelihood ratio comparing the probability that a speaker intending the target would produce the description against the probability that a speaker intending an alternative distractor in the display would produce it. On prior trials without an informative description, choice is guided by this same representativeness mechanism alongside familiarity base rates.
