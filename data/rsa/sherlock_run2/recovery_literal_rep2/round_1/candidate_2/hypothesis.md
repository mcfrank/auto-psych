Listeners evaluate referent salience by conditioning on the speaker's affective framing: positive or neutral framing increases the prior salience of feature-rich objects, whereas negative framing ('least favorite') inverts this preference so that simpler objects are favored. On displays where a speaker refers to their least favorite object, this sharply contradicts the current best model, which ignores valence and continues to predict a bias toward feature-rich referents. Consequently, listeners hearing an ambiguous description under negative framing shift their choices toward simpler objects rather than feature-rich ones.
