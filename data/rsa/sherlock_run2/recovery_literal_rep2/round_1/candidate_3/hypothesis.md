Speakers incur an utterance cost when using specific features that apply to only a single referent, reflecting the greater cognitive retrieval effort of fine-grained distinguishing descriptors compared to shared features. Pragmatic listeners reason about this cost-sensitive speaker, expecting that a speaker may choose an accessible shared feature even when a specific feature is available. Consequently, hearing a shared feature moderates scalar implicatures according to the trade-off between informativeness and specificity cost.
