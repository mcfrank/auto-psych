Listeners resolve referring expressions through contextual prominence alignment, where the communicative meaning of unmentioned features depends on framing. Under neutral framing, listeners penalize candidate referents with unmentioned features because speakers are expected to provide distinguishing descriptions; under evaluative framing ('least favorite'), unmentioned features act as compounding negative attributes that reverse this penalty to favor feature-rich referents. Visual highlighting (color contrast) provides an orthogonal perceptual prominence boost to matching referents across both prior and communicative contexts.
