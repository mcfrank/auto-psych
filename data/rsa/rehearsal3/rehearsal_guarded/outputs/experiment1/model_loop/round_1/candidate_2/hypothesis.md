Speakers in reference games choose referring expressions by evaluating each feature's distractor elimination power—the proportion of contextual distractors that the feature rules out in the visual scene—rather than naive probability matching. Pragmatic listeners invert this distractor-eliminating speaker, expecting that a speaker intending a referent with unique or highly discriminative features would never use a non-discriminating word shared by the entire display. This mechanism disagrees sharply with the current best model on displays where a heard word is true of all objects, correctly suppressing choices of the unique singleton referent whose unmentioned unique features should have been used instead.
