Listeners interpret referring expressions under lexical uncertainty about which visual feature a heard word designates, where lexical interference is driven by visual feature co-occurrence across objects in the scene. Candidate words and features that appear together on the same referent compete during communication, naturally elevating the perceived plausibility of non-matching distractors on ambiguous displays with high feature overlap while preserving sharp semantic boundaries on unambiguous displays. Choices on uninformative prior trials are governed by visual singleton pop-out, contextual distinctiveness, and feature complexity.
