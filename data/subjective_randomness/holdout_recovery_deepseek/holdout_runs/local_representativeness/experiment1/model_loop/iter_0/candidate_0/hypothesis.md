People judge a sequence as random by accumulating a subjective sense of typicality across its length, where each event contributes a baseline typicality discounted by how far the sequence's head-rate and alternation-rate deviate from a mental prototype. Unlike a strictly length-proportional accumulation, the total subjective impression grows with sequence length raised to a freely inferred exponent: some observers are increasingly swayed by additional tosses (accelerating concern for how much longer sequence is), while others grow less affected the longer the sequence gets (saturating). For a fixed per-event typicality, a mere one-toss difference at short lengths can matter more or less than the same difference at long lengths depending on that single exponent.
