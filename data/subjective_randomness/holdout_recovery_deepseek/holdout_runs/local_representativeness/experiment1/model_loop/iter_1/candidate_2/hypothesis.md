People judge how random a sequence looks by tracking whether each toss
repeats the toss that came right before it, and they weight what happened
more recently more heavily. A sequence reads as random when its recent
tosses keep flipping back and forth (few repeats, near 50-50), and reads
as suspiciously structured when its recent tosses keep repeating the last
one — a long final run, or a run that grows back into a repeating tail,
signals a sticky process rather than a fair coin. Critically, this depends
on *where* the repetition falls: a burst of repetition at the very end
weighs more than an equal burst early on, because the mental count of
run-continuations is recency-weighted. The model flags the louder repeat
tail as non-random and the cleaner-recent sequence as random.
