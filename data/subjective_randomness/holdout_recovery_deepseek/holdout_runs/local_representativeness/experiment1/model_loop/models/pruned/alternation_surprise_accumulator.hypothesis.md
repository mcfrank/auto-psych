People judge how random a sequence is by reading it toss by toss and
tracking how surprising each new outcome feels given the outcome that
came before it: in particular, they hold a mild expectation that outcomes
will alternate rather than repeat, so a long run of the same symbol
repeatedly violates that expectation and accumulates surprise. A sequence
is judged more random when its total accumulated surprise over the run is
close to what a genuinely independent (fair-coin) run would produce, and
less random when it accumulates too little surprise (a tightly forced,
artificially even alternation) or too much (suspiciously long, repeating
streaks).
