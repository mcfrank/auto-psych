Listeners and speakers in reference games operate under Shepard-Tversky similarity crowding, where candidate referents with similar feature profiles and candidate words with similar extensions in the scene group into perceptual and semantic clusters rather than competing as independent tokens. Inside the communicative hierarchy, listeners divide literal and intermediate reference weight across each object's similarity cluster while speakers divide lexical choice weight across co-extensional or similarly extending words true of their intended target, preventing redundant features or duplicate objects from artificially inflating choice probabilities. At the top decision level and on uninformative trials without a heard word, listeners favor perceptually uncrowded pop-out objects over crowded clusters, separating attentional crowding aversion on wordless prior displays from pragmatic speaker rationality on informative utterance displays.
