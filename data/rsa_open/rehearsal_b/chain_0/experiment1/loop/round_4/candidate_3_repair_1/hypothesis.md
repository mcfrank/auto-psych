Speakers choose referring expressions by balancing communicative recovery against the regret of omitting a more informative description available for the target. Listeners invert this regret-averse speaker, attributing underspecified descriptions to minimal referents that lack distinguishing alternatives rather than multi-feature singletons. On displays where a heard word is shared between duplicate minimal targets and multi-feature singletons, this mechanism predicts listeners will strongly favor the minimal targets because the speaker would regret omitting the singletons' distinguishing features, disagreeing sharply with models whose visual singleton biases inflate belief in the complex competitor.
