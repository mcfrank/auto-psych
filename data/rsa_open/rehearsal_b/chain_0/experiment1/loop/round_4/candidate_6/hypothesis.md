Listeners interpret referring expressions by combining depth-1 pragmatic communicative inference with a feature parsimony penalty, favoring candidate referents with the fewest total visual features because speakers provide minimal descriptions for simple targets rather than leaving distinguishing features unmentioned. Speakers communicate by selecting expressions that guide this parsimony-biased listener to the target referent, while spontaneous visual attention on uninformative displays without a word follows contextual visual salience.
