Listeners rely on visual singleton salience during spontaneous visual exploration on uninformative trials, but interpret informative utterances by inverting a speaker who communicates under an unbiased, uniform target prior. In displays where a heard word is true of all objects but some are minimal duplicate targets and others are multi-feature singletons, this mechanism predicts that listeners strongly favor the minimal targets because the speaker would have chosen distinguishing features for the multi-feature singletons, sharply disagreeing with models that project visual singleton biases into communicative intent.
