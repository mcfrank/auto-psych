Listeners interpret referring expressions under referential feature uncertainty, being unsure which visual feature of a candidate referent the speaker intended to pick out. When evaluating literal semantics, listeners weight an object's compatibility by the probability that the speaker chose the heard feature from that object's feature bundle, naturally favoring minimal referents whose features are exhaustively described over cluttered multi-feature competitors. Communicative speakers select expressions expecting this feature-attribution inference, while spontaneous visual attention on uninformative displays defaults to contextually unique singletons.
