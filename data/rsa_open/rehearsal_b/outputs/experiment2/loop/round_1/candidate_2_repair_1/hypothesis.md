People interpret and produce referring expressions by reasoning over perceptual equivalence classes (referential types) rather than raw object tokens: identical visual objects in a scene are perceptually grouped into a single type, and communicative speakers choose utterances to distinguish the target's type from alternative types. Pragmatic listeners invert this type-discriminating speaker while maintaining spontaneous visual attention on contextually unique singletons. In displays where a heard word is universally true of all objects and distractors are identical duplicates, this mechanism predicts listeners will strongly attribute the word to the visual singleton, disagreeing sharply with standard token-based models that impose spurious M-implicature penalties on singletons.
