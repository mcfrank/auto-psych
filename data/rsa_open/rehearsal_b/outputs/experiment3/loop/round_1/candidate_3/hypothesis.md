Communicative speakers select referring expressions by penalizing avoidable ambiguity: candidate words are discounted proportionally to the excess competitors they fail to eliminate compared to the most specific alternative available for that referent. When an object cannot be uniquely named, the speaker communicates without penalty using the most informative description available. Pragmatic listeners invert this ambiguity-sensitive speaker, attributing underspecified words to referents that lack more specific alternatives, while spontaneous visual attention on uninformative displays defaults to contextually unique singletons.
