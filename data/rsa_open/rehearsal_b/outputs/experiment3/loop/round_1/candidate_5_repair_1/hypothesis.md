Listeners interpret referring expressions under referential feature uncertainty, weighting literal semantic compatibility inversely by a referent's total visual feature count, while communicative speakers select expressions expecting this feature-attribution inference. Spontaneous visual attention weights candidate referents by combining discrete singleton salience with continuous contextual distinctiveness (feature contrast relative to all display competitors), addressing empirical failures to account for contextually unique unmentioned features and under-prediction of singleton salience in redundant or large competitor sets. Differences from feature_uncertainty_listener_2: recursion depth remains unchanged (depth 1, listener calls L1); parameter w_distinct is added with prior dist.Normal(0.0, 1.0); no parameters are removed (retaining alpha and w_singleton); and the visual prior is extended by adding w_distinct times the continuous contextual distinctiveness (summed Hamming feature distance across display objects).
