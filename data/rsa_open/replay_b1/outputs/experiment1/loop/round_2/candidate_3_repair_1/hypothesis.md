Listeners interpret referring expressions by recognizing that word-to-feature mappings are subject to uncertainty rather than deterministic one-to-one bindings, inverting a rational speaker while marginalizing across the visual features present in the scene. A referent possessing multiple distinctive features remains robustly identifiable under this feature uncertainty even when a shared descriptor is used, resolving the over-penalization of overspecified competitors. On uninformative displays without an informative word, referent expectations are governed by visual singleton salience and contextual distinctiveness.
