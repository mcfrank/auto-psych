We refine contextual_contrast_listener by incorporating continuous perceptual isolation into the visual referent prior alongside discrete singleton salience, as established in attentional_bottleneck_listener. Speakers choose referring expressions to maximize contextual contrast against visually similar competitors in the immediate display, and listeners invert this contrastive speaker, but referent expectations now weight both discrete singleton status and continuous nearest-neighbor feature distance (perceptual isolation), explaining why completely isolated singletons like empty objects attract greater belief than singletons sharing features with nearby competitors and resolving the discrepancy on empty versus non-empty singletons. Differences from contextual_contrast_listener: recursion depth is unchanged (calls L_contrast); parameter w_isolation is added with prior Normal(0.0, 1.0); the prior logit adds w_isolation multiplied by the object's minimum Hamming distance to all other context objects; no parameters or likelihood terms are removed.
