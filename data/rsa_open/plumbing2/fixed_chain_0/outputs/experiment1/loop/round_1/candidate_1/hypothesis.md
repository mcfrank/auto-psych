Listeners and speakers ground reference in Tverskyan contrastive categorization and Euclidean psychological space: a word's literal prototype fit narrows toward minimal-feature objects in proportion to the fraction of display distractors the word eliminates, while a non-contrastive word shared by every object in the display attenuates the speaker's pragmatic penalty on residual ambiguity. Perceptual salience over referents combines sublinear Euclidean feature distance across the display with singleton uniqueness, visual complexity under evaluative framing, and color contrast. Contrastive diagnosticity is separated from global speaker rationality by contrasting scenes where the heard word eliminates sparse distractors against scenes where the heard word applies to every object in the display.
