# Experiment 1 Rationale: Graded Pragmatic Belief Allocation Across Competitor Structures

## 1. Purpose and Why This Paradigm Generalizes Beyond Earlier Data

All nine experiments in the prior closed campaign tested listener interpretation using **single-click forced choice (`choice`)** over 16 trials per participant. As Yarkoni (2020) emphasizes, theories fitted exclusively to a winner-take-all forced-choice task risk overfitting to the modal decision boundary while mischaracterizing the underlying probabilistic representations. Specifically, when three or four candidate referents appear in a scene, a forced-choice click predominantly reflects which single referent has the highest posterior probability, compressing the entire runner-up hierarchy—the relative credibility assigned to second-, third-, and fourth-choice candidates—into low-frequency tail events that are easily absorbed by a uniform lapse parameter.

To test whether current theories genuinely capture human pragmatic inference **across paradigms**, we transition from discrete forced choice to **simultaneous graded belief allocation (`bet` via the `allocation` Dirichlet-multinomial link, distributing 10 points across the candidate referents on each screen)** across 3- and 4-object scenes (`[3, 3]`, `[4, 3]`, `[4, 4]`) with both utterance-interpretation trials and wordless prior-elicitation trials (`prior_queries: true`).

Why this task and response type matter most now:
1. **Direct measurement of full posterior distributions on every trial:** On a 4-object scene where a heard word applies to three referents $r_1, r_2, r_3$, a 10-point allocation simultaneously reveals the listener's exact graded trade-off between the primary pragmatic target ($r_1$), a partially preempted or moderately confusable competitor ($r_2$), and a strongly preempted or clone competitor ($r_3$), while verifying truth-conditional filtering on the literal non-match ($r_4 = 0$ points).
2. **Simultaneous separation of perceptual priors and recursive implicature:** Pairing wordless prior allocations (`heard = null`) with utterance-conditioned allocations (`heard = u`) within the same scene geometry forces every theory to account for both baseline referent salience (how points are distributed before any word is spoken) and how hearing a word shifts that distribution across competitors.
3. **Near-ceiling information gain and verified parameter recovery:** Across the 15 theories in the comparison set (maximum possible entropy $\log_2(15) = 3.907$ bits), full EIG evaluation (`reports/eig_b0b54591df50.json`, 64 posterior draws $\times$ 1000 scenarios) shows that this paradigm achieves **3.737 bits of expected information gain** ($p_{\text{correct}} = 0.74$, $0.01869$ bits/\$, $2.91\times$ the required floor of $0.00643$ bits/\$, outperforming the closed-campaign forced-choice reference on both total bits [$3.737$ vs. $3.427$] and bits per dollar [$0.01869$ vs. $0.01285$]). Joint parameter recovery (`reports/link-check_b0b54591df50.json`) converged across all 3 replicates with **0 misses across all 9 theory and `allocation` link parameters** (`alpha`, `w_singleton`, `w_distinct`, `w_features`, `w_color`, `w_familiar`, `kappa`, `lapse`, `temperature`).

## 2. Theoretical Disagreements Targeted

The six live theories (alongside the nine baseline bar theories) embody fundamentally different computational mechanisms for reference resolution that make similar modal predictions on simple forced-choice trials but diverge sharply in their full graded distributions across 3- and 4-object scenes:

### Disagreement A: Heuristic Oddity / Preemption vs. Recursive Pragmatic Inference
- **`oddity_heuristic_listener`** vs. **`rsa_l1` / `rsa_l2` / `isolated_graded_costly_l3`**:
  - `oddity_heuristic_listener` has no recursive Theory of Mind; it weights literal matches solely by a binary indicator `is_singleton` ($1$ if the object has no exact duplicate in the scene, $0$ if it has an identical twin). Consequently, **in any scene where all 3 or 4 objects are distinct (`is_singleton = 1` for all items), `oddity_heuristic_listener` collapses completely to the `literal_listener`**, predicting a flat, equal division of points (e.g., $5\text{--}5$ across two matching objects or $3.3\text{--}3.3\text{--}3.3$ across three matching objects) regardless of whether one object could have been uniquely named by another feature!
  - Furthermore, in scenes containing two identical simple objects (e.g., $r_1 = [0, 0, 1]$, $r_2 = [0, 0, 1]$) and a feature-rich singleton ($r_3 = [1, 1, 1]$) when the speaker says the shared feature $u_2$ (`[*, *, 1]`), recursive RSA models (`rsa_l1`, `rsa_l2`) penalize $r_3$ because the speaker could have used $u_0$ or $u_1$ to single it out, whereas `oddity_heuristic_listener` predicts shifting points **toward** the feature-rich singleton $r_3$ simply because $r_1$ and $r_2$ are duplicates (`is_singleton = 0`).
- **`lexical_preemption_heuristic`** vs. **Recursive RSA (`rsa_l1`, `rsa_l2`, `isolated_graded_costly_l3`)**:
  - `lexical_preemption_heuristic` penalizes a candidate referent only if it possesses an unmentioned feature whose extension in the display is **strictly equal to 1** (`is_dist = (extension == 1.0)`).
  - In a 4-object scene where the heard word $u$ applies to 3 objects ($r_1, r_2, r_3$), suppose $r_1$ has only $u$ (`extension = 3`), $r_2$ also possesses a feature $u'$ shared by 2 objects (`extension = 2`), and $r_3$ possesses a unique feature $u''$ (`extension = 1`). Recursive RSA predicts a strictly ordered 3-tier point allocation ($r_1 > r_2 > r_3$) because $u'$ ($L_0 = 1/2$) is more informative than $u$ ($L_0 = 1/3$). By contrast, `lexical_preemption_heuristic` assigns zero penalty to $u'$ (`extension = 2 != 1`), predicting an equal split between $r_1$ and $r_2$ ($r_1 = r_2 > r_3$).

### Disagreement B: Competitor-Confusion Avoidance vs. Standard Informativity
- **`competitor_confusion_speaker`** vs. **`rsa_l1` / `rsa_l2`**:
  - `competitor_confusion_speaker` posits that speakers penalize candidate utterances proportionally to `confusion[u, r] = sum_{r' != r} lex[u, r'] * exp(-dist(r, r'))`, where `dist(r, r')` is the Hamming distance between $r$ and competitor $r'$.
  - In 4-object scenes where a heard word $u$ applies to candidates that have identical feature counts and identical alternative-utterance informativity, `rsa_l1` and `rsa_l2` predict equal point allocations between those candidates. `competitor_confusion_speaker`, however, predicts that listeners will allocate substantially more points to a candidate whose $u$-sharing competitors are visually distant (large Hamming distance) than to a candidate whose $u$-sharing competitors are near-clones (Hamming distance 1).
  - On prior trials (`heard = null`), `rsa_l1` and `rsa_l2` predict a uniform allocation ($2.5\text{--}2.5\text{--}2.5\text{--}2.5$), whereas `competitor_confusion_speaker` predicts higher point allocations for objects with lower total exponential similarity $\sum_{r' \neq r} e^{-\text{dist}(r, r')}$ to the rest of the display.

### Disagreement C: Perceptual Isolation & Graded Truth vs. Additive Salience Priors
- **`isolated_graded_costly_l3`** (and `familiar_isolated_graded_costly_l2`, `softmax_surprisal_isolated_costly_l4`) vs. **`rsa_l2_singleton_feat_color_valence_l0`** (and `evaluative_prominence_l2`):
  - **Minimum vs. Summed Hamming Distance:** `isolated_graded_costly_l3` defines visual pop-out by `object_isolation`, the **minimum** Hamming distance to any single neighbor ($\min_{r' \neq r} \text{dist}(r, r')$). Thus, an object with even one close neighbor (distance 1) has isolation 1 regardless of how far it is from the other two objects, whereas an object at distance $\ge 2$ from all neighbors gets a large isolation boost. By contrast, `rsa_l2_singleton_feat_color_valence_l0` combines a binary singleton indicator (`is_singleton`) with `distinct`, the **sum** of Hamming distances to all other objects ($\sum_{r'} \text{dist}(r, r')$).
  - **Graded Truth Dilution vs. Feature-Count Prominence:** `rsa_l2_singleton_feat_color_valence_l0` and `evaluative_prominence_l2` weight feature-rich objects *positively* in the prior (`w_features * feature_count`) while keeping literal semantics `L0` binary and un-diluted. Conversely, `isolated_graded_costly_l3` scales an object's literal truth applicability in `L0` by $\exp(-\gamma (\text{feature\_count} - 1))$, which compounds across three recursive tiers (`L1 -> L2 -> L3`) to penalize multi-feature objects even when their extra features are shared across the scene, while also penalizing broad-extension words via `cost_weight * ext`.

### Disagreement D: Recursion Depth (`L0` vs. `L1` vs. `L2` vs. `L3` / `L4`)
- In 3- and 4-object chain scenes (e.g., $r_1 = \{f_1\}$, $r_2 = \{f_1, f_2\}$, $r_3 = \{f_2, f_3\}$, $r_4 = \{f_3, f_4\}$), hearing $f_2$ or $f_3$ separates depth-1 (`rsa_l1`), depth-2 (`rsa_l2`), depth-3 (`isolated_graded_costly_l3`), and depth-4 (`softmax_surprisal_isolated_costly_l4`) listeners by how sharply probability mass propagates along the chain of mutual exclusivity.

## 3. Summary of Expected Predictions by Theory

| Theory | Prior Trials (`heard = null`) | Distinct-Object Scenes (All Singletons) | Scenes with Clones & Near-Clones | Multi-Feature Target vs. Simple Target |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`competitor_confusion_speaker`** | Favors objects with lowest exponential similarity $\sum_{r'\neq r} e^{-d(r,r')}$ to context | Favors candidates whose $u$-sharing competitors are distant in Hamming space | Strongly penalizes words shared with clones ($d=0$) or near-clones ($d=1$) | Neutral on feature count per se; driven by pairwise competitor distance |
| **`isolated_graded_costly_l3`** | Favors objects with highest **minimum** Hamming distance $\min_{r'\neq r} d(r,r')$ | Depth-3 recursion + utterance extension cost (`cost_weight * ext`) | Clones drop minimum isolation to $0$; isolated singletons ($d_{\min} \ge 2$) strongly favored | Graded truth dilution $e^{-\gamma(fc-1)}$ in `L0` compounds over 3 tiers to penalize high-$fc$ items |
| **`oddity_heuristic_listener`** | Concentrates points on unique singletons; **uniform** if all objects are distinct | **Completely flat/literal** across all matching objects (no pragmatic implicature) | Shifts points onto any unique singleton ($is\_singleton=1$) over duplicate clones ($0$) | Can favor a feature-rich singleton $[1,1,1]$ over clones $[0,0,1],[0,0,1]$ (anti-implicature) |
| **`rsa_l2_singleton_feat_color_valence_l0`** | Combines `is_singleton`, **summed** Hamming distance, and positive `feature_count` weight | Depth-2 RSA modulated by summed Hamming distinctiveness and feature complexity | Discrete prior boost for singletons + continuous boost for summed feature contrast | Positive prior preference (`w_features > 0`) for feature-elaborated objects; binary `L0` |
| **`rsa_l2`** | Strictly uniform ($2.5$ pts each on 4 objects; $3.3$ pts on 3 objects) | Depth-2 recursive implicature; resolves 2-step ("complex") reference chains | Treats identical clones symmetrically with $1/k$ split of their combined mass | Penalizes multi-feature objects only when their extra features are more informative in `L1` |
| **`rsa_l1`** | Strictly uniform across all objects | Depth-1 scalar implicature; does not complete 2-step chain eliminations | Standard 1-step speaker informativity based on `L0` extension size | Penalizes objects that have alternative words with smaller literal extension |
| **`literal_listener`** (bar) | Strictly uniform across all objects | Splits all 10 points equally among all objects possessing the heard feature | Equal point share per matching object regardless of competitors | Zero sensitivity to unmentioned features |
| **`lexical_preemption_heuristic`** (bar) | Strictly uniform across all objects | Penalizes candidates **only** if they have another feature with `extension == 1` | Ignores alternative features with `extension == 2` in 4-object scenes ($r_1 = r_2 > r_3$) | Step-function penalty for unique labels only; no multi-step recursion |
| **`rsa_l1_salience` / `rsa_l1_shared_prior` / `evaluative_prominence_l2`** (bar) | Monotonic in `feature_count` alone (ignores singleton status and Hamming isolation) | Depth-1 or Depth-2 implicature tilted toward high-feature-count objects | Identical prior for clones and singletons if they have the same `feature_count` | Prior favors high `feature_count`, partially offsetting scalar implicature |
| **`familiar_isolated_graded_costly_l2` / `softmax_surprisal_isolated_costly_l4`** (bar) | Minimum Hamming isolation (+ feature surprisal in `l4`) | Depth-2 (`l2`) vs. Depth-4 with power-law belief sharpening `beta` (`l4`) | Strong isolation preference; `l4` adds cumulative feature rarity (`surprisal`) | Graded truth dilution (`gamma`) + extension cost (`cost_weight`) |
